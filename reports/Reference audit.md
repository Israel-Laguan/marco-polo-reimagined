# Reference audit for The Book of the Million

The retelling follows *Le Devisement dou monde*, also known in English as *The Description of the World* and in Italian as *Il Milione*. In the prologue, Marco Polo is the traveller who recounted what he saw and heard, and Rustichello da Pisa is the writer he had set down the account. The 1903 Henry Yule translation revised by Henri Cordier is the working text. Cordier’s 1920 *Notes and Addenda* is a separate work. [Prologue of BnF fr. 1116, Eusebi’s edition, p. 35](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-224-6/978-88-6969-224-6-ch-02_39X5zyK.pdf#page=11)

## Volume titles and subjects

## Corrections

- The six printed references to editions or translations of the medieval travel account now credit Polo and Rustichello. Eusebi’s 2018 edition identifies Mario Eusebi’s text of the Franco-Italian **F** manuscript and Eugenio Burgio’s separate glossary and indexes. Samuela Simion’s 2019 edition covers Venetian redaction **V**. Moule and Pelliot’s 1938 edition combines manuscripts in its English translation and prints the transcription of the Toledo Latin **Z** witness separately. [2018 publisher record](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/en/edizioni/libri/978-88-6969-224-6/), [2019 publisher record](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/it/edizioni4/libri/978-88-6969-322-9/), [1938 catalog record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA27447834)
- Giovanni Battista Ramusio is responsible for the 1559 Italian redaction cited in all three volumes. The notes now cite the Burgio–Simion critical edition. Volume III’s comparative survey now gives an edition for Odoric of Pordenone’s travels. [Ramusio edition](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/it/edizioni/libri/978-88-6969-00-06/), [Odoric edition](https://kansalliskirjasto.finna.fi/Record/fikka.2031232)
- Volume III’s Waṣṣāf notes cited Joseph von Hammer-Purgstall’s 1856 German edition. Yule’s note on Ghazan’s later embassy names Elliot and Dowson, vol. 3, p. 47. The corrected entry names Waṣṣāf’s extracts in that English edition. The Volume III notes also identify the actual author and work in David Cook’s translated collection of Mamluk chronicles. [Waṣṣāf edition](https://archive.org/details/historyofindiaas03elli), [Cook edition contents](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780429769702_A38996117/preview-9780429769702_A38996117.pdf#page=6)
- Igor de Rachewiltz translated and annotated the *Secret History of the Mongols*. Ibn Juzayy wrote up Ibn Baṭṭūṭa’s account. The names Ibn Baṭṭūṭa, Rashīd al-Dīn and ʿAbd al-Muʾmin ibn ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq al-Baghdādī now print in their reading order. [Secret History catalog](https://iucat.iu.edu/iub/5729305), [Ibn Baṭṭūṭa manuscript record](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021666192/)

- The signed Treccani biographies now begin with Giuseppe Gullino. “Polo, Marco” and “Polo, Matteo” are the entry titles and identify the people he writes about. The Champa notes use the Yuan annals for the campaign and identify Wolters and Hall as regional context. [Marco Polo entry](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marco-polo_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Matteo Polo entry](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/matteo-polo_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

## Outstanding source checks

- The exact 1959 edition of Tao Zongyi and the 2015 edition of Wu Zimu were identified through bibliographies or related catalog records. Check the print copies for edition-specific wording.
- Dutschke’s dissertation was identified through published bibliographies. Confirm any detail about the Pipino manuscript families against the dissertation before citing a page.
- For the Ethiopian embassy, the Cook preview identifies the translated work and its author. Checking the translation’s page images and the Arabic would establish the wording independently. The proposed link to Polo’s bishop remains conjectural.
- The claim about diversion of frankincense and the account of the monopoly still need a locator in the edition of *Marāṣid al-iṭṭilāʿ*. The work abridges Yāqūt and constitutes a single line of textual evidence.
- The notes also name older works through Yule–Cordier. Those citations lead readers to the edition actually consulted. Claims stated without a locator still need a passage-level check before they can be treated as direct corroboration.
