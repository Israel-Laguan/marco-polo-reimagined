A character reference sheet of the three adult daughters named in Marco Polo's will of 1324. Show exactly three women, full-length and discrete, once each on one blank sheet, left to right: Fantina, Bellela, and Moreta. Use the same style and scale for all three with clear room between their mantles, equal open margins above and below, and every shoe and hem entirely in frame. No labels or panels.

Face and body conventions: the three sisters share dark hair and dark eyes, straight brows and a family resemblance in the nose and jaw, with clearly different faces and builds. Fantina, the eldest daughter, has an oval face and a tall upright stance, in a deep blue gown. Bellela has a rounder face and more compact silhouette, in a muted wine gown. Moreta has a slender face and a lightly turned, attentive posture, in a soft olive gown. Exact ages and relative height are visual conventions only. All three look like grown women.

Dress convention (Venice, January 1324): each wears a long gown of good plain cloth and a mantle. Fantina and Bellela are married and wear folded white linen veils over fitted wimples. Moreta is unmarried at the date of the will, and wears a simple short light linen veil over her arranged hair, without the enclosing wimple selected for her sisters.
