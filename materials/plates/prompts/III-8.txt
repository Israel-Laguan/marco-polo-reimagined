4:3 landscape illustration in the thirteenth-century Baghdad school al-Wasiti Maqamat manuscript idiom: bold fine ink contours, flat opaque earth, red and blue-green pigments, compact expressive figures, shallow stacked space on lightly aged manuscript paper. At old mainland Hormuz in noon heat, a few people rest in the shade near simple buildings topped by tall wind-catcher towers, with high vents open to breezes. Beyond on the Gulf a small ship is drawn after a 1237 comparative source: double-ended planked hull with raked stem and stern, one mast with a masthead lookout box, a little domed cabin, stern rudder worked by lines and four-fluked anchor hanging at the bow; crew haul ropes. The exact older town, towers and ships are speculative; surviving wind-catchers are later.
