Paint in the manner of a Yuan court handscroll, using Liu Guandao's Khubilai Khan Hunting and the Yuan imperial portrait album. Use fine continuous ink contours, pale woven silk with visible open ground, thin washes of grey-green and russet in the setting, chalky ivory clothing, quiet three-quarter faces, shallow stacked perspective and fine straight ruled timber lines. This New Year procession is late Yuan-period court idiom, with neither later Forbidden City grandeur nor Western oil modelling.

Scene: the White Feast at Cambaluc (Dadu), a representative New Year about 1285. Follow one continuous vista along a low paved terrace before a Yuan timber hall. Compose a subdued dark-grey tiled roof, short uplift at the ridge corners, latticed doors, plain red-brown timber and a raised stone platform. This is a generic Yuan hall based on C38, not a detailed reconstruction of Daming Hall. The hall is an open-sided backdrop to the central visible group and does not take over the picture.

Cublay watches from a low cushioned seat on the terrace, his broad round and ruddy face clearly visible, dark eyes, fine moustache and small beard, very heavy, around seventy, in the white crossed-collar feast robe with short white outer sleeves over long white inner sleeves, fur at his wrists, looped braids behind his ears and a small pale rounded cap with a narrow brim and a knob. Use the accepted Kublai sheet for his face, then age him. Do not show Chabi or any Polo. Every human visible wears white, including each cap and under-sleeve. Use serene spacing, about seven attendants or visitors. One anonymous married noblewoman, small in group, wears the tall narrow flared flat-top boqta shape in white cloth and ivory pearls, adapting C2's shape to this all-white day. No red hats or coloured human clothing. Animal trappings and wood may have muted colour.

In the foremost clear plane place a single unmistakable set of nine identical closed, pale silk gift parcels on a plain low offering platform. Place them as a simple clearly readable three-by-three arrangement, exactly three parcels in each of three staggered rows. All nine upper surfaces and countable separations are visible. Each small tied parcel is unmarked. This is one representative grouping from the Ramusio description.

Farther along the same broad terrace two separate Asian elephants and two distinct Bactrian camels pass at a walk, all clearly recognisable and fully in frame. The elephants' plain coffers are strapped over low rich warm-red or ivory cloth, with a few gold winged-lion or griffin-in-roundel motifs taken from C8. The camels carry small unlabelled packs. No other parade animals, altar, tablet, or massed spectators. Keep the scene integrated, with the gift set and seated face prominent and the four animals in the same line of sight.

Use the accepted Kublai sheet for identity, the Met fragment for layered court garment shapes (translate all robes to white), and the Cleveland cloth for animal textile ornament only. Compose entirely new staging.

Format: wide 2:1 landscape handscroll. Place the seated ruler, every head, animal feet, and the offering platform well within the central sixty-five percent of the image height so a 2:1 crop is safe if needed. Pale silk, light even ageing, open edges without frames or mounting seams.

Strictly avoid: all lettering, writing, pseudo-script, inscriptions, numbers, caption, seal, red stamp, signature, watermark or colophon anywhere; a ceremonial inscribed tablet, black or coloured robes and hats, yellow imperial gown, crown, Qing dragon robes, queues, Ming palace roof shapes, Chabi, a large host, devotional subject, damaged scroll, photographic realism or 3D shading.
