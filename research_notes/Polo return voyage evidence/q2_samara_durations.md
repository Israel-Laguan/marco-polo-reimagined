## What do the principal witnesses say about the island after three months, the five-month detention and the 18 months?

### Takeaway
F, Ramusio and Yule's composite all agree on "about three months" to an island called **Java** and "eighteen months" more on the Sea of India before reaching Argon's land (V and VB differ in the apparatus). The five-month stay is **not** in the Prologue at all. It is in the Book III chapter on Samara/Samatra, as a first-person stay ("I myself, Marc Pol") caused by weather and contrary winds. VB gives six months there.

### Cited Findings
- F (Benedetto ch. XIX): "najerent bien trois mois … une ysle, qui est ver midi, qui a non Java … najerent por la mer de Indie bien xviii mois avant que il fuissent venus la ou il volient aler." Provisions "por II ans". Benedetto's apparatus shows the MS has "X anz" here, which he emends. [DIRECT] — [Benedetto 1928 OCR](https://archive.org/download/marcopolomilionebenedetto/marcopolomilionebenedetto_djvu.txt)
- M-P ch. 18 n. 3: "por .x. anz" in F; "FA, FB, TA, LT, VA, P, V, L, VL, R all read 'two'". This concerns the provisions, not the voyage length. [APPARATUS] — [Moule–Pelliot I p. 90](https://archive.org/download/descriptionofwor01polo/descriptionofwor01polo_djvu.txt)
- M-P ch. 18 n. 1 to "eighteen": "V: nuoue VB: [?zo]rni disenoue". The OCR is damaged. V seems to read "nine", and VB a "nineteen" whose unit cannot be read. No other variant is recorded. [APPARATUS, OCR-damaged] — [Moule–Pelliot I p. 91](https://archive.org/download/descriptionofwor01polo/descriptionofwor01polo_djvu.txt)
- Ramusio: "circa tre mesi … Iaua … mesi disdotto auanti che potessero arriuare al paese del Re Argon"; provisions "per duoi anni". [DIRECT, 1574 reprint] — [Ramusio II 1574 OCR](https://archive.org/download/secundovolumedel00ramu/secundovolumedel00ramu_djvu.txt)
- Samara/Samatra, F: "je meisme, Marc Pol, hi demorai por v mois, por le tens que ne nos lasoit aler nostre voie." Z: ".v. mensibus". Ramusio: "cinque mesi per il tempo contrario". M-P n. 1: "VB: 'six'". [DIRECT for F and R; APPARATUS for Z and VB] — [Benedetto CLXVIII](https://archive.org/download/marcopolomilionebenedetto/marcopolomilionebenedetto_djvu.txt); [M-P p. 373](https://archive.org/download/descriptionofwor01polo/descriptionofwor01polo_djvu.txt)
- Yule–Cordier vol. I p. 34–35, Prologue ch. 18, gives three months and eighteen months more. Vol. II p. 292 says "detained five months by the weather". [DIRECT] — `book/refs/yule-vol1.txt` l. 14005–11; `yule-vol2.txt` l. 15989–96

### Inferences
- The "two years" in the witnesses is the provisioning ("necessaries for two years"; F's MS "x anz"). It is not a voyage length. Yule's own phrase "two years or upwards passed" (Intro §21) is his summary, not a reading of the text.

### Gaps
- No direct check of Z, P, TA or the 1559 Ramusio for the Prologue durations, and no reading of Eusebi or of the fr. 1116 images on Gallica. The V/VB variants are known only from damaged OCR of M-P's apparatus.

## Is identifying the Prologue's island and delay with the Samara stay explicit in any witness?

### Takeaway
No witness I read links the two. The Prologue names only "Java", with no stop or delay, and the Samara chapter does not mention Cocachin, the embassy or the fleet. The identification is editorial. Yule makes it explicitly: his note 4 says Java means Sumatra "as will appear hereafter", and his Introduction adds 3 + 5 + 18 months. Pelliot's position was not inspected.

### Cited Findings
- Yule, Prologue ch. 18 n. 4: "_I.e._ in this case Sumatra, as will appear hereafter." [DIRECT] — `yule-vol1.txt` l. 14094 (vol. I p. 35 n. 4)
- Yule, Introduction §21 n. [19]: "The voyage to Sumatra occupied three months (vol. i. p. 34); they were five months detained there (ii. 292); and the remainder of the voyage extended to eighteen more (i. 35),—twenty-six months in all." [DIRECT] — `yule-vol1.txt` l. 6254–58
- In both F and Ramusio the Samara chapter is a first-person or Marco-centred stay with "companions" or "2,000 men". It does not say this was the princess's fleet. [DIRECT] — Benedetto CLXVIII; Ramusio cap. 15
- The Yule index entry "detained five months in Sumatra, stockade party against wild people, 292" sits under Polo's biography, not under Cocachin. [DIRECT] — `yule-vol1.txt` l. 41817

### Inferences
- The identification is reasonable: the island is on the right route, the stay is in the first person, and the 2,000 men match a large convoy, not a merchant party. Still, it is a reconstruction. Marco could in principle have been in Sumatra on another voyage, although no witness says so.

### Gaps
- Pelliot, *Notes on Marco Polo* ("Cocacin", "Samara/Sumatra" entries) was not reached, so I cannot say whether Pelliot accepts, qualifies or dates the identification.

## Did Yule count the five months inside or on top of the 18 months? How do other editors read it?

### Takeaway
Yule's main reckoning is **additive**: 3 + 5 + 18 = 26 months, with a sailing in January 1292 and Ghazan's camp in March 1294. He then offers an **alternative**: if the arrival in Persia came earlier, the 18 months "must _include_" the five-month stop. He does not choose between them. The project registry entry S18-F3 ("the 18 months probably include it") overstates his position. The witnesses give no basis for either reading: the 18 months run from leaving "that island" (F) or "la detta Isola" (R). No witness I read counts 18 months from the start or gives a "two years" voyage.

### Cited Findings
- Yule, Intro §21 n. [19], additive: "twenty-six months in all … They remain in port till September (five months) … reach Hormuz in the winter, and the camp of … Gházán … in March, twenty-six months from their departure." [DIRECT] — `yule-vol1.txt` l. 6254–71
- Same note, alternative: "the five months' stoppage in Sumatra _must_ have been in the S.W. Monsoon; and if the arrival in Persia is put earlier, Polo's numbers can scarcely be held to. Or, the eighteen months mentioned at vol. i. p. 35, must _include_ the five months' stoppage. We may then suppose that they reached Hormuz about November 1293." [DIRECT] — `yule-vol1.txt` l. 6275–82
- Yule's narrative wording is hedged: "They appear to have sailed from the port of Zayton … in the beginning of 1292 … two years or upwards passed." [DIRECT] — `yule-vol1.txt` l. 5998–6008
- Registry `book/plans/registry/facts.md` row S18-F3 says "Yule: … the 18 months probably include it". This misreports note [19], where inclusion is the fallback, not the preferred reading. [DIRECT, local]

### Inferences
- Read literally ("then they set out from that island and sailed … eighteen months"), F counts the 18 months from leaving Java. If the five months were spent at that same island, the plain sense is additive, which is Yule's first reading. The inclusive reading is a harmonisation driven by the date of arrival in Persia, not by the text.

### Gaps
- Pelliot (Notes, "Cocacin"), Moule (M-P vol. I introduction), Benedetto's commentary, Latham (Penguin 1958) and Kinoshita (2016) were not inspected on this point. I found no evidence either way that any of them reads the duration differently. Do not attribute positions to them.

## Which Samara details are Ramusio-only?

### Takeaway
Almost none of the camp is Ramusio-only. **F itself** has the landing and the "castles of wood and logs" against the man-eaters. **Z** (Latin, Toledo) has the ~2,000 men, the ditches ending on the shore, the five *pergama vel trebeschas* (bertesche) on the ditches, the plentiful timber and the trade in victuals. Ramusio reproduces Z here. The cure for dropsy is in Z, V and R. Red and white wine is in F. What remains R-specific, as far as I checked, is the coconut "as big as a man's head" (M-P's margin assigns head-size to other sigla, so it may not be R-only either) and the coconut liquid "better and more delicate than wine". The current III-3 and III-9 drafts wrongly say "Ramusio-only" and "in every version".

### Cited Findings
- F: "Nos desendimes des nes et feimes en terre chastiaus de fust et de busches; et en celz castiaus demoravames por doutance de cel mauvais homes bestiaus que menuient les homes." [DIRECT] — Benedetto CLXVIII p. 172
- Z (as quoted by Benedetto): "cum circa duo milia hominum … fecit fodi fossata magna … quinque pergama vel trebeschas … mercabantur cum istis de victualibus … nam fiebat fiducia inter eos — (riprodotto in R)." [APPARATUS] — Benedetto CLXVIII n. a), p. ~173
- Benedetto's apparatus also reports that Pauthier (1865, p. 572) judged the defensive arrangement "un développement postérieur à la rédaction primitive" that could only have come from Marco himself. [APPARATUS, reported by Benedetto, intro p. CLXI n.] — [Benedetto OCR](https://archive.org/download/marcopolomilionebenedetto/marcopolomilionebenedetto_djvu.txt)
- M-P marks the 2,000 men, five castles, ditches and trade with "z" in the margin (and "p", "lt" on some lines). [APPARATUS/OCR] — [M-P p. 373](https://archive.org/download/descriptionofwor01polo/descriptionofwor01polo_djvu.txt)
- Wine cures dropsy: Z "liberat ydropicos et habentes tysicum et splengiam"; also L and V; "R uguale a V". [APPARATUS] — Benedetto CLXVIII n. b)
- F wine: "il en i a de blance e de vermoille"; coconuts "mout groses et bonnes". [DIRECT] — Benedetto p. 173
- Ramusio coconuts: "grosse come è il capo dell'huomo … di sapor miglior, et più delicato, che il vino". [DIRECT, 1574] — Ramusio cap. 15
- Yule brackets the camp as Ramusio and translates the bertesche as "bulwarks or stockades of timber". Because his base is Pauthier, he did not credit F's "chastiaus de fust" as the older witness of the forts. [DIRECT] — `yule-vol2.txt` l. 15995–99; bracket convention at `yule-vol1.txt` l. 12300

### Inferences
- The safe description: forts of timber appear in F; the larger camp (about 2,000 men, ditches, five timber towers, local trade) appears in Z and in Ramusio, which follows Z. The coconut superlatives are in Ramusio, and their distribution in other witnesses is unverified.
- The manuscript's "toddy from the gomuti palm" is Yule's modern identification (note 3), not the text.

### Gaps
- I could not read the italics boundaries in M-P's OCR, so I could not confirm which siglum carries "as large as a man's head". P and TA content was not checked.

## Which ports are named on the outward leg and the return?

### Takeaway
No Prologue witness I read names the departure port. F's Prologue says only "the land of the great Kaan"; Ramusio and Yule's composite are the same. **Zayton is Yule's inference** ("appear to have sailed from the port of Zayton"). The Prologue's only named stop is "Java". The Samara stay comes from Book III. Ferlec, Basman, Samara and Fansur are named as the kingdoms of the Java the Less description, not as an itinerary. Hormuz as landfall is also Yule's inference ("which they probably did on landing at Hormuz").

### Cited Findings
- Yule: "They appear to have sailed from the port of Zayton … in the beginning of 1292." [DIRECT] — `yule-vol1.txt` l. 5998–6000
- Yule, Prologue n. 6: "on learning Arghún's death (which they probably did on landing at Hormuz)". [DIRECT] — `yule-vol1.txt` l. 14121–22
- In the M-P Prologue OCR (ch. 18) and in Benedetto ch. XIX, no Zaiton/Caiton appears. "Caiton" occurs only in the Book II/III chapters. [DIRECT, grep-based] — M-P OCR; Benedetto OCR
- F Bk III gives the Java the Less kingdoms in order: Ferlec, Basman, Samara (MS *samara*), Dagroian, Lambri, Fansur. [DIRECT] — M-P ch. 165–170; Benedetto CLXVI–CLXX

### Inferences
- Ceylon, Maabar, Kayal and Western India as calls of 1291–93 are a reconstruction from the order of Book III plus monsoon timing (Yule's "touching (perhaps) at Ceylon, at Kayal"). They are not stated stops.

### Gaps
- I did not check whether Z or P adds a departure port. The Simion–Burgio Ramusio edition was not consulted.

## Suggested formulations (for the editor; manuscript not edited)

**III-3 (Samara), replacing "Ramusio-only" and "in every version":**
> In the French text Marco says that he himself stayed five months in Samara because the weather would not let them go on, and that "we" came down from the ships and built castles of wood and logs on land for fear of the man-eaters. The Latin Toledo manuscript (Z), which Ramusio follows here, enlarges the camp: about two thousand men, great ditches running down to the shore at either end, five timber towers above them, and islanders who traded food with the party once trust was established. One Venetian copy (VB) makes the stay six months.

Note [10] fallback: "Forts of timber: F. 2,000 men, ditches, five towers, local trade: Z and Ramusio (Benedetto 1928, ch. CLXVIII and apparatus; Moule–Pelliot ch. 167). Coconut 'as big as a man's head' and 'better than wine': Ramusio (bracketed in Yule); other witnesses unverified."

**III-9 (Cocachin return):**
> The Prologue counts about three months to an island it calls Java, then eighteen months more on the Sea of India. It names no port of departure and mentions no stop at that island. Yule identified the island with Sumatra and the five-month Samara stay of Book III with this voyage. On his first reckoning that makes 3 + 5 + 18 = twenty-six months; if the fleet reached Persia earlier, he allowed, the eighteen months must include the five. Zayton as the port of sailing is his inference.

Note fallback: "Prologue F/R: 3 months, 18 months (V, VB vary: M-P I p. 91 n. 1). Five months: Bk III Samara only. Identification and the 26-month total: Yule, Intro §21 n. 19. 'Two years' in the witnesses is provisions, not voyage length."

**Narrowest safe wording if none of the above can be re-checked:** "The Book gives about three months to 'Java' and eighteen months more to Persia. In another chapter Marco says he waited five months in Samara for the weather. Editors since Yule join the two, and whether the five months fall within the eighteen is unclear."

**Not inspected (state in notes):** BnF fr. 1116 on Gallica (folios), Eusebi 2010, Ronchi 1982, the 1559 Ramusio first edition, the Simion–Burgio digital Ramusio, Z and P in their own editions, Pelliot's *Notes*, Latham, Kinoshita.
