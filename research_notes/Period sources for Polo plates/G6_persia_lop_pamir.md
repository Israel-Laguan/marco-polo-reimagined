# G6 — Persian town and caravan life, the Lop desert, the Pamir and Badakhshan, c. 1260–1295: period image sources

Confidence labels: **OPENED** = I opened it and confirmed it. **CATALOGUE** = I found it in a catalogue or citation but did not open it. **MEMORY** = from memory, not checked. **NEW** = published after 2013.
Session budget: about 15 tool calls. Several leads remain unchecked; they are listed under Gaps.

## Which sources most likely hold a dated, viewable image from 1260–1295? (ranked)

### Takeaway
Only one source is both dated by colophon inside the window and confirmed scriptable: the Munich Qazwini, BSB Cod.arab. 464 (Wasit, 1280), with an open IIIF manifest of 428 canvases. Its subjects are cosmographic "wonders" rather than caravans, but its figures, dress, animals and buildings are datable to 1280. The Morgan Manafiʿ (Maragha, 1297–1300) is just outside the window. It is the best Ilkhanid source for camels, animals and dress, but it can only be viewed in Morgan's viewer. For the Lop desert, the Pamir and Badakhshan there is no in-window imagery. Use dated early-20th-century Stein photographs for terrain only.

### Cited Findings

**1. BSB Cod.arab. 464 — al-Qazwīnī, ʿAǧāʾib al-maḫlūqāt (OPENED, IIIF confirmed)**
- (a) Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich. The title is *Kitāb ʿAǧāʾib al-maḫlūqāt wa-ġarāʾib al-mauǧūdāt*, by Zakarīyā ibn Muḥammad al-Qazwīnī (1203–1283). The scribe is Muḥammad ibn Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī. Its older shelfmarks are Cimelia 98 and Quatremère 14. BSB identifier: bsb00037026. URN: urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb00037026-7 — [DDB record](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/BQHHGMBPMO4EDGXRUPFX7T4JDZYKKFRW). IIIF manifest, tested and returning HTTP 200 JSON with 428 canvases: `https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/presentation/v2/bsb00037026/manifest`. Image pattern: `https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb00037026_00001/full/full/0/default.jpg`. Resolver: http://mdz-nbn-resolving.de/urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb00037026-7.
- (b) The description says it was made at Wāsiṭ in 1280, during the author's lifetime and three years before his death. It calls this the oldest known copy and says it has "über 400 Miniaturen und Zeichnungen" — [DDB](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/BQHHGMBPMO4EDGXRUPFX7T4JDZYKKFRW); [Biblissima IIIF collections](https://iiif.biblissima.fr/collections/manifest/49de588a2cde4cc459974215163922ffc35f8d86); [Qalamos](https://qalamos.net/receive/DE12Book_manuscript_00003827).
- (c) Fully open: IIIF Presentation v2 and the Image API.
- (d) Search terms: "Cod.arab. 464", "bsb00037026", "Qazwīnī", "ʿAǧāʾib al-maḫlūqāt", "عجائب المخلوقات", "Wāsiṭ 678". Use the manifest's per-canvas labels as folio numbers.
- (e) Risks:
  - The date field in the record reads "678 oder 778 [h.q.] [1279–1377]", so the metadata keeps open a 1377 reading. The prose description and the scholarly consensus say 1280. Treat this as a known caveat to state.
  - The manuscript is Iraqi (Wasit), not Iranian.
  - The subjects are angels, planets, animals and marvels, not bazaars. It is useful for figure types, headgear, robes and animals (camels may appear in the animal section, but I did not verify any folio).
  - Some miniatures may have been retouched. I did not check this.

**2. Morgan Library MS M.500 — Manāfiʿ-i ḥayavān (Ibn Bakhtīshūʿ, Persian translation) (CATALOGUE + viewer reachable)**
- (a) The Morgan Library & Museum, New York — [Morgan record](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/77363). Viewer (HTTP 200 confirmed): http://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/thumbs/77363, linked from [bestiary.ca](https://bestiary.ca/manuscripts/manu2739.htm).
- (b) Maragha, 1297–1298 or 1299–1300. The colophon is on fol. 84v. The manuscript has 86 leaves and 103 miniatures. It was bought by J. Pierpont Morgan in 1912 — [Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/77363); [bestiary.ca](https://bestiary.ca/manuscripts/manu2739.htm). The miniatures include animals such as camels, horses, mules and asses, with human figures in Ilkhanid dress. That last point is MEMORY: I did not verify specific folios.
- (c) Viewer only, with JPGs in HTML pages. No IIIF manifest was found, and I did not search for one in depth.
- (d) Search terms: "M.500", "Manafi al-Hayawan", "منافع حيوان", "Maragheh", "Ibn Bakhtishu".
- (e) Risks:
  - It is 2–5 years after the window (1297–1300).
  - Morgan's own date line adds "and 19th cent.", which points to later additions or repainting of some leaves. Check each folio before using it.

**3. BnF Supplément persan 205 — Juvaynī, Tārīkh-i Jahāngushāy (CATALOGUE only)**
- (a) Bibliothèque nationale de France. Catalogue ark: https://archivesetmanuscrits.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cc33978s (WebFetch returned 403). A Gallica SRU query for "persan 205" returned no hits; my query may have been badly formed. **Not opened.**
- (b) Dated AH 689 / 1290 CE. The double frontispiece is usually described as showing Juvaynī writing before (Arghun Aqa?). That identification is disputed in the literature (MEMORY). It shows dress, attendants and a horse — this comes from secondary citations only.
- (c) Unknown. Gallica access is likely but not confirmed.
- (d) Search terms: "Supplément persan 205", "Djouveini", "Tarikh-i Djihangushay", "تاریخ جهانگشای", "Juwaynī".
- (e) Risks: only the frontispiece is illustrated, and it is a court scene, not a caravan. Do not confuse this manuscript with BnF Suppl. persan 2018, a different copy — [Islam-Anatolia record](https://www.islam-anatolia.ac.uk/data/documents/FR_BNF_supplément_persan_2018?hl=History).

**4. Takht-i Sulayman (Takht-e Soleyman), Ilkhanid summer palace of Abaqa — excavation finds and tiles (OPENED, two items)**
- (a) Two items:
  - **NEW:** the Museum für Islamische Kunst (SMB) project, "The Ilkhanid Summer Palace at the Throne of Solomon: Chinese-Persian Visual Worlds", Dec 2023–Dec 2025. Partners are DAI Eurasia/Tehran, the Rathgen-Forschungslabor and the Iran Bastan Museum. The holdings are about 5,000 objects from the 1959–1978 DAI excavations, including about 1,200 tile fragments in Berlin (lustre and lajvardina, dragons, phoenixes, Shahnama verses). Results are promised "after the project concludes" — [SMB project page](https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/museum-fuer-islamische-kunst/collection-research/research-cooperation/summer-palace-takht-e-soleyman/). For single objects, use SMB Collections Online at search.smb.museum (not checked by script).
  - Naumann, R., *Takht-i Suleiman: Ausgrabung des Deutschen Archäologischen Instituts in Iran. Ausstellung München 1976. Einführung und Katalog*. München: Prähistorische Staatssammlung, 1976. Ausstellungskataloge der Prähistorischen Staatssammlung 3. 71 + 12 pp., ill., maps. Free PDF (22.9 MB), "May not be reused for commercial purposes" — [Stony Brook AMAR 303](https://commons.library.stonybrook.edu/amar/303), PDF: https://commons.library.stonybrook.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1302&context=amar; handle http://hdl.handle.net/11401/88907.
  - Also: Naumann, R., *Die Ruinen von Tacht-e Suleiman und Zendan-e Suleiman und Umgebung*. Berlin: Reimer, 1977 — [DAI Zenon](https://zenon.dainst.org/Record/000003049). This is CATALOGUE only. A search snippet said AMAR also hosts it (amar/464), but I did not open it.
- (b) Dating comes from excavation context: Abaqa began building "from 1270" — [SMB](https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/museum-fuer-islamische-kunst/collection-research/research-cooperation/summer-palace-takht-e-soleyman/). Some tiles carry dated inscriptions in the 670s AH / 1270s (MEMORY; see Masuya). These finds show palace architecture, tile figures and dress, not town or caravan life.
- (c) Direct PDF (AMAR). SMB Online: viewer, with access by script not tested.
- (d) Search terms: "Tacht-e Suleiman", "Takht-e Soleyman", "تخت سلیمان", "Lüsterfliese", "Ilchaniden", "lajvardina".
- (e) Risks: the material is fragmentary; it shows court rather than town imagery; and the photo licences restrict reuse. Since the illustrator describes in words and does not copy, the licence is acceptable.

**5. Aurel Stein, *Serindia* (1921) and *Innermost Asia* (1928), on Digital Silk Road (NII / Toyo Bunko) — terrain only (CATALOGUE; pages indexed, fetch blocked)**
- (a) National Institute of Informatics, Digital Silk Road, Toyo Bunko rare books. Example *Innermost Asia* vol. 1 page: https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/T-VIII-5-A-a-3/V-1/page/0005.html.en. Both WebFetch attempts returned 403, so this is **not opened**. The search index shows page and high-resolution page views and facing-page views for vols. 1–3, plus *Serindia* plate pages. Rights: "copyright NII and Toyo Bunko, all rights reserved" (from the search snippet).
  - Stein, M. A., *Innermost Asia: Detailed Report of Explorations in Central Asia, Kan-su and Eastern Īrān*, 4 vols. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1928.
  - Stein, M. A., *Serindia: Detailed Report of Explorations in Central Asia and Westernmost China*, 5 vols. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1921.
- (b) Photographs taken 1906–08 and 1913–16 of Lop Nur, Charklik, Miran, Dunhuang and the Wakhan/Pamir routes. **Not in-window**: these are terrain and landscape references dated 1906–1916. They are not period imagery of Polo's time.
- (c) HTML page images; bot-blocked (403) for my fetcher. The British Library holds the original prints: Stein Photo 392 series, e.g. a Miran print "Photo 392/28(353)" from the 3rd expedition, 1913–16 — [BL archives](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-003073427).
- (d) Search terms: "Charklik", "Miran", "Lop-nor", "Tun-huang", "Wakhan", "Sarikol". In Chinese: 若羌 (Charklik), 罗布泊 (Lop Nur), 沙州 (Shazhou).
- (e) Risks: the dates are modern; the site is blocked to scripts; and the rights are restrictive.

**6. Dunhuang Mogao Cave 465 — Tantric murals, possibly Yuan (CATALOGUE / NEW study)**
- (a) Dunhuang Academy (cave). The study is Liang, H. et al., Scientific Reports, 2020, doi 10.1038/s41598-020-76457-9 — [Springer link](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-020-76457-9); [NTU press release](https://www.ntu.ac.uk/about-us/news/news-articles/2020/11/scientists-reveal-700-year-old-caves-hidden-secrets). **NEW.**
- (b) The date has been debated: 9th-c. Tibetan, 11th–13th-c. Tangut, or 13th–14th-c. Yuan. The pigment analysis "corresponds best with the Mongol/Yuan period". Reports differ on whether that means the late 12th–13th or the 13th–14th century — [the-past.com summary of the study](https://the-past.com/news/800-year-old-mistake-uncovered-by-modern-imaging/). These are secondary reports of the Sci. Rep. paper; I did not open the paper itself.
- (c) Digital Dunhuang (e-dunhuang.com) was **not checked**. Cave 465 is famously restricted.
- (d) Search terms: "莫高窟 第465窟", "元代 壁画", "敦煌 元代", "榆林窟 第3窟 西夏".
- (e) Risks: the dating is contested, and the iconography is esoteric Buddhist with almost no daily life. Rank it low for town and caravan scenes. Yulin Cave 3 (Xixia, with a famous smithy and ploughing scene) was not checked; from MEMORY it is generally dated to the Xixia period, before the window.

**Not ranked but relevant — Kleiss, *Karawanenbauten in Iran* (CATALOGUE).** Kleiss, W., *Karawanenbauten in Iran*, Teil 1–6. Berlin: Reimer, 1996– (Materialien zur Iranischen Archäologie). DAI Zenon records: [Teil 1](https://zenon.dainst.org/Record/000922873), [Teil 3](https://zenon.dainst.org/Record/000897420), [Teil 4](https://zenon.dainst.org/Record/000900959). It contains measured plans and photographs of caravanserais, some of them Ilkhanid. The photographs are modern; the buildings date from the period. I found no free PDF, and I did not confirm coverage of Sarcham or Sin. Kleiss and Kiani's 1970s–80s survey is cited in the [UCLA Iran Gazetteer (Ribat-i Sharaf)](https://irangazetteer.ucla.edu/catalogue/rebat-sharaf/). The Encyclopaedia Iranica article "Caravansary" is a free overview: https://iranicaonline.org/articles/caravansary (not opened).

### Inferences
- No in-window illustrated manuscript depicts a Persian bazaar or a caravan scene. The closest dated material shows figures, dress, animals and architecture in other contexts (Qazwini 1280, Juvayni 1290, Manafiʿ 1297–1300). The illustrator will have to assemble town and caravan scenes from these parts and from surviving Ilkhanid buildings.
- For the Lop desert and the Pamir, the honest answer is terrain only, and the plates' sources should say so with the photo date (1906–16).

### Gaps
- **Badakhshan mining:** I could not find a good source for Sar-i Sang lapis mining, Kuh-i Lal spinel mining or silver mining c. 1260–1295. No period image is known to me. The best substitutes, with their dates:
  - Captain John Wood's 1838 visit to Sar-i Sang, described in his *A Personal Narrative of a Journey to the Source of the River Oxus* (1841; 2nd ed. 1872). This is text, with few or no illustrations (MEMORY) — [Mindat excerpt](https://zh.mindat.org/article.php/110/Mining+Lapis+in+1838).
  - Modern photographs in Wyart, J., Bariand, P. & Filippi, J., "Lapis Lazuli from Sar-e-Sang, Badakhshan, Afghanistan", *Gems & Gemology* 17.4 (Winter 1981): 184–190 — [GIA PDF](https://www.gia.edu/doc/Lapis-Lazuli-from-Sar-E-Sang-Badakhshan-Afghanistan.pdf) (not opened; the page range is from MEMORY).
- Not checked: the Marzubannama (Istanbul Archaeological Museum, 1299) and its access; the Kalila wa Dimna copies c. 1280–1300 (I did not verify BL Or. 13506 and do not cite it); the IDP new site (it relaunched May 2024 with a Mirador/IIIF viewer — [Digital Orientalist](https://digitalorientalist.com/2024/05/08/customising-digital-tools-for-non-western-materials-the-launch-of-the-redeveloped-idp-website); whether Stein photos are on it is unconfirmed); Digital Dunhuang's terms for scripted access; Gallica's digitisation of Suppl. persan 205; caravanserai surveys of Sarcham and Sin.
- I could not resolve the BSB metadata ambiguity ("678 oder 778") from the record itself.

## Scholars and works a specialist would read first

### Takeaway
Read Huff (2006) on the excavation results at Takht-i Sulayman, Masuya (1997), Blair's work on Ilkhanid manuscripts, Kleiss's caravanserai corpus, and Herrmann (1968) on lapis. Only Naumann's catalogue was confirmed free.

### Cited Findings
- Huff, Dietrich. "The Ilkhanid Palace at Takht-i Sulayman: Excavation Results." In Linda Komaroff (ed.), *Beyond the Legacy of Genghis Khan*. Leiden: Brill, 2006. I found no free copy — [search summary of the Cambridge History of the Mongol Empire, "Archaeological sources"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-history-of-the-mongol-empire/archaeological-sources/FF88FA8870156C73E9275FF1CB454E08) (CATALOGUE).
- Naumann, Rudolf. *Takht-i Suleiman* (exhibition catalogue, 1976), free PDF — [AMAR](https://commons.library.stonybrook.edu/amar/303) (OPENED).
- Kleiss, Wolfram. *Karawanenbauten in Iran*, 1996– — [Zenon](https://zenon.dainst.org/Record/000922873) (CATALOGUE; not free).
- Masuya, Tomoko. *The Ilkhanid Phase of Takht-i Sulaimán*. PhD diss., New York University (Institute of Fine Arts), 1997. MEMORY: I did not check the title wording or open access. Look in ProQuest.
- Blair, Sheila S. Works on Ilkhanid manuscripts, e.g. *A Compendium of Chronicles: Rashid al-Din's Illustrated History of the World* (1995). MEMORY; this source is post-window (c. 1314).
- Herrmann, Georgina. "Lapis Lazuli: The Early Phases of Its Trade." *Iraq* 30.1 (1968): 21–57. MEMORY; on JSTOR. Its focus is ancient, so it serves only as background on the Badakhshan source.

### Inferences
- Rank Masuya and Huff first for anything datable to the 1270s.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether Masuya's dissertation is open access, or whether Huff 2006 has a free copy.
