# G5 — Indian Ocean ships and coasts c. 1250–1300: period image sources

Scope note: a rate limit cut this run short at about 16 tool calls. Leiden Digital Collections refused direct fetches (ECONNRESET, three tries), so the Leiden items below come from search-result listings, not opened records. Confidence labels: **opened and confirmed** / **found in a catalogue or citation** / **from memory**. "NEW" = published after 2013.

## Q1. Ranked sources likely to hold a dated, viewable, in-window image

### Takeaway
The strongest in-window material is **Javanese (Singhasari)**: Candi Jago reliefs, Candi Singasari statues, and their colonial-era photographs in Leiden and on Commons. **No source I checked shows a ship that is securely dated 1250–1300.** Every ship image I found is either too early (hero stones, Java Sea wreck, Maqamat C-23) or too late (Shinan, Rashid al-Din). The ship side of G5 has to rely on descriptions and specialist reconstructions, not a dated in-window picture.

### Cited Findings

**Rank 1 — Leiden University Libraries Digital Collections (OD / Kern Institute / KITLV photo archives)**
- (a) Holder: Universiteit Leiden. Base URL https://digitalcollections.universiteitleiden.nl/ . Search results list OD-numbered Javanese temple photographs there, e.g. "Relief fragment of candi Sukuh" OD-7120 and OD-2282 (Prambanan). I could not open any record: the site reset the connection every time. **No Djago/Jago record surfaced in search** — [search listing](https://digitalcollections.universiteitleiden.nl/view/item/809238). Confidence: found in a catalogue or citation (the collection exists and holds OD photos). Whether Jago, Singasari, Jawi or Pasai photos are there is from memory.
- (b) The Oudheidkundige Dienst (colonial archaeological service, c. 1901–1940s) systematically photographed East Javanese temples, including relief series. Candi Jago is traditionally the funerary temple of Wisnuwardhana (d. 1268) per the Nagarakretagama (from memory). Candi Singasari is linked to Kertanagara (r. 1268–1292); see Rank 3.
- (c) Viewer plus IIIF manifests (from memory; not verified this run). Scriptable once the site responds.
- (d) Search terms: "Tjandi Djago", "Djago", "Toempang"/"Tumpang" (the Jago temple sits in Tumpang; Tropenmuseum titles use "Toempang"), "Singasari", "Singosari", "Tjandi Djawi", "Pasei", "Samoedra", "Malik as-Saleh", "OD-" plus the temple name. In Indonesian: "Candi Jago relief".
- (e) Risks: the photos date from c. 1900–1940 and show weathered, partly collapsed or restored monuments. **Dating risk for Jago:** many art historians think the surviving Jago fabric and reliefs belong to a 1343 rebuilding under Adityawarman, not the 1268–80 original (from memory). Treat the reliefs as "Singhasari foundation, possibly 14th-c. fabric".

**Rank 2 — Wikimedia Commons, Category:Candi Jago**
- (a) https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Candi_Jago — **opened and confirmed**. It holds about 10 Tropenmuseum scans, e.g. "Tempel Jago Malang Oost-Java TMnr 10016221.jpg", "Reliëfs op de Candi Tumpang TMnr 60027346.jpg" and "Reliëf voorstellende het hoofd van Kala op de Candi Tumpang TMnr 60016439.jpg". It also holds a modern numbered relief series with titles naming the Tantri, Arjunawiwaha and Parthayajna stories, plus a subcategory "Manjusri Candi Jago".
- (b) Same monument as Rank 1. The narrative reliefs give dress, architecture, pavilions and landscape for Singhasari-era Java.
- (c) Commons API; licences are open (Tropenmuseum scans were released CC-BY-SA, from memory). Fully scriptable.
- (d) Search "Candi Jago", "Candi Tumpang", "Toempang", "Tantri Candi Jago".
- (e) **No file name mentions a ship or boat**, so I found no ship relief at Jago. The Jago date risk from Rank 1 applies here too. The modern photos are photographers' own work: check each licence.

**Rank 3 — Candi Singasari statues, Wereldmuseum / NMVW (Leiden): RV-1403-1622 Durga, -1623 Mahakala, -1624 Nandishvara, -1681 Ganesha (plus -1680 Bhairava, -1682 Nandi)**
- (a) Dutch Colonial Collections Committee (Commissie Koloniale Collecties), advice no. I-2023-3, dated 12 May 2023 — [PDF](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/blg-1100343.pdf), **opened and confirmed** (text extracted). Indonesia requested the return of the four statues on 1 July 2022. **NEW (2023).**
- (b) The advice describes andesite statues 154–175 cm high from the Singasari temple complex near Malang, "daterend uit de late 13e eeuw" ("dating from the late 13th century"). The temple is described as a sanctuary dedicated to Kertanegara, last ruler of Singhasari, "van 1268 tot aan zijn dood in 1292" ("from 1268 until his death in 1292"). Dating basis: dedication to Kertanagara, i.e. stylistic and historical, not an inscription on the statues. Provenance: Nicolaus Engelhard removed six statues after 1802; Bhairava, Ganesha and Nandi reached the Netherlands in 1819. Historia.id pages labelled "repatriasi2024" suggest the four requested statues were returned ([Mahakala](https://repatriasi2024.historia.id/en/mahakala), [Nandhiswara](https://repatriasi2024.historia.id/en/nandhiswara)). I did not confirm the final status or date.
- (c) The Wereldmuseum collection database (collectie.wereldculturen.nl, from memory; not opened) is a viewer with downloadable images, licence unknown. Commons likely holds museum-visitor photos (not checked).
- (d) Search the inventory numbers "RV-1403-1622" etc., "Singasari", "Singosari", "Tjandi Singasari".
- (e) Four of the six statues have probably left Leiden, so online records may change. Image rights are unclear. These are iconic deities, not coastal or ship scenes.

**Rank 4 — Ship hero stones of the west coast of India (Goa, Eksar)**
- (a) Tripati, S. 2006. "Ships on hero stones from the west coast of India." *International Journal of Nautical Archaeology* 35(1): 88–96. DOI 10.1111/j.1095-9270.2005.00081.x. Found via the [ICAR OHS record](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/ohs-2.3.1/index.php/record/view/207167) and the [WA Museum bibliography](https://museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/bibliography/ships-hero-stones-west-coast-india). The article was not opened. Secondary summary: [INHCRF blog](https://inhcrf.org/blogs/revisiting-maritime-in-the-west-coast-of-india-through-the-lenses-of-depiction-of-shipsboats-in-hero-stones) (secondary; use only as a lead). Confidence: found in a catalogue or citation.
- (b) Four hero stones showing ships are in the ASI Archaeological Museum, Old Goa, attributed to the Kadambas of Goa (c. 950–1300). One stone from Malcornem showing a naval battle is dated c. 12th century. The Eksar (Mumbai) stones are linked to Bhoja of Malwa, 1020. Per Heras, a viragal in the Old Goa museum is dated 1354 and records a chief killed in a naval battle (all per the search summaries).
- (c) The article is paywalled (Wiley). The ASI Old Goa museum has no known open image API.
- (d) Search "naval hero stone", "viragal ship", "Old Goa Archaeological Museum hero stone", "Eksar", "Malcornem". In Kannada: "ವೀರಗಲ್ಲು" (viragallu).
- (e) **Date risk:** the stones are dated stylistically or by dynasty span. None is securely 1250–1300, and the one explicitly dated stone is 1354. The ships are schematic.

**Rank 5 — Gravestone of Sultan al-Malik al-Salih, Samudra-Pasai (d. 1297)**
- (a) The stone stands in situ at Pasai, Aceh. I found no museum holder or open image record. Search summaries tie its date to his death in 1297 (via the epitaph, from memory: AH 696). Debate: Moquette argued it was imported from Cambay; Lambourn (2008) disputed this ([scite author page](https://scite.ai/authors/elizabeth-lambourn-6v1zp)). There is a 3D model of the **successor's** grave, Malik al-Zahir (d. 1326, granite): [Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/grave-of-malik-al-zahir-at-samudera-pasai-18415027a6684cb1bf74314529c4811c), not opened. Confidence: found in a catalogue or citation.
- (b) This is a dated in-window object from Polo's "Samara/Basman" coast. It shows script, a mihrab niche and knot motifs, useful for a Sumatran harbour plate.
- (c) No open source was confirmed. Colonial-era photographs (Moquette c. 1913; OD/KITLV) probably sit in Leiden (Rank 1), from memory.
- (d) Search "Malik as-Saleh", "Malikussaleh", "Samoedra", "Pasei grafsteen", "Moquette", "Meunasah Beringin" (from memory). In Indonesian: "makam Sultan Malikussaleh".
- (e) No verified free image. The stone is not marble per the sources I saw; a search summary flagged that as unverified. The 1326 Malik al-Zahir model is out of window.

**Rank 6 — Java Sea Shipwreck, Field Museum, Chicago (note: pre-window)**
- (a) The Field Museum holds more than 7,500 objects donated by Pacific Sea Resources. Press release (May 16, 2018): https://www.fieldmuseum.org/about/press/shipwreck-and-800-year-old-made-china-label-reveal-lost-history (found in search, not opened). Main paper: Niziolek, L. C. et al. 2018. "Revisiting the date of the Java Sea Shipwreck from Indonesia." *Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports* (vol., pages and DOI not confirmed; ScienceDirect returned 403). Earlier paper: Niziolek 2015, LA-ICP-MS study of the ceramics, *Journal of Indo-Pacific Archaeology* ([JIPA](https://journals.lib.washington.edu/index.php/JIPA/article/view/14893/12501), not opened; probably open access).
- (b) Ceramic box bases are inscribed "Jianning Fu", a place name used only 1162–1278. New AMS dates put the wreck in the mid-to-late 12th century, about a century earlier than first thought ([Archaeology magazine](https://archaeology.org/news/2018/05/16/18051-china-trade-ceramic/)). **Pre-window. NEW (2015, 2018).**
- (c) I found no online catalogue or API for the collection; a search turned up none. JIPA is open access.
- (d) Search "Java Sea Shipwreck", "Jianning Fu", "Pacific Sea Resources".
- (e) Too early. Little of the hull survives, and the images are mostly cargo.

**Leads checked and set aside (too early or too late, or not checked)**
- St Petersburg IOM Maqamat C-23 (c. 1225–35): not checked; pre-window (from the brief).

### Inferences
- Use Rank 1, 2 and 3 material for Singhasari-period Javanese figures, dress, architecture and sculpture style. Label Jago reliefs as "possibly 1343 fabric".
- For ships, the honest basis is a composite: earlier sewn-hull evidence (Belitung, Jewel of Muscat) and later Mamluk/Ilkhanid ships, bridged with Agius's typology. The illustrator should describe the ship in words, not cite a dated in-window ship image.
- The Malik al-Salih stone is the best dated (1297) object for the Sumatra plate, but it needs an image source confirmed by hand, most likely in Leiden.

## Q2. Scholars and works a specialist would read first

### Takeaway
Read Agius for ship types, Manguin for Southeast Asian hulls, Tripati for Indian ship iconography, and Lambourn for the Pasai stones. Hourani is the classic, now old, overview.
