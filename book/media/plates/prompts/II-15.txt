Use thin warm-brown ink outlines, compact articulated figures, calm profiles, thin matt vermilion and blue washes, rhythmic curved folds, sparse ground tufts and one faint ribbon cloud. Use the compact early Ilkhanid idiom instead of later Safavid or Mughal detail. The paper is lightly and evenly aged, with no ruled frame, mounting seams, or text panels.

Scene: Kaidu's Central Asian steppe court, in the wrestling tale Polo places in 1280. Before a low open-sided felt pavilion, the princess Aijaruc has just thrown the visiting adult prince at the conclusion of a wrestling bout. The result is instantly readable. She is on her feet, towering over him, strong, alive and composed, hands open as she takes a half-step back. He is uninjured, on his side on the low paving in the foreground, turning himself up to sit, with a clear intact silhouette. This is a contest observed by a small court group.

Aijaruc is twenty, exceptionally tall and solidly built with broad shoulders, distinctive dark braids visible, in a close-fitting plain muted-russet sammet jerkin, trousers and soft boots. She is unmarried and her head has no boqta. Preserve her features, build and tightly controlled braids from the attached house reference. The prince shares the world of Central Asian dress but wears a plain separate muted blue-grey close jerkin, trousers and boots. On a simple low backed seat just inside the pavilion opening, watchful Kaidu and his unnamed consort sit side by side as in C6's court arrangement, with no extra entourage. Kaidu looks hard and older, with a greying beard, belted prince's robe and fur hat, keeping the accepted house profile. The unnamed consort wears an appropriate tall married woman's boqta and long robe, distinct from Aijaruc. Keep all three main faces clear.

Three compact wedge-headed steppe horses wait in profile near a plain hitch rope to one side, one lead line per horse, as a representative group of the wagered horses, with only simple headstalls. There is no herd across the steppe. A few tufts and a low sweep of land locate the sparse court. Use the attached manuscript source for colour, flowing curves and court furnishings, not for composition or likeness.
