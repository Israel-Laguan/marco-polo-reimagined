Painted in the manner of Yuan court painting (Liu Guandao's “Khubilai Khan Hunting”, 1280, and the Yuan imperial portrait album): fine even ink contour, flat mineral colours, spare lines for folds, and a calm three-quarter figure on an undecorated silk ground. Keep the lightly aged silk very pale and even. Leave the background entirely plain, with no architecture or landscape.

A character reference sheet of one man, the Venetian merchant Marco Polo at about 31 in 1285, in the Great Khan's service. Show him once, a single full-length figure standing calmly at court in three-quarter view, eyes looking slightly past the viewer. Leave generous, balanced silk margins on this wide sheet, showing the entire hat and both boots.

Face and body: slim, dark-haired and dark-eyed, with a longish oval face, dark brows, a high straight prominent nose and a short full dark beard. His youth relative to the elder Polos is apparent. Draw him by the Yuan convention for a western foreigner in Mongol court robes, with the face of a man of 31. Short hair visible at the sides.

Dress (Cathay and Manzi): Mongol-style robe over trousers and boots, cut as S-22 C1: crossed collar, short-sleeved outer robe over a long-sleeved under-robe; at court the single colour of the feast (white at the White Feast); the small rounded hat with a narrow brim and a knob on top (C1); his own short hair, not the looped braids of Mongol men; still bearded, still the purse. Show the White Feast version, with plain white robe layers and clear sleeve edges. Make the trousers visible above the boots. A simple merchant's belt-purse (scarsella) hangs where it is clearly visible at the hip. A small oblong gold paiza tablet on a cord hangs against his chest, with a completely plain, smooth, unmarked gold face. The purse and tablet are distinct objects.
