# Character sheet styles

The Book of the Million · CHR.T9 · 18 November 2026

Choose the painting tradition from the plate's setting and the dress from the figure's date and role. The I-12 figures use Ilkhanid painting. In III-09, the Persian-Mongol envoys are drawn in the Yuan idiom at the Kaan's court. The 1324 will group uses Venetian Gothic. | Tradition | Drawing and colour | Sheet ground | Working examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yuan court painting | Fine ink contours, broad fields of mineral colour, and faint cheek washes on composed three-quarter figures. Let crossed collars and sleeve layers read through contour and overlapping cloth. | Pale silk with a fine, even weave | [Kublai](kublai-1280-sheet.webp) for the baseline · [Arghun's three envoys](arghuns-envoys-1291-sheet.png) for a group |
| Ilkhanid (*Jāmiʿ al-tawārīkh*) | Compact silhouettes in thin warm contours. Render the cloth with flat, opaque pigment and rhythmic curved fold lines. Reserve small blocks of stronger colour for cuffs, caps and collars. | Unruled cream manuscript paper | [Toghrul](toghrul-1203-sheet.png) for a steppe ruler · [Aijaruc](aijaruc-1280-sheet.png) for a figure in motion |
| Italian Gothic / Venetian | Long, clearly outlined silhouettes and flat tempera-like colour. Use angular turns and selective linear highlights in the mantle folds, with composed donor-like poses. | Pale ivory parchment | [Donata](donata-1305-sheet.png) for a single figure · [Polo daughters](polo-daughters-1324-sheet.png) for a group |

The Yuan baseline follows the contours and figure handling of Liu Guandao's hunt and the Yuan portrait album. The Ilkhanid reference is early-fourteenth-century Tabriz figure painting. The Diez court page supplies comparisons for drapery and garment shapes, with a substantial date gap for Toghrul. The Venetian sheets use the San Marco Sant'Alipio figures for dress comparisons and the early-trecento Venetian donor idiom for poses and drapery. The San Marco mosaic predates the 1324 scene, and Paolo Veneziano's securely dated works come later. [National Palace Museum](https://theme.npm.edu.tw/selection/Article.aspx?lang=2&sNo=04000995) · [Edinburgh University Library](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/145535) · [C5, C6 and C39](../../plans/registry/visual-references.md) · [Basilica di San Marco](https://www.basilicasanmarco.it/en/sculpture/the-decoration-of-the-facades/) · [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artists/1767-paolo-veneziano)

Keep the Gothic regional variants where the setting calls for them. Genoese scenes can use the firmer outline and angular folds of the [Rustichello sheet](rustichello-1298-sheet.png). The [Tedaldo sheet](tedaldo-1270-sheet.png) takes its figure drawing from the Acre Bible tradition, and the [Cepoy sheet](cepoy-1307-sheet.png) gives a French visitor the bearing of a Gothic donor figure. Select garments independently of these drawing references.

For a character sheet, use one continuous 4:3 ground with light, even ageing and enough room to see every silhouette. For a group, use distinct silhouettes, headwear or veil shapes, and restrained clothing colours so the figures are distinguishable without labels. At review, check the faces, hems, garment bands, objects and corners for script and seal-like marks. Use the examples as visual targets. The image-input permissions remain those in the style guide and source manifests.
