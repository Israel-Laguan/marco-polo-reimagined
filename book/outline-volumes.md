# Marco Polo — Three-Volume Polished Formal Retelling
## Working Chapter Outline (Yule–Cordier / *Il Milione* tradition)

**Working title (series):** *The Book of the Million: A Formal Retelling of Marco Polo*

**Textual base:** Yule–Cordier English text (1903 / Cordier addenda 1920), eclectic toward the “Geographic” Old French tradition, with Ramusian expansions flagged; collation notes deferred to apparatus.

**Visited vs reported:** Every Vol. II–III chapter carries a standing tag — **[V]** largely or partly from personal travel/service; **[R]** chiefly report, embassy hearsay, or court intelligence; **[M]** mixed. Tags are provisional writing guides, not final judgements.

**Reliability legend (used in front matter of each volume):**
| Mark | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| ★★★ | Corroborated by Yuan/Persian/other contemporary sources or archaeology |
| ★★ | Plausible; minor chronological or toponymic stress |
| ★ | Contested, compressed, or romance-coloured |
| ◇ | Wonder-tale / legendary accretion (retain as *what the book says*) |

**Arc:** How the book was made; the Polo family; Genoa and Rustichello; first and second journeys; overland Book I from Lesser Armenia to Chandu; arrival and first favour.

### Ch. I-1 — *In a Genoese Prison: How This Book Was Made*
**Beats**
- Battle of Curzola (1298): Venetian defeat; Marco taken
- Prison at Genoa; fellow-prisoner Rustichello (Rusticiano) of Pisa
- Dictation: French of the courts; romance scribe meets merchant witness
- Framing address: “emperors and kings… take this book”
- Liberation; the book begins to circulate while the man still lives

**Key places (book names):** Curzola; Genoa; Venice (as origin remembered)

**Dispute hooks**
- Capture-at-Curzola rests on later tradition and Jacopo d’Acqui-type notices; not airtight documentary proof
- Degree to which Rustichello’s Arthurian/romance diction shaped marvels and battle set-pieces
- Whether an “original” French of 1298–99 is recoverable vs early remoulded French / Pipino Latin / Ramusio Italian types
- How much prison collaboration invents a *persona* of the traveller

**Comparative sources:** Yule Intro. §§VI–X; Benedetto / Moule–Pelliot text typology; John Larner, *Marco Polo and the Discovery of the World*; Consuelo Wager Dutschke on MS tradition; Jacopo d’Acqui; Pipino’s prologue
