# Proposed Voice: The Polyphonic Storyteller & Witty Companion

This folder contains the blueprint for the revised narrative voice and two-tier architecture of *The Book of the Million*, based on the Deep Research findings ([`narrative-voice-models-raw.md`](file:///home/anthony/code/marco-polo-reimagined/book/plans/research/results/narrative-voice-models-raw.md)) and the selection of **Alternative C (Polyphonic Framing + Optional Academic Apparatus)**.

---

## 1. Core Philosophy: The Two-Tier Architecture

The fundamental rule of this new voice is:
> **The main narrative must be amusing, vivid, and effortless to read. The academic load must be 100% optional.**

A reader should be able to enjoy the book cover-to-cover (or as an audiobook) purely for the wit, scale, and drama of the journey, without tripping over academic caveats. A curious or scholarly reader can consult the notes, appendices, maps, and tables whenever they want deeper proof.

## 2. The Voices (The Dynamic Cast)

Instead of a detached narrator reciting "The Book says..." or "Ramusio adds...", the text embraces the real historical collaboration behind the book:

1. **Marco Polo (The Merchant Eyewitness):** 
   - Sharp, pragmatic, observant. 
   - Obsessed with weights, measures, trade commodities, physical appearance, horses, and administrative efficiency.
2. **Rustichello da Pisa (The Chivalric Romancer):** 
   - The Arthurian romance author who co-wrote the book in a Genoese prison.
   - Loves martial valor, chivalric hyperbole, court splendor, and grand oral formula flourishes (*"Now you must know that when the Great Kaan sits at table..."*).
3. **The Modern Narrator (The Witty Guide):** 
   - Intelligent, charismatic, slightly wry companion.
   - Paces the journey, frames the cultural context, highlights the audacity of the spectacle, and delivers sharp, understated observations without pedantry.

## 3. Key Rules & Changes from v2

### A. Elimination of the "Provenance Stammer"
* **OLD RULE:** Every detail found only in Ramusio had to carry an inline 3–6 word flag (*"Ramusio's version adds..."*, *"In Ramusio's text..."*).
* **NEW RULE:** **Zero repetitive provenance flags in the reading line.** 
  - Weave Ramusio’s vivid details (e.g. the buffet chest, the giant wine vessels) directly into the scene.
  - If a layer is dramatically interesting to mention, treat it with charm as a collaborator's contribution (*"Ramusio later turned up a manuscript with..."*).
  - Otherwise, record provenance strictly in the Note: `[4] ? Ramusio-only. Carat appraisal...`

### B. Evacuation of Mid-Scene Asides
* **OLD RULE:** Long em-dash asides (`— The Chinese records trim this...`) interrupted scenes mid-paragraph.
* **NEW RULE:** Let dramatic scenes play out without academic brakes. Move factual corrections and historical context to **Footnotes / Endnotes**, or keep them as a single, punchy, witty clause inside the narrative.

### C. Catalogues & Numbers (Carlin / Cooper Method)
* Compress endless inventories into vivid physical scenes.
* State Marco's astronomical numbers with the awe they inspired, then calibrate in a swift, clean clause without stopping the train:
  > *"Rustichello counts twelve thousand horsemen in the guard—an impossible daily muster, but an accurate picture of an empire that weaponized spectacle."*

### D. Signposting Conventions
When pointing the reader to companion visual or academic resources, keep it seamless:
* **Visuals / Plates:** `[Plate 3]` or `*(see Plate 3 for the court buffet)*`
* **Maps:** `*(see Map 2 for the route)*`
* **Deep Debates / Appendices:** Leave deep arguments for the endnotes or a crisp pointer: `*(see Appendix B for the currency debate)*`

### E. Inline Note Anchoring (Direct-Claim Placement)
* **Do NOT bundle notes at the end of the paragraph.** 
* Place the note anchor `[n]` **inline directly beside the specific fact, name, number, or claim** it substantiates:
  > *Polo calls them Keshican [1]—"Knights devoted to their Lord"—and the name is no invention... Four captains divide twelve thousand horsemen into companies of three thousand [2].*
* **Why this works:**
  - Casual and semi-casual readers mentally skip over a small inline `[n]` without friction.
  - Curious or academic readers immediately know *exact claim X* connects to *Note X* without having to guess which part of a long paragraph the endnote refers to.

### F. The "Attention Trap" Opening Hook & Epigraph
* **The Epigraph:** Keep the authentic Yule–Cordier 1903 epigraph with its original medieval/archaic cadence (*"You must know..."*, *"hath a guard..."*). It sets an antique, formal stage.
* **The First Paragraph:** Must act as an irresistible "attention trap" for anyone skimming:
  - Bridges forward from the previous chapter's arrival or milestone (*"We have seen the outer walls, the chess-board avenues, and the midnight curfew bell. Now the doors swing open..."*).
  - Explodes with immediate scale, danger, and sensory wonder to hook the reader before subsequent paragraphs unpack the technical and historical details.

### G. The Three Flavors of Footnotes & Companion Cross-Linking
* **Rule:** **Make notes reward, never require.** Replace telegraphic database shorthand with readable, human, engaging prose. No bolded label headers; open directly with the verdict marker and natural prose.
* **Structure:** Each note opens with its verdict marker (`✓ ~ ✗ ?`) and flows directly into a crisp 2–3 sentence explanation in one of three flavors:
  1. **Curiosity Note:** Illuminates an alien custom, bizarre word, or material detail (e.g. nomadic yurt-threshold taboos, Parisian-made silver wine fountains).
  2. **Witness Contrast Note:** Juxtaposes how different cultures perceived the same event (e.g. Mongol white as celestial luck vs. Chinese white as death and mourning).
  3. **Authority / Navigational Note:** Provides the scholarly verdict, identifies the base source, and actively offloads extended debates to companion resources:
     - **Glossary:** `*(see Back Matter II-2, Glossary: "Keshican")*`
     - **Trivia & Disputes:** `*(see Back Matter II-1, Trivia: "Robes of one colour")*`
     - **Maps & City Plans:** `*(see Map A9 for Dadu palace plan)*`
     - **Plates & Drawings:** `*(see Plate 5 for banquet reconstruction)*`
     - **Appendices & Ledger:** `*(see Appendix C, §2; Reliability Ledger L-18)*`
