## R-1. The S-25 works: do they say what the Notes cite them for? (task APP.T8)

```text
I am checking endnote citations in a commercial English retelling of Marco Polo (base text Yule–Cordier, 1903). Four works are cited; their existence and bibliographic details are already confirmed. I need to know whether each work actually supports the specific claim it is cited for. For each item, read the cited work (or the specific passage), give the locator (page, chapter or entry), a short faithful paraphrase or a quotation of no more than 25 words, and a verdict: supports / supports in part / does not support / NOT SEEN. Do not infer from the work's general reputation.

Works:
A. Igor de Rachewiltz, "Marco Polo Went to China", Zentralasiatische Studien 27 (1997): 34–92. The author's own posting is open access at ANU: hdl.handle.net/1885/41883.
B. Tao Zongyi 陶宗儀, Nancun chuogeng lu 南村輟耕錄 (Zhonghua shuju 1959; full Chinese text is on ctext.org and Wikisource).
C. Consuelo Wager Dutschke, "Francesco Pipino and the Manuscripts of Marco Polo's Travels" (PhD dissertation, UCLA, 1993).
D. Christopher P. Atwood, Encyclopedia of Mongolia and the Mongol Empire (Facts on File, 2004).

Claims to test:
1. (de Rachewiltz; cited in 6 Notes) That he replies to Frances Wood's argument from the Great Wall's absence. Where, and what does he say about the Wall?
2. (de Rachewiltz) That he argues Polo could have served the Khan without leaving a Chinese biographical record, in the context of Polo's claimed Yangzhou "governorship". What does he say about Yangzhou and Polo's office?
3. (de Rachewiltz) That his reply to Wood rests on philology, itinerary and administrative or linguistic detail "harder to fake than tourists assume". Summarize his main lines of argument with locators.
4. (Tao) A "Palm Hall" (棕殿, Ch. zongdian; Palladius's "Tsung tien") at Shangdu, or elsewhere: where does Tao place it (which garden, which capital)? Give juan and the passage.
5. (Tao) Imperial door-wardens or guards described as giant men with clubs or jade axes (Palladius cites this). Is there such a passage? Give juan and the passage.
6. (Dutschke) That she classifies the manuscript tradition (Pipino's Latin and others) in a way relevant to whether "a pure 1298–99 French original" can be recovered. What does she conclude about Pipino's Latin and the manuscript families?
7. (Dutschke) That she distinguishes the Geographic Old French from Pipino and Ramusio as different "Polos". Give the relevant chapter.
8. (Atwood) An entry on Shangdu (Xanadu): what does it say about the palace and the cane palace, and about Polo?

OUTPUT AND SOURCE RULES
- Cover every numbered question in a ledger: answered, partly answered, or not found. For each finding give the precise claim; verdict (supported / contradicted / disputed / not found); exact source and locator; what it actually says; access level; limitations; and recommended cautious wording. Separate a manuscript's reading, independent contemporary witness, physical evidence, and a modern interpretation. A related fact does not establish Marco's presence or eyewitness status.
- Access levels: original image/object seen; edition/transcription or full text read; extract/OCR read; abstract/review read; catalog metadata only; or inaccessible. Give stable institutional, publisher, library or DOI links. Supply printed pages only if actually visible; clearly distinguish a printed page from a scan number. Attribute any second-hand report to its actual intermediary. A search snippet, an unexamined citation or catalog entry does not verify a passage. Do not invent texts, page numbers, links, dates or consensus.
- Cite sources actually consulted with complete bibliographic details, access status, and BibTeX if verifiable. Cite a short original-language passage and translation only where seen. Name disagreements and identify where the underlying evidence stops. End with unresolved questions and the best safe fallback. Say NOT FOUND or NOT SEEN when appropriate; that is not proof that evidence does not exist.
```
