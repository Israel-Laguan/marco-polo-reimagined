# Deep dive: M. V. Gorelik, *Армии монголо-татар X–XIV веков* (2002)

**Result: blocked. The book could not be opened, so no element of it is assessed.** What follows is what the web says about it, which is little.

## 1. What it is
- An illustrated "historical-artistic and popular-science" book on the warfare and arms of the Mongol empires of the 10th–14th centuries and their neighbours, including the Khitan, issued by Издательский дом «Техника — молодёжи» with ООО «Восточный горизонт»; said to rest on more than 35 years of the author's research and to carry his own colour reconstructions ([listing](https://avidreaders.ru/book/armii-mongolo-tatar-x-xiv-vekov.html)). The listing does not print the year; "2002" comes from citations of it as "Gorelik, 2002" in *Археология евразийских степей*.
- Gorelik (d. January 2015, aged 71) is described as a specialist in armour studies who reconstructed the arms of Eurasian nomads; I. L. Izmailov's 2017 article in *Археология евразийских степей* no. 5 sets out his method of "graphic and real reconstruction of warrior costume".

## 3. Research sources and consultants
- His own earlier study, «Ранний монгольский доспех (IX – первая половина XIV в.)», 1987, is named by later authors as the base for the view that Mongol lamellar has Khitan roots. Publication details not confirmed.
- The excavated pieces behind each plate: **not documented** from anything I could open.

## 4. Criticism found
- A. L. Kubik (2020, *Historia i Świat*) discusses two lamellar plates from Samarkand that Gorelik published in 2002 as Mongol, 13th–14th century, and argues they belong to a local Central Asian tradition older than the Mongol invasion ([article](https://czasopisma.uph.edu.pl/historiaswiat/article/download/1768/3116/6721)). One article, one object group.
- A reenactors' forum post says Gorelik over-armours the Tatars and points to Kuleshov's work instead. An opinion, not scholarship; recorded only as a warning that the "heavily armoured Mongol" is disputed.
