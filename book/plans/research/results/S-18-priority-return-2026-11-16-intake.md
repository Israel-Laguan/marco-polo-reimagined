## Findings and handoffs

- **Pasai, III-03:** the inscription gives 1297, roughly **five years after**, not 15–20 years before, a proposed c. 1292 visit. Lambourn dates the extant markers to the 1470s–1520s and suggests they may replace an earlier marker; an earlier stone is not independently established by that suggestion. Ismail et al. argue for contemporaneity with the death on script and ornament. Neither proposition establishes a tomb Marco saw. Lambourn challenges the Gujarati attribution of this particular monument; that does not settle the origin of every Pasai stone. No basis was found here to label either side a minority or established consensus. Guillot and Kalus's view of this particular monument remains unseen.
- **Abash, III-07:** Tamrat p. 127's footnote cites Conti Rossini in a discussion of Yikunno Amlak and possible campaigns; it does not establish the attribution of the bishop story to Yagbeʾa Ṣeyon. This is a limited passage check, not proof no relevant discussion exists elsewhere. Trimingham and Pankhurst remain unread. Yule explicitly proposes Yikunno Amlak and a date around 1278; his Aden/Adel reading is a hypothesis. The manuscript's unqualified language is now attributed to him.
- **Takashima, III-01:** distinguish the 2008 thesis from the 2015 book and from later excavations. The former contains the 502-timber count, 864 ± 18 BP uncalibrated sample and burned windlass discussion; it cannot report a 2011 excavation. Their occurrence in the thesis does not show they are absent from the 2015 book. A [local site authority](https://matsuura-guide.com/kanko/rekishi-shiseki/takashima-genkoshi/suichu-kokogaku/) describes the dated inscription on the seal's side and Phags-pa characters on its face; association of a dated artifact is not absolute dating of a particular hull. Fire traces alone do not resolve the historical storm or its effect. The 2019 article and 2015 book chapters remain to be read directly.
- **Trebizond, III-09/10:** Moule–Pelliot reproduces Latin as well as translation in accessible OCR; this intake did not inspect the manuscript, Orlandini, or the edited page image. It records losses involving Comnenus and his district, without describing robbery or mechanism. This is documentary evidence outside Polo's travel narrative, not an episode established in its Prologue. The date and identity of the will should not be treated as settled: the [S-15 intake](S-15-priority-return-2026-11-16-intake.md) records conflicting source descriptions.
- **Pelliot, III-03/04:** secondary bibliographies support the three printed page locators, not the return's readings. SEILAN's alleged absence and speculation that Pelliot never wrote it are unconfirmed; prior intake found a relevant SAGAMONI BORCAN entry. Recheck actual images and index before making an absence claim.
