# Source-check ledger: Polo vol. II annotations (five questions)

**Status:** Q1 partly answered; Q2 answered at translation level; Q3 partly answered (the attribution fails); Q4 partly answered (no excavation report opened); Q5 answered for the Chinese side, with Polo's figures not re-read.

**Access labels:** FT = full text or transcription read. EX = extract or search snippet only. AB = abstract or publisher page. MD = catalogue metadata. NS = not seen.

Western dates for the 1287 campaign are my own conversions from the cyclical days (Julian calendar); check them against a concordance before printing.

## 1. Nayan, 1287 (II-01) — partly answered

### What the Yuan shi says

| Topic | Source and locator | Content | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronology | Yuan shi j. 14, Shizu benji 11, Zhiyuan 24 | 2nd month, day wuwu: Nayan levies troops from the eastern princes. 4th month, undated: "this month Prince Nayan rebelled". 5th month, in the renyin entry (c. 24 June): the emperor leads the campaign in person and leaves Shangdu. 6th month, renxu (c. 14 July): arrival at Sa'erdulu, where Nayan's partisan Tabudai brings 60,000 men against the imperial camp and the vanguard defeats him. Later that month: the emperor halts at a place the editors emend to Shira-ordo, and over a thousand of Nayan's baggage carts are taken. 8th month, yichou (c. 15 Sept): return to Shangdu. | FT (Wikisource, Zhonghua-based text) |
| Nayan's capture | Same annals | No entry records his capture or execution. | FT |
| Command and places | j. 154, biographies of Hong Dagu, Hong Junxiang, Hong Wan, Zheng Zhiyi | Hong Wan fights Nayan's general Huanghai at "Salituolu" alongside Jaril-temür, then Tabutai beside the emperor, then goes on to Shira-ordo and serves under Yusu-temür against Nayan; in the 7th month he defeats Jinjianu. Hong Dagu bluffs 10,000 enemy horse with under 3,000 men using improvised banners. | FT |
| Battlefield detail | j. 162, biography of Li Ting | Tabutai and Jinjianu claim 100,000 men; Li Ting is wounded; a volley from a hundred crossbows; ten men carry a fire-pao into the enemy camp at night and the enemy scatters; Li Ting and Yuerlu's Mongols advance together and Nayan is bound and presented. | EX (snippets only; page not fetchable) |
| Yuxi-temür's biography | j. 119 | NOT SEEN. | NS |

### Other witnesses

- **Lost eyewitness record.** Hong Junxiang, who ringed each imperial halt with war-carts, compiled the emperor's daily movements into a "Record of the Eastern Campaign". I found no surviving text.
- **Rashid al-Din.** Reported second-hand by Pelliot; see the elephant finding below. I did not read Rashid directly. A search snippet from Boyle's translation showed a kettledrum order near this passage but no elephant wording.
- **Goryeosa.** Not consulted. The annals do record that the Korean king offered troops and 500 were sent, so Korean records are a plausible place to look.

### Comparison with Yule–Cordier

Yule–Cordier vol. 1, Bk II ch. iv, note 6 (DSR scan 0648; the OCR running head suggests printed p. 342) is Gaubil's summary. It has Nayan's general reconnoitring with 100,000 men, the emperor in danger, horsemen carrying foot soldiers behind them, Li Ting's fire-pao with ten men, attacks by Li Ting, Yusitemur and Tutuha, Nayan taken and killed, and the battle near the Liao.

| Element | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Personal command by Khubilai | Supported | Annals; j. 154 |
| Li Ting, fire-pao, ten men | Supported | j. 162 (EX) |
| Yuxi-temür's role | Supported | j. 154 (Hong Wan) |
| Tutuha's role | Not checked | j. 128 not read |
| "Near the Liao" for the main battle | Not found | The texts I read place the Liao crossing in the separate Xianping/Yizhou action by Shidur's men, not at Sa'erdulu |
| Infantry riding pillion | Probably derived from Polo | Pelliot's note (snippet) breaks in mid-sentence: something "on the crupper" must be taken from Marco Polo himself. Read the full page before relying on this. |

### The elephant platform

- **An elephant conveyance on this campaign: supported, second-hand.** Pelliot, *Notes on Marco Polo* II, s.v. "293. Naian" (DSR scan 0193; printed page not seen): Rashid al-Din says Qubilai, old and gouty, travelled in a litter on an elephant's back. Pelliot's references are to d'Ohsson II, 460 and Blochet II, 534. Rashid is independent of Polo.
- **Elephant litters as court equipment: supported.** Pelliot notes the first elephant-litters made in late 1280 (Yuan shi 11, 3b) and another in 1325 (29, 8a). I did not open those two passages.
- **The bartizan itself: not found outside Polo.** That means the wooden tower on four elephants, with the standard and crossbowmen. The Chinese passages I read (j. 14, j. 154, Li Ting extract) contain no elephant at all.

**Limitations:** Rashid is known to me only through a search-snippet view of Pelliot; j. 119 and j. 128 were not read.

**Cautious wording:** "Chinese annals date Khubilai's departure to the fifth month of 1287 and the first engagement, at Sa'erdulu, to the sixth. They mention no elephants. The Persian historian Rashid al-Din, independently of Polo, has the ageing Khan travel in an elephant-borne litter; the four-elephant tower is Polo's alone."

## 2. Yangzhou (II-11) — answered at translation level

**What I read:** Vogel's Yangzhou section in the authorised Chinese translation (Peking University Press, 2022), excerpted in full by The Paper. I did not read the Brill English edition, so I give no Brill page numbers. Peng Hai's 2010 book and Zhu Jiang's articles: NOT SEEN; everything below about them is Vogel's report.

### Three levels, kept apart

**(a) A document naming Polo: none.** Peng Hai searched the Yangzhou offices at every level and found no trace of Polo as route administrator or darughachi; the eight Huaidong pacification posts for 1276–88 are likewise unconnected with him.

**(b) Historically possible appointment.** Foreigners did hold route-level office: the Nestorian Mar Sargis was darughachi and vice-darughachi of Zhenjiang from 1277/78, attested in both Polo and the Zhishun Zhenjiang gazetteer. Yang Zhijiu thought a darughachi or deputy post more plausible than route administrator.

**(c) Specific hypotheses.**

| Scholar | Proposed post | Date | Evidence offered | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peng Hai | Audit commissioner (lisuan guan), rank 3, "Yangzhou and other places" | Between May and Nov 1282 to Feb 1284, then Hangzhou | Yuan shi entry for Zhiyuan 21, 1st month, jiazi (1284), abolishing the Yangzhou audit officials and passing their business to the province | Indirect; names nobody. Vogel calls one of Peng's seven arguments somewhat self-contradictory and says the link to Polo cannot be fully proven. |
| Zhu Jiang (1995) | Darughachi of Yangzhou route | Same time as Mar Sargis | Inference from Polo's knowing the Zhenjiang governor of the adjoining route | Inference only |
| Pelliot | Salt administration | — | — | Vogel thinks this very unlikely, because Polo never mentions the Liang-Huai salt office at Yangzhou. |
| Vogel | Some office not excluded; "three years" may be inflated | c. 1282–84 | 27 dependent cities match the Huaidong circuit list; a 1282 three-year rotation rule | Modern interpretation |

Other views Vogel reports: de Rachewiltz (boast, perhaps a temporary inspector or envoy) and Haw (two or three years, perhaps tied to the arms industry).

### Witnesses for seignora / séjourna

| Witness | Reading | Seen via |
|---|---|---|
| F (BnF fr. 1116) | Polo "seigneurie" this city three years | Yule's note (EX); Vogel (FT, in translation) |
| Pauthier's MS A | "ot seigneurie … trois ans" — Yule says it "appears to read" so | Yule (EX) |
| Pauthier's MS C and the Bern MS (Vogel's siglum: B1, ed. 2006) | "sejourna … iii ans accompliz" by the Khan's command | Vogel quoting Yule |
| Ramusio | "ebbe il governo" in place of one of the barons | Vogel |
| Z (Toledo) and two other early MSS | Passage absent | Schütte, as reported by Vogel |

Yule himself allowed that séjourna may be a slip for seigneura. Schütte blames Ramusio for fusing the "twelve barons" sentence with Polo's own post. I saw no manuscript image and no critical apparatus.

**Limitations:**
- The 1284 annals entry is taken from Vogel's quotation; I did not open j. 13.
- The translation dates paper money in Jiang-Huai to "Zhiyuan 20, 6th month (1280)". Zhiyuan 20 is 1283, so one of the two is wrong; check Brill.

**Cautious wording:** "No Chinese document names Polo at Yangzhou. The best French manuscripts say he 'held lordship' there for three years; a group of others says only that he 'stayed'. Proposals that he was an audit commissioner (Peng Hai) or a route overseer (Zhu Jiang) are hypotheses built on indirect evidence."

## 3. Kinsay / Hangzhou (II-13) — partly answered; attribution not confirmed

### The 347 bridges and 35 li

- **Not in Mascherpa 2017.** I read "Sulla fonte Z del Milione di Ramusio. L'enigma di Quinsai" in full. Neither "347" nor "35 li" appears.
- **Mascherpa 2018 not seen.** His chapter "Una Venezia d'Oriente" (in *Predicatori, mercanti, pellegrini*) may carry the figures.
- **347 traces to Moule, second-hand.** Pochmalicki's footnote reports that Moule, *Quinsai* (1957), pp. 23–24, counts 347 bridges, 117 in the city and 230 in the environs. So the figure already mixes two areas, city and environs.
- **Moule's Chinese source, date and boundary: NOT SEEN.** A Southern Song gazetteer is likely but unverified.
- **35 li: NOT FOUND anywhere.**

### What the Polo witnesses say (Mascherpa 2017, pp. 55 and 59)

| Witness | Circuit | Bridges |
|---|---|---|
| F 151 | 100 miles | 12,000, of stone |
| Zt 85 | 100 miles | 12,000, mostly stone, some wooden |
| Ramusio II 68 | 100 miles "by common opinion" | 12,000 "by repute" |

F presents these figures as taken from a written document, not as Polo's own count.

### Registration, door plaques, fire watches, baths

- **Baths.** Mascherpa says Ramusio's cold-bath passage, doubted by Yule, is accepted by Gernet and supported by Ming-period documentation cited by Schafer. That is Ming evidence, not Yuan.
- **Carriage bridges and pleasure carriages.** His parallels come from the Mengliang lu in Moule's translation. That is a Song-oriented text, not Yuan evidence.
- **Door notices.** One lead, snippet only and second-hand: a Yuan dianzhang clause on writing an offender's label in large characters on the whitewashed wall at his gate, and "door-by-door whitewashed-wall" postings in Yuan dianzhang. These are punitive or public notices in general Yuan law. They are not Polo's household lists and not specific to Hangzhou.
- **Household registration and fire watches at Yuan Hangzhou: NOT FOUND in this session.**

**Cautious wording:** "A modern count from Chinese sources (Moule) gives 347 bridges, about a third inside the walls. Polo's 12,000 is reported hearsay in every version." Do not print "35 li". Do not attribute 347 to Mascherpa without checking his 2018 chapter.

## 4. Dadu and Shangdu (II-02; I-13) — partly answered

No excavation report was opened. What follows rests on publisher contents pages, an abstract, snippets and one heritage-database summary.

### Excavated or surveyed (Dadu)

- **The 2024 report.** *Yuan Dadu: 1964–1974 nian kaogu baogao* (Wenwu, March 2024). Part I reports walls, streets and waterways; the Heyimen barbican gate; water culverts; and the Houyingfang and two Yonghegong residential sites. Access: MD/AB.
- **Heyimen.** Found in 1969 under the Ming Xizhimen arrow tower, with the gate platform and passage well preserved.
- **Culverts.** Two stone culverts under the east and west wall footings.
- **Houyingfang.** A large courtyard residence, over 89 m east–west.
- **Preservation.** The houses survived because they were buried under the north wall when Xu Da shortened the city in 1368.
- **Finds.** Ceramics, iron objects, tools and building components.

### Reconstructed, not excavated (Dadu)

- **The report itself separates them.** Part II is special studies, including a chapter on planning and reconstruction.
- **Palace city.** The heritage-database summary puts the south gate "approximately" at the Taihe dian, says the north gate's rammed-earth base was found north of Jingshan, and says the east and west wall footings were removed in the Ming, surviving at most about 16 m wide.
- **Halls.** Fu Xinian's 1993 study reconstructs the Daming dian and Yanchun ge groups and lists calculated dimensions. The dimensions derive from the Nancun chuogeng lu. These are textual, not measured.
- **Method.** Xu Bin describes the layout reconstruction as relying on street-pattern comparison and module calculation. His 2022 book says the standard plan was inferred from historical maps and surviving streets.
- **Dispute.** A critic calls the Taihe dian = Chongtian men equation subjective and without archaeological basis, and rejects inferring the Yanchun ge from building rubble found by drilling at Jingshan. I did not verify the author of that book.

**Xu Bin's review.** The item you mean is evidently "Yuan Dadu guihua fuyuan yanjiu zongshu", *Gugong xuekan* 2019 no. 1, pp. 44–55; the Palace Museum's PDF is filed under November 2020. Access: AB plus snippets.

### Shangdu

- **Excavated.** The 2009 campaign clarified the Mingde gate, moats and the extent of the Muqing ge. Wei Jian's *Yuan Shangdu* (2008) is the report; finds are also cited from the Da'an ge site. Wei Jian's report: NS.
- **Cane palace.** I found no excavation evidence. Its identification with the "Zongmao dian" appeared only in a non-scholarly essay placing it outside the imperial city's north gate. Treat as textual identification of a demountable structure; site unconfirmed.
- **Marble.** One visitor essay, not a report, says the Shangdu halls were mostly earth and timber, not marble.

**Cautious wording:** "Excavation has documented Dadu's earthen walls, one barbican gate, drains and several houses. The palace plan and hall dimensions are reconstructions from fourteenth-century texts and later street lines; the standing Forbidden City is Ming." For Shangdu: "the cane palace is known from texts; no excavated remains have been identified in the sources consulted."

## 5. Vochan / Burma, 1277 (II-08) — answered for the Chinese side

**Source:** Yuan shi j. 210, "Mian" (FT, Nagoya University e-text).

| | 3rd month, Zhiyuan 14 (spring 1277) | 10th month (autumn 1277) |
|---|---|---|
| Yuan commanders | Hudu (Mongol chiliarch of Dali), Xinju Ri (Dali route administrator), Tuoluotuohai | Nasr al-Din, sent by the Yunnan province |
| Yuan force | "Only 700": 281, 233 and 187 in three detachments | Over 3,840 Mongol, Cuan, Bo and Mosuo troops |
| Burmese force | About 40,000–50,000 men, 800 elephants, 10,000 horses | None stated |
| Place | By a river after a forced march from Nandian; pursuit to Qian'e | Jiangtou |
| Action | Armoured elephants with fighting towers and bamboo tubes of short spears; a rout over some 30 li; 17 stockades broken; Hudu wounded; one Mongol killed handling a captured elephant | Over 300 stockades and 35,200 households submit; withdrawal because of heat. No elephant battle. |

**Against Polo:**
- Yule–Cordier II, chs. 51–52. I saw only the running head "Battle with the King of Mien", p. 103, and the contents entry for notes on chronology and on the numbers alleged to have been carried on elephants.
- From memory, not re-read: Polo dates it 1272, names "Nescradin" as commander, and gives 12,000 horse against 60,000 men and 2,000 elephants. Verify on vol. 2 pp. 98–104 before printing.
- If those figures hold, Polo's commander belongs to the October expedition, his elephant battle to the March one, and none of his numbers matches either.

**Later events to keep separate:** the 1283 capture of Jiangtou and the 1287 advance to Pagan with over 7,000 lost.

**Other Burmese or Yuan evidence:** NOT SEEN (no Burmese chronicle or inscription consulted; j. 125 not opened).

**Cautious wording:** "The Chinese account dates the elephant battle to spring 1277 and names Hudu, with 700 men, as commander. Nasr al-Din, whom Polo credits, led a separate expedition that autumn."
