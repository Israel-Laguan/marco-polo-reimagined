## Newly checked independently: Rashid's Kököchin passage

**Found:** John Andrew Boyle, “[Rashīd al-Dīn and the Franks](https://www.jstor.org/stable/41926863),” *Central Asiatic Journal* 14, no. 1/3 (1970): 62–67, at pp. 66–67 (`boyle1970rashidfranks`). An [accessible article scan](https://ia800504.us.archive.org/8/items/Boyle1970RashidFranks/Boyle_1970_Rashid_Franks.pdf) was read, including the passage and surrounding notes. It was located during this intake, **not** by the Consensus report. We inspected Boyle's English rendering of an extract, not the Persian original or the entire chronicle.

On p. 66 Boyle renders Rashid as saying that Khwāja and the envoys whom Arghun sent to the Qa'an brought Kököchin, and that Ghazan met and married her at Abhar. **The translated extract does not name the Polos or describe the sea route.** Boyle identifies Khwāja with Polo's Coja and argues on pp. 66–67 that the unnamed companions were presumably the Polos. That is **Boyle's inference**, not a name appearing in the quoted Persian passage. His identification of Khwāja is interpretive too: he notes that Arends translated the term as an office rather than treating it as a proper name. His date is a reconstruction and must not be passed off as a date stated in the passage. The extract is too limited to establish a blanket negative about all of Rashid's writings.

Boyle's footnote 18 cites **Karl Jahn, *Geschichte Ġāzān-Ḫān’s*, Persian text p. 40**, and **A. A. Alizade, *Dzhami-at-Tavarikh*, III, p. 280**; footnote 19 cites A. K. Arends's Russian translation, p. 157. We have not inspected those underlying pages, nor located the corresponding passage in Thackston. Peter Jackson independently identifies Alizade III p. 280, with p. 281, in a [1998 article note](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-school-of-oriental-and-african-studies/article/abs/marco-polo-and-his-travels1/43D0A1957AEB261843E441018C033DE8). These are **verified scholarly source references, not directly verified Persian-page contents**. Cleaves 1976 remains uninspected.
