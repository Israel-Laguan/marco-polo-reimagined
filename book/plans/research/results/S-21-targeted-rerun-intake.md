## Ten purposive claim-level citation checks

The [research protocol](../README.md) requires ten checks. These test selected report-cited sources against the particular propositions listed below. **7/10 hits** at the reported level of access. A miss can reflect an overbroad conclusion or an inaccessible passage, not a fabricated work or false underlying historical event. This is a purposive sample, not a statistical estimate of the report's accuracy. Since the hit rate is below 80%, the protocol requires a [stricter rerun](../S-21-targeted-rerun-2-prompt.md); do not certify the other claims based on this sample.

## Decisions and remaining gaps

### High priority — affects an existing assertion

- **III-07 Socotra.** Keep the hunt attributed to the Ramusio/Yule tradition; the draft's Note already identifies it as Ramusian, but its main narrative does not. A buoy marks the line in Yule. Casks appear as a quantity of head oil in a different English rendering, not as a line float in the checked texts. F, Z and the original 1559 Italian remain uncollated. The suggested change is a provenance label, not an assertion that Marco witnessed a whale hunt.
- **II-07 Carajan.** The report's suggested wording jumps from an unidentified traveler account to actual large crocodilians native to those precise rivers. That conclusion exceeds the evidence it accessed. Safer: “The Book describes large, clawed ‘serpents’ in Carajan, often interpreted as crocodilians; their species and the report's local historical basis remain uncertain.” Wen et al. 1980 is an identified targeted lead, not a verified historical-range result.
- **I-07–08 Kerman.** Use the book's region and qualified “ondanique”; neither the specific Shahr-i Babak mine nor a Chahak source for Polo's steel is established. The GIA date remains ambiguous, and the underlying Weisgerber chapter still requires inspection if the specific claim is retained.
- **III-05 pearls and horse trade.** Textual fishery rates can be attributed to the Book; archaeological middens do not independently establish them for Polo's decade. Do not state either 1856 or 1885 as the settled institutional end. These dates concern later Ceylon history, not proof of medieval continuity.
- **II-14 / III-06 ceramics and indigo.** Describe Dehua as Lin and Zhang's archaeological identification with Changting as an alternative; dating the San Marco jar does not prove who brought it. Coilum indigo remains the Book's reported claim until period-specific Kerala evidence is inspected.
- **III-06 diamonds.** Retain the valley episode; earlier named analogues and a transmission chain are not verified by this return. Sources' claims about real extraction should be checked independently of the legend.
- **II-07 salt money.** The required F/Z/R comparison was not completed; Yule's own Ramusio attribution remains an edition-level finding. Do not call a specific difference a manuscript fact without checking that witness.

### Low priority — optional unless selected

- Trace the precise 1856 and 1885 colonial records if a later-history Note is wanted; otherwise omit the end date.
- Inspect Epiphanius, Buzurg and early Sindbad witnesses if naming a specific lineage; otherwise call the diamond episode a reported marvel.
- Trace the original wording behind the English casks-of-oil rendering if that detail is selected. Do not infer it describes a float.
- Obtain Weisgerber's 2004 chapter, the 1980 Chinese crocodile study, and the named Kerala sources if their specific claims are selected. A bibliography entry or unrelated forestry item is not a substitute.
