## 2. Short prose: where scholars disagree

**Arghun’s death date.** The cleanest explanation is not a scholarly quarrel but a calendar error. The Persian sources give **7 Rabīʿ I 690**, which converts to **10 March 1291**. The “7 March” form arises when the Hijri day-number is read as a Gregorian day. Yule/Cordier print 10 March and note that a Georgian source gives 12 March, probably for burial or a different ceremony. I did not find a scholar who explicitly adjudicates “7 vs 10 March,” so the reconciliation should be presented as calendrical, not as a documented scholarly consensus.

**Nayan’s descent and religion.** Pelliot argued that Nayan descended from **Temüge Otchigin**, not from Belgutai, in *Notes on Marco Polo*. The Christian label rests overwhelmingly on Marco Polo’s cross-standard story; Rashid al-Dīn and the Yuan shi do not, in the reports I saw, give a baptism or a formal Christian affiliation. The “age of thirty” is likewise Marco Polo’s figure, not a Chinese administrative datum.

**Marco’s capture.** The two positions are clear: **Ramusio** places it at **Curzola in 1298**; **Jacopo d’Acqui** places it at **Ayas/Laiazzo in 1296**. Yule rejected Ayas on chronological grounds; Larner treats the exact place as uncertain. This is a genuine disagreement, not a resolvable documentary fact on present evidence.

**Fiordelisa.** The name is a trap. Yule’s genealogy has **Fiordelisa (Trevisan?)** as Niccolò’s wife and mother of Maffeo, but Yule’s index also lists a **Fiordelisa, daughter of Maffeo the Younger**. The user’s suspicion is correct: the wife is not the daughter; there are two persons with the same name in the Polo materials.

**Zhenjiang churches.** The count depends on whether one counts only Zhenjiang or includes Hangzhou. Yule/Moule report **six in Zhenjiang**; the gazetteer’s seven foundations include the **one in Hangzhou**. So “six” and “seven” are not necessarily contradictory, but the basis of the count should be stated.
