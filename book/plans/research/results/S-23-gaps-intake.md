## What the Quanzhou report says (read directly; supersedes the earlier ships dive where they differ)

Source: Burningham and Green, ch. 4 of Green (ed.) 1997, [PDF](https://museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/sites/default/files/no._1_china_1997.pdf), pp. 32–48. The text layer has OCR slips; figure numbers are the report's.

| Topic | The report states |
|---|---|
| Size | Surviving hull about **24 m by 9 m**; the full beam "must have been substantially greater", about 10 m. The authors compute with an **overall hull length of 28 m**. The 34.6 m often quoted is not their figure; it comes from the 1975 Chinese reconstruction (*Wenwu*), which they say hardened the bilge to keep the beam down. |
| Bow and stern | A **transom stern**, with a "false counter" of outer planking extending aft; a **transom bow** is argued from the keel's forward extension, from all the regional iconography and from the Shinan ship. |
| Keel | Three parts: pine fore and aft, **camphor wood centre 12.57 m long, 420 mm wide, 270 mm deep**; scarfs hold seven iron coins and a bronze mirror (forward) and 13 coins and a mirror (aft), set as luck charms (Ursa Major, the Moon). |
| Planking | **Cedar**; inner planking 80 mm, outer 50 mm, a third layer 25 mm above the bilge; a mix of carvel and clinker seams with rabbets; planks nailed with **skew nails at exactly 200 mm**, about 200 mm long. |
| Bulkheads | Planks about 80 mm thick, **12 numbered bulkheads**, half-frames on one side, limbers 250 × 90 mm for bilge water; hull plank shell tied to bulkheads with **iron L-brackets 400–550 mm long** ("ju-nails"). Built plank-first, so the bulkheads are not what shaped the hull. |
| Seams | Lime putty with pounded jute fibre over **ramie** caulking; tung oil in the putty; lime coat inside and out. |
| Masts | A **fore** step at the keel and forward-extension junction and a **main** step with a tabernacle; uprights **375 mm apart**, so masts were slender. The mast could be **lowered**: a gap in the bulkhead ahead of the main step lets the heel swing forward; the pivot is about 2.75 m above the keel, so the hold was shallow. Authors expect a battened lugsail and a "fairly light" vessel. |
| Rudder | A **slot for an axial rudder stock cut in the outer transom**; brackets would have held the stock higher. Whether this rudder lifted is not shown by the hull. |
| Cargo | Scented woods in every compartment (mostly the 2nd and 5th), pepper from the bases of the cabins, areca nuts, frankincense, dragon's blood, cinnabar, quicksilver, tortoiseshell; **504 copper coins (33 Tang, 358 Northern Song, 70 Southern Song, 43 uncertain)**; 96 wooden tablets, 88 with ink writing (cargo labels); ceramics 56 pieces, mostly bow and stern; a bamboo ruler; rattan hats. |
| Upper works | Possibly **removed in a salvage** while the hull lay in mud: the top planks are in excellent condition, with a sharp cut-off, and some brackets were chiselled out. |
| Iconography the authors use | Zhang Zeduan's *Qingming shanghe tu* (c. 1126), Figs 10–15: river vessels with **axial semi-balanced rudders on hinges and chains "so that they can be raised and lowered"**, and one drawn with the rudder unshipped; a Bayon relief (1185) with a two-masted ship; a **drawing by Ma Hezhi dated 1170** of large river craft with a bipod mast, probably for a tow line; the *Mōko Shūrai Ekotoba* (the report dates it 1292). |

## Effect on the plates

- **III-2, rudders lifted in the shoal strait.** The raisable rudder is shown by the Northern Song *Qingming* scroll (c. 1126, river craft, 150 years early), not by the Quanzhou hull, which has only the stock slot. Caption and cue must say so.
- **II-12, trackers on the Kiang.** The Ma Hezhi drawing of 1170 (large river craft with a bipod tow mast) is a better lead than 1938 film. Holder and number unknown.
- **II-14, Zayton.** The hull gives a transom stern, probable transom bow, slender lowering masts and a shallow hold; pepper sat in the cabin bases and scented wood in every compartment. The "34.6 m, 13 holds" in `illustration-plan.md` C13 should read about 28 m as computed by the authors.
- **Armour, Dadu, Venice, ger-cart: still open.** The raw file found no in-window armour, no Yuan palace picture, no tent-on-cart image. It confirms the Cilician Armenian leaves of 1262–63 as the nearest period dress source for I-4, none viewed.
