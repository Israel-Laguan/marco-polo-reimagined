# Partial **Marco Polo Reception** Findings — Fourth Pass

The supplied corpus supports a **Dominican-reception finding**, a **Fra Mauro Japan-label finding**, and a **Columbus-marginalia finding**; it does not resolve the Chinese, Mongolian, Iranian, food, or Toscanelli questions. Most requested items remain **not found in accessible passages**, not disproved.

## Dominican Reception and the 1323 Document

Gadrat reports that the Z version was likely held in the Venetian convent of SS. Giovanni e Paolo in the fourteenth century, and that Polo's will of 9 January 1324 names the convent and two friars (Gadrat 2022, 286-301). Conte, Montefusco and Simion publish the 1323 document itself, naming "Marco Paulo de confinio Sancti Iohannis Grisostomi" among the capitular signatories (Conte et al. 2020). Montefusco attributes Z to this Venetian Dominican milieu in the first thirty years of the fourteenth century (Conte et al. 2020).

- Gadrat identifies the opening fol. 1r historiated initial as Polo with a Dominican friar, probably Pipino (Beal-Jr. 1993, 151-160).
- Conte disputes that the two figures appear to converse (Conte 2020).
- Gadrat dates the earliest located English use of Polo to John Ridewall's *Fulgentius metaforalis*, c. 1330–32 (Grčić 2021, 111-140).

## Fra Mauro, Ramusio and the Z Tradition

Davies frames the Fra Mauro map and Ramusio's compendium as the two central Renaissance sites where readers weighed Polo's non-eyewitness claims (Davies 2012, 215-234). O'Doherty locates a Fra Mauro legend using *Cimpangu* for Japan "from China via Marco Polo" (#1334) (O’Doherty 2011, 30-36), and reports a lost copy sent to Afonso V of Portugal in 1459 (O’Doherty 2011, 30-36). Grčić notes Falchetta's transcription edition but reports many toponym misidentifications (Grčić 2021, 111-140).

- Mascherpa's synoptic reading of the Quinsai chapters in F, Z and R is reported to confirm the old hypothesis that Z and Ramusio preserve Polo notes unused in 1298 (Cruse 2022, 233-240).
- Z survives in a single Toledo exemplar, Zelada 49-20, transcribed in the Veneto around the mid-fifteenth century (Olshin 2007, 1-23; Conte et al. 2020).
- The Ca' Foscari digital critical edition of Ramusio's *Dei viaggi* went online in February 2015 (Garkavets 2017, 522-565; Conte 2020).

**Access limit:** the specific FM *2442 and *2306 inscriptions are not transcribed in any supplied passage; the Cimpangu/Cimpagu and Hachbaluch/Hacbaluch variants cannot be resolved against a map image here.

## Columbus's Annotated Copy

Shawcross reports that the surviving exemplar is an incunable of Pipino's Latin translation and that its margins contain "copious notes and drawings attributable to Columbus himself and other members of his immediate circle" (Shawcross 2023, 376-424). Columbus's notes consist of brief references to objects or lists of objects associated with specific places, with emphasis on gold, pearls, spices and mineral deposits (Shawcross 2023, 376-424). Beal confirms that several volumes bearing Columbus's handwritten annotations survive in the Columbian Library in Seville (Beal-Jr. 1993, 151-160), and Wood notes that Columbus's own copy with marginal notes still survives there (Wood 2018, 140-151).

**Access limit:** the supplied Shawcross excerpts give classmarks 10-3-2 and V-117-13 and Hernando's call numbers 1069 and 2741 (Shawcross 2023, 376-424), but do not contain the disputed fol. 66v note, the 1499/1500/1501 dating, or the shelfmarks cited in the second dossier. Those claims remain **unverified** (level 6).
