## Editorial triage

**High: existing prose and source fidelity.** III-06 already contains the diamond-valley episode: retain it as a reported episode and do not import the pass 2 denial. Its older parallels, Epiphanius content and any line of transmission remain open. III-07 already identifies the whale-hunt in its Note as Ramusian; ensure the main retelling also makes this provenance clear where necessary. A buoy is textual; a barrel/cask has not been located and must not be silently substituted. This is Yule's attribution, not a completed witness-by-witness collation. II-07's crocodilian identification remains qualified to a crocodilian; no exact species or medieval local range follows from the mismatched citation. I-07–08: the Chahak studies concern an actual Persian steel tradition, but do not demonstrate the origin of Polo's ondanique. III-05: preserve horse mortality and fishery shares as the Book's claims; later parallels do not independently confirm exact 13th-century figures. The 1885 source is evidence for a later fishery institution, not proof of uninterrupted continuity.

**High: priorities still open.** Inspect named F/Z/R witnesses for any publication-critical additions, including Socotra and salt, rather than inferring a witness from a composite translation. Get period- and location-specific studies for crocodilians, shark-charmer institution and pearl shares, Kerman turquoise and ondanique, Coilum indigo, and horse mortality if the prose makes stronger independent historical claims. For Pamir and Lop, do not treat general physics or an unrelated desert as proof of a specific observation or explanation. Check current III-06/III-07 manuscript wording against these qualifications; make chapter-level edits in the normal editorial pass, not by accepting report verdicts.

**Low: optional enrichment.** Find Epiphanius and early Sindbad parallels with exact witnesses if their genealogy is selected; distinguish Rubruck's mechanical fountain from Polo's magical cups without asserting that no relationship could exist. A documented 1885 end to employment is usable as an attributed 1908 statement; whether this corresponds to a separate alleged 1856 administrative decision is still open. The Met textile and later Mannar assemblages can furnish dated examples, not confirmation of an individual Polo-period object or practice. The San Marco jar is traditionally associated with Polo but has no established Polo ownership. Do not turn any of these into mandatory research once safe wording exists.

Moule–Pelliot, Yule, and different manuscript witnesses are editions or textual traditions of Polo; their agreement is not independent observation of the historical event. Conversely a source's failure to mention a proposed connection is not proof it never existed. The raw reports' proposed conclusions and bibliographic entries remain unpromoted unless independently checked above or in a future intake.
