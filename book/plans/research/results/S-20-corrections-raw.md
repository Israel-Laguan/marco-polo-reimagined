### Editorial Implications

Hargrave provides more than a date: she interprets Marsden’s annotations as assembling limited British knowledge into an apparently comprehensive account that portrayed China as culturally stagnant. This is **Hargrave’s reception argument**, not a neutral description of China or proof of Marsden’s intentions (Hargrave, 2016).

The Cordier date also corrects an inconsistency within the supplied scholarship: Cruse’s 2017 bibliography calls the 1903 publication a “2nd ed.,” whereas Haw identifies it as the third edition; the supplied Dover reprint title independently describes an “unabridged third edition (1903).” Use **third edition**, while retaining the distinction between inspected bibliographic descriptions and an unchecked original title page (Cruse, 2017; Haw, 2020). Laufer’s April 1921 review is a separate reception milestone, not the publication date of Cordier’s volume (Laufer & Cordier, 1921).

Conte rejects Gadrat-Ouerfelli’s suggestion that the manuscript figures appear to converse: she observes that Polo looks upward rather than toward Pipino. A caption can identify the represented figures and acknowledge interpretation, but should not call the image an authenticated likeness (Conte, 2020).

## Textual and Map Qualifications

**Z is a textual tradition, not simply the surviving Toledo manuscript.** Montefusco describes Toledo, Zelada 49.20 as a northern Italian paper copy from the second half of the fifteenth century, preserving an abbreviated Latin recension (Montefusco, 2020). Consequently, “Fra Mauro used Z” should not imply that he used the extant Toledo volume.

Mascherpa’s Ghisi discussion concerns the transmission of the **Quinsai chapters**. He favors supplementation from notes retained by Polo or his family, while explicitly offering interpolation from other Venetian travel collections as a less economical alternative (Mascherpa, 2018). His alternative remains important: it prevents treating additional detail in Ramusio as automatically authenticated Polo testimony (Mascherpa, 2018).

Magone supplies a related competing assessment. Reviewing Vogel, he finds the attempt to rehabilitate Ramusio’s salt passage somewhat strained, particularly the assertion that no source other than Polo could have supplied the information; Magone leaves that conclusion uncertain (Magone, 2015). These arguments concern textual additions, **not an identified Fra Mauro legend or a demonstrated Ghisi-to-map chain**.

The Eusebi–Burgio edition’s supplied description supports the linguistic proximity of fr. 1116 to the original work and identifies a revised text, glossary, and indexes; it supplies **no 1320–30 dating or Foligno watermark argument** (Eusebi & Burgio, 2018).
