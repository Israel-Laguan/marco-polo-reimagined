**Book-form analysis for the English retelling.** Policy tested against Yule (Yule–Cordier 1903/1920 base), Latham (Penguin 1958), Moule & Pelliot 1938, Pelliot *Notes on Marco Polo*, and English Wikipedia as the yardstick for common modern usage. ### Specific questions answered first
- **Chandu vs Shangdu/Xanadu**: Yule uses **Chandu** (or Ciandu/Xandu in notes/Ramusio). Wikipedia title is **Shangdu** (also known as Xanadu). Xanadu dominates popular English via Coleridge; Shangdu is the scholarly/Chinese form. Recommendation: keep **Chandu** as book form (Yule + Polo manuscripts); gloss "Chandu (Shangdu / Xanadu)".
- **Kinsay vs Quinsay**: Yule uses **Kinsay** (chapter headings and text; "City of Heaven"). Variants include Quinsay/Quinsai (common in other editions and some modern scholarship). Wikipedia article is Hangzhou; historical references use both Kinsay and Quinsay. Recommendation: keep **Kinsay** (Yule dominant).
- **Zayton vs Zaiton**: Yule uses **Zayton**. Zaiton/Zaitun also frequent (Arabic olive-tree association). Wikipedia: Quanzhou (historical Zayton/Zaiton). Recommendation: **Zayton** (Yule).
- **Hormos vs Hormuz**: Yule uses **Hormos** (mainland Old Hormuz). Modern English overwhelmingly **Hormuz**. Wikipedia: Hormuz (island/old city). Recommendation: keep **Hormos** as book form for fidelity; gloss "Hormos (Old Hormuz)".
- **Tauris vs Tabriz**: Yule **Tauris**. Modern English and Wikipedia: **Tabriz**. Recommendation: keep **Tauris**; gloss "Tauris (Tabriz)".
- **Kisi vs Kish**: Yule **Kisi** (or variants). Modern: **Kish**. Recommendation: keep **Kisi**; gloss "Kisi (Kish)".

**Yule vs Latham differences** (selected): Latham often modernizes slightly or follows different manuscript traditions (e.g., more "Quinsai", "Ormuz", smoother English forms). Yule stays closer to the Franco-Italian/Latin manuscript spellings with heavier annotation. Use Yule for book forms per policy.

**Widely known English exonyms not Polo's** (Wikipedia treatment):
- Peking → Beijing (Wikipedia redirects Peking to Beijing; historical use noted).
- Canton → Guangzhou.
- Ceylon → Sri Lanka (Wikipedia: Sri Lanka; Ceylon historical).
- Persia → Iran (Wikipedia: Iran; Persia historical/cultural).
- Others: Siam → Thailand, etc. Policy works well: retain Polo/Yule form in prose, gloss the modern/common name.
