**3. The inventory.** Partly answered, from a different work.
- **Source and access:** Formentin & Schiavon, "Per il Codice Diplomatico Poliano: una sentenza memorabile (Venezia, 13 luglio 1366)," *Reti Medievali Rivista* 25.1 (2024): 193–231, [open-access PDF](https://www.rmoa.unina.it/7054/1/RM_1-2024_Formentin-Schiavon.pdf). Access level is extract/OCR read. The page numbers below come from running heads in the extracted text and need checking against the PDF.
- **Authorship:** An editorial note says Formentin wrote §3, the edition and commentary. The 2024 authors do not claim this is the 2023 article's text.
- **Evidence status:** The 1366 notary's copy is a manuscript reading. Formentin calls it a "fedelissima" copy of the inventory Bragadin drew up in 1324. That is the editors' view, which I did not check against the image.
- **Silver belt:** Contradicted in its popular form. The edition's silver items are "Item çentura d'arçento j, ll. j, s. 2 de gross." and "çentura j de fil d'arçento" (p. 216). I found no "Tartar knight" in the transcription, though I only searched for the keywords below, so I cannot rule it out. Haw's abstract of his 2024 *kešig* paper (profile, abstract only) says the belt "of a Tartar knight" "has been questioned".
- **"bocheta":** The transcription reads "Item bocheta j d'oro con piere e perle — ll. 3, s. 8 de gross." (p. 216). Formentin glosses it as "gioiello d'oro e di gemme", from Latin *bŭcca* (p. 218). The commentary says the mongol *boghta* headdress reading is "un abbaglio" that keeps being repeated from a transcription error ("bochta") in manuscripts M and P (apparatus, p. 217; commentary, p. 218).
- **Asian items, with certainty as the editors state it:**
  - "tola j d'oro granda de comandamento": the *table d'or des comandemens*, a Mongol paiza, valued at 20 lire of grossi, the most valuable single item (pp. 218–219). Matteo's 1310 will mentions "tribus tabulis de auro" of the Great Khan, which the editors cite.
  - "choltre 3 a lavorieri tartaresci": three Eastern imports (pp. 220–221).
  - "varnimento j biancho ala ‹trar› tartaresca": a Tartar-style garment, with a reading marked as uncertain in the edition (p. 216).
  - Musk, aloe wood and rhubarb appear in the inventory. The origins are given by the editors' commentary; I did not trace each one.
  - "sacheto … de pelo ch'è dela bestia": possibly yak hair, a suggestion from Montesano and Molà. This is a modern interpretation only.
- **Limits:** None of these items shows that Marco was present in Asia or an eyewitness. They are objects in a Venetian inventory.

**Cautious wording:** "A Venetian inventory of 1324, preserved in a 1366 notarial copy, lists a gold *tavola de comandamento* and Tartar-style quilts. A 'silver belt of a Tartar knight' and a Mongol *boghta* headdress do not appear in the editors' reading."
