# Four Marco Polo endnotes need correcting

None of the four endnotes is fully supported as written. Each is either compressed or misattributes something. **(1) Hunting:** the endnote merges two rules. In Yule–Cordier the 20-days'-journey radius applies to *keeping hawks and hounds*. The March–October game ban applies "throughout all the Emperor's territories". Pétis de la Croix gives the **same** months as Polo, not the reverse. The "reverse" comes from Yule's own note 9 and looks like Yule's slip. No Yuan legal text (Yuan dianzhang, Tongzhi tiaoge, Yuan shi) was read: access was blocked, so Yuan corroboration is **NOT SEEN**. **(2) Persia:** all eight identifications match Yule–Cordier. Pelliot confirms the four entries we checked (Suolstan/Cielstan, Istanit, Soncara, Tunocain). In the 1270s–90s, Great and Little Lur, Fars (until 1284) and probably Shabankara were held by local dynasts under the Ilkhans. Aubin and Morgan were not seen, and we read Melville only in passing. **(3) Golden King:** Pelliot places Caichu at **Jiangzhou 絳州**. Jiezhou 解州 is Wylie's and Richthofen's proposal, Jizhou 吉州 is Yule's, and Hezhong/Puzhou is Polo's *Cacionfu*, not Caichu. "Golden King = Jin emperor (Altun Khan)" goes back to Marsden and Yule. Every searcher, from Wylie to Cordier, found no trace of the story in the Jin or Liao histories. Haw 2006 was not seen. **(4) Household gods:** "Itoga" is **Carpini's** form, not an earlier Polo reading. Pelliot equated Natigai with Ytoga and Ötükän. Mostaert and Ragagnin instead link it to a goddess *Načigai*, attested in a Mongolian fragment of 1312. Yule himself thought the Cathayan-household passage was interpolated. 天地君親師 tablets are Ming–Qing, not Yuan.

## Conclusion: where the evidence stops

All four endnotes inherit Yule's framing rather than independent evidence. In two places they amplify an error. Yule's "reverse" remark about Pétis does not survive a reading of Pétis. And the endnote moves the 20-day radius from hawks and hounds onto the game ban. The strongest independent check would be the Yuan legal texts, and that is exactly where access failed. Until the *Yuan dianzhang* 打捕 articles and the Shizu edicts are read, any statement that Chinese law "confirms" Polo is unsupported. Elsewhere the scholarship has moved beyond Yule: Pelliot on Jiangzhou and on Ispaan, and Mostaert and Ragagnin on Načigai. A 1903-based retelling should flag these updates rather than present Yule as the last word. None of the corroborating facts (real Lur atabegs, a real Jin "Golden" dynasty, real Mongol felt idols) establishes that Polo was present or saw these things himself.

**Unresolved questions:**
- Do Yuan codes set a seasonal hunting ban? If so, which lunar months, and over what radius in li?
- Does the slip in Yule's note 9 go back to his 1871 edition?
- Which translation supplied the endnote's "fallow deer"?
- Where, if anywhere, did Pelliot treat the «Roi d'Or» (check the vol. III index)?
- What do Haw 2006 and the Chinese commentators say about Caichu?
- What are Pelliot's views on Casvin, Curdistan, Lor and Serazy?
- What was the administrative status of Qazvin, Kurdistan and Quhistan c. 1280 (Aubin, Iranica)?
- What is Mostaert's correct date, and what is Carpini's exact Latin context?
- Is there any evidence for domestic ancestor or Heaven tablets in Yuan China?

**Best safe fallback wording, one line per endnote:**
- **II.20:** "Polo reports an empire-wide ban on hunting hare and deer from March to October, and a ban on keeping hawks or hounds within 20 days of the court; whether Yuan law matches is unverified."
- **I.15:** "The eight 'kingdoms' are regions of Ilkhanid Iran, several still under local dynasts in Polo's day."
- **II.38–39:** "The tale is unattested; the 'Golden King' is usually taken to be the Jin emperor, and Pelliot placed the castle at Jiangzhou in Shanxi."
- **II.34 / I.53:** "Natigai's identity is debated (Etügen vs a goddess Načigai), and Yule suspected the Chinese household passage was partly interpolated."
