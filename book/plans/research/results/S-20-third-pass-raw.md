## Fra Mauro, Ramusio and the Z Tradition

Davies frames the Fra Mauro map and Ramusio's compendium as the two central Renaissance sites where readers weighed Polo's non-eyewitness claims (Davies, 2012). Mascherpa's synoptic reading of the Quinsai chapters in F, Z and R is reported to confirm an old, debated hypothesis: that Z and Ramusio's Italian version (mostly from a lost Z manuscript) preserve rewriting incorporating Polo notes unused in the 1298 Franco-Italian text (Mascherpa, 2018). Z survives in a single Toledo exemplar, Zelada 49-20, transcribed in the Veneto around the mid-fifteenth century (Mascherpa, 2018).

- Ragagnin and Simion list Z as the Toledo Zelada 49.20 manuscript and R as Ramusio's 1559 text (Ragagnin & Simion, 2018).
- The Ca' Foscari digital critical edition of Ramusio's *Dei viaggi* went online in February 2015 (Simion, 2017; Ragagnin & Simion, 2018).
- Grčić notes Falchetta's transcription edition but reports many toponym misidentifications (Grčić, 2021).

**Access limit:** the specific FM *2442 and *2306 inscriptions are not transcribed in any supplied passage. O'Doherty does locate a Fra Mauro legend using *Cimpangu* for Japan "from China via Marco Polo" (#1334) (O’Doherty, 2011), and reports a lost copy sent to Afonso V of Portugal in 1459 (O’Doherty, 2011). That is a Japan-label datum, not the Ghisi-source argument.

## Dominican Reception and the 1323 Document

Gadrat reports that the Z version was likely held in the Venetian convent of SS. Giovanni e Paolo in the fourteenth century, and that Polo's will of 9 January 1324 names the convent and two friars (Gadrat, 2022). She also reports Polo as a witness on 31 March 1323 to the Dominicans' acceptance of a gift (Gadrat, 2022). Conte, Montefusco and Simion publish the 1323 document itself, naming "Marco Paulo de confinio Sancti Iohannis Grisostomi" among the capitular signatories (Conte et al., 2020).

- Gadrat identifies the opening fol. 1r historiated initial as Polo with a Dominican friar, probably Pipino (Gadrat, 2022).
- Montefusco attributes Z to this Venetian Dominican milieu in the first thirty years of the fourteenth century (Conte et al., 2020).
- Gadrat dates the earliest located English use of Polo to John Ridewall's *Fulgentius metaforalis*, c. 1330–32 (Gadrat, 2022).

## Mongolian, Chinese and Iranian Reception

Ragagnin and Simion describe their joint Mongolian–Romance philology on Polo's medieval sources, with a *Mongolica Pragensia* piece on Polo's boodog recipe forthcoming (Ragagnin & Simion, 2018). They do not identify the 2019 Ramusio translation or any 2024 expanded edition; the relationship between those editions remains **unresolved** (level 6).
