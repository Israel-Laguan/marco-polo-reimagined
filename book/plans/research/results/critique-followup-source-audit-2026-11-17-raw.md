# Audit overturns Ramusio-only claim on Samara

The audit does not support the earlier report as written. Of **36 checked claims, 25 held (69 percent)**, short of the 80 percent bar, so the corrections and holds below must be applied before any of that report's statements enter the manuscript. The **wooden defences on landing are in F and V as well as in Z and Ramusio**, so the "Ramusio-only" tag is wrong. The **2,000 men and the ditches** appear in Z and Ramusio but not in F, and whether the coconut-liquid comparison is exclusive to Ramusio **remains unresolved**. Yule's notes reckon the embassy's timing two ways, not one, and Pelliot himself dates the departure to **early 1291**. Rashid's date for Arghun's death, **10 March 1291 (Julian)**, makes Arghun's status at sailing a conditional matter, not a fact. The Chinese notice records a dispatch order and a rations decision, not a sailing; the stele's burial in 845 is conjecture; and Jacopo's numeral 1296 is contested, while Bolognari's 2026 deposition depends on an identification the author does not argue.

**Basis of this audit.** Every "inspected" passage below was read by the note-takers, not by me in this pass. Their reads were publisher PDF text, OCR text layers, HTML, or fetch-tool extractions. None is a page-image check. Where a tool returned a model-mediated extraction, the label says so.

**The coconut comparison.** The answer is not established. The man's-head size and the liquid "more delicate than wine" are present in R. The liquid comparison is not found in F. Z's nut passage was only partly transcribed, so its absence is not confirmed. V was not checked. MP's sigla may place the man's-head comparison with VB and FB, which would make the claim non-exclusive to Ramusio, but the OCR cannot confirm those sigla. Until the Z and V nut passages and the MP images are checked, the comparison should be attributed to Ramusio and Yule and described as "not shown to be exclusive".

**What Yule's brackets mean.** In his Introduction §90 Yule says he bracketed "everything peculiar to Ramusio's version" that he judged authentic, and that could be added without "harshness or mutilation". His base was Pauthier's text, with substantive items from the Geographic Text (= F) printed unbracketed. The brackets therefore mark Yule's judgement about Ramusio-peculiar material relative to his base texts. Z was not among his sources. Yule also brackets a sentence that includes F's "chastiaus", so the brackets prove nothing about exclusivity (A10).

**Competing readings.** Yule and Marsden tie the five-month stay to the bridal fleet by reconstruction (Yule's note 1; Marsden's note 3, as the earlier report records). No witness read here makes that link. Parker's Siam identification for Samara (Yule's note 3, H.C.) is a dissent the earlier report records but this audit did not re-read.

**Suggested formulation for III-3.** In the Samara chapter (Yule-Cordier III.10), Marco says he stayed there five months, held by the weather. The Franco-Italian text says so in the first person. Z and Ramusio say he had about two thousand men with him, that ditches were dug whose ends reached the harbour on both sides, and that the islanders traded food with them. Z gives five timber "pergama or bertesche"; the Venetian V gives one great wooden castle; F speaks of timber and logs without a number; Ramusio speaks of some wooden bertesche. The chapter does not link the stay to the bridal voyage; that link belongs to Marsden and Yule.

## Embassy chronology: departure disputed, Arghun's death fixed

**Verdict: disputed.** The departure date has no witness. Yule (1292), Yang (early 1291, as reported), Pelliot (early 1291, from the durations) and the tertiary Wikipedia "spring 1291" are competing reconstructions. The Chinese notice records preparations and a dispatch order, not a sailing.

**The Chinese notice.** The entry is a rations memorial that cites a dispatch order from the 3rd month. The memorial itself is dated the 17th day (十七日). Its month comes from the transcriber's bracket, not from the entry text. The transcription's envoy names are 兀鲁□ (a rare character read as dǎi), 阿必失呵 and 火者. Its route is "by way of Maʿbar". It names no sailing, no Quanzhou and no princess. The juan 19418 reading is supported by two intermediaries, Douban and Li Zhi'an, but no facsimile has been seen.

**Yule's Introduction §21 arithmetic.** Yule gives two reckonings, and the earlier report presented only the second.

For (b), January 1292 plus three months to Java plus eighteen months gives about October 1293, which is close to his "about November 1293". That is my own month arithmetic, not Yule's. Yule's "beginning of 1292" rests on Hammer's date, which he says he could not trace ("I have been unable to trace Hammer's authority", A13).

**Competing departure dates.** Yule's "beginning of 1292" is built backwards from Hammer's untraced arrival date (A13). Yang Zhijiu's early-1291 reading (1941) is known only through Li Zhi'an and Ma Xiaolin, who date it to "early 1291" and to "the very end of 1290 or the first days of 1291" respectively (A20, A21). Pelliot reasons from the durations and dates the crossing of the western Indian Ocean to the winter monsoon of 1292–93 (A17). Beijing ribao (2023) gives January 1291 as a later reckoning, with no author named. Cleaves (1976) is reported as endorsing Yang, but JSTOR blocked access, so his own wording is unseen. Jackson (1998) gives no sailing year at all.

**Arghun's death, conditional only.** Rashid's date, 7 Rabīʿ I 690 (10 March 1291, Julian), is in Thackston's bracket and in the TDV entry (A23, A24). ## Xi'an stele: the 1620s anchor holds, the burial stays conjectural

**Verdict: corrected** on the "silence" framing, and **disputed** on the discovery year. Nothing read shows the stone was visible in Polo's time, and nothing read treats Polo's silence as evidence.

Yule's burial date is Pauthier's supposition: the stone "may have been buried in 845" (A27). Keevak says "perhaps" and "as far as we can tell" (A25). Neither cites a record of the burial, and Keevak's flat remark that the stone had been "underground for eight hundred years" rests on the same supposition, so it should not be cited as evidence. The only physical fact is that workmen dug the stone up. The earliest dated witnesses are Li Zhizao's publication "in the same year" and the Jesuit report for 1625, both reported through Keevak (A26).

The competing dates are 1625 (Keevak; the conventional date), 1623 (a popular thepaper article with no argument; Wang and Cavayero 2025 give a range "between 1623 and 1625" without attributing either date), and "1621–1625" (English Wikipedia, citing Ma 2025 and Saeki 1951, neither read). I did not find who argues for 1623. Hong Ye's 1932 paper concerns the place (Zhouzhi), not the date; Havret and Pelliot concern the place debate, according to Keevak. The "accidentally found in 1625 by some workmen" sentence in Yule's note 3 was not re-verified, because DSR image 0062 failed to load.

For the omission, the search reached Keevak's preview to p. 195, Satya's 1999 review of Wood, and the Wikipedia article on Wood. None treats Polo's silence on the stele as evidence. Wood (1995), Haw (2006), Vogel (2013) and Jackson (BSOAS 1998) were not read, so their silence is not established. The seventeenth-century reception ran the other way: Keevak says Ricci and others used Polo to support the presence of Christians (p. 18).

**Suggested wording for II-6.** "Polo's chapter on Kenjanfu calls its people idolaters and does not mention the Christian monument of 781. The silence carries little weight. The stone was unearthed only in the 1620s, conventionally 1625. It is conjectured, though no record says so, that it was buried in the persecution of 845. Nothing shows it was visible in Polo's time, and no scholar read so far argues from his silence." If the manuscript says the stone was buried throughout Polo's lifetime, that is not supported.

## Jacopo, Ayas and the 2026 deposition: four layers kept apart

**Verdict: disputed.** The manuscript's readings, Bolognari's emendation, Yule's harmonisation and Ramusio's Curzola tradition are different layers. Only the first is a text; the rest are interpretations.

**Yule's harmonisation.** Yule keeps Jacopo's "sea-fight" as corroboration of the Ramusian tradition, which he says derives from "a genuine tradition", and rejects the 1296 date, saying "LXXXXVI is as certainly a mistake for LXXXXIV". The numeral is contested. The DSR OCR reads LXXXIV and the Gutenberg text reads LXXXXIV (A29). Yule also concedes that "no contemporary authority" for the Curzola capture has been found (A28).

**The 2026 deposition.** The Latin transcription gives a witness "Marcus Paulo", testifying on "the penultimate day of February" in a quire labelled 1300–1301. He reports that in the October just past it was a year since the parish priest of S. Biagio, Marino Lombardo's wife and a friend called at his house. The priest paid him £120 in grossi as profit on £1,200 that Marino held from him in colleganza "pro uno anno". The transcription names the visitor as Marino's wife and gives no name for her; the summary's "Francesca" is not in the transcription. Bolognari notes that no parish is given for the witness. The arithmetic is my own: £120 on £1,200 is 10 percent, which may be a year's profit rather than a term that ended at the payment.

**What the document establishes.** A witness so named received money from a colleganza in the years before the deposition. It establishes nothing about the contract's date, the witness's residence, or his identity with the traveller. Bolognari's October 1298 contract date and his claim that Marco "must already have been in Venice" are inferences, and he asserts the identification without argument (A34).

**Date style.** The quire's date style is not verified. If "1301" is modern style, the visit falls in October 1299 and the contract, if October 1298, pre-dates it. Under the Venetian (more veneto) convention, which began the year on 1 March, a February "1301" would be February 1302 in modern style. On that reading the visit moves to October 1300 and the contract to about October 1299, and the argument against Curzola disappears. The notes do not settle the convention.

**Bearing on capture and release.** A visit in October 1299 is compatible with every hypothesis: it falls after the May 1299 Milan treaty and after the 28 August 1299 release date that Gullino reports (not re-checked here). An October 1298 contract with Marco in Venice would be incompatible with a capture at Curzola (September 1298) followed by Genoese imprisonment through 1298–99. It would push the capture earlier, toward the 1296 Laiazzo hypothesis. That requires a release before October 1298, which the Prologue's statement that the book was written in prison in 1298 does not accommodate without a short captivity ending early that year. The hypothesis therefore does not fit the Prologue cleanly, and it is not proven by appearing in a recent publication.

## Conclusion

The earlier report's main correction is methodological: its "Ramusio-only" tag and its single-reckoning account of Yule's arithmetic both fail the passages read, and the sources show the editorial layer more clearly than before. The Samara details belong to several witnesses, and Yule's brackets record his judgement, not exclusivity. The embassy's timing, the stele's burial and Jacopo's capture each turn on a single contested reading, and the notes support only conditional statements about them. Arghun's death on 10 March 1291 is now the firmest chronological fixed point, and it matters only as a conditional: the fleet's status depends on whether it sailed before or after that day.

The audit also shows that the cheapest remaining gains are in access, not argument. Page images of Yule–Cordier, Moule–Pelliot and the Z nut passage would settle the ship counts, the survivor numbers and the coconut comparison, and would move most of the "hold" rows in the handoff to "safe". The 2026 deposition should not reach the manuscript as a finding until the archive folio and its date style are checked. No manuscript section was edited for this report.
