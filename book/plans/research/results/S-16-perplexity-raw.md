## B. Substantive disagreements

### Old Hormuz and route

**Daniel Potts** presents K-103 as a possibility and raises a possible connection with Stein’s Kalātun; the wording does not license a precise coordinate or identification as settled fact. The underlying archaeological chain—Williamson’s survey, Morgan’s discussion and Stein’s site description—still requires inspection. [iranicaonline](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/minab-2/)

**Henry Yule** reconstructs an outward Kerman–Hormuz journey followed by the Khorasan route and supplies fear of unsuitable ships as a probable motive. His route reconstruction and psychological explanation must be cited separately; the introductory wording itself signals that the motive is conjectural. [gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10636/10636-h/10636-h.htm)

### Tonocain and Pem

**Yule** treats Tonocain as a province and proposes an approach around Tabas rather than identifying a visit to one named town. **Bernardini** preserves multiple Tree-name readings, so geographical, botanical and Alexander-literary interpretations remain distinct problems. [exploretraveler](https://exploretraveler.com/the-travels-of-marco-polo-v1-part3/)

**Huntington**, as quoted by **Cordier**, argues for Keriya through descriptive comparison and field observation. **Bernardini** retains Uzuntatï as a possibility; the retrieved evidence is insufficient to determine whether later archaeology has decisively shifted the balance. [iranicaonline](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/polo-marco/)

### Baghdad and Alamut

**Nassima Neggaz** approaches conflicting accounts of the caliph’s death as literary and political constructions rather than simply selecting one as factual. That makes precise motif attribution indispensable: a rebuke over unused treasure and a death from starvation are different assertions. [cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/volume/BBFBB60FBE663D72E2191C7B004C2EF2)

**Farhad Daftary’s** Iranica chronology distinguishes Rokn al-Din’s personal surrender at Maymundiz from Alamut’s capitulation. It supports correcting any narrative that merges those events, but does not explain an unidentified conflicting date elsewhere. [iranicaonline](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/korsah-roknaldin/)
