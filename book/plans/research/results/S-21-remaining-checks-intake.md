## Disposition

- **III-05 shark-charmers.** Do not print 1856 or 1859 as a ban year. Write that dates in accounts run from the 1850s to 1885, or attribute 1885 to Kunz and Stevenson (1908). Ostroff's stages are worth a Note: state payment, then a ban on private patronage, then the end of the system. Next step if a firm year is wanted: Twynam 1902, p. 24 (HathiTrust in a normal browser) or Ceylon Sessional Papers.
- **III-07 Socotra.** The suggested wording in the [narrow-followup intake](S-21-narrow-followup-intake.md) is now backed by the 1559 print itself. Cite “Ramusio 1559, II, fol. 57v.” It is still a later-version narrative, not Polo's eyewitness account.
- **II-07 Carajan** and **I-07–08 Kerman.** No change: the fallbacks stand. Two library/CNKI requests remain if the stronger claims are wanted: Wen et al. 1980, and Weisgerber 2004 (Bochum catalogue or CD-ROM).

### Disposition after follow-up

- **III-05:** If the text needs a date: “Kunz and Stevenson (1908) and H. M. Smith (1912) say British Ceylon had put the last charmers on salary, then abolished them in the mid-1880s.” Keep Fernando's 1856 and Ostroff's 1859 out, or note them only in a Note as competing dates.
- **II-07 Carajan:** No Yunnan record is established. Keep the fallback. A Note may add that Bronze Age long-snouted crocodilian bones from Guangdong were described in 2022 as an extinct gavialid, *Hanyusuchus*, which shows large crocodilians lived in southern China in historical times. This says nothing about Carajan.
- **I-07 Kerman:** No change.
