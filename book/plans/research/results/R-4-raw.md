**Q1(a), mercury–sulphur elixirs: partly answered.**
- **Indian texts, supported:** The practice is real by the 12th–13th century. The *Rasārṇava* (c. 12th c., disputed) and White's *Alchemical Body* describe elixirs of processed mercury, usually bound with sulphur. A 13th-century medical text, the *Śārṅgadharasaṃhitā*, has mercury-and-sulphur recipes taken as honey mixtures, drinks or pills.
- **Not found elsewhere:** Nothing I could open describes a twice-monthly mercury-and-sulphur drink taken from childhood. That detail rests on Polo alone.
- **al-Bīrūnī:** He calls rasāyana an Indian alchemy-like art, but I saw no mention of mercury or sulphur.
- **Ibn Baṭṭūṭa:** He reports yogi pills, but I could not see any mercury named. I saw only secondary quotations, not the Rihla itself.
- **Arghun:** Yule says Indian bakhshis gave the Ilkhan Arghun a sulphur-and-mercury elixir for eight months before his death in 1291. I could not check Rashīd al-Dīn's own passage.
- **Placement:** Polo puts the Chughi in "Lar", which Yule calls "very obscure", so "South India" needs hedging.

**Q1(b), the naked ox-worshipping, ash-daubing ascetics: disputed.**
- **Śaiva ascetics (Pāśupata, Kālāmukha):** They fit the ash. Lorenzen's thesis says the ash bath is a central Pāśupata ritual. I found no ox emblem and no cow-dung ash in the sources I read.
- **Jaṅgamas:** Yule offers them only as an analogy for the forehead emblem.
- **Digambara Jains:** They fit the nakedness and the non-killing of insects, but not the ash or the ox. This rests on weak sources.
- **Polo's text:** One variant has ox-bones instead of dung.
- **Conclusion:** No source I read names the group. I recommend wording like "Polo calls them Chughi; scholars see Śaiva ash-smearing and Jain-like scruples".

**Q2, Yuan hunting season: answered.**
- **Closed season:** The 1st month, 1st day, to the 7th month, 20th day (正月初一日爲頭，至七月二十日).
- **Dianzhang:** Juan 38, 兵部卷之五, article 禁治打捕月日, in the edict of Dade 1/2/18 (1297). The Chinese and a translation are in the ledger. This was read from OCR only, so some characters are marked as uncertain.
- **Tongzhi tiaoge:** Juan 28 雜令, 圍獵 section. The entry for Zhiyuan 28/8/27 (1291) gives "自正月至七月…普例禁約". The entry for Zhiyuan 28/11/22 also permits drives only in the 9th–11th months.
- **Yuan shi cross-check:** A Qing encyclopedia quoting the Chengzong annal for Dade 1 (1297) gives the same months and adds "大都八百里內亦如之". It records the ban under the 3rd month, where the Dianzhang has the 2nd. So the months are stated in the annals, though not in juan 101.
- **Area:** The edicts say "不揀是誰" and give no territory. The 800 li around Dadu and 500 li around Zhongdu appear in separate articles.
- **Discrepancy:** One secondary source gives "七月二十八日", but all the primary-text readings I saw say 二十日.
- **Polo:** One secondary source quotes a Chinese translation of Polo as saying March to October, which would conflict with the edicts. I did not find Polo's own passage.
