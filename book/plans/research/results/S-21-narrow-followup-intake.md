## New independent check: Socotra's barrel float

**Correction to our previous work:** [Burgio and Fornasiero, “Il terzo libro” (2011), printed p. 133, notes 19–20](https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/e4239ddb-4ac3-7180-e053-3705fe0a3322/burgio_atti%20ramusio.pdf), quote Ramusio III.34.2 and compare Z 123. In their transcription Ramusio's line is attached to a *bottesella*, a small cask floating on the sea; the account separately speaks of *botte d'olio*, casks of head oil. Thus our earlier warnings that a cask on the line had not been found, including S21-E30's “no barrel attached to line,” are **superseded**. Yule's English “small buoy” is still an accurate description of its function; it does not specify its shape.

The scholars quote Z 123.19–20 as a line bearing a *boticula* and an *asser* at each 50-pace interval; they provide a boat/chase explanation in Italian. They identify Z 123.3 and 17 onward as a source for R's longer account and cite short ambergris wording in F CLXXXIX.3, P III.38.2, and other redactions. These are **directly inspected scholarly transcriptions and attributions**, not this session's inspection of manuscript images or the 1559 print itself. They support a textual comparison, not independent proof that the reported Socotra hunt occurred, nor proof of Marco's personal observation. The exact full F and Z passages, the nature of any intermediate Z witness, and the lexical issue in Srinivasan remain to be checked if needed.

Suggested III-07 wording: “In Ramusio's later version, a barbed iron was tied to a long cord with a small floating cask, so the hunters could locate the whale; the account also speaks of oil from its head.” A Note can distinguish the more elaborate Z account. The currently drafted Yule-style “small buoy” remains supported; it need not be replaced unless the book chooses that extra textual detail.
