## 2. Corrections the notes force on the draft

Several draft assertions do not survive contact with the sources. **Rockhill 1915, p. 623 glosses Kan-mai-li as "Comoro Islands (?)"**. The entry, as OCR'd, does not mention Hormuz, so a Ganmaili = Hormuz equation needs a later authority ([archive.org OCR](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.56082/2015.56082.Toung-Pao-Ou-Archives-Serie2-Vol16_djvu.txt)). **The *bocheta* is "'gioiello d'oro e di gemme', dal lat. bŭcca"**. The 2024 editors call the *boghta*-headdress reading an error that "continua a essere ripetuto" ([Formentin and Schiavon 2024 PDF](https://www.rmoa.unina.it/7054/1/RM_1-2024_Formentin-Schiavon.pdf)). **Rubruck's kumis is on pp. 62–63, 66–67 and 85, but p. 55 is not confirmed** ([archive.org OCR](https://archive.org/stream/journeyofwilliam00will/journeyofwilliam00will_djvu.txt)). **hdl 1885/41883 is a 2002 ANU working paper reviewing Wood, not the ZAS 27 (1997) article**, so it cannot supply journal page numbers ([ANU record](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/e70f6bcf-b65a-457e-a871-56eacb668e19/full)).

On the Polo family: **Juliana and Clara are "sororibus … filiabus anime mee sancte Catarine de Maiurbio"**, nuns of Santa Caterina di Mazzorbo. Their biological relationship to the testator is not established by the wording ([archive.org OCR](https://archive.org/stream/archiviovenetotridentino9/archiviovenetotridentino9_djvu.txt)). **Bellela was apparently dead by 1326–27, but this rests only on Bolognari 2020** citing Gallo ([Edizioni Ca' Foscari](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-440-0/978-88-6969-440-0-ch-02_5Lfc2Qh.pdf)). **The *Yuan shi* gives no birth year for Chabi** ([Wikisource 卷114](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/元史/卷114)).

On plates and science: **the BnF caption dates Arabe 5847 to 1236**, against 1237 in the brief ([BnF](https://expositions.bnf.fr/savoirs/visite42.htm)). This is consistent with 634 AH but not proven. **The Weisgerber 2004 citation may be misattributed**: his 2004 *Anschnitt* pieces found concern Afghanistan and Nakhlak, not Kerman turquoise ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerd_Weisgerber)).

On rights and sources: **Gibb 1929 is PD in the US but protected in the UK/EU until the end of 2041**. **The Luce archive.org upload was treated as unauthorised and not opened**. The **Thackston and Cook uploads on archive.org were judged unauthorised and not used**.

## 3. ACCESSED items: passages and what they settle

**Item 1 (Orlandini 1926, doc. 6).** The heading reads "6. 1310. 6 febbraio. — Testamento di Matteo Polo seniore". The dating clause is "Anno ab incarnacione … millesimo trecentesimo nono mensis februarii die sexta intrante Indictione octava Rivalto". Marta appears as "condam Marthe uxori mee". The bequest: "Item dimitto sororibus Juliane et Clare filiabus anime mee sancte Catarine de Maiurbio libras denariorum venecialium". The continuation (reported as 400 lire "de meis imprestitis … donec vixerint") was not obtained verbatim ([OCR](https://archive.org/stream/archiviovenetotridentino9/archiviovenetotridentino9_djvu.txt)). This settles the dating style: Venetian year, Indiction 8, so 1310 modern (inference consistent with the editor's heading). It also settles Marta's status as deceased wife and the nuns' identity. A per-clause page within pp. 25–31 needs the page image.

**Item 4 (Rustichello).** Cigni's DBI entry records the form "Rusticiaus (de Pise), preceduto da maistre (= magister)". It notes that "Rustikelli era inoltre il nome di famiglia di notai medievali provenienti da Vicopisano". It dismisses Joris's identification with a "chierico contabile" of Henry VII as "generica" and outside his literary years ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/rustichello-da-pisa_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)). Yule 1871, § 41 (p. xc) quotes "Maistre Rusticiens de Pise … compila ce rommant" but says nothing on status ([OCR](https://archive.org/stream/bookofsermarcopo01polo/bookofsermarcopo01polo_djvu.txt)). This settles only that the evidence is the title *magister* (inference: a title used by both lay and clerical men). The 1903 Yule–Cordier sections were not read.

**Item 5a (*Yuan shi*).** Juan 114 reads "世祖昭睿順聖皇后,名察必,弘吉剌氏,濟寧忠武王按陳之女也。生裕宗。中統初,立為皇后", with death in the 18th year, emended (1281). There is no birth year ([Wikisource 卷114](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/元史/卷114)). Juan 107 reads "世祖皇帝,十子:長朵而只王;次二皇太子真金" and names no mother ([Wikisource 卷107](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/元史/卷107)). This settles that any Chabi birth year, or any claim that she was Dorjé's mother, is modern reconstruction. The chronicle says only that she bore Zhenjin.

**Item 7 (Rubruck, Rockhill 1900).** On making it, p. 66: "they pour it into a big skin or bottle, and they set to churning it with a stick". On the aftertaste, p. 67: "it leaves a taste of milk of almonds on the tongue". On first reaction, p. 85: "at the taste of it I broke out in a sweat with horror and surprise, for I had never drunk of it". Same page: "It seemed to me, however, very palatable, as it really is" ([OCR](https://archive.org/stream/journeyofwilliam00will/journeyofwilliam00will_djvu.txt)). This settles the first-reaction and later-taste beats and confirms pp. 66–67 and 85. Page 55 should be dropped or re-checked.

**Item 8 (Gibb, 1992 AES reprint of the 1929 edition).** Page 234: "These vessels are built only in the towns of Zaytun and Sin-Kalan". The ships carry "a thousand men, six hundred of whom are sailors and four hundred men-at-arms". Page 286: "The port of Zaytun is one of the largest in the world, or perhaps the very largest", and "I saw in it about a hundred large junks" (OCR spellings normalised) ([OCR](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.39643/2015.39643.Ibn-Battuta_djvu.txt)). This settles the ship and Zaytun passages and their probable pages. The 1929 pagination still needs a check on an original copy, and the wording cannot be reproduced in UK/EU editions (item 10).

**Item 9 (Rockhill 1915).** The entry opens on p. 623: "This country is the land of the fleet horses of the south-west, and is not far from Fo-lang". In paraphrase, the rest of the entry covers: horse ships of sewn planks carrying frankincense below and several hundred horses above; putchuck and ambergris; a cargo list; and a footnote that Oman was the main horse-shipping point in Polo's time ([OCR](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.56082/2015.56082.Toung-Pao-Ou-Archives-Serie2-Vol16_djvu.txt)). This settles that Rockhill's identification is "Comoro Islands (?)", not Hormuz. Whether 甘埋里 is printed on the page is unverified.

**Item 10 (rights).** The evidence and the rules applied are in section 5.

**Item 15b (2024 *Reti Medievali* edition).** The inventory entries read "Item tola j d'oro granda de comandamento — ll. 20 de gross." and "Item bocheta j d'oro con piere e perle — ll. 3, s. 8 de gross.". There are three *çentura* entries, of silver, silver thread, and green silk with silver. The editors on the *tola*: "una delle tavole auree date ai Polo dai sovrani mongoli come insegna di comando (la parola mongola corrispondente è paiza)". They add that, "con le sue 20 lire di grossi (200 ducati)", it is "di gran lunga il pezzo singolo più prezioso dell'inventario" (p. 220) ([PDF](https://www.rmoa.unina.it/7054/1/RM_1-2024_Formentin-Schiavon.pdf)). This settles the paiza, the *bocheta*-as-jewel point, and the belt wording. The editors make no comment on the belts in the part that was read.

**Item 18a (Hammer-Purgstall 1842).** At about p. 391: "Sie gaben ihm ein aus Schwefel und Merkur zusammengesetztes Mittel als die Panacee der Lebensverlängerung an". The subject is the Indian "Bachschi". In paraphrase: Arghun took it for eight months, and a forty-day seclusion in Tabriz followed. An earlier passage at about p. 375 treats his hair loss as a sign of poison ([OCR](https://dn760108.eu.archive.org/0/items/geschichtederilc01hamm/geschichtederilc01hamm_djvu.txt)). This settles that the elixir episode is in Hammer at about pp. 391–392. The Quatremère cross-check (p. 194) remains open.

**Item 20a (*Tashrīf al-ayyām*, OpenITI).** The section is headed "ذكر ورود رسل الحبشة إلى أبواب مولانا السلطان" and opens "في شهر رمضان سنة تسع وثمانين وستمائة، ورد رسول من جهة الحبشة". The envoy's nephew Ṣāliḥ, arriving from Mecca, found that "عمه قد وصل رسولا من الحبشة وأنه مات بعيذاب". He then complained about the zakāt official ʿUmar b. al-Fākihānī ([OpenITI raw text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0700AH/master/data/0692IbnCabdZahir/0692IbnCabdZahir.TashrifAyyam/0692IbnCabdZahir.TashrifAyyam.GVDB20200720-ara1)). This settles the date (Ramaḍān 689) and the envoy's death at ʿAydhāb. The pages fall within the brief's pp. 170–174, if OpenITI's end-of-page markers follow the 1961 edition (an inference; the OCLC identity was not verified). The file is flagged MANY_TYPOS.

**Item 23a (Maspero, *T'oung Pao* 1911).** Chapter VIII, "Indravarman VI & les Mongols". On p. 461: "Sagatou alors prend ses dispositions de combat, et annonce à ses soldats l'attaque pour le quinzième jour du premier mois". On p. 474: "Le Champa se trouvait ainsi délivré des Mongols qui y avaient perdu quantité d'hommes et d'officiers". Sögetü's death is on p. 474, with a footnote from the Đại Việt annals saying he drowned ([OCR](https://archive.org/stream/2tungpaotoungpao12corduoft/2tungpaotoungpao12corduoft_djvu.txt)). This settles the campaign narrative with serial pagination; the pages are not the 1928 book's. The tribute question stays partial.

**Item 24 (Brandes, *Pararaton*).** Javanese text: "Satékaning utusan saking Tatar amërép ing Daha." Dutch translation, Hoofdstuk VI: "Toen de gezanten (met de troepen) van Tatar gekomen waren, viel men Daha aan." and "Rangga lawe viel die aan, die buiten de paseban waren" ([OCR](https://archive.org/stream/verhandelinge49189697bata/verhandelinge49189697bata_djvu.txt)). This settles that the chronicle frames the Tatars as attacking Daha and then being turned on over the princesses. The printed translation page, and Raden Wijaya's exact wording, need the page image.
