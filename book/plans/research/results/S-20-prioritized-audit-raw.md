## What the Corpus Cannot Support

Several high-priority items have **no supporting sentence** in the supplied papers and must be reported as unresolved, not inferred. No paper mentions Zhang Xinglang, Feng Chengjun, any Mongolian Ramusio edition, an Iranian reception example, a Chinese textbook, a government speech, the 2012 embassy launch, Bukal, Montaldo, the Venice airport, Korčula, Pasini, Acqui, Carlsson's 1930 article, or the Columbus postils. The pasta-legend chain is documented only at the level of **Serventi & Sabban's abstract attributing the story to the 1929 *Macaroni Journal*** (Serventi and Sabban 2002, 40-4585), with Wood repeating the legend-status without inspecting the original (Wood 2018, 76-80); Ramusio's 1559 passage itself was not read, only its description in Mascherpa and Magone (Mascherpa 2018; Magone 2015, 787-793).

On maps, the corpus refines two points but leaves others open. Fra Mauro's Japan label is **Cimpangu** at legend #1334 per O'Doherty's citation of Falchetta (O’Doherty 2011, 30-36); the *2306 Hachbaluch/Hacbaluch* spelling is **not addressed** in any supplied sentence. Behaim's dependence on the Yale map is argued in a footnote-level secondary account ( 2016), but Ravenstein's page was not located. The broader cartographic context — Martellus, Ptolemy, and Polo's text shaping late-15th-c. Asia — is described by Richardson (Richardson 2000, 1-22), and the globe-vs-mappa-mundi framing by Schmieder (Schmieder 2021, 163-178), but neither resolves the specific label spellings.
