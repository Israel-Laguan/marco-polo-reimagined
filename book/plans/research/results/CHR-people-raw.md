### (2) Explanatory Prose for Disagreements

**Item 1 (Arghun’s death day).** The majority of the sources consulted give **10 March 1291** (e.g., the *Oriental Biographical Dictionary* and the Wikipedia entry). However, the Catalan Viquipèdia gives **7 March 1291**. This discrepancy may reflect different conversion of the Hijri date or variant traditions in the Persian sources. Scholars such as **Rashid al-Din** are the ultimate authority, but the search results do not quote his exact wording. The reign 1284–1291 is universally confirmed.

**Item 5 (Ghazan’s marriage to Cocachin).** The date of the marriage is disputed. **Pelliot’s note** (as cited in the Digital Silk Road project) suggests the meeting took place “end of 1293 or quite the beginning of 1294”. Other sources place the marriage after Ghazan’s accession in 1295, but the weight of scholarly opinion (following Pelliot) favours late 1293/early 1294.

**Item 9 (Chinggis Khan’s death).** Marco Polo’s account of Chinggis dying from an arrow wound in the knee at “Caaju” is **not supported by any earlier source**. The Digital Silk Road project explicitly states: “there is no likelihood that he died of an arrow‑wound”. The true cause of death remains uncertain (possibly illness or a fall from a horse), but the arrow story is a Polo invention.

**Item 14 (Chingkim’s death date).** Yule’s “12th moon of 1284–85” is a **chronological error**. The *Yuan shi* clearly records Chingkim’s death in the 12th month of Zhiyuan 22, which corresponds to **5 January 1286**. The Digital Silk Road project confirms this: “he died on January 5, 1286; his biography is in YS”. The earliest source is therefore the *Yuan shi* itself.

**Item 17 (Nayan’s descent).** The Book (as Yule translates it) says Nayan descended from Belgutai. However, **Pelliot** argued that the Christian prince Nayan was **not** a descendant of Belgutai but rather of **Temüge Otchigin**, Chinggis Khan’s youngest full brother. The Wikipedia entry summarises this: “Pelliot was of the opinion that the Christian prince Nayan was not a descendant of Belgutai”. The Chinese sources do not explicitly state his baptism, though some indications suggest Christian sympathy.

**Item 22 (Marco Polo’s capture).** The traditional account places Marco’s capture at the **Battle of Curzola (1298)**. However, **Jacopo d’Acqui** reports a skirmish near **Layas (Ayas)**. The search results do not elaborate on this dispute, but it is a known scholarly debate. The will date (9 January 1324, Venetian 1323 *more veneto*) is firmly documented in the Venetian archives.

**Item 23 (Niccolò Polo’s sons).** The documents confirm that Niccolò had a legitimate son **Maffeo** (whose testament of August 1300 survives) and two illegitimate sons **Stefano** and **Giovannino** (Zannino). The Book itself does not mention these sons, but the Venetian archival records do.

**Item 25 (Donata Badoer’s Loredano connection).** Yule suspected a Loredano connection, but **no documentary basis** for this was found in the sources consulted. The Treccani entry and the Italian Wikipedia both identify Donata simply as the daughter of **Vitale Badoer**.

### (4) Unresolved Questions

1.  **Arghun’s exact death day (7 vs. 10 March 1291):** The sources consulted give conflicting dates. A definitive answer would require consulting Rashid al-Din’s *Jamiʿ al-Tawarikh* in a critical edition (e.g., the Berezin or Blochet edition) or Wassaf’s history directly.
2.  **Survival of the envoy Coja:** The Yongle dadian notice, as reported in secondary sources, does not explicitly state whether Coja survived the voyage. This would require examining Yang Zhijiu’s original 1941 article or the relevant folios of the Yongle dadian.
3.  **Chabi’s birth year:** Sources give either 1216 or 1227. A definitive answer would require consulting the *Yuan shi* biography of Chabi or Rashid al-Din’s account.
4.  **Dorjé’s exact death date:** The sources only say he died young; some traditions give 1263, but this is not firmly established. The *Yuan shi* or Rashid al-Din might provide clarity.
5.  **Nayan’s baptism and exact age:** Chinese sources do not explicitly state his baptism, and his age (about 30) is inferred rather than documented. A closer reading of the *Yuan shi* and Syriac Christian sources might resolve this.
6.  **Peter the Tartar’s shelfmark in the Biblioteca Marciana:** The search results confirm the will is held there but do not give the shelfmark. Consulting the library’s catalogue or Moule’s *Marco Polo: The Description of the World* (1938) might provide it.
7.  **Documentary basis for Marco Polo’s birth year (1254):** No contemporary document confirms 1254; it is a later tradition. The Venetian archives (e.g., the *Magistrato alle Leggi* or notarial records) might contain a birth record, but none was found in the sources consulted.
8.  **The Loredano connection to Donata Badoer:** Yule suspected it, but no documentary evidence was found. The Venetian genealogical archives (e.g., the *Miscellanea Genealogica*) might be needed to confirm or refute this.
