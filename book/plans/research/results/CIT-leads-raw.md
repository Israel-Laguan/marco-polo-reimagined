## 3. Marāṣid and Yāqūt

## 2. Who wrote the postils

**Answer:** The fullest analysis I could open is Juan Gil’s, reported in a contemporary review: the marginalia in the Colombina copy are written in **at least three hands** — an anonymous amanuensis (possibly Fray Gaspere Goriccio), **Hernando/Ferdinand Colón**, and **Christopher Columbus**. Shawcross gives the total as **some 366 postils**, with Columbus responsible for approximately **98** notes. On **Cipangu**, the two notes **“aurum in copia maxima”** and **“margarite rubee”** at **III.2, fol. 57v** are securely Columbus’s in Gil’s analysis. On **gold**, Gil treats the treasure/commodity notes as Columbus’s characteristic layer, and gives **“aurum pro argento”** at **II.41, fol. 40v**; I did not see a numbered postil inventory confirming every gold note. On the **Great Khan**, Gil says Columbus left Kublai’s government almost unannotated, so I could not confirm a securely Columbus postil on the Great Khan. I found **no evidence** attributing the Marco Polo postils to **Bartolomé Colón**; the Bartolomé claim I saw in secondary snippets concerns the *Imago Mundi*, not this copy.

**Cipangu, gold, Great Khan:**
- **Cipangu:** “aurum in copia maxima” and “margarite rubee” at **III.2, fol. 57v**; Gil calls these the only two notes Cipangu receives and attributes them to Columbus’s hand.
