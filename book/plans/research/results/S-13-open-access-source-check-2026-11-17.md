## Findings and application

1. **Medieval readers:** Gadrat-Ouerfelli documents ownership and use across courts, merchants, scholars, and religious communities (pp. 202–207), then distinguishes owning a book from reading it and knowing why (pp. 208–209). The merchant evidence she discusses does not establish that the owners used it for commercial purposes; this is her assessment of evidence, not proof that no merchant ever did so. She discusses uses in maps, histories and religious texts (pp. 209–211). The evidence challenges a blanket story that Polo was not taken seriously, but does not establish universal acceptance.
2. **Textual variant:** Andreose explicitly notes an intermediate V reading *Madaschor*, as well as Z's *Mogdaxo* and the form in F. The earlier project shorthand that only Z escapes the usual reading was too absolute. His Mogadishu and textual-corruption explanation is a scholarly reconstruction; his account of the island's later naming was not independently verified here. He also calls the wider manuscript classification unresolved.
3. **Applied:** added the 2024 readership evidence to III-11 and its first Note, removed an unsupported assertion that Venice as a whole had doubted Marco, corrected the bibliography range, and updated the scholarship registry and III-11 handoff. The 2026 article remains summary-only. The 2024 chapter does not settle the truth of Polo's travels or independently validate the individual archival sources it cites.
