# S-21 — high-priority natural history research prompt

```text
I am editing a new English retelling of Marco Polo based on Henry Yule's translation, revised by Henri Cordier (3rd ed., 1903; Notes and Addenda, 1920). Please investigate ONLY the high-priority questions below. They can change claims already in the book's prose, Notes, captions, or planned commodity tables. Work in order; a well-sourced partial answer is more useful than a broad unsupported one.

A prior research report is only a lead: it did not reliably check sources, witnesses, species or chapter tags. You do not have the book repository, so the project IDs below are rough routing labels, not independent locators. Give Yule's volume, book and chapter and identify the textual version/variant for each passage. Distinguish a literal claim of the Book from Yule's interpretation, a modern explanation, and historical evidence for a real practice. Avoid assuming Polo personally saw a thing because the description is accurate. Where the answer cannot support a precise claim, give a safe hedge or recommend omission.

SOURCE RULES: Prefer directly inspected primary texts and critical editions, peer-reviewed specialist scholarship and institutional collection/species records. Search relevant languages beyond English and translate short relevant passages. State exactly what you inspected: original page or full text, specific passages, abstract, catalogue, snippet, or an identified secondary account. Supply precise pages/folios/sections only when actually seen. Do not present a work's existence or a secondary citation as proof of its contents. Attribute interpretations and counterarguments to named scholars, and separate similarity from a demonstrated transmission route. No invented quotations, pages, dates, DOIs, URLs, taxonomy, evidence of absence, or consensus. If a work cannot be accessed, list it separately as a lead. Distinguish an organism's current range, a historic location, a thirteenth-century occurrence and local cultivation or trade. If relevant, distinguish a Ramusio-only expansion from the base tradition. Date new scholarship after 2013 as NEW. Treat the Book's stereotypes about peoples as attributed medieval assertions, not ethnographic facts.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN OR NEEDS CARE:
• The local Yule text and this retelling's I-07 explicitly describe white humped oxen that kneel on the Reobarles plain near Camadi. A previous research report incorrectly failed to find this passage, having searched elsewhere. Whether they are zebu and whether the breed was locally present in the thirteenth century remain separate questions.
• The fish-fed livestock passage is at Esher/Shihr in III-08 in this retelling, not at Hormuz. Date-wine is a separate claim. The manuscript already has Coilum's indigo process at III-06. The draft places Mutfili's diamond valley in III-06, not II-03; Maabar's pearls and horses are III-05, not III-07.
• Lin and Zhang (2018, https://doi.org/10.1017/eaa.2017.21) attribute the San Marco Qingbai jar/Polo connection to Oscar Raphael. Its archaeological date does not prove that Polo owned it. Siriwardana et al. (2024, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10761-024-00739-5) report *Pinctada fucata* as 81% of their excavated assemblage and an Arippu midden date in the fifteenth–seventeenth centuries; their study does not establish uninterrupted fishing from the fifth century BCE or verify Polo's specific fishery. Brito, Jordão and Pierce (online 2015 / issue 2016, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0025315415000910), abstract, describe ambergris from sperm-whale intestines and present irritation by cephalopod beaks as a possible mechanism. Oxford University Herbaria distinguishes Asian sappan from American brazilwood; that alone does not identify Polo's plant. Do not redo these facts except to address a specific unresolved question below.
• The project has directly inspected Talbot (1959), whose opening treats the Basma animal as a composite of Javan and Sumatran rhinos, and the abstract/results of Granger et al. (2026, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2026.02.001). Granger et al. give their species-identifiable historical dataset as 1630–2010 (Javan) and 1835–2015 (Sumatran), across their study area; those ranges do not prove there was no medieval evidence elsewhere. Neither source alone proves Polo personally saw a rhino or determines the identity. Another report attributes a Shahr-i Babak identification to Weisgerber through a GIA article, not the original: treat that connection as a lead.
• A prior report's section B.7.10 summarizes Yule on the diamond valley, while its B.8.5 did not investigate the same story. Yule's account is not a direct inspection of Epiphanius or evidence of direct literary transmission. Failure to find a Rubruck/Polo connection is not proof it is false.

QUESTIONS, IN PRIORITY ORDER:
1. RHINOCEROS AND HUMAN MARVELS [III-03–04]. In the Basma unicorn passage, inspect Yule and relevant earlier witnesses for whether the text claims personal sight; distinguish rhinoceros identification, one- versus two-horn features, possible composite descriptions, and species/range in the period. Use L. M. Talbot, “Marco Polo's Unicorn,” Natural History 68(10) (1959), pp. 558–565, as one attributed interpretation, and assess any more recent scholarship and historical/archaeological evidence. Separate the Book's own debunking of counterfeit monkey “pygmies” from the Andaman dog-head claim. Identify dated earlier comparable depictions and any scholar's specific transmission argument. Seek regional/Indigenous-centered history; do not imply monstrous stereotypes describe real peoples.
2. SPICES, DYEWOODS AND PLANTS [III-02–04, I-11, II-07, III-08]. The Java passage says the island produces nutmeg, cloves, pepper, cubeb, spikenard and galangal; assess each term, local cultivation around 1270–1300, and trade/re-export evidence separately. Check Nicobar cloves and the Lambri, Ceylon and Coilum “brazil” passages and potential species. Address Sukchur rhubarb, Caindu clove/cassia and cinnamon, Fansur camphor, Champa agarwood, Dhofar frankincense and Sumatra's tree flour: what can be identified to commodity, genus or species, and what is established at the time and place? Identify where Yule already advances an interpretation versus new scholarship.
3. CUSTOMS [II-07, II-09, III-05]. Locate the exact passages for couvade, tattooing and gold-covered teeth, compare important textual variants, and consult regional ethnohistory before naming an ethnic group. Philippine/Luzon evidence alone cannot identify a Yunnan population. For Maabar pearl-fishery shark-charmers, seek contemporary and specialist evidence for the actual institution and its payments; do not infer efficacy from a reported ritual.
4. HORSES AND LIVESTOCK [I-07, III-05, III-08]. Separate Polo's horse-mortality claims, diet and alleged exclusion of farriers from independently documented trade, animal deaths, husbandry and veterinary explanations. Seek local historical evidence for identification of the Reobarles humped oxen. Find direct, dated parallels for Shihr fish-fed livestock; test the draft's assertion of an uninterrupted reporting tradition. Attribute contested causes and avoid claiming Indian horses could not be bred locally just because the Book says so.
5. ANIMALS [II-04, II-07]. Check the Carajan “serpents,” their gall and features against specialists and Chinese-language archaeology on historic Yunnan crocodilians: compare alternatives and leave species open if evidence does not establish one. Identify Polo's terms for hunting “lions,” “leopards,” “lynxes” and falcons with manuscript witnesses and named modern zoological/historical scholarship. Separate descriptions of medical practices from evidence of medical efficacy. Any precise figures must be sourced and attributed.
6. METALS, MINERALS AND MONEY [I-07–11, II-05, II-07, III-04, III-06]. Check Kerman turquoise and claimed Shahr-i Babak/Nishapur links (seek Weisgerber's original), ondanique's etymology/steel production and import, and Kuh-Banan *tutia*/*spodium* metallurgy. Compare Badakhshan spinel/lapis, the Chingintalas asbestos locality and mineral, the Ceylon royal ruby, and Mutfili diamonds with specific evidence for place, date and practice. Check coal and Caindu salt-cake currency, including seals/denominations and any Ramusio layer; examine Yuan Yunnan cowries, their currency species and routes using primary numismatic/archaeological scholarship. A real modern deposit, substance or practice is not by itself proof of Polo's mine, process or 13th-century claim.
7. PEARLS, PORCELAIN, TEXTILES AND TRADE [III-05–06, II-14, I-05, II-03]. Verify Polo's fishery's location, season, depth, tithe and payment descriptions and independent period evidence for each. Identify Tingui against named scholarly alternatives to Dehua, independently from the San Marco jar. Check the specific Coilum indigo passage against Yule and seek independent period-specific Kerala evidence, not only later Indian evidence. Test whether Polo's gold textile terms map to *nasij* and particular techniques/workshops by inspecting Allsen or other specialists directly. Verify Zayton's “100 to 1” passage but do not present its rhetorical estimate, or even a purported direction of difference, as measured without sources.
8. AMBERGRIS [III-04, III-07]. Locate the Socotra, Madagascar and Zanzibar passages and check which whale-hunt details are Ramusian. Compare medieval descriptions of ambergris and its acquisition/trade to the modern sperm-whale-origin account; the latter does not itself confirm Polo's mechanism. Inspect named historical works, such as Margariti and relevant Arabic accounts, if accessible. Identify which route and period each actually covers.
9. MARVELS AND MECHANISMS [I-10–11, I-13, II-03, III-01, III-06–07]. Check Polo's Pamir fire/cold/cooking claims, their versions, and independent altitude/fuel evidence; do not equate boiling-point effects and all combustion phenomena. For Lop voices, establish any local singing dunes and dated earlier spirit-voice tales without asserting a causal connection. For the roc, evaluate actual evidence for giant-bird, egg, raffia or other identifications. For the Mutfili diamond valley compare Epiphanius, Sindbad and other dated versions by specific motifs, not an assumed line of borrowing. Locate and investigate the Male/Female Islands, Socotra wind magic, Chipangu invulnerability stones, and Bacsi weather magic and moving cups. A Rubruck fountain is a separate object; identify any scholar who actually argues a connection, or leave it unestablished.
10. CROSS-CUTTING LIBRARY CHECK. A prior pass could not access A. C. Moule and Paul Pelliot, The Description of the World (1938), or Paul Pelliot, Notes on Marco Polo. Find legitimate catalogue/scan/library access and inspect only entries relevant to questions 1–9; cite the exact volume, page and position, and check each edition's own front matter before asserting its manuscript base. If inaccessible, give actionable catalogue details and continue with alternative scholarship. Do not make access to these volumes a blanket condition for a bounded answer.
```
