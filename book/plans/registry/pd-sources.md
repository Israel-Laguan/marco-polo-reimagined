**Verdict key (last column).**
- **yes**: OK for commercial print now, with the credit line given.
- **fee**: the work is public domain, but the holder charges for commercial reuse of its scan. Print only after buying the licence, or switch to the fallback.
- **no source**: no copy with commercial terms was found, so the slot goes to GEN.

**Print target.** Measure 300 effective ppi at the placed size, as described in the [style guide](../illustration-style-guide.md#technical-spec-for-latex). The 1,177-pixel Omont miniature in I-2 prints at 60% of the text width. The II-4 hunt uses the museum image.

## Sources selected at placement · 23 November 2026

- **II-3, banquet and procession.** Omont’s plate 43 identifies the Khan at table in fr. 2810, fol. 39r. The image shows the table beneath a Gothic canopy. It supports a caption about French reception of the Book. The Paris artist supplies the dress and hall. In the separate White Feast reconstruction, nine parcels abbreviate the nine-times-nine tribute bracketed in Yule–Cordier II.15. Three trays and the architecture are staging choices. For actual tableware, Met 1997.33.1–.6 is silver with gilding, late thirteenth to early fourteenth century. It remains an optional vessel comparison. [Getty facsimile](https://archive.org/details/gri_33125001125638/page/n120/mode/1up) · [Met service](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/51249)
- **II-4, hunt and litter.** The NPM’s signed hanging scroll supplies a mounted court hunt, falconers and the cat riding pillion. The inscription reads Zhiyuan 17 (1280). The NPM catalogue’s era field differs. Canvas PAB carries the whole composition, whereas PAS isolates a single rider. Use the credited museum derivative in the production register. The separate litter scene comes from Omont pl. 47, fol. 42v. The European miniaturist shows the chamber side-on. The thrown-open roof and released gerfalcon belong to Yule–Cordier II.20. The miniature supplies no evidence for the engineering of a Yuan litter. [NPM record](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/opendata/Pub/DetailEng/745?dep=P&mode=full) · [Getty facsimile](https://archive.org/details/gri_33125001125638/page/n128/mode/1up)
- **III-11, marginalia.** The right page of the Abyssinia–Aden opening retains several marginal entries of uncertain authorship. The adjacent prose discusses a different folio in the same volume, 57v on Chipangu. [Photograph and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marco_Polo_anotado_por_Crist%C3%B3bal_Col%C3%B3n.jpg)
- **III-12, caravan.** The Library of Congress identifies its item as the slightly reduced Barcelona 1959 facsimile. Its Rights & Access text allows reuse of the division’s digitized material unless the item carries a contrary advisory. No such advisory appears on the record. Credit the division and identify the facsimile, including its changed colours. The inscription above the caravan names its destination and point of departure. The riders’ identity remains open. [Item record and reuse terms](https://www.loc.gov/resource/g3200m.gct00215/?sp=1&st=list#rights-and-access) · [Image 5](https://www.loc.gov/resource/g3200m.gct00215/?sp=5)

Exact files, cropping rectangles and checksums are in the [production register](../../media/public-domain/README.md#production-derivatives--23-november-2026). The [printed credits](../../media/ATTRIBUTIONS.md#historical-images-and-facsimiles) give the licence for each reproduction.

## Captions and credit lines

Written in the build's figure form: `![Caption. *(Credit.)*](../media/public-domain/<file>)`. The caution from `illustration-plan.md` is part of the caption.

**I-2.** The brothers Niccolò and Maffeo Polo kneel before Kublai. A Paris painter imagined the scene about 1410, more than a century after it: a turbaned Khan receives them in a green meadow before a tent. *(Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS fr. 2810, fol. 2v; Mazarine Master and workshop, c. 1410–12.)*
- Rubric under the miniature: "Comment les .ij. freres vindrent au grant kaan". The miniature is landscape; the P slot takes the whole folio.

**I-2 alternative (1907 facsimile).** Same caption, credit: *(After Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS fr. 2810, fol. 2v, in H. Omont, Livre des merveilles, 1907, pl. 3.)*

**II-front.** Cublay (Shizu), from the album of portraits of the Yuan emperors. *(Album of Yuan emperors, National Palace Museum, Taipei. Detail via Shuge and Wikimedia Commons, marked public domain in the United States.)*
- No artist in the caption: the attribution to Anige (Araniko) is traditional, not documented.

**II-4.** Kublai, in white fur over a red robe, rides out with a woman usually identified as Chabi and his hunting party; one rider carries a hunting cheetah behind his saddle. Liu Guandao, dated 1280. *(National Palace Museum, Taipei, CC BY 4.0 @ www.npm.gov.tw.)*
- The accompanying litter image uses Omont pl. 47. The period hunt and the later illustrated reading have separate captions.

**II-5 pairing.** A Zhiyuan two-*guan* note design and printing plate; the Zhiyuan issue began in 1287. *(Currency Museum, Bank of Japan, Tokyo. Photograph: PHGCOM, CC BY-SA 3.0.)*
- Do not call the sheet "an original note": the photographer does not say whether it is a period note or a later pull from the plate. The 1287 issue date alone does not date the individual plate. S-17 identifies different 200/500-*wen* bronze blocks in the CUHK catalog; it does not authenticate this 2-*guan* object.

**III-1.** Takezaki Suenaga's men board a Yuan ship during the second invasion, 1281. The scroll he commissioned paints the fighting, not the storm. *(Mōko Shūrai Ekotoba, scroll 2, from the Edo-period copy in Kyushu University Library Collections; original in the Museum of the Imperial Collections, Tokyo.)*
- **Content check:** the scrolls contain no typhoon scene. The plan's hero ("The 1281 typhoon wrecks the Yuan fleet") is changed to this boarding scene.
- The original was retouched after it was made, and parts were later added; captions should not lean on any single figure as eyewitness detail.
- Better master if found: the original via ColBase (Sannomaru Shōzōkan items are there, CC BY-compatible, commercial use allowed); whether these scrolls are listed was not confirmed.

**III-11.** A surviving copy of Marco Polo's book in Pipino's Latin translation, printed by Gerard Leeu at Gouda in 1483–84. Its margins carry notes made by Columbus and others in his circle; the dates of the annotations are uncertain. *(Seville, Biblioteca Capitular y Colombina. Photograph: Hispalois, CC BY-SA 4.0.)* The selected photograph shows the Abyssinia/Aden opening; confirm the final crop and visible details during production. Neither the visible hand nor a pre-1492 date is established. [S-20 closure](../research/results/S-20-closure.md).
- Who wrote each note, and when, is debated (`facts.md` F-19). Gouda, not Antwerp (F-19).

**III-11 alternative.** Marco Polo as a Nuremberg gentleman: the title woodcut of the first printed edition of his book (German, Nuremberg, 1477), as re-cut for Dibdin in 1822. *(T. F. Dibdin, Aedes Althorpianae, 1822.)*
- A facsimile, not the 1477 print itself; the original's inscription border is not in the image.

**III-12.** A caravan on the road to Cathay, from the Catalan Atlas made in Majorca in 1375. Its inscription says it has left "the empire of Sarai" to go to Cathay. The riders are often read as the Polos; the map does not say so. *(Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS Espagnol 30; attributed to Cresques Abraham.)*

**III-12 alternative (1959 facsimile).** Same caption, credit: *(Hand-coloured facsimile, 1959, of the Catalan Atlas; Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division.)* Say "facsimile" in the credit: its colours are not the original's.

## Holder terms

- **BnF (Gallica).** Non-commercial reuse is free with the source credited. Commercial reuse (a book sold is commercial) "est payante et fait l'objet d'une licence", rates set by decision of the BnF president. Researchers are exempt for their own publications (BnF, 2019); whether a trade edition qualifies is for the BnF to say. The fee rests on French public-information law (loi 78-753), not copyright, so the work's PD status does not remove it, and a Commons copy of a BnF scan is still a BnF scan. Gallica's terms page could not be fetched here (Cloudflare block); confirm the current text and rate there before buying. Sources: [BnF press release 2019](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2019-10/CP_politique_diffusion_images_BnF.pdf); [BnF licence CGU 2023](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2023-04/CGU.pdf).
- **National Palace Museum, Taipei.** [Open data](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/opendata): about 1 MP images CC0; about 6 MP images CC BY 4.0, "不限用途" (no restriction on use), no application, no fee. Required credit: "The National Palace Museum, Taipei, CC BY 4.0 @ www.npm.gov.tw". Do not imply the museum made the product. Larger files: ask the museum.
- **Kyushu University Library.** [Reuse page](https://www.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/en/reuse): public-domain images reusable without application or fee since October 2018, commercial use and modification included; credit the source and note modifications. The IIIF manifest states "Public Domain" and the attribution "Kyushu University Library Collections".
- **ColBase** (national museums and the Museum of the Imperial Collections): commercial and non-commercial use without application, credit required; government standard terms 2.0, CC BY-compatible ([bunka.go.jp](https://www.bunka.go.jp/prmagazine/rensai/bunkazai/bunkazai_119.html)).
- **Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA.** Commercial use allowed. Credit the photographer and the licence beside the image. Share-alike binds the image (and any edit of it), not the book it sits in.
- **British Library (Cocharelli, Add MS 27695).** The IIIF manifest's usage terms read "Public Domain in most countries other than the UK" (checked 2026-11-13; rights statement bl.uk/rights/raqs/16.2/20170119). Anonymous IIIF requests return at most about 1200 px wide, below the 1350 px target. For a print master, order an image from the BL; for a UK edition, check BL terms first.
- **Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana** (and every Italian state collection). Codice dei beni culturali art. 108 with DM 108 of 21 March 2024: commercial publication needs a concession and fee ([Marciana](https://bibliotecanazionalemarciana.cultura.gov.it/en/services/photoreproductions/reproduction-rights/)). The Naya or Fraser copies avoid this.

## Leads not taken

- **I-2 without BnF** (checked 2026-11-10, second pass). No free colour copy exists:
  - **BL Royal MS 19 D I** (Paris, c. 1333–40): the Polo miniatures are f. 58r (three scenes: Baldwin, the patriarch, the Black Sea), f. 59v (the Khan gives the brothers the golden tablet) and f. 61r. The BL catalogue still marks its images "currently unavailable" (after the 2023 cyberattack), and no copy is on Commons. Recheck later.
  - **Bodleian MS Bodl. 264**, ff. 218–271 (England, c. 1410; modelled on Royal 19 D I): the arrival at Kublai's court is f. 220r (Bridgeman's label). Digital Bodleian images are CC BY-NC 4.0 (per Biblissima), so commercial use is again a paid licence.
  - **Omont's 1907 facsimile** of fr. 2810: PD and free, but monochrome and 1177 px wide (row I-2 alt). The Google scan of the same book (`livredesmerveillesmarcopolo1`, 600 ppi) is near-bitonal and carries a "Digitized by Google" mark; the Getty scan is the better one.
- **III-7 rukh.** Rukh-with-elephant images exist but none is free for commercial use:
  - the Met's double title page from an *'Aja'ib* (rukh or 'anqa attacking an elephant; [449012](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/449012)) is not open access;
  - the Munich Qazwini of 1280 (BSB Cod. arab. 464) is probably CC BY-NC-SA like its neighbour Cod. arab. 463, and no rukh folio was confirmed in it;
  - the Freer *'Aja'ib* folios carry "usage conditions apply".

  Two PD birds are on Commons but neither is the rukh: an 'anqa in NLM MS P 2, fol. 187b (Mughal, 18th c.; US government, PD) and a two-headed bird in Walters W.659 (CC0). Either could be a small inset beside the GEN plate.
- **III-12 without BnF** (checked 2026-11-10, second pass):
  - **LoC 1959 facsimile** (row III-12 alt). The caravan, inscription included, is on LoC image 5. The sheet is printed and hand-coloured with gold leaf, slightly reduced. Its outlines are redrawn and some colours changed (pink and green horses). Commons tags the scan PD-USGov and the work PD-Art. The facsimile itself is a slavish copy, so it probably carries no new rights in the US; in Spain any "mere photograph" right (25 years) has long expired. The "Atles català (full N, ca00000N)" files on Commons are the same LoC scans.
  - **Buchon and Tastu 1839** (*Notices et extraits* XIV): the Commons and Google copy has the text only; the facsimile plates are not scanned.
  - **Not reached:** Santarém's *Atlas* (1842–53) and the 1883 *Choix de documents géographiques*. Neither is on Internet Archive or Commons, and the Library of Congress site blocked automated search.
- **III-11 woodcut, other copies.** The BSB digitisation (`urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb00004552-7`) is reported as CC BY-NC-SA; Columbia's and the Morgan's copies could not be checked (bot block). The Dibdin facsimile avoids the question.
- **I-1 period image (2026-11-13).** Two leads:
  - **Cocharelli f. 8r** (BL Add MS 27695, Genoa c. 1330–40), a counting house with clerks, coins and townswomen. It is the strongest free candidate for an I-1 secondary or inset: PD outside the UK, but only 1200 px from the IIIF service (see Holder terms).
  - **Annales Ianuenses, BnF lat. 10136**: the consuls in council (PDF p. 231) and the siege of Savona with labelled *trabucos* (pp. 289–290). These are Gallica scans, so **fee** for print (BnF terms above). Reference copies and use rights are in `media/references/README.md` § Added 2026-11-13.
