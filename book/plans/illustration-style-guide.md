# Illustration Style Guide — *The Book of the Million*

**Status:** draft v1 (2026-10-21) · **Feeds:** `illustration-plan.md` (per-chapter scene list + prompts)

## Concept

Each region of the journey is illustrated in **the art of that region, around Polo's time** (c. 1250–1330). The reader sees Persia the way Persian painters saw it and Yuan China the way Yuan court painters saw it. This also helps with the book's central theme: the line between what Polo saw and what he only heard about.

- **Witnessed scenes ([V])** use the *local* tradition: how the people who lived there pictured their own world.
- **Marvels and hearsay ([M], [R])** may instead use the **European marvel lens**: the style of the illuminated *Livre des merveilles* (BnF fr. 2810, c. 1410–12). That is how Polo's first readers *imagined* the East. The image then signals "this is the legend" without a caption having to argue it.

Wherever a scene is disputed, this two-lens rule is the main safeguard against an image presenting legend as fact.

Two further rules:
- **Real before generated.** Where a genuine public-domain artwork of the very event exists (Liu Guandao's *Khubilai Khan Hunting*, the *Mōko Shūrai* scrolls, the *Livre des merveilles* miniatures, al-Qazwini's rukh, the Catalan Atlas), reproduce it instead of generating an imitation. See `illustration-plan.md`.
- **The tradition follows the scene's location**, not the chapter's. A Kublai-court scene in a chapter that opens in Acre is painted in the Yuan manner. The table below gives each chapter's dominant setting.

## Rules for every plate

**Honesty**
1. Every caption says what the image is: *"Modern illustration in the manner of [tradition]. Not a historical artifact."* Period-style images can be mistaken for real manuscripts, so this label is never optional.
2. Disputed or legendary scenes use the marvel lens, or carry a caption flag that matches the chapter's Notes marker (✗ / ◇ / ?).
3. Do not depict anything the chapter itself says is later embroidery (Ramusio or Pipino additions) as if the Book said it.

**Generation guardrails** (image models fail predictably here)
4. **No legible script or inscriptions in the image.** Models garble Arabic, Persian, Chinese, Armenian and Latin. Leave cartouches and colophons blank, and put all text in the LaTeX caption.
5. **No imitated signatures or seals.** Do not reproduce a named artist's seal, signature, or a specific folio composition. The goal is the *idiom*, not a copy.
6. **Religious figures:** no depiction of the Prophet Muhammad. Buddhist and Christian sacred iconography only when the scene itself is religious (e.g. St Thomas's shrine, Sakyamuni on Seilan), never as decoration.
7. **People:** dress, hair and arms must fit the date and region. Mongol court dress (*jisün* robes, *boqta* headdress for married noblewomen) differs from Song Chinese dress, which differs from Persian. Each scene entry in the plan carries its own accuracy notes.

**Consistency across traditions.** Because the styles vary, the *frame* is the constant:
- Every plate sits in the same LaTeX float: a thin rule border, a numbered caption (`Plate II.4`), and the tradition credit line.
- Fixed aspect ratios by format: **portrait miniature 3:4 · landscape miniature 4:3 · handscroll 3:1 (spread across two pages, or cropped to 2:1) · palm-leaf strip 4:1**.
- Background: each tradition's own ground (paper, silk, plaster), aged lightly and *the same amount* across the series. No heavy grunge on some plates and pristine finish on others.

## Recurring figures

*Added by S-05 (2026-10-22).* About twenty people appear in more than one plate, often in very different traditions. Marco is painted in Gothic, Ilkhanid, Yuan and Venetian idioms; Cublay ages from about 50 (I-2) to about 76 (III-9). Without fixed anchors each tradition invents a new man. The anchors live in `plans/registry/character-cards.yaml`, one card per figure:

- **likeness:** the period image to follow for face and dress, if one exists (the NPM Yuan portraits of Cublay, Chabi and Chinghis; Liu Guandao's hunt; the *Jami' al-Tawarikh* Ilkhans). Never copy its composition (rule 5). **No authentic likeness of any Polo exists**: the Doria Pamphilj portrait and the 1477 woodcut are inventions and are not used.
- **house:** our fixed conventions for build, hair and beard, so a figure stays recognisable. They are not facts and are never captioned.
- **attributes:** what identifies the figure in every idiom. Marco: the youngest of the three Latins before 1291, a merchant's belt-purse, a gold paiza only from 1275. Niccolò: the heavy-set elder who speaks. Rustichello: a layman with quires and an ink-horn. Cublay: carried in a chamber or litter after c. 1280. Cocachin: unmarried, so no *boqta*, until her marriage to Casan.
- **dress by period:** what the figure wears in each year and place. Marco in Cathay wears a Mongol-style robe; he is not a Venetian in silk at the Yuan court.
- **traditions:** how each idiom draws that person. In Ilkhanid painting the Polos are Franks, bareheaded or in soft caps. In Yuan painting they are western foreigners by Chinese convention (high nose, full beard) in Mongol robes. In the *Livre des merveilles* lens they are French gentlemen, and Cublay wears a crown.
- **ages and co-presence:** `python3 tools/characters.py ages` gives every figure's age in every plate scene, and fails if a scene puts someone where the dates forbid (no Polos at Xiangyang; no Chabi after 1281; no Argon at the bride's arrival).

Every prompt with a recurring figure takes its `[PERIOD]` and `[AVOID]` lines from `python3 tools/characters.py cue <id> <year>`. Review checks the card's attributes along with the scene's "must get right" column.

## Technical spec for LaTeX

- **Resolution:** 300 dpi at final printed size. For a 6×9 in book with a ~4.5×7.2 in text block: full page **1350×2160 px**; half page **1350×1000 px**; handscroll spread **2700×900 px**.
- Generators usually output ~1–2 MP, so plan an **upscale pass** (e.g. Real-ESRGAN) and check fine detail (faces, hands, rigging) after upscaling.
- **Formats:** plates as PNG (lossless master) → JPEG at 95% quality for the build. Maps and diagrams as **PDF/SVG vector**.
- **Color:** sRGB masters. If the printer requires CMYK, convert at the end, and proof the saturated pigments (lapis, malachite, vermilion), which shift most.
- **Files:** `book/media/plates/<vol>-<ch>-<slug>.png` + a sidecar `.yaml` holding the prompt, model, seed, date, tradition and caption. Each sidecar becomes a row in `ATTRIBUTIONS.md`.

## Prompt template

```
[SCENE] <what is happening, who, where; 1–2 sentences, concrete>
[STYLE] Painted in the manner of <tradition> (<reference work, date>):
        <3–5 idiom cues, e.g. "flat mineral pigments, gold ground,
        stacked perspective, figures in three-quarter view, scalloped clouds">
[PERIOD] <dress / architecture / ships / animals cues from the scene entry;
         recurring figures: the lines from `tools/characters.py cue <id> <year>`>
[FORMAT] <aspect ratio>, <ground: paper/silk/plaster>, light even aging
[AVOID] legible text or inscriptions, signatures, seals, modern objects,
        photographic realism, <scene-specific anachronisms>
```
