# Illustration Style Guide — *The Book of the Million*

**Final v1 · 2026-11-25.** For scene briefs and approved treatments, see the [illustration plan](illustration-plan.md). For current print placements, see the [proof and audit record](status/visual-placement-2026-11-23.md#illustration-package-audit--25-november-2026).

## Concept

Use the visual traditions of each region around Polo’s time (c. 1250–1330). A scene Polo presents as witnessed ([V]) ordinarily uses the local tradition. Read the chapter’s Notes to check what the evidence supports.

Marvels and hearsay ([M], [R]) can use the **European marvel lens**, based on the early fifteenth-century *Livre des merveilles* (BnF fr. 2810). Its illuminated miniatures show a later European reading of the Book. A local tradition can also carry a marvel, as the Dunhuang-style Lop plate and the Persian wonders-style rukh plate do. In either idiom, the caption names the story’s evidential status.

Let the scene’s setting determine the local tradition. I-3 opens in Acre, but its hero plate takes place at the summer court of Kaiping and uses Yuan court painting. The table below offers regional references. The [scene briefs](illustration-plan.md#hero-plates) identify the treatment selected for each plate.

Prefer a relevant historical reproduction when the exact source file has documented terms for the intended print use, enough detail at the intended size, and a caption that can explain what it depicts. Check the work and the supplied photograph, scan, or facsimile separately. A later depiction can illustrate reception or the transmission of a story. For example, Liu Guandao’s 1280 hunting scroll shows a Yuan hunt, while Omont’s facsimile of the French elephant-litter miniature shows a later reading. The rukh uses a modern Persian wonders-style illustration because the sourcing review found no eligible reproduction of the matching subject. See the [historical source register](registry/pd-sources.md).

## Rules for every plate

**Captions and evidence**

1. Give each modern plate the disclosure *“Modern illustration in the manner of [tradition]. Not a historical artifact. This edition.”* A caption may identify a broader style where the plate only approximates its brief, as in II-9. Keep the disclosure with the image.
2. Name the evidential limit where it affects what a reader sees. Identify a legend as a legend, attribute a second-hand account, and specify the conjectural or contested feature. Follow the chapter’s Notes and use plain language in the caption. Apply this rule to both regional and marvel-lens plates.
3. Attribute details from later witnesses to the witness that supplies them. Omit details the scene brief excludes. For a historical reproduction, name the object, its date or period, the supplied image or edition, and the source credit. Give the photographer and licence when recorded for that file. Disclose material cropping, rotation, or other alterations in the caption or linked credit record. Keep the source and alterations in the [media credits](../media/ATTRIBUTIONS.md#historical-images-and-facsimiles).

**Generation guardrails** (image models fail predictably here)
4. **No legible script or inscriptions in the image.** Models garble Arabic, Persian, Chinese, Armenian and Latin. Leave cartouches and colophons blank, and put all text in the LaTeX caption.
5. **No imitated signatures or seals.** Do not reproduce a named artist's seal, signature, or a specific folio composition. The goal is the *idiom*, not a copy.
6. **Religious figures:** no depiction of the Prophet Muhammad. Buddhist and Christian sacred iconography only when the scene itself is religious (e.g. St Thomas's shrine, Sakyamuni on Seilan), never as decoration.
7. **People:** dress, hair and arms must fit the date and region. Mongol court dress (*jisün* robes, *boqta* headdress for married noblewomen) differs from Song Chinese dress, which differs from Persian. Each scene entry in the plan carries its own accuracy notes. Period objects to check against, and the cue lines drawn from them: `registry/visual-references.md`; reference images: `media/reference/s22/`.

**Modern works as references** (S-22, adopted 2026-11-13). Manga, anime, films, games and reconstruction art may be consulted, under five rules:
8. **Use modern works as viewing aids.** They can suggest objects, dress, gear, and staging. Draw the scene in its selected historical tradition.
9. **Never in a prompt.** No modern title, artist or character name goes into a generation prompt, and no frame, page or still is used as an image input. Period reference images (`media/references/`, `media/reference/s22/`) may be attached as image inputs **only** when their manifest marks them `edit OK` (public domain or CC0), and accepted project-owned character sheets, place sheets, or earlier masters may be attached for a targeted revision. `look only` files (BnF scans, rights-reserved museum images) never go in. Describe the period evidence in words and list each attached file with its role, credit, and reuse terms. See the [S-11 manifest](../media/references/README.md) and [S-22 manifest](../media/reference/s22/README.md).
10. **No character likenesses.** Recurring figures come from the S-05 cards and period portraits, never from an actor's or a drawn character's look.
11. **Period check.** Look for evidence from the time and place of the scene before treating a detail as documented. Date later comparanda in the cue record. If the edition uses one to show a conjectural feature, record the limit in the sidecar and name the feature in the caption. See the [period cues](illustration-plan.md#s-22-period-cues-2026-11-13).
12. **Artwork for print.** Use edition illustrations and historical reproductions with documented reuse terms. Modern reference frames, panels, and stills are viewing aids only.

**Consistency across traditions**

- Figures use the edition’s LaTeX caption style and volume numbering, such as Plate II.4. Caption credits identify the illustration tradition or the source of the reproduction.
- Use **3:4** for portrait miniatures, **4:3** for landscape miniatures, and **4:1** for palm-leaf strips. A long handscroll brief can start at **3:1**. The current edition places single-page crops, commonly **2:1**. Preserve the proportions of historical sources and crop only for the subject and legibility.
- Use the tradition’s own ground, such as paper, silk, or plaster, with light, even aging.

## Recurring figures

*Added by S-05 (2026-10-22).* About twenty people appear in more than one plate, often in very different traditions. Marco is painted in Gothic, Ilkhanid, Yuan and Venetian idioms; Cublay ages from about 50 (I-2) to about 76 (III-9). Without fixed anchors each tradition invents a new man. The anchors live in the [character cards](registry/character-cards.yaml), one card per figure:

- **likeness:** the period image to follow for face and dress, if one exists (the NPM Yuan portraits of Cublay, Chabi and Chinghis; Liu Guandao's hunt; the *Jami' al-Tawarikh* Ilkhans). Never copy its composition (rule 5). The Polos have no authenticated likeness. Treat the Doria Pamphilj portrait and the 1477 woodcut as reception material.
- **house:** our fixed conventions for build, hair and beard, so a figure stays recognisable. They are not facts and are never captioned.
- **attributes:** what identifies the figure in every idiom. Marco: the youngest of the three Latins before 1291, a merchant's belt-purse, a gold paiza only from 1275. Niccolò: the heavy-set elder who speaks. Rustichello: a layman with quires and an ink-horn. Cublay: carried in a chamber or litter after c. 1280. Cocachin: unmarried, so no *boqta*, until her marriage to Casan.
- **dress by period:** what the figure wears in each year and place. Marco in Cathay wears a Mongol-style robe; he is not a Venetian in silk at the Yuan court.
- **traditions:** how each idiom draws that person. In Ilkhanid painting the Polos are Franks, bareheaded or in soft caps. In Yuan painting they are western foreigners by Chinese convention (high nose, full beard) in Mongol robes. In the *Livre des merveilles* lens they are French gentlemen, and Cublay wears a crown.
- **ages and co-presence:** `python3 tools/characters.py ages` gives every figure's age in every plate scene, and fails if a scene puts someone where the dates forbid (no Polos at Xiangyang; no Chabi after 1281; no Argon at the bride's arrival).

Every prompt with a recurring figure takes its `[PERIOD]` and `[AVOID]` lines from `python3 tools/characters.py cue <id> <year>`. Review checks the card's attributes along with the scene's "must get right" column.

## Technical spec for LaTeX

- The edition trims to **6 × 9 in** with a **4.5 × 7.2 in** text block. The [LaTeX template](../tools/build/book.latex) fits unqualified figures within the available width and a **5.4 in** height limit, with proportions preserved. A chapter can set a narrower width for a particular placement.
- Target **300 effective ppi in both dimensions at the printed size**. A full-width image needs at least **1,350 px** across. At that width, a 2:1 handscroll crop needs at least **1,350 × 675 px**. The 2,700 × 1,350 px master used in the pilot embeds at 600 ppi when placed 4.5 in wide.
- The [print filter](../tools/build/book.lua) checks raster width against the requested percentage of the text block and assumes full width when no percentage is given. It emits a labelled placeholder for a missing file or a header/extension mismatch. Confirm effective ppi in the finished PDF with `pdfimages -list`.
- Keep modern plate masters as sRGB PNGs. The print build accepts PNG and JPEG and uses the PDF companion of an SVG when available. Keep maps and diagrams in their source vector formats with a PDF for print. If the printer requires CMYK, convert and proof the saturated colours at that stage.
- Keep modern masters and their YAML sidecars together under `book/media/plates/`. Record the prompt, negatives, generator, known model version, job ID or seed when available, date, tradition, caption, attached sources, processing, and review. Link the accepted sidecar in [media credits](../media/ATTRIBUTIONS.md#generated-plates-regional-idioms--s-10-onward).

## Prompt template

```
[SCENE] <what is happening, who, where; 1–2 sentences, concrete>
[STYLE] Painted in the manner of <tradition> (<reference work, date>):
        <3–5 idiom cues, e.g. "flat mineral pigments, gold ground,
        stacked perspective, figures in three-quarter view, scalloped clouds">
[PERIOD] <dress / architecture / ships / animals cues from the scene entry;
         recurring figures: the lines from `tools/characters.py cue <id> <year>`>
[FORMAT] <aspect ratio>, <ground: paper/silk/plaster>, light even aging
[AVOID] legible text or inscriptions, signatures, seals, modern objects,
        photographic realism, <scene-specific anachronisms>
```

Join `[STYLE] [SCENE] [PERIOD] [FORMAT]` in that order. Describe concrete actions and objects. With Flux Krea, left/right staging language can produce a diptych. Send the `[AVOID]` list as bare nouns through the API’s `negative_prompt` field. For chat-based routes, include the exclusions in the prompt.

The [image generation guide](image-generation-guide.md) gives the Codex, Akool, and Gemini workflows and filing instructions. For the S-10 pilot, compare the [Akool handscroll](../media/plates/II-13-west-lake-barges.yaml), the [Akool Chola mural](../media/plates/III-5-pearl-divers.yaml), and the [Gemini Ilkhanid plate](../media/plates/I-7-carauna-escape.yaml). Use the accepted sidecar for the route and settings of a particular plate.

Negatives that worked:
- **Song–Yuan:** text, calligraphy, inscription, writing, seal, red stamp, colophon, signature, Qing dynasty, Ming dynasty, queue hairstyle, photograph, 3d render.
- **Chola:** text, script, inscription, writing, deity, god, crown, halo, heavy damage, peeling paint, cartoon, teeth, photograph, 3d render.

## Review checklist (per plate)

Review the selected modern candidate against its scene brief and the checks below. The Akool pilot generated four candidates per round. For a historical reproduction, verify the exact source, credit, alterations, and evidential limits, then apply the same print check.

1. **Hard: no script.** Zoom every cartouche, border, banner and corner. Pseudo-calligraphy and red seal stamps count as script.
2. **Hard: period.** Reject a clear anachronism or a misleading representation of a scene-defining feature. Check dress, headgear, architecture and ships against the scene row's "must get right" column. Common misses: Safavid turbans in an Ilkhanid scene, Ming/Qing roofs in Song scenes, crowned deity figures in Chola scenes. From S-22 (the errors modern media make are the ones generators make): late samurai armour and curved katana in 1274; the Forbidden City or a yellow-tiled Ming hall for Dadu; a bent-pole Turkic yurt for a Mongol ger; fur-and-leather "barbarian" dress on Mongol nobles; loose hair or a tiara on a Mongol court woman where the scene calls for the *boqta* or braids. Where the row names S-22 cues, check the plate against those lines.
3. **Hard: scene.** The people and action named in `[SCENE]` are present: count them. Recurring figures match their card attributes (`tools/characters.py cue`).
4. **Hard: rule 6.** No sacred figure used as decoration.
5. **Idiom.** Check for the specific cues that distinguish the intended tradition, such as the grounds, contours, and figures of Ilkhanid manuscript painting.
6. **Ground and aging.** Light and even, matching the series. Reject heavy flaking or fake mounting seams, or crop them out.
7. **Artifact risk.** Check that the mandatory modern disclosure appears in the caption. Note any resemblance to a historical folio in the sidecar.
8. **Print check at final size.** Check effective ppi, crop, and caption legibility in the volume proof. Inspect faces, hands, and rigging at 100%. Start from a sharper candidate when the source detail is too soft for print.
9. **Sidecar.** Record the prompt and negatives, generator details, sources, caption, processing, and a disposition for each review item. Use the actual generation record for identifiers.

A **borderline** period or style detail can be retained if it leaves the scene and the evidence intelligible. Record what the sources support, what the image supplies, and the reason for retaining it in the sidecar or placement record. Name the consequential uncertainty in the caption.
