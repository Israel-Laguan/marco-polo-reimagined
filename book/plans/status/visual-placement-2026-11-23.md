## Decisions from the proof

- The banquet and the New Year parade have separate subjects and anchors. The rotas belong with the guard passage. The nine parcels in the modern procession abbreviate Ramusio’s eighty-one gifts. The full width retains the last camel.
- The museum’s whole-scroll photograph keeps the high landscape and the mounted party. The elephant-borne room is visible in a different work, the Parisian miniature. Polo supplies the roof opening and the cast. Dates, file-level rights and the two facsimile leaves are documented in the [historical source register](../registry/pd-sources.md#sources-selected-at-placement--23-november-2026).
- The Kyushu crop brings the boarding party into view at book width. The Colombina crop retains the handwritten lines beside the printed text. Its caption names the Abyssinia–Aden opening to distinguish the nearby discussion of fol. 57v. The Atlas crop retains the inscription above the riders. The holding library supplies affirmative reuse terms for its 1959 facsimile.
- The current manuscript had lost C2, C9, C17 and A14. All four are back at the passages that explain them. In-volume links target printed figures or map headings, and captions retain the edition’s A/B/C identifiers. The raw C12 base came out of the III-1 note pending a labelled diagram.
- Four previously queued drafts were verified and placed after source checks ([visual-references.md](../registry/visual-references.md#exemplum-divination-bridge-and-shore-camp--production-findings-23-november-2026)):
  - **I-5 (`#fig-i-5-caliph-treasure`, Plate I.12, p. 45, 385 ppi):** Comparison of the early French illuminated tradition confirms that BL Royal MS 19 D I, fol. 64r (c. 1333–1340) shows only the walled city of Baudas and Bodleian MS Bodl. 264, fol. 222r (c. 1410) shows its military siege, whereas BnF MS fr. 2810, fol. 9r (Omont 1907, pl. 12) specifically illustrates the treasure-tower exemplum as a Gothic cutaway chamber. The modern French Gothic marvel-lens plate is placed at the treasury-door paragraph with a caption contrasting the Book’s exemplum and the Muslim accounts in the facing comparison table (p. 44).
  - **I-12 (`#fig-i-12-cane-divination`, Plate I.24, p. 111, 322 ppi):** The 2026-11-19 psalter-wash composite replaces the earlier Latin Vulgate text with non-linguistic wash bands and a vermilion rubric, and its caption notes that Church of the East clerics read the psalm in their Syriac liturgy (attested by Turfan/Bulayïq psalter fragments) while contrasting Polo’s 1200 Tenduc setting with Toghrul’s 1203 defeat.
  - **II-6 (`#fig-ii-6-pulisanghin-bridge`, Plate II.19, p. 70, 600 ppi):** Historical check of the Lugou Bridge (Jin 1189–1192; Ming Jiajing embankment repairs) in Yule–Cordier (Bk II, ch. 35, n. 2, citing Magaillans, Ament, Intorcetta and Bretschneider) confirms that pre-1668 witnesses record thirteen arches before the flood of 25 July 1668 destroyed two arches and the Kangxi reconstruction of 1698 established the eleven-arch span and single lion-topped pilasters. The plate is placed facing Map A10 (p. 71) with a caption distinguishing the 1698 eleven-arch configuration from the thirteen arches in pre-1668 accounts and the twenty-four arches and double-lion columns in Polo.
  - **III-3 (`#fig-iii-3-samara-camp`, Plate III.8, p. 31, 322 ppi):** Closed spike [S-27](../spikes/S-27-samara-shore-camp.md) traced the wooden posts cited in Oetomo and Surachman 2012 (*Berkala Arkeologi Sangkhakala* 15.1: 142–154) to the 2008 Puslitbang Arkenas test pits at Lampoh Kota and Ulee Tanoh (~25 cm circular black-soil post-moulds at 30–40 cm depth, interpreted as decayed domestic house piles) alongside the 2010 Cot Astana *Kotak I* collapsed brick wall. Because neither deposit supplies a c. 1290 date or superstructure elevation, the modest shore-camp relief is placed with a caption distinguishing F and V’s wooden defences from Z and Ramusio’s ditches, five timber towers and two thousand men, and identifying the shelters, vessels and dress as conjectural. The linked [`s27-shelter-crop.png`](../../media/plates/drafts/III-3/s27-shelter-crop.png) is a retained detail crop from this accepted plate for comparison and proofing, not a separate candidate or placement.
- Eight final hero plates and five cutaway diagrams were verified and placed:
  - **I-9 (`#fig-i-9-balas-mines`, Plate I.20, p. 85, 322 ppi):** Reconstructed spinel mine in Badakhshan. Passes all review lines; caption explicitly states it is a reconstruction after Polo and balas rubies are spinels, not verified archaeological excavations.
  - **II-1 (`#fig-ii-1-nayan-elephant-tower`, Plate II.5, p. 4, 600 ppi):** Nayan battle of 1287 with Kublai seated atop the 4-elephant timber tower. Passes all review lines (white tug standard, cross pennon on rebel side, older Kublai, no Polos on field). Caption notes the inflated spectacle totals.
  - **II-5 (`#fig-ii-5-paper-money`, Plate II.15, p. 58, 322 ppi):** Printing the Kaan's paper money. Blank sheet convention adheres to review guidelines; paired with the photograph of the Zhiyuan bronze plate and note (p. 59) and C1 diagram (p. 60).
  - **II-8 (`#fig-ii-8-vochan-battle`, Plate II.24, p. 98, 322 ppi):** Battle of Vochan in Pagan temple mural idiom. Dismounted archers shooting into elephant towers. Caption qualifies numbers and debated identity.
  - **II-11 (`#fig-ii-11-xiangyang-trebuchet-turned`, Plate II.30, p. 136, 322 ppi):** Turned variant of the Xiangyang trebuchet with counterweight on river/city side and sling on loading side; Muslim engineers and Yuan troops only. Paired with C14 comparative schematic (p. 134).
  - **III-2 (`#fig-iii-2-shoal-rudders`, Plate III.7, p. 23, 322 ppi):** Junks lifting axial rudders in Bintan shoal strait. Caption identifies the hoist mechanism as conjectural.
  - **III-6 (`#fig-iii-6-malabar-corsairs`, Plate III.12, p. 70, 600 ppi):** Corsair cordon off Malabar in Western Indian Jain palm-leaf style (4:1 strip).
  - **III-8 (`#fig-iii-8-hormos-windcatchers`, Plate III.15, p. 96, 322 ppi):** Old mainland Hormuz wind-catchers in Baghdad school style. Caption qualifies that surviving wind-catchers are later reconstructions.
  - **C12 (`#fig-c12-seagoing-junk`, Fig III.4, p. 3, vector):** Cutaway labels attribute the four-mast description (plus two spares) to the Book and the twelve bulkheads, thirteen compartments, limber holes and axial-rudder stock socket to the Quanzhou wreck and Ramusio where appropriate. Three masts appear in the base drawing, so the callout now says plainly that the rig is schematic. The caption distinguishes the Book’s description from the archaeological features; the upper works remain schematic.
  - **C13 (`#fig-c13-sewn-plank-hormos`, Fig I.15, p. 66, vector):** Sewn-plank ship of Hormuz with TikZ callouts identifying coir stitching, unnailed planks, hide cover, horses, single mast/sail, quarter rudder.
  - **C14 (`#fig-c14-trebuchet-comparison`, Fig II.29, p. 134, vector):** Comparative schematic of traction pao and counterweight Huihui pao.
  - **C15 (`#fig-c15-qanat`, Fig I.16, p. 72, vector):** Pure TikZ schematic cross-section of desert qanat irrigation system.
  - **C16 (`#fig-c16-cane-palace-roof`, Fig I.26, p. 123, vector):** Cane palace roof section with TikZ callouts for split half-cane tiles, silk tie cords, and gilded dragon columns.
