## Decisions from the proof

- The banquet and the New Year parade have separate subjects and anchors. The rotas belong with the guard passage. The nine parcels in the modern procession abbreviate Ramusio’s eighty-one gifts. The full width retains the last camel.
- The museum’s whole-scroll photograph keeps the high landscape and the mounted party. The elephant-borne room is visible in a different work, the Parisian miniature. Polo supplies the roof opening and the cast. Dates, file-level rights and the two facsimile leaves are documented in the [historical source register](../registry/pd-sources.md#sources-selected-at-placement--23-november-2026).
- The Kyushu crop brings the boarding party into view at book width. The Colombina crop retains the handwritten lines beside the printed text. Its caption names the Abyssinia–Aden opening to distinguish the nearby discussion of fol. 57v. The Atlas crop retains the inscription above the riders. The holding library supplies affirmative reuse terms for its 1959 facsimile.
- The current manuscript had lost C2, C9, C17 and A14. All four are back at the passages that explain them. In-volume links target printed figures or map headings, and captions retain the edition’s A/B/C identifiers. The raw C12 base came out of the III-1 note pending a labelled diagram.
- Continuous builds covered 177, 232 and 191 pages. Each of the 36 placements was located by its figure label and inspected with the facing page. Every raster had its expected embedded dimensions, at least 322 effective ppi, and a caption on the image page. Greyscale checks included the feast, hunt, litter, court calendar, western realms and postils. These are digital print proofs. The original hunting silk has low tonal contrast, and the Colombina notes reward enlargement for individual words. The full books reported zero placeholders, undersized raster warnings or overfull lines of 10 pt or more.
