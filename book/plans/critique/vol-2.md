# Critique — Volume II, *The Realm of Kublai*

Scope: `vol-2/` (ch-01 to ch-15, front-01, front-02, back-01, README) checked against VOICE-GUIDE.md, outline-volumes.md (Vol. II), gazetteer.csv, and the Yule–Cordier text in `refs/` (searched by phrase and with an automated 7/8-word n-gram comparison; no ref file was read whole). The review was read-only, and this file is the only thing written.

---

## 1. Volume verdict

The structure is sound. Every outline beat is present, the citation mechanics are clean (every [n] has a Note, numbering runs in order, nothing is orphaned), and the asides usually handle the big disputes well: Saianfu chronology and engineers, Kinsay numbers, Litan, Nayan's kinship, the pork silence, the Mien battle against the *Yuan shi*. But the volume is not yet a retelling. It is mostly Yule's 1903 English with hinge phrases attached.

The top three systemic issues:

1. **Near-transcription of Yule.** In 13 of 15 chapters, 45–80% of the narrative words fall inside 7-word strings that also appear verbatim in Yule–Cordier. The Vol. I pilot ch-02 scores 28%. The result is 82 archaisms ("hath", "wist", "natheless", "trowed", "gat", "nill") and 38 first-person lines in the Book's own voice ("I assure you", "we will quit").
2. **Ramusio material absorbed without a flag.** About 25 passages that Yule prints in square brackets, his marker for Ramusio-only text, appear in ch-02, 03, 04, 05, 12, 13 and 14 with no flag in the text.
3. **Scaffolding left in the reader's text.** Drafting instructions and outline-beat lists appear in the narrative ("narrate as the Book does, and frame comparatively", "the project gazetteer lists…", "What Book II.lvi–lix promised… is done:"). Recap paragraphs add about 2,500 words of padding across the volume.

There is also one critical historical error. ch-05 presents the *Yuan shi* "Po-lo, Assessor of the Privy Council" as Marco and calls it a ✓ "presence pillar". Yule–Cordier's own addenda, and Pelliot after them, identify this man as the Mongol official Bolad.

Severity counts: **3 critical · 13 major · 15 minor · 9 nits.**

## 2. Findings (ranked)

### Critical

**C1. ch-05:25 and Note [7] (ch-05:77). Fidelity and scholarship attribution. The *Yuan shi* "Polo" passage is a misidentification, presented as fact.**
- **Problem.** The body says "Chinese annals… echo that the Emperor desired Polo, Assessor of the Privy Council, to explain Wangchu's motives… That echo is a pillar of the presence debate." Note [7] marks it "✓ De Mailla/Gaubil notice is a presence pillar (Vogel; Haw; Rossabi)".
- **Evidence.** This is Yule's 1870s note (yule-vol1, NOTE 5 to Bk II ch. xxiii). Cordier's addendum in yule-vol2 prints E. H. Parker (1904): "Puh-lo… had already received honours from Kúblái many years before Marco's arrival in 1275… neither can be Marco Polo." Modern scholarship (Pelliot, *Notes*; Allsen on Bolad Aqa) identifies this Po-lo as Bolad Chingsang. Crediting the "pillar" reading to Vogel, Haw and Rossabi misrepresents their positions. Verify each one, but none of them rests the case on this passage.
- **Knock-on effects.** ch-11 Note [5] (line 67), "Yule conjectured ~1282–1287/88", rests on the same identification: Yule's 1282 anchor is "vol. i. p. 422", the Po-lo note. Vol. III ch-12 (item 5, "presence-pillar readings… still argue") repeats the error. Flag it to the Vol. III reviewer.
- **Fix (ch-05:25).** "Victorian editors once read a 'Po-lo' in the Chinese annals of these events as Marco himself; Yule–Cordier's own addenda and later scholars show that man to be the Mongol minister Bolad, so the episode's Chinese echo confirms the plot, not the Venetian. For the present the narrative keeps faith with Ramusio's telling…" Change Note [7] to "✗ Po-lo = Bolad (Parker in Cordier; Pelliot), not Marco; presence rests elsewhere (salt, paper money, Cocachin embassy)."

**C2. Whole volume. Voice: chapters paraphrase Yule's archaic English rather than retell it.**
- **Evidence (n-gram check, narrative only).** Share of words inside 7-word strings found verbatim in Yule: ch-01 61%, ch-02 80%, ch-03 80%, ch-04 72%, ch-05 31%, ch-06 64%, ch-07 47%, ch-08 77%, ch-09 55%, ch-10 69%, ch-11 57%, ch-12 63%, ch-13 45%, ch-14 72%, ch-15 52%. The pilot Vol. I ch-02 scores 28%.
- **Examples.**
  - ch-01:11 "he is the most potent man… that existeth in the world, or ever hath existed from Adam" is Yule word for word.
  - ch-01:17 "waxed proud in the insolence of his youth… he would fain wrest the empire".
  - ch-08:29 "Then might you see swashing blows dealt and taken from sword and mace… God might have thundered".
  - ch-06:23–25 (Golden King) and ch-12:65 ("Now let us quit Suju… so we shall now proceed").
- **Why it matters.** The voice guide forbids archaic pastiche and a narrator who "pretends to *be* the Book". 82 archaic tokens and 38 first-person Book lines ("I assure you", "Let me tell you", "We could tell you about the other six") break both rules. The first-person lines cluster in ch-12 (11), ch-14 (8), ch-10 (5) and ch-13 (4).
- **Fix.** Do a dedicated re-voicing pass and aim for 30% overlap or less. Keep Yule's words only as short, marked quotations, at most one or two per scene, chosen for flavour ("Sire! Look out for Cranes!"). Turn catalogue passages (ch-02 palace, ch-03 feasts, ch-08 battle, ch-10 and ch-12 city strings) into modern storyteller summary. Example rewrite of ch-01:17: "Nayan, Polo says, was a young lord of about thirty who could put three hundred thousand horsemen in the field, and the power went to his head: he decided he would no longer be the Kaan's vassal, and sent to Caidu proposing they strike from two sides at once."

**C3. front-01:4, front-02:4, back-01:4. Fidelity: invented epigraphs.**
- **Evidence.** None of these three quotations appears in Yule–Cordier (whitespace-normalised search of both volumes):
  - front-01: "Now I have told you of the splendour of Cublay… and I will tell you of the city of Cambaluc…"
  - front-02: "And so I will tell you of the great province of Manzi…"
  - back-01: "All these cities are so great that…"
- The ch-01 to ch-15 epigraphs all match, apart from small wording drift; see nit N5.
- **Fix.** Replace them with real lines.
  - front-01: "Now am I come to that part of our Book in which I shall tell you of the great and wonderful magnificence of the Great Kaan now reigning" (Bk II.i, verified).
  - front-02: "And when you pass this river you enter the great province of Manzi" (Bk II.lxv, verified).
  - back-01: a verified Kinsay line, e.g. "the city is beyond dispute the finest and the noblest in the world". Alternatively, drop the quotation marks and the source label.

**M2. Cachanfu given as Xi'an. Continuity and naming. front-01:45, front-02:41, gazetteer.csv row seq 54.**

- ch-06:29–31 and Note [8] correctly separate Cachanfu (Puzhou/Hezhong at the Yellow River bend) from Kenjanfu (Xi'an), two days and eight days beyond the river respectively.
- "Nestorians noted" also contradicts ch-06:33, which makes the *silence* about the Nestorian stele its dispute hook.
- **Fix.** front-01: "…toward Shanxi, the Yellow River crossing at Cachanfu, and Kenjanfu (Xi'an)". front-02: add a Kenjanfu row (Xi'an, Prince Mangala's seat), set Cachanfu to Puzhou/Hezhong, and delete "Nestorians noted". Gazetteer: the same correction plus a Kenjanfu row. The "Pazanjin / Hezhong" row (seq 66) appears in no chapter and duplicates the Hezhong ID, so reconcile it.

**M4. Recap padding and template tics. Prose and structure, whole volume.**
- Every chapter ends with a recap paragraph:
  - ch-02 to ch-06 use "Here the chapter stops short of… For the present it has shown…" (5 times).
  - ch-07 to ch-14 use "…has done what Book II.x promised…" (8 times).
- ch-02 to ch-05 also *open* with "The Book has shown/told…" recaps.
- Mid-chapter "So/Thus [place]…" restatements re-list what the previous paragraph said: ch-09:15, :21, :29; ch-11:13, :19, :23, :31, :51; ch-12:15, :27, :35; ch-13:23, :41, :73; ch-14:19, :37, :45. Together these come to about 2,500 words.
- Of the 37 em-dash asides, 27 end with the identical "Anyway, next he mentions X". Across a 150k-word series this formula will grate.
- **Fix.**
  - Cut every closing recap to at most one sentence, or remove it.
  - Merge the useful identification content of the mid-chapter "So…" paragraphs into the Notes.
  - Vary the aside exits ("Back to the road:", "The Book moves on to…", or simply resume), and cap "Anyway, next he mentions" at one per chapter.

**M5. ch-01:19 (and Note [5], ch-01:63). Historical error: Nayan's descent.**
- The body says "great-great-grandson of Chinghiz's brother Belgutai".
- Yule's note says Uchegin (Temüge Otchigin), "called in the Chinese annals Pilgutai", and Cordier/Palladius add that "Belgutai was Chinghiz's step-brother". The chapter merged the two. The standard modern account makes Nayan a descendant of Temüge Otchigin.
- **Fix.** "…a great-great-grandson of Chinghiz's youngest brother Temüge Otchigin". Note [5]: "Otchigin line (Yule's *Uchegin*; Chinese transcription confused with Belgutai)".

**M6. ch-11:45 and :39. Fidelity: Saianfu details carried over from superseded Yule.**
- (a) "Alawating (Alauddin) of Mufali and his pupil Ysemain (Ismail) of Huli (Herat)" states Yule's guess as fact. The Yule–Cordier addendum (Bushell/Parker, 1906, in yule-vol2) already corrects him: "not 'A-la-wa-ting of Mu-fa-li and Ysemain of Huli'… but A-la-pu-tan of Mao-sa-li [Mosul] and Y-sz-ma-yin of Shih-la [Shiraz]". Modern readers usually take *Huli* as Hülegü's ulus.
  - Fix: "two Muslim engineers sent from the Ilkhan Abaqa's Persia, whom the annals call Alawading and Isma'il".
- (b) "The Polo family's standard arrival at court falls at the end of 1274". Yule says only that they "*could not* have reached the Court… before the end of 1274"; the usual date is 1275.
  - Fix: "The Polos could not have reached the court before late 1274 at the very earliest, and are usually placed there in 1275, two years after the twin cities surrendered."

## 4. Illustration candidates

★ marks the hero image for each chapter. General period cues for c. 1275–92:
- **Dress:** Mongol elite in *jisun* single-colour robes, crossed left-over-right; *bocca* (boqta) headdress for married Mongol women; Han Chinese in round-collared robes.
- **Architecture:** Yuan timber halls on stone terraces; Song brick pagodas.
- **Arms:** Mongol composite bows; lamellar armour.
- **Scripts and badges:** 'Phags-pa script on paiza and banknotes after 1269.
- **Avoid:** Ming/Qing tell-tales (queues, Qing hats, the Ming Great Wall, the Beihai White Dagoba of 1651, Jingshan as "Coal Hill").

**ch-01**
- ★ **Kublai's elephant-tower at the Nayan battle.** Anchor: "borne by four well-trained elephants, and over him was hoisted his standard" (ch-01:25). Scene: the aged Kaan on a timber bartizan carried by four elephants on a Manchurian plain at dawn, with massed horse below and lancers riding pillion. Period: Kublai about 72, heavy and gouty; white yak-tail *tug* standard; lamellar armour; Liao-river steppe in summer 1287. Caution: the four-elephant tower and the troop numbers are Polo spectacle, so show a modest host.
- **Before the clash.** Anchor: "singing and playing on a certain two-stringed instrument of theirs" (:25). Scene: two hosts singing, *nakkara* kettledrums on horseback waiting to sound. Period: two-stringed fiddle of the *morin khuur* type; Persian-style naqqāra.
- **Bloodless execution.** Anchor: "he was wrapt in a carpet, and tossed to and fro" (:29). Treat obliquely (rolled felt, turned-away guards). Caution: the exact method is contested.
- **Gerfalcon paiza bestowed.** Anchor: "a tablet with gerfalcons on it" (:47). Base it on surviving Yuan gold and silver paiza (long rounded plaques, 'Phags-pa inscription).

**ch-02**
- ★ **Trees carried up the Green Mount.** Anchor: "he gets it carried by his elephants" (:37). Scene: an elephant hauling a root-balled evergreen up the artificial hill, green-tiled hall on top, lake below. Period: Qionghua island / Wansui shan in the Taiye lake; Yuan Guanghan Hall. Avoid the Qing White Dagoba. Caution: the elephant transplanting is Polo's vignette.
- **The Kaan's portrait.** Anchor: "His complexion is white and red; the eyes black and fine" (:13). Model on the Yuan court portrait of Kublai in the National Palace Museum (Taipei): white fur-trimmed robe, Mongol cap.
- **Chess-board city at curfew.** Anchor: "the whole city is arranged in squares just like a chess-board" (:47). Scene: a lantern-carrying midwife's messenger crossing an empty avenue at the third bell. Period: Dadu earth walls; bell tower (1272). Pair with map-cambaluc.jpg.

**ch-03**
- ★ **White Feast procession.** Anchor: "covered with rich and gay housings of inlaid cloth representing beasts and birds" (:35). Scene: a few elephants with paired coffers and camels before the hall, everyone in white, tribute laid out in nines. Period: Mongol *Chaghan sar*; white robes; Daming Hall terrace. Caution: "5,000 elephants" and "100,000 white horses" are inflated, so depict a handful.
- **Threshold guards.** Anchor: "a couple of big men like giants, one on each side, armed with staves" (:21). Scene: a foreign envoy being coached over the threshold. Period: *Chuogeng lu*-type guardsmen with clubs.
- **Muffled cupbearers.** Anchor: "mouth and nose muffled with fine napkins of silk and gold" (:23). Scene: barons kneeling as the Kaan drinks. Use the Liu Guandao court style for costume.

**ch-04**
- ★ **Cranes from the elephant litter.** Anchor: "Sire! Look out for Cranes!" (:33). Scene: the roof of the timber chamber thrown open, gerfalcon cast, barons riding alongside, Toscaol watchers in pairs. Period reference: Liu Guandao's *Kublai Khan Hunting* (1280) for faces, furs and the cheetah on a horse croup. Spring marshes south-east of Dadu.
- **Red and blue hound corps.** Anchor: "the one in red and the other in blue" (:21). Scene: a battue line of mastiffs a day wide. Caution: 10,000 men per brother is inflated.
- **Tiger hunt.** Anchor: "every Lion has a little doggie with him" (:15). Scene: a tiger in a covered cart with a small dog. Caution: label it a tiger.

**ch-05**
- ★ **Ahmad's assassination.** Anchor: "Achmath bowed before Vanchu, taking him for Chinkin; Chenchu cut off his head" (:21). Scene: a night hall lit by torches, a false prince on the dais, Cogatai bursting in. Caution: this is Ramusio's narrative, and the Chinese record has Wang Zhu strike with a bronze mace, so the caption should note both.
- **Printing paper money.** Anchor: "smears the Kaan's Seal with vermilion and impresses it in red" (:27). Period: surviving Zhiyuan/Zhongtong notes (grey mulberry-bark paper, Chinese plus 'Phags-pa text, vermilion seals).
- **Yam runner.** Anchor: "Each wears a wide belt set with bells" (:37). Scene: a runner at a relay fort handing a slip to a clerk.

**ch-06**
- ★ **Pulisanghin bridge.** Anchor: "a marble column stands upon a marble lion's loins" (:13). Scene: a caravan crossing the marble bridge with its lion-capped balustrade. Period: Jin-built 1189–92, 11 arches; many of today's lions are Ming/Qing re-carvings, so keep them archaic. Caution: Polo's 24 arches.
- **Roofed bridge market at Sindafu.** Anchor: "the bridge is roofed over from end to end with timber" (:43). Scene: morning stalls going up, the Kaan's *Comercque* toll-house.
- **Gallery road of Cuncun.** Anchor: "timber corbels in the rock" (:35). Plank road over a gorge in the Qinling.

**ch-07**
- ★ **Bamboo fires in the ravaged Tebet marches.** Anchor: "they burst asunder, and this makes so loud a report" (:15). Scene: a night camp, green canes exploding, horses hobbled and hooded, eyes of beasts in the dark. Period: eastern-Tibet borderland, Sichuan–Kham forest.
- **Salt-cake money.** Anchor: "salt which they boil and set in a mould" (:27). Scene: a merchant weighing gold against moulded salt loaves in a hill market.
- **The "serpent" trap.** Anchor: "each crowned with a sharp steel blade hidden under sand" (:39). Caution: draw a crocodile, not a two-legged dragon.

**ch-08**
- ★ **Battle of Vochan.** Anchor: "every man should dismount and tie his horse to the trees" (:25). Scene: Mongol archers on foot at a wood's edge loosing at war-elephants with timber castles, which are breaking into the trees. Period: 1277 Yongchang plain; Burmese elephant howdahs. Caution: show dozens of elephants, not 2,000.
- **Pagan towers.** Anchor: "girt all about with bells" (:41). Scene: gilded and silvered stupas with *hti* bell-canopies in sun. Caution: plating "a finger thick" is rhetoric, so paint them as gilded brick stupas.
- **Gold-for-silver market** on the long descent (:35).

**ch-09**
- ★ **Dogs and tiger on the Cuiju road.** Anchor: "two of them together will attack a lion" (:37). Scene: two great dogs worrying a tiger at bay against a tree while travellers draw bows. Caution: a tiger, not a lion.
- **Coloman cave burials.** Anchor: "placed in great caverns, where they are hung up" (:25). Visual parallel: cliff-hung burials of south-west China. Caution: the ethnic identification is uncertain.
- **Caugigu tattooing** (:13). Use Tai/Shan tattoo traditions.

**ch-10**
- ★ **The Song surrender to Bayan.** Anchor: "she gave herself up to Bayan, and surrendered to him the whole kingdom" (:61). Scene: Empress Dowager Xie's envoys presenting the seal to Bayan's camp outside Lin'an, 1276. Caution: the Book's "King flees by sea" is compressed. The child emperor Gong was surrendered; loyalists carried other princes to sea. Do not depict the "Hundred Eyes" prophecy as fact.
- **Changlu salt-earth works.** Anchor: "boil it well in great iron cauldrons" (:15). Diagram-friendly.
- **Sinjumatu summit split** (:33). Junks at the canal summit. Diagram-friendly.

**ch-11**
- ★ **Counterweight trebuchet at Xiangyang.** Anchor: "a stone was shot from each of them into the town" (:47). Scene: a counterweight engine across the Han from the walled twin cities, the stone in flight. Period: Persian-style *manjanīq* with a hinged box counterweight; Song city walls; winter 1272–73. Caution: credit the Muslim engineers. The Polos were not there, so do not show Venetians.
- **Causeway into Manzi.** Anchor: "you cannot enter the province except along this causeway" (:15). Scene: a stone-paved embankment between sheets of water near Gaoyou.
- Avoid any image of "Marco governing Yangzhou"; the claim is contested.

**ch-12**
- ★ **Trackers on the Kiang.** Anchor: "the tow-line, which is some three hundred paces in length, is made of nothing but cane" (:23). Scene: teams hauling a single-masted cargo junk upstream on split-bamboo hawsers, with Golden Island's monastery in mid-river. Period: Yuan river junks; Jinshan temple.
- **Grain fleet at Caiju** (:29). Canal mouth with embanked towpaths.
- **Suzhou bridge.** Anchor: "six thousand bridges, all of stone" (:59). One high-arched stone bridge over a canal. Caution: the count is formulaic.

**ch-13**
- ★ **West Lake pleasure barges.** Anchor: "pleasure barges holding ten, fifteen, twenty or more" (:67). Scene: polers on the roof-deck, a family party at open windows, the city shore of temples. Existing plate: `drawing-kinsay-west-lake.png`. Period: the brick Leifeng Pagoda existed (975); avoid Qing pavilions. Caution: label it Ramusio's description.
- **Fire alarm.** Anchor: "a man with a mallet beats upon the slab" (:29). Scene: a night fire, bridge-watchmen running, goods carried to stone store-towers.
- **Household registers.** Anchor: "writes over his door his own name" (:51). Scene: a street of doors with name boards.

**ch-14**
- ★ **Zayton harbour.** Anchor: "for one shipload of pepper that goes to Alexandria… there come a hundred" (:49). Scene: ocean junks unloading pepper sacks, customs clerks tithing, Arab and Tamil merchants. Period: the Quanzhou ship excavated in 1974 (c. 1270s; V-hull, 13 bulkheads); the stone twin pagodas of Kaiyuan temple (1228–50); the Qingjing mosque. Existing plate: `drawing-manzi-junk.png`. Caution: the 100:1 ratio is rhetoric.
- **Tattooists for Indian visitors.** Anchor: "have their bodies painted with the needle" (:55).
- **Silky fowls at Kelinfu.** Anchor: "fowls which have no feathers, but hair only" (:31). A light, accurate image (silkie chickens).

**ch-15**
- ★ **Aijaruc throws the prince.** Anchor: "threw him right valiantly on the palace pavement" (:23). Scene: a wrestling bout before Kaidu's court, with a thousand wagered horses outside. Period: Central Asian Mongol court tent or palace; sammet and sendal jerkins. Caution: a folkloric wager; the historical princess is Khutulun.
- **Dog-sledge post.** Anchor: "six dogs to a sledge, a bear-skin for the courier" (:39). A frozen-marsh relay.
- **Land of Darkness raid.** Anchor: "riding mares that have foals" (:41). Caution: legendary geography.

### Explanatory diagrams
- **Paper-money process** (ch-05): bark, then sheet, then cut by denomination, then officials' seals, then the vermilion imperial seal, then circulation, with 3% replacement and bullion purchase. Add the 1287 Zhiyuan 1:5 revaluation as a sidebar.
