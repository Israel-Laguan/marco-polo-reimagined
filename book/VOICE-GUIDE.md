# Voice Guide — Marco Polo Retelling

Use this for all chapter rewrites after the Genoa / House of Polo pilot (Vol. I, chs. 1–2).

## Core voice

Write as a **storyteller recounting what Marco Polo / *Il Milione* narrates** — not as an epic novelist inhabiting the Middle Ages, and not as a dry encyclopedia.

Typical openers and hinges:

- “Marco Polo starts by narrating that…”
- “The book itself opens by explaining…”
- “He says that…” / “According to the book…”
- “Next he mentions…” / “Then Polo describes…”
- “Tradition has it that…” (for frame material about how the book was made)
- “Anyway, next he mentions…” (after a short aside)

Tone: clear, oral, intelligent, readable. Warm storyteller. Correct, publishable English that still sounds spoken. Not slangy, not help-desk, not archaic pastiche, not high-literary third-person epic.

## Naming

- Book / medieval form first; modern equivalent in parentheses on first mention in a chapter (e.g. Bocara (Bukhara)).
- Prefer gazetteer book names thereafter (`/workspace/marco-polo/gazetteer.csv`).

## Inline dispute notes

When something is contested, inaccurate, second-hand, or exaggerated, **interrupt briefly in the flow**, then resume:

> …as he narrates [3] — though this is disputed; it may be a misremembering and it isn’t the center of the story — anyway, next he mentions…

Keep asides short and useful; don’t kill momentum. Do not dump all disputes into a separate “Reliability” table.

## Citations and Notes

1. Place **numbered citations** inline in the narrative: [1], [2], [3]…
2. After the narrative, a single **## Notes** section listing [1], [2], … with brief explanations (source / why disputed / comparative witness).
3. This **replaces** the old split of “Source notes” + “Reliability.”
4. You may still use markers inside Notes entries: ✓ corroborated · ~ plausible · ✗ likely wrong/exaggerated · ◇ second-hand · ? contested.

## Chapter shape

1. Title (+ optional short epigraph if it earns its keep)
2. Continuous storyteller narrative (~1,800–3,200 words; leaner is fine if clearer)
3. **## Notes** with numbered entries

No separate Reliability table. No “## Narrative” heading unless needed for tooling.

## Base text and honesty

- Working base: Yule–Cordier (PG / 1903–1920 tradition), eclectic toward the Geographic Old French line.
- Keep factual coverage from the outline beats; do not invent itinerary or quotations.
- Flag Ramusio, Pipino, Jacopo d’Acqui, and later embroidery honestly in asides + Notes — never silently absorb them as primary fact.
- Modern scholars by well-known positions (Yule, Larner, Benedetto, Moule–Pelliot, de Rachewiltz, etc.); no fabricated page numbers.

## What to avoid

- High-literary epic pastiche (“In the late summer of the year of Our Lord…”)
- Silent omniscient third-person that pretends to *be* the Book
- Long academic digressions mid-paragraph
- Help-desk phrasing (“In this chapter we will explore…”)
- Slang or jokey sarcasm that undercuts the material
