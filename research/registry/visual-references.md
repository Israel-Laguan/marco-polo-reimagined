## Exemplum, divination, bridge and shore camp · production findings, 23 November 2026

- **I-5 (Caliph in the treasure tower):** In the early French illuminated tradition, **BL Royal MS 19 D I** (Paris, c. 1333–1340, *Li Livres du Graunt Caam*), fol. 64r, depicts the walled city of Baudas above stylized river waves with inhabitants inside, and its English derivative **Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Bodl. 264** (London, c. 1410), fol. 222r, depicts Hulagu’s military siege and capture of Baudas; neither illustrates the treasure-tower starvation exemplum. **BnF MS fr. 2810** (*Livre des merveilles*, Paris, c. 1410–1412), **fol. 9r** (Omont 1907, Vol. I, pl. 12, *De la noblesce de la cité de Baudas, et de l'estat et du siege du calife*; Mandragore *Musta'sim Billâh enfermé par Hülegü*), specifically depicts the treasure-tower exemplum as a Gothic cutaway chamber filled with gold vessels and coin, while **fol. 10r** (Omont 1907, pl. 13) depicts the moving-mountain miracle. *Limit:* The French Gothic marvel-lens illustration reflects European reception of Polo’s exemplum rather than historical 1258 Baghdad or the Muslim accounts compared in Chapter I-5.
- **I-12 (Cane divination before Chinggis’s battle):** Church of the East clerics in Inner Asia used a Syriac liturgical psalter (attested by the Turfan/Bulayïq fragments, including Syriac psalter leaves in Syriac and Old Uyghur script; Latin liturgy reached the Mongol court only with John of Montecorvino from 1294). The 2026-11-19 master (`plates/I-12-cane-divination.png`, from `drafts/I-12/psalter-wash-composite-1.png`) replaces the earlier Latin Vulgate text with non-linguistic grey-brown wash bands and a vermilion rubric (`CORRECTION-I-12-2026-11-19.md`). *Limit:* The scene illustrates Polo’s Book theatre (which places the confrontation at Tenduc in 1200, whereas Toghrul’s historical defeat came at the Jeje'er Heights in 1203).
- **II-6 (Pulisanghin / Lugou Bridge):** Begun under Jin Emperor Shizong in **1189** and completed under Emperor Zhangzong in **1192** (1194 in Ament), with river-embankment repairs under Ming Jiajing (**1522–1566**). Both Gabriel de Magalhães (*Magaillans*, 1668/1688) and the late-16th-century Chinese account cited by W. S. Ament (*Yule–Cordier*, Bk II, ch. 35, Note 2, `book/refs/yule-vol2.txt`, lines 1363–1560) record **thirteen arches** before a flood on 25 July 1668 (Prospero Intorcetta in Bretschneider, *Peking*, pp. 50–54; printed 17 August 1688 in Magaillans) carried away two arches and collapsed the span. The Qing **Kangxi reconstruction of 1698** (repaired under Qianlong) established the extant **eleven-arch** bridge and its balustrade of ~140 lion-topped pilasters per side, whereas Polo (`2.35`) gives **twenty-four arches** with columns resting on the loins of lower lions as well as topped by lions. *Limit:* The eleven arches and single lion-topped posts in `plates/II-6-pulisanghin-bridge.png` reflect the post-1698 configuration rather than a documented Jin or Yuan elevation.
- **III-3 (Samara shore camp):** As documented in closed spike [`S-27`](../../editorial/plans/spikes/S-27-samara-shore-camp.md), the wooden posts cited in Oetomo and Surachman 2012 (*Berkala Arkeologi Sangkhakala* 15.1: 142–154) trace to the 2008 Puslitbang Arkenas test pits at **Lampoh Kota and Ulee Tanoh** (~25 cm circular black-soil post-moulds at **30–40 cm depth**, interpreted as decayed house piles) alongside the 2010 **Cot Astana** *Kotak I* collapsed brick enclosure wall (~15 courses / 75–100 cm collapse). *Limit:* Neither find carries an absolute date tied to c. 1290 or a shelter superstructure elevation; `plates/III-3-samara-camp.png` is placed with an explicit caption distinguishing `F`/`V`’s wooden defences from `Z`/Ramusio’s ditches, five towers, and ~2,000 men, and identifying the pictured shelters, vessels, and dress as conjectural. Remaining held drafts are tracked in [`S-28`](../../editorial/plans/spikes/S-28-queued-plate-comparanda.md).
