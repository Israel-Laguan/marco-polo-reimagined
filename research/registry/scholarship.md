## The presence debate

**Where it stands.** Several recent surveys accept Polo’s journey; the searches performed do not establish unanimity. Three surveys report: Orlando (`orlando2025novita`, read) says the replies to Wood re-established the journey beyond doubt; Ma (`ma2025chinese`, read) says Polo "most likely did reach China"; Gadrat-Ouerfelli (`gadrat2026marco`, summary only) says most researchers accept the travels while not trusting every passage. No sceptical book or article after 2013 was found. Wood's book was reissued in 2018 (`wood2018did`, record). Ma reports that at Tianjin in 2000 Wood "neither denied Marco Polo's presence in China nor advanced any new doubts".

**What the case rests on.** Jackson reports that the Chinese and Persian embassy notices at issue do not name the Polos. Yang Zhijiu related the Chinese notice of three envoys to Polo’s narrative; Cleaves discussed it alongside a Persian record of their arrival. Ma calls this direct confirmation, but that is her interpretation; the notices do not directly identify the travellers. The embassy’s dating and the connection to the Polos remain interpretations (F-15; S20-E40). Around it the evidence is cumulative: Vogel's money, salt and revenue (`vogel2013marco`; reviewed in `atwood2015review`; Chinese translation 2022), and since then a run of place-name and institution studies by Haw (`haw2020hangzhou`, `haw2020mangi`, `haw2020overview`, `haw2021orthography`, `haw2023units`), Atwood (`atwood2016toponyms`, `atwood2020three`), Dang Baohai (`dang2024toponyms`) and Luo Wei (`luo2024designations`).

**The frame has changed.** Ma's formula is the one to borrow: the Book is "a complex product of personal memory, co-authorship, and medieval literary conventions". The question is no longer "did he go?" but "what in this layered text is his, and from when?".

**For the book.** III-12 can describe the responses to Wood and attribute the checked survey positions; `gadrat2026marco` has since been read in full (§§1–23, 2026-11-17). It should keep Wood's case stated in full (it is what a reader has heard of) and add that the doubt is older than Wood (see *The silences*). Before the rewrite, read `haw2024anachronisms`: only its title is known.

## Q1. The stemma and the "original" — moved

- **Agreed:** the first text was Franco-Italian, made with Rustichello in Genoa in 1298 (`andreose2024textual`, read). Wehr's Venetian original is rejected. F belongs with over thirty French manuscripts copied in Genoa by Pisan prisoners (raw 2.2): a fact for I-01.
- **The stemma:** Benedetto's two branches are "inadequate". Burgio and Eusebi (2008; `burgio2008nuova`, via) put Z alone on one branch and everything else on the other, with F four copies below the archetype. Andreose (2024): the data do "not permit us to establish a definitive classification".
- **Several redactions (Venice school):** V is the first step of Polo's own rewriting (`simion2019devisement`, record); Z is "a second redaction … prepared in Venice, probably in a Dominican environment, on the basis of notes and perhaps oral memoirs of the traveller" (`mascherpa2025milione`, record); Andreose counts "at least four stages" in Z. Proof of a late layer: the Toqta–Noqai passage, an event of autumn 1299 (raw 2.5).
- **One redaction:** the extra passages are original and in Rustichello's style. This is Benedetto's model: Z translates a text close to the Genoa original, which was later pruned into the ancestor of everything else (`mascherpa2018fonte`, read). Andreose cites for it Casella (1929), Benedetto (1930), Peretti (1930), Terracini (1933), Pasquali (1952) and Barbieri (2004), and judges its arguments "certainly compelling, but far from being satisfactory" (`andreose2024textual`, read). No current scholar defending it against the Venice school was found (follow-up).
- **A third, small group** denies that some or all of the additions are Polo's and treats them as late interpolations: Dieu (1947), Reichert (1997), as Andreose reports.
- **For the book:** rewrite open question 1. "Can the original be recovered?" becomes "which layer is which?". Say that the Venice editors are reconstructing the contents, not one wording (`simion2024integral`, read).

## Q2. The four languages — moved, now disputed

- **Persian, and neither Chinese nor Mongolian:** Luo Wei (`luo2024designations`, read), citing Yule, Shao Xunzheng, Yang Zhijiu and Cai Meibiao.
- **Not even Persian is proven:** Haw: "there is no clear evidence that Marco Polo learned Persian" (`haw2020hangzhou`, abstract; the fuller case is `haw2014persian`).
- **Some Chinese:** Atwood (`atwood2016toponyms`), as Gadrat-Ouerfelli reports him (via).
- **For the book:** III-12 must not say "Persian was his working language" as settled. Name the three positions.

## Q3. Hormuz on the way out — unchanged

Nothing since 2013 was found, by the main run or by the follow-up in two more tools. Qiu Zhirong (`qiu2021persia`, via) treats the Persian road on the way home, not the outbound turn. The one new fact is textual: a late stage of Z re-orders the chapters on the plain between Qamadin and Hormuz (`mascherpa2018fonte`, read). **For the book:** keep the present Note in I-07; label the turn-back as Yule's reading (he has the Polos "abandon this project and turn north again from Hormuz"). Sykes's different road into Persia is in Cordier's note and can be cited from `yc`.

## Q4. Yangzhou — unchanged; the question has moved to Polo's status

No Chinese record of an office has appeared. The 2024 volume keeps its distance: "governor" in inverted commas (`theobald2024military`, read), "is said to have governed" (Kinoshita's note in `vogel2024marco`, read). Three positions on what Polo was:

| Position | Proponents | Rests on | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| An *ortoq* ("partner") merchant with privileged capital; this is why no official record names him | Cai Meibiao (1992); Chen Dezhi (2009); Rong Xinjiang (2024 interview) | the parallel of the Uyghur sea-merchant Yighmish | `ma2025chinese` (read); `cai1992shilun` (via) |
| A member of the keshig, the Great Kaan's household guard | Haw (2006; restated 2024) | Jean le Long's *miles* read as "knight"; Polo's knowledge of the guard; the archery passage in one redaction | `haw2024kesig` (read) |
| An official rising in the Sino-Mongol service (the Book's own picture) | kept by the Venetian historians as what the Book claims | the Book | `bolognari2025pratiche` (abstracts) |

**For the book:** II-11 keeps "the Book says he governed". III-12 replaces "what office?" with the three positions and says none has a document. Whether any manuscript reads "stayed" for "governed" was not checked (raw, unresolved 2).

## Q5. The Ahmad affair — moved, settled

- The *Yuan shi* "Boluo" is the Mongol Bolod: the identification with Marco "was soon rejected by Feng and subsequent scholars" (`ma2025chinese`, read). This supports `facts.md` F-08 with a source that was read.
- Ramusio's two assassins are titles, not names: Cenchu = *qianhu* (commander of a thousand), Vanchu = *wanhu* (of ten thousand); the men are Wang Zhu and Zhang Yi. Cai Meibiao (1998), after Shao Xunzheng and Pelliot; Luo Wei calls the reading "beyond question" (`luo2024designations`, read).
- Luo turns it into an argument for presence: hearsay in Venice could not have kept such titles straight.
- Luo also says Ramusio is the only version that tells the murder "minutely". This does not settle F-09 (is it in Z?).
- **For the book:** strike this from the open questions. II-05 gains a Note on the two titles.

## Q6. Yunnan, Burma, Bengal — unchanged

Fang Guoyu and Lin Chaomin's monograph (printed 1994) and Li Xinyu (2021) on the date of the Yunnan mission exist (`ma2025chinese`, read); none was seen. **For the book:** no change.

## Q7. Champa on the return — unchanged

Nothing new was found, by the main run or the follow-up. What the base text gives: the Book puts Marco in Champa in a stated year, and the manuscripts differ (1285 in the Geographic Text; 1280 in Pauthier and Ramusio; 1288 in the Geographic Latin). Yule prefers 1288 and joins it to the mission to the Indian seas from which Marco came back about 1290 (`yc`). No scholar was found arguing that the fleet of 1291 called there. **For the book:** III-02 can say that the Book dates his visit before the voyage home, by Yule's reading, and leave the return call as unknown.

## Q8. Madagascar or Mogadishu — moved

Andreose (`andreose2024textual`, pp. 76–77, reread 2026-11-17) argues that the passage refers to Mogadishu and that *Madeigascar* is a corruption; Z reads *Mogdaxo* and V has the intermediate *Madaschor*. F's chapter index has *Mogclasio*, which he interprets as a misreading of *Mogdasio*. He also asserts a connection with the later island name. **For the book:** attribute these as Andreose's textual and historical reconstruction; the witnesses and Pelliot were not independently checked in this pass. Avoid saying simply that Z is the sole variant or treating the naming history as independently demonstrated.

## Q9. Where Book IV came from — moved a little

- The Toqta–Noqai passage is in Z alone and reports autumn 1299 (raw 2.5): Golden Horde news was still being added in Venice.
- Qiu Zhirong (`qiu2021persia`, via Ma): in Persia in 1293–94 Polo learned that Temür was named heir and left before news of Qubilai's death (February 1294) came. That explains why the Book never knows the Kaan is dead.
- Chen Xi (2021) on the date of Qaidu's war: not read.
- **For the book:** II-15 and III-09 may use the Qiu point as "a Chinese study argues", after it is seen.

## Q10. Ramusio-only passages — moved

Mascherpa (`mascherpa2024quinsai`, read) argues that Ramusio used Z, P, VB, L and V without contamination from outside texts, and that his long Quinsai additions derive from a Z-related source richer than the Toledo copy. These are source reconstructions. Andreose adds that Ramusio's model may hold further authorial variants. Qiu Zhirong: Ramusio keeps "some authentic data" with "obvious editorial amplifications" (via Ma). The two articles behind this, both open access and read in the follow-up: Mascherpa (`mascherpa2018fonte`) proposes that Polo or his family kept notes and memories of Quinsai, used only in part in 1298 and later worked into one or more Z copies, with or without his supervision (*con o senza la sua supervisione*); Andreose (`andreose2018giovanetto`, abstract) concludes that the additions are "the product of Marco Polo's reworking of his book". **For the book:** retain the "Ramusio-only." label for the textual layer. Attribute Mascherpa’s fuller Z-related exemplar and family-notes reconstruction to him, not as a demonstrated lost physical manuscript or proven Polo authorship; he also discusses an interpolation alternative (S20-E34).

## Q11. The Kinsay figures — moved a little

Mascherpa (`mascherpa2024quinsai`, p. 138, reread 2026-11-16) sets these counts beside the Book's: 347 bridges, not 12,000; a circuit of 35 *li*, about 20 miles, not 100; West Lake 6 miles round, not 30. His three explanations: sources misread, copying, or deliberate praise in the manner of a city eulogy. He takes the Book's Chinese "writing" to be the Song empress's dispatch of 1276. Wang Yidan (2019) tests the bridges against Persian texts (via Ma). **For the book:** attribute these figures to Mascherpa; the original Chinese sources, dates and boundaries remain unverified. Pochmalicki separately reports Moule's 347 as including 117 in the city and 230 in the environs, but Moule was not read for that claim. The 2026 return checked a different 2017 Mascherpa article and does not refute the 2024 attribution; see [intake](../investigations/results/S-17-priority-return-2026-11-16-intake.md).

## Q12. Readership before print — moved

`gadrat2024read` was read in the indexed open-access PDF on 2026-11-17, pp. 201–211 (argument) and 212–214 (references); underlying inventories and manuscripts were not independently inspected. Gadrat-Ouerfelli identifies manuscript ownership among merchants and diverse courtly, clerical and scholarly audiences, but stresses that ownership does not establish reading or motive. In her assessment the surviving merchant evidence does not demonstrate commercial use (pp. 205, 208–209). She also describes the Book's use in maps, histories and religious works (pp. 209–211). Her `gadrat2015lire` monograph and the works `gadrat2022dominicans` and `conte2020consolationem` remain unread in this pass. **For the book:** cite the 2024 chapter for the qualified reception picture, without claiming that every medieval reader believed Polo or that merchants used the work as a practical guide. See the [source check](../investigations/results/S-13-open-access-source-check-2026-11-17.md).

## The date of departure — unchanged

Yang Zhijiu's conclusion as Ma reports it: "at the very end of 1290 or the first days of 1291". The notice is of the eighth lunar month of 1290 and names Uludai, Abishqa and Qoja "together with 160 companions"; the Persian source records Qoja's arrival with the princess (`ma2025chinese`, read). No one was found arguing for 1292. **Source name, settled:** the *Yongle dadian*, as F-15 has it; Lin and Zhang (`lin2017chinese`, summary) say so and cite Yang and Ho (1945) and Cleaves (1976), and they too give late 1290 or very early 1291. Ma's "in the *Yuanshi*" is a slip. **Not yet seen:** what Cleaves, Vogel and Haw each say; Haw has a whole article on it (`haw2023departure`, record only). One follow-up tool's "June/July 1291" has no source and is not to be used. **For the book:** F-15’s hedge stands: attribute Yang’s late-1290/early-1291 inference and distinguish the 1290 preparation notice from the sailing; Yule proposed early 1292.

## The silences — unchanged, better documented

Huang Shijian answered each: tea (1993, 1994), the Great Wall (with Gong Yingyan; `huang1999great`, via), foot-binding (2006, 2011). Ma's summary: the "missing" things "were not prominent in Yuan China". New for III-11: the doubt is old. John Green pointed to the silence on the Great Wall in 1747, and the Jesuit Gabriel de Magalhães had already listed five proofs of Polo's presence in 1688 (`gadrat2026marco`, summary; Antonucci's chapter on Magalhães in `mocella2025milione`, record). **For the book:** III-11 gains a short "doubters before Wood" passage once Gadrat-Ouerfelli 2026 is read directly (it is open access).
