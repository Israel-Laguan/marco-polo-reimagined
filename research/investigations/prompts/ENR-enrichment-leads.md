BATCH A — Acre, the Venice–Genoa wars, Baybars and the Genoese prison (1255–1298)

I am preparing a new English retelling of Marco Polo's book (base text: Yule–Cordier, 1903, with Cordier's 1920 Addenda) for print. The endnotes, asides and captions use short, vivid facts, each tied to a source a reader can check. Earlier research runs gave me the leads below. Some are cited only through Wikipedia or an unnamed source. For each one, I need a citable scholarly source, preferably one I can open for free, and a verdict.

LEADS TO CHECK
1. (E31) Acre in 1271, when the Polos left it for the East: was the city still divided after the War of St Sabas? Was the Genoese quarter restored only in 1288? In the war, did Pisa change to Venice's side in 1257, did the Templars and Teutonic Knights back Venice while the Hospitallers backed Genoa, did Lorenzo Tiepolo break the harbour chain in 1257, and did he beat Rosso della Turca in June 1258? The lead names David Jacoby, in The Experience of Crusading, vol. 2 (2003), and Martin da Canal's Estoires de Venise. Give the exact Jacoby chapter title and its DOI if it has one.
2. (E32) The chain of Venice–Genoa naval battles from St Sabas to Curzola (1298), where tradition says Marco was taken prisoner: Settepozzi (1263), Trapani (1266), the "Tower of Flies" at Acre (1267), and any I am missing. For each, give the date, the place, the winner and one scholarly source (e.g. Lane, Venice: A Maritime Republic; Epstein, Genoa and the Genoese; Caro, Genua und die Mächte am Mittelmeer). Say whether Marco's capture at Curzola rests on Ramusio alone, and who doubts it.
3. (E33) The porphyry Tetrarchs and the "pilastri acritani" at St Mark's. Yule says that four porphyry figures came from St Sabas at Acre. Marilyn Perry, "Saint Mark's Trophies" (JWCI 40, 1977), and later work send them to Constantinople in 1204. What does the 2023 Pyrenae article (vol. 54.2, doi:10.1344/pyrenae2023.vol54num2.7, "El relieve de Nicomedia (Izmit) y los 'Tetrarcas' de Venecia") argue? Who is its author? Does it date the Tetrarchs to Constantine and place them at the Philadelpheion? What is the evidence that the pilastri came from Hagios Polyeuktos (Harrison's excavation, the inscriptions)? Is the foot fragment found in Istanbul (1960s) still accepted as the Tetrarchs' missing piece?
4. (E38) Baybars's death in 1277. The lead: he drank a cup of poisoned kumiz meant for someone else (al-Malik al-Qāhir?), according to al-Yūnīnī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Maqrīzī. Hans-Ulrich Kühn, Sultan Baibars und seine Söhne (V&R unipress, 2019), has a chapter "Qalāwūn als Mörder von Sultan Baibars? – Eine These". Set out each version (illness, kumiz excess, the mistaken poison cup, Qalāwūn's guilt), each with its primary source and the modern historian who accepts or rejects it (Thorau 1992; Holt; Amitai; Kühn).
5. (E39) Hulagu's army at the Terek in the winter of 1262–63: was it defeated by Nogai, Berke's kinsman? What was Nogai's exact relation to Berke (nephew, grandnephew, cousin)? Give the date and one scholarly source (e.g. Jackson, The Mongols and the West; Vásáry; Favereau, The Horde, 2021).
6. (E25) The Genoese prison as a place of writing. After Meloria (1284), Pisan prisoners in Genoa copied and wrote manuscripts there. Alvise Andreose (2015, in the journal Francigena 1) is said to link the language of the Devisement dou monde to this milieu without saying that Rustichello was himself taken at Meloria. Confirm the article: its exact title, issue, pages and URL. Name other scholars of the Pisan prisoners' manuscripts (e.g. Fabrizio Cigni; the Genoa codices).
7. (E58) Rialfri, the online repertory of Franco-Italian literature: its URL, who runs it, and what it says of Rustichello's Arthurian compilation and of the language of the F text.

SOURCES TO PREFER: David Jacoby's articles on Acre; Lane (1973); Epstein (1996); Caro (1895–99, open on Internet Archive); Martin da Canal (ed. Limentani); the Annales Ianuenses; Perry (1977); Otto Demus; R. M. Harrison on Saraçhane; Peter Thorau; P. M. Holt; Reuven Amitai; Kühn (2019); Peter Jackson; Marie Favereau; Alvise Andreose; Fabrizio Cigni; Rialfri.

BOOK SECTIONS (use these IDs): I-01 (Genoa prison), I-03 (the second setting forth, Acre 1271), I-F1 (front matter: St Sabas, porphyry caption), I-02 / II-15 (Nogai, Berke), CHR (character registry: Baybars, Nogai), E25, E31, E32, E33, E38, E39, E58.
