# S-21 — remaining selected-source checks

Use this stricter prompt only where a stronger claim is desired. The specific Socotra barrel-float question has now been answered at scholarly-transcription level and should not be presented as an absence.

```text
Perform a strictly primary-source-driven audit for a Marco Polo retelling.
Do not claim you inspected an original if you only saw a quotation or metadata.
Give direct source images or text, exact page/folio, archival identifier,
transcription and cautious translation. Mark inaccessible sources as such.

1. Ceylon shark-charmers: Tamara Fernando (2022), “Seeing Like the Sea,” says
1856; Kunz and Stevenson (1908), The Book of the Pearl, pp. 115–116, say 1885.
Consult Fernando's note 121, Ostroff's Beds of Empire p. 133, and dated Ceylon
administrative records. Also check the actual passage and sourcing in Natasha
Eaton (2016), “In Search of Pearlescence,” DOI 10.1177/1359183515624453.
Determine whether the dates concern a legal ban, public payroll, private
practice or different events; do not infer a reconciliation without records.

2. Historical Yunnan crocodilians: inspect Wen Huan-ran, He Yeheng, Huang
Zhujian et al. (1980), Journal of East China Normal University: Natural Science
3, pp. 109–121, on historical crocodile distribution. Map each useful record
to date, place, textual/physical evidence and historical and modern taxonomic
identifications. The unrelated Wen 1980 forestry study pp. 1–44 is not a
crocodilian source. Do not extrapolate from modern range or a distant fossil.

3. Kerman turquoise: inspect Gerd Weisgerber (2004), “The turquoise mines at
Nishapur,” in Persian Antiques Splendor, pp. 502–509, which Shirdam et al.
2021 cite. Does the chapter actually name Shahr-i Babak, Polo or 1390–1430,
and what does each date refer to? Polo's 1298 composition does not disprove
a possible later manuscript date. If the chapter cannot be read, say so.

4. Optional facsimile-level Socotra check: Burgio and Fornasiero, “Il terzo
libro” (2011), p. 133 nn. 19–20, quote Ramusio III.34.2 using bottesella for
the line float and botte for oil, compare Z 123.19–20 boticula/asser, and cite
F CLXXXIX.3. Check the actual 1559 print and named F/Z manuscripts if
available and record their folios. Do not treat that edition's reported hunt
as an independent observation by Polo.

Return for each proposition: verdict; source and directly inspected locator;
evidence type; uncertainty and opposing evidence; and safe manuscript wording.
Supply a full bibliography and a list of inaccessible sources. Do not invent
quotes, dates, page numbers, names, archival references or witness readings.
```
