## 1. Champa

Sources and access:
- **Yuan shi j.210, Zhancheng.** Full text read in the Nagoya University transcription, which states the Zhonghua punctuated edition as its base.
- **Yuan shi j.129, Sogetu and Baijianu.** Full text read on Wikisource, with collation notes.
- **Yuan shi j.13, annals 1284–85.** Full text read in a simplified-character transcription (gushiwen), edition unstated.
- **Yuan shi j.11, annals 1280–81.** Search extract only.

**1278 expedition under "Sagatu": contradicted.** Under Zhiyuan 15 (1278), j.210 has only a diplomatic contact: Sogetu sent a man to Champa, who reported that the king was inclined to submit; a tiger tally, a title and the rank "Prince of Champa" were granted. Seen: 「還言其王…有內附意，詔降虎符，授榮祿大夫，封占城郡王」. The j.11 extract carries an investiture entry as well, which also sets up the Champa branch secretariat with Sogetu as its Assistant of the Right; its month and year were not visible to me.

**Course of the campaign: supported, with different dates.**
- **Late 1282:** a province was ordered set up in Champa in the 10th month of Zhiyuan 19; the king's son resisted and envoys to Siam and Ma'bar were seized, so troops were sent; they sailed from Guangzhou in the 11th month.
- **Early 1283:** the assault on the stockade came on the night of the 15th of the 1st month of Zhiyuan 20; the king burned his stores and fled to the hills, then strung out surrender talks while his men killed the detained envoys.
- **Later 1283:** a column sent inland was cut off and fought its way back.
- **1284:** Sogetu led the army back on the 6th of the 3rd month of Zhiyuan 21.
- **Limitation:** Sogetu's biography is far more triumphant, claiming 1,000 ships, 50,000 enemy killed or drowned, 60,000 heads, and "Champa surrendered". j.210 speaks of several thousand killed and drowned. Treat the biography's figures as panegyric.

**Withdrawal "out of pity" after the king's offer: contradicted.**
- **Sequence:** Sogetu left in the 3rd month of 1284. Only in the 8th month did the king ask for Sogetu's army to be recalled, promising yearly tribute of local products and presenting three elephants.
- **How the court saw it:** the annals call the force "Sogetu's routed army".
- **Kublai's response:** he ordered Prince Toghon against Champa in the 7th month of 1284, an expedition that, per j.210, did not go through.

**Submission: partly supported.** After Sogetu had gone, the king told the relief force that Sogetu had stripped his country bare, that he had nothing to offer, and that he would send gifts and his heir next year; a grandson then brought a letter of submission (12th of the 4th month, 1284). The king never came in person in any passage I read.

**Tribute of twenty elephants a year: NOT FOUND.** What the Yuan shi records:
- **1280:** "local products" with a letter of submission (2nd month, Zhiyuan 17). A Siku-edition extract adds an embassy from Champa and Ma'bar with precious goods, rhinoceros and elephant; its date was not verified.
- **1283:** hostage gifts of 200 bolts of cloth, silver ingots and a gold-leaf spear.
- **1284:** three elephants in the 8th month; gifts and two elephants in the 11th month.
- **1285:** ten musicians, medicines and crocodile skins, sent jointly with Zhenla (9th month, Zhiyuan 22).
- **A parallel, not a source:** the same chapter records a 1297 Burmese offer of twenty tame elephants a year, after Polo had left China.

**Death of Sogetu: supported; date and place disputed inside the Yuan shi.**
- **Annals:** ordered back toward Wuli in the 5th month of Zhiyuan 22 (1285), pursued by Annamese troops and killed.
- **His biography:** narrated under Zhiyuan 21 and placed at the "Qianman river".
- **His son's biography:** Zhiyuan 22.
- **Qing criticism:** Zhao Yi already flagged the inconsistencies and concluded he died in Jiaozhi, not Champa (extract only).

**Modern studies.** Maspero was NOT SEEN. Wikipedia reports him as dating the invasion to 1282, with an embassy on 6 October 1285 after Sogetu's death; that matches the annals' 9th-month entry but is second-hand. The only Wade work found was a paper on the Ming shi account of Champa, not opened; Momoki was NOT FOUND.

**Polo's own text.** I did not check the manuscript readings (F, Z, Ramusio) for the date or the elephant number. From memory, unverified, the date of Marco's own visit varies between manuscripts.

**Cautious wording.** "Chinese records know no expedition in 1278. In that year Sogetu opened relations and the king was invested as a Yuan prince. The invasion ran from late 1282 to March 1284 and ended in an unvictorious withdrawal. Later in 1284 the king asked for the army's recall, promised annual tribute and sent three elephants. Sogetu was killed in Vietnam in 1285. No tribute of twenty elephants is recorded. Polo's account appears to compress these events." The last sentence is my inference, not a source statement.

## 2. Java

Sources and access:
- **Yuan shi j.210, Zhaowa.** Full text read (Nagoya).
- **Yuan shi j.162, Shi Bi and Gao Xing.** Full text read (gushiwen, simplified).
- **Yuan shi j.17, annals 1292–93.** Full text read (same site).
- **Yuan shi j.131, Ike Mese.** Extract only.
- **Nagarakrtagama canto 44.** One line seen in an unsourced Wikimedia Incubator transcription.
- **Pararaton.** NOT SEEN.
- **Bade 2013.** Chapter list and abstracts only.

**Meng Qi: supported in substance; the name and date are weak.**
- **The envoy:** j.210 names him only by title. Kublai's justification is 「後刺詔使孟右丞之面，以此進討」 ("they later cut the face of the envoy, Assistant of the Right Meng; hence this punitive attack").
- **The name:** it appears only in Gao Xing's biography, 「爪哇黥使者孟琪」 ("Java tattooed the envoy Meng Qi"). Wade's translation of a later Qing chronicle has "Meng Hong".
- **The injury:** the verbs are 刺 and 黥, so "branded or tattooed on the face", not loss of nose or ears.
- **The date:** no date for the mission appears in any passage I read; "1289" is modern convention that I could not trace to a primary text.
- **Who did it:** the Yuan shi passages do not name Kertanagara as the one responsible. That attribution, in Bade and others, is modern interpretation.

**Commanders: supported.** Shi Bi, Ike Mese and Gao Xing were all made pingzhang of Fujian. The annals give the division of labour: Ike Mese for sea matters, Shi Bi for military command.

**Fleet size: disputed inside the Yuan shi.**
- **j.210:** 20,000 men and 1,000 ships.
- **Annals:** 20,000 men and "500 ships large and small".
- **Annals, 4th month of 1293:** the Bureau of Military Affairs says 20,000 were paid but "afterwards only 5,000 went".
- **Shi Bi's biography:** "with 5,000 men, joined with the other armies".
- **Inscription:** a Gelam Island inscription reading "500 ships" is quoted on Chinese Wikipedia; I did not see the stone or an edition.

**Politics: supported, with a Chinese–Javanese difference.**
- **Yuan shi:** the ruler of Java (Haji Kertanagara) had already been killed by the ruler of Gelang (Haji Katang, i.e. Jayakatwang); his son-in-law Tuhan Pijaya (Raden Wijaya) had failed against Gelang and fallen back on Majapahit, and offered submission and maps in return for help.
- **Nagarakrtagama:** it says only that Wijaya, together with the Tatars, destroyed Jayakatwang, and in the lines seen is silent on turning against them.
- **Kertanagara's death:** the date 1292 rests on the Gajah Mada inscription (Śaka 1214), known to me only via Wikipedia.

**Outcome: supported, with two conflicts.**
- **Sequence:** Daha fell on the 19th of the 3rd month of Zhiyuan 30 and Jayakatwang surrendered. Wijaya was sent home with a 200-man escort on the 2nd of the 4th month and turned on the 19th. The army left on the 24th.
- **Jayakatwang's fate:** Gao Xing's biography says he and his son were executed; j.210 says only that his family and officials were carried off.
- **Punishments:** the annals have all three commanders beaten and fined a third of their property; Gao Xing's biography says he alone was rewarded with 50 liang of gold.

**"About 3,000 dead": attested once.** Shi Bi's biography, after the 68-day voyage home: 「達泉州，士卒死者三千餘人」 ("reached Quanzhou; more than 3,000 officers and men had died").
- **Scope:** the figure is given on arrival, so it covers the whole expedition, not the retreat alone.
- **Elsewhere:** j.210 gives no total and names only three officers killed.
- **Denominator:** unknown, whether 5,000 or 20,000.
- **Javanese side:** I found no Javanese figure.

**Cautious wording.** "According to Shi Bi's biography in the Yuan shi, more than 3,000 of the expedition's men had died by the time it reached Quanzhou. The same history gives the force as 20,000 men in 500 or 1,000 ships, while elsewhere implying that only 5,000 sailed. The envoy, called Meng Qi in one biography, had been branded or tattooed on the face. The Chinese record says Kertanagara was already dead when the fleet arrived."

## Unresolved, and the safe fallback

- **Printed locators.** Every Yuan shi passage needs a page reference from the Zhonghua edition or a page image; Harvard hosts a scan of the 1623 Nanjing print that I did not open.
- **Meng Qi.** His identity with the 孟祺 of Yuan shi j.160, and the 1289 date, are unverified.
- **Fleet numbers.** The Yuan wenlei (Jingshi dadian) version of the Java account, which web pages quote for the fleet figures, was not read.
- **Javanese texts.** The Pararaton and kidung accounts should be checked in Bade's translations (pp. 249–259 for the Pararaton).
- **Champa scholarship.** Maspero's pages on 1278–85 remain to be read.
- **Polo manuscripts.** The readings for the date and the elephant number are unchecked.

Fallback: use the two wordings above and attribute every number to the Yuan shi chapter that carries it.
