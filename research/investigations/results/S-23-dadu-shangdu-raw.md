# Deep dive: Dadu and Shangdu as excavated (《大元三都》 2016; 《元大都：1964~1974年考古报告》 2024; Shangdu)

## 1. Period fit
Dadu was begun in 1267; Shangdu (Kaiping) was renamed in 1263 [C]. The remains are the buildings Polo saw, except where noted (the Dadu barbicans are 1359).

## 3. Research trail
The Yuan Dadu team (Institute of Archaeology, CASS, with the Beijing cultural relics team), 1964–74, led by 徐苹芳; report compiled chiefly by 蒋忠义, published March 2024 (publisher not stated in [A]). Shangdu: 魏坚 and the Inner Mongolia institute; 2025 season under 宋国栋. Palace Museum archaeology: 徐海峰.

## 4. Criticism
None of the pages criticises the findings. My own caution: [C] gives the textual height of the Da'an Pavilion, not an excavated one; the "round base = cane palace" reading is the excavator's.

## 5. Takeaways
1. **Shangdu's audience hall was a transplanted Song pavilion**, tall, timber, on a platform at the centre of a brick-faced palace city, with white marble dragon-carved corner pillars. This serves I-3 and I-13 and agrees with the plan's "timber hall on a platform".
2. **The cane palace was round**, like a great tent, and stood outside the palace in the outer city's western park. I-13 should not draw it as a rectangular hall.
3. **Dadu's walls were bare rammed earth and its gates had no barbican** in Polo's time. Add to the avoid list beside the Forbidden City: no brick-faced walls, no Ming barbican gates.
4. **Roof glaze from the objects so far is green (Shangdu temple) and peacock blue (Dadu palace).** The plan's "green-tiled hall" for II-2 fits. Yellow rests on Polo's words, not on a find.
5. Dadu was a grid of broad avenues and regular lanes with courtyard houses of every size; rich houses held lacquer, agate game pieces and inkstones. Useful for any Dadu street secondary.

## 6. Not resolved
