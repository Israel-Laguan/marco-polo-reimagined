# S-20 — fourth-pass reception intake (2026-11-13)

[The supplied Consensus dossier](S-20-fourth-pass-raw.md) is preserved verbatim. It is mostly a repetition of material already checked in the [third-pass intake](S-20-third-pass-intake.md), not a new solution to the remaining questions. It accurately leaves Shawcross's fol. 66v dating and Carlsson's page unverified, but its citations are unreliable enough to require claim-by-claim checking. Its “not found” entries describe its own corpus, not the state of research in this project.

| Topic | Assessment and access |
|---|---|
| **Mismatched citations** | The dossier attaches Grčić's article on the Balkans in Fra Mauro's map to Gadrat's purported Ridewall dating; Beal's Columbus/Pipino article to the identification of a Dominican manuscript image; and Garkavets's article to the precise 2015 online publication date of the Ramusio edition. Its timeline repeats some of these substitutions. Those citations do not establish those claims; do not treat a cited title as evidence merely because it appears nearby. Its metadata also omits **NEW** on some post-2013 items and supplies no BibTeX. |
| **Already checked** | The 1323 text is critically edited by **Marcello Bolognari** in the collection edited by Conte, Montefusco and Simion; see [third-pass intake](S-20-third-pass-intake.md). That edition identifies Marco Paulo as the traveller, but the act alone does not prove the origin of the Z tradition. Conte's image interpretation is already registered at S20-E21. O'Doherty's Cimpangu label and scholarly transcriptions of *2442/*2306 are already at S20-E32–E33. Simion herself reports the 4 Feb 2015 digital Ramusio date at S20-E36. The Portugal-map identity remains disputed; the dossier's blanket description of a “lost copy” must not override F-20. |
| **Columbus classmarks and dating** | [Shawcross 2023, p. 377 n. 2](https://www.academia.edu/116101732/Teresa_Shawcross_Materiality_and_Marginalia_Across_the_World_The_Role_of_Things_in_Christopher_Columbus_s_Annotations_on_Marco_Polo_Medieval_History_Journal_2023_376_424), author-posted opening previously read here, gives 10-3-2 and former V-117-13 and says Hernando appears to have initially numbered it 1069 and then 2741. **Retain the uncertainty** on the first Hernando number. Her disputed p. 380 n. 12 was not accessible here or supplied by this pass; its annotation, hands and dating remain open. The excerpt does not by itself prove when the book was acquired or carried. |
| **An attributed annotation count** | [Irene Malfatto 2020, “Cosmography and Transatlantic Voyages,” *Encyclopedia of the History of Science*](https://ethos.lps.library.cmu.edu/article/id/41/), article read, reports **366 annotations** in Columbus's Polo copy. She does not identify their hands or counting rules in that passage, and gives an outdated Antwerp 1485–86 imprint: the project’s catalogue-checked F-19 is Gouda 1483–84. **Reported figure, not an independently counted total**; do not assign all 366 to Columbus. The count's evidentiary basis and compatibility with other totals require checking against Gil or another named study. |
| **Ridewall** | Gadrat's actual 2022 passage and Ridewall's text were not inspected in this intake; the citation to Grčić does not verify the dossier's “earliest English use” claim. The previous intake records [Eh's report](https://mrijournal.riccimac.org/index.php/en/issues/issue-14and15/353-mrij14-15-4-en) of an earlier Polo citation by English Dominican Thomas Waleys in a work completed by 1327, possibly written in Bologna. An English author's use is not automatically reception **in England**. |
