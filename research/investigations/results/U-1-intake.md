## Findings by claim, with handoffs

The current Note text was re-read on 2026-11-17. II-10 [6], II-15 [3] and I-12 [13] had already been revised in the working tree with *Yuan shi*, Pelliot and Boyle citations; the handoffs below build on that wording rather than replace it.

1. **Li Tan (II-10 [6]).** The current Note is consistent with the checked sources. One change follows: Pelliot calls Aguil "certainly" Aju, son of Uriyangqadai, and *YS* 128 records Aju's march against Li Tan in 1262. "The identification of Polo's Aguil remains uncertain" can become "Aguil is Aju, son of Uriyangqadai (Pelliot, following Charignon)", cited `[@pelliot1959notes, vol. 1, "Agiul"]` and `[@yuanshi1370, juan 128]`. Mongotay stays open ("very hypothetical", Pelliot). Pelliot also gives the execution date (6 August 1262) and notes that Polo places the revolt at Tundinfu (Tung-p'ing), not Jinan.
2. **Negüder and the Qaraunas (I-07 body l. 23, Note [7]).** The body says "Nigūdarī bands are historically attested". Keep that, but source it: Pelliot finds the first Qarauna mention in 1270 and none earlier, and the name Nägüdäri survives in Babur's memoirs. Correct the rest: Pelliot finds no nephew of Chagatai called Negüder and dates Polo's account after Hülegü's arrival, so the genealogy is the Book's. "Dalivar" is Lahore for Marsden and Yule, and possibly Delhi for Pelliot. "Asedin Soldan" is Ghiyāth al-Dīn, sultan of Delhi, per Pelliot; whether that means Balban is NOT SEEN. Cite `[@pelliot1959notes, vol. 1, "Caraunas"; vol. 2, "Dilivar"]`. Aubin 1969 and Jackson 1999 stay leads.
3. **Möngke's funeral (I-12 [13]).** The current hedged Note already says what the evidence supports. The return adds nothing checkable for Möngke. No change.
4. **Kaidu against Nomoghan (II-15 [3]).** The current Note already cites Pelliot on Giorge and Caidu. It can add, from the checked Nomogan entry and *YS* 12, that the princes who seized Nomoghan at Almaliq in 1277 acted without Qaidu ("海都不從"). It can also add Pelliot's suggestion that Polo put George in place of his father Ai-buqa, who fought Sarban after 1277 (`[@pelliot1959notes, vol. 2, "Nomogan"; "Giorge"]`).
5. **Jews in Yuan China (II-01 [12]).** The Note's "thinly documented at this date" holds. Loewe reports *Yuan shi* mentions of Jews under 1329, 1340 and 1354, in a fiscal context; nothing independent places Jews in the 1287 campaign. Add `loewe1987jewish` (status below) if the Note gains a citation. Whether the gibe passage is Ramusio-only was not checked.
6. **Chagatai and the Samarkand church (I-10 [10]).** The conversion stays unverified. The pillar legend is not Polo's alone: it appears in Liang Hsiang's inscription for one of Mar Sargis's churches at Chên-chiang, and Pelliot thinks Polo heard it from Mar Sargis, whose family came from Samarkand. The two witnesses may share one informant. Cite `[@pelliot1959notes, vol. 2, "Marsarchis"]`; do not date that inscription to 1281 on this evidence.
