## What Masuya says (printed pp. 76–78), read from the PDF images
- The Great Khans' *ordu* (camp) travelled with wives' households, officials, guards and troops; Abaqa (r. 1265–82) regularised the Ilkhans' seasonal migration.
- **Carpini at Güyük's camp, 1246:** a huge white-and-yellow pavilion ("more than two thousand men could have got into it") with a **painted wooden palisade** round it (Sira Ordu); a **golden** tent on pillars covered in gold plates fastened with gold and wooden nails, its top and sides hung with baldachins (enthronement); a **red velvet** tent (feasts). Each tent had one purpose.
- **Hülegü, 1255:** at Samarqand a tent of *nasij* (cloth of gold) with a felt covering, lined, with a hall and an antechamber; in 1256 a "tent of cloth of gold" for Arghun Aqa (Juvaini quoted).
- **Shangdu (fig. 82, plan):** the Palace City holds the audience hall, **Da'ange**; the Imperial City round it; the Outer City to the west and north is the pleasure ground. West: **Xinei**, with a pleasure palace **Bai Ordu** and at least five permanent buildings, location not found. North: **Beiyuan**, with a garden, a zoo and a pleasure palace **Sira Ordu** in a walled area where **excavators found no trace of a permanent structure**.
- **Masuya's reading: Khubilai's "cane palace" "must be this Sira Ordu"**, a temporary building of canes on gilt and lacquered columns each bearing a gilt dragon (Polo). In Chinese it is the *zongmaodian* ("palm-fibre palace"); Yesün Temür ordered a new one in 1325 with two carpets of 2,343 *chi*.

## Conflict with the earlier Dadu and Shangdu dive
The 2025 Guangming Daily piece by 郑玉 and **魏坚** (the Shangdu excavator) puts the 棕毛殿 (*sira ordo*) on a **round base in the west of the outer city**, near the imperial city's west gate. Masuya (2002, citing Jia 1977's plan) puts the Sira Ordu in **Beiyuan, the north**, with no permanent trace, and Bai Ordu in the west. These are two named scholars twenty years apart. **Record both; the book follows neither until the excavation report is read.** The earlier avoid-line ("a rectangular hall for the cane palace; its base is round") is softened accordingly.
