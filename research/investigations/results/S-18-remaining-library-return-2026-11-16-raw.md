## 2. Guillot & Kalus (2008), *Les monuments funéraires et l'histoire du sultanat de Pasai à Sumatra*

**Access: NOT SEEN.** I found no open digitization or preview of this book (Cahiers d'Archipel 37, Paris, 2008, 404 pp.) reachable from here — no Google Books snippet, no archive.org copy, no publisher PDF surfaced in search. I could not open the book's own text on Malik al-Salih's tomb.

What I can report, with attribution, is only what *other* secondary sources say Guillot & Kalus concluded — this is hearsay about the book, not a reading of it:
- A footnote in Peacock & Francis's 2015 piece in *Indonesia and the Malay World* states: "the tombstone of the first Islamic ruler of Pasai, Sultan Malik al-Salih, dated 1297, was most probably carved in the 15th or 16th century to replace an earlier grave monument (**Lambourn 2008: 273**)" — attributing this specific "replacement/re-carving" claim to **Lambourn**, not to Guillot & Kalus, even though the same footnote cites "Kalus and Guillot (2008)" for a separate, adjacent claim (about two other 11th-century Kufic stones at Champa and Leran being imported as ballast, unconnected to their inscriptional findspots).
- Archnet's description, citing Guillot & Kalus's book by title, independently states that Pasai's early royal gravestones (including Malik al-Salih's) were "imported by Gujaratis from Cambay, India" — an **origin** claim (Gujarat/India manufacture), distinct from the **dating** claim (that the extant stone is a later replacement).

So the secondary literature attributes two distinct and not obviously identical claims to this cluster of scholarship: (a) Lambourn 2008 — the present stone is a 15th/16th-century replacement of an earlier monument; (b) Guillot & Kalus / Archnet's summary of them — the Pasai royal gravestones generally (including, by implication, Malik al-Salih's) were manufactured in Gujarat and imported. **I cannot confirm from Guillot & Kalus's own text whether they state the existing Malik al-Salih stone is itself a Gujarati import, or a later local replacement, or both, or whether they address the "replacement" question at all** — that specific attribution in the literature goes to Lambourn, not to them, in the one source I could check. This is consistent with — not proof of — disagreement among specialists about dating/origin of the *existing* stones; I am not asserting a consensus, and I am not asserting discord beyond what these two citations show: different authors are credited with different (and not fully reconciled) claims about the same object.

**Result: NOT SEEN for the book itself; cautious, attributed secondhand report only, with the Lambourn/Guillot-Kalus attribution kept distinct as found.**

## 5. Venice — ASVe Testamenti b. 830 and the 1328 Domini Advocatores record

**Access: NOT SEEN for the archival document images themselves.** The Archivio di Stato di Venezia's Notarile testamenti fondo is not openly digitized for this item as far as I could determine; I found no viewable image or transcription-with-facsimile of b. 830, prog. 5 (notary Pietro Pagano) reachable from here.

What I could reach and cross-check are scholarly citations of the same archival unit:
- **Treccani** (Dizionario Biografico, "POLO, Matteo"): "il testamento, in Archivio di Stato di Venezia, *Notarile testamenti, Atti Pietro Pagano*, b. 830, **5**... rogato il **6 febbraio 1310**... è stato trascritto da G. Orlandini, *Marco Polo e la sua famiglia*, in *Archivio veneto-tridentino*, IX (1926)."
- **Treccani** ("POLO, Marco"), independently, citing the same unit: "il lunghissimo testamento dello zio Matteo (**1310**), in Archivio di Stato di Venezia, *Notarile testamenti, Atti Pietro Pagano*, b. 830, **f. 5**, trascritto da Orlandini."

These two independent Treccani entries agree on the shelfmark (b. 830, piece 5 — called "5" in one entry and "f. 5" in the other, which I take to be the same reference, consistent with the user's "prog. 5," though I note the two Treccani entries themselves use slightly different sub-unit notation, "5" vs "f. 5," which I am reporting rather than silently reconciling) and on the date **6 February 1310** (modern style) — matching the editors' own stated equivalence of "1309 Venetian style / 1310 modern" that the user already has from Orlandini/Moule–Pelliot.

- **Yule** (Gutenberg text, *Travels of Marco Polo* vol. 1, Introductory Notices §VIII): the document Yule discusses is explicitly a **different, later instrument**: "In **March, 1328**, Marco Polo, called Marcolino, of St. John Chrysostom..., represents **before the *Domini Advocatores*** of the Republic that certain *imprestita* that had belonged to the late Maffeo Polo the Elder, had been alienated and transferred **in May 1318**..., without regard to the rights of the said Marcolino, **to whom the said Messer Maffeo had bequeathed 1000 lire by his will executed on 6th February, 1308** (*i.e.* 1309)." Yule's own footnote glosses "6th February 1308" as "i.e. 1309" — meaning Yule is using the *old* Venetian-style year for the will's date in his main text (1308) and glossing it to the modern-style equivalent (1309) in brackets, which is one year earlier than the 1310 modern-style date the Treccani entries and the user's already-read Orlandini/Moule–Pelliet edition give.

**This is the one-year discrepancy, and I can now describe it precisely rather than just flag it:** Yule's cited original source material gives the will's execution date as "6 February 1308" Venetian style = "1309" modern style (per Yule's own bracket). The critical edition the user has already read (Orlandini/Moule–Pelliot) gives "1309 Venetian style = 1310 modern" for what is evidently the same testament (same testator, Maffeo; same notary context; same archival fondo). That is a one-Venetian-style-year difference in the date itself (1308 vs. 1309 old style), which — given that the Venetian calendar year began on 1 March — propagates to a one-year difference in the modern-style equivalence (1309 vs. 1310). **I cannot adjudicate which dating is correct from what I accessed; I can only report that Yule's own cited wording and the modern critical edition's wording disagree by one year on the will's execution date, independent of the old-style/modern-style conversion issue, which is a separate axis of potential confusion.** I did not silently correct either figure.

**On whether the March 1328 Domini Advocatores record and the b. 830/prog. 5 will are "the same will":** They are **not the same document** — the 1328 record is a later petition by Marco "Marcolino" Polo *before a different body* (the Domini Advocatores), *referencing* and *quoting from* Maffeo's 1308/1309-dated will as a prior instrument; b. 830, prog. 5 is (per Treccani, following Orlandini) the will itself, dated 1309/1310. So: same underlying testamentary bequest (the 1,000 lire to "Marcolino"), referenced twice — once in the will itself (b. 830/prog. 5) and once in the 1328 complaint about its non-execution (the Domini Advocatores record Yule reports) — but these are two different instruments eighteen-odd years apart, not two competing copies of one will. The "1,000 lire" figure is consistent across both: Yule's 1328-record quote says "1000 lire... bequeathed... by his will," matching the sum the user already found in the OCR'd edited will text (which notes the 1,000 lire covered losses including other affairs, with no robbery specified — a detail Yule's quoted 1328 fragment does not contradict, since it only restates the bequest amount, not its stated purpose).

**Result:** archival images NOT SEEN; but via independent citation cross-checking I can report with more precision than "there is a discrepancy" — specifically, Yule's quoted original gives the will's date as 1308/1309-modern, one year earlier than the 1309/1310-modern dating in the edition the user already read, and the two documents in question (the will itself vs. the 1328 Domini Advocatores petition) are related-but-distinct instruments, not two versions of the same will.
