## Chronology and Disputed Episodes

The available secondary chronology places Arghun’s reign in **1284–1291**, followed by Gaykhatu in **1291–1295** and Baydu in **1295**; Ghazan took the throne in **1295** (Gasimov & Azimli, 2018). Thus, Arghun’s reign ending in 1291 and Ghazan’s accession in 1295 must not be collapsed into a single succession event. An exact death date and the princess’s arrival and marriage dates remain unverified here.

A separate review confirms that Ghazan governed Khurasan following his father’s accession in 1284, before Ghazan’s own accession in 1295 (Peacock, 2012). This supports distinguishing his earlier princely position from his later position as Ilkhan.

For **Xiangyang**, secondary material dates the final Mongol victory to **1273**, after a five-year siege (Wood, 2018). It also attributes to Rashid an account of Persian engineers constructing “Frankish” mangonels, but the Persian passage itself is unavailable (Wood, 2018). Kaziev’s study explicitly uses Chinese chronicles, Rashid, and Polo as separate source bodies; its available summary cannot substitute for the requested *Yuan shi* locator (Kaziev, 2024).

For **Burma**, relevant scholarship also covers campaigns in **1253–1257**, making chronological identification essential before importing figures into a comparison with Polo (Kaziev, 2023). Its observation that *Yuan shi* does not specify the size of certain Alan guard units concerns those units—not a verified total for Polo’s battle (Kaziev, 2023).
