## 2. Pasai tomb of Malik al-Salih

### Othman Mohd. Yatim, read in his 1985 Durham PhD thesis

- **Source:** Othman bin Mohammed Yatim, *Batu Aceh: a study of 15–19 century Islamic gravestones in Peninsular Malaysia*, PhD thesis, Durham, 1985: [etheses.durham.ac.uk/1615](https://etheses.durham.ac.uk/id/eprint/1615/), PDF [1615.pdf](https://etheses.durham.ac.uk/id/eprint/1615/1/1615.pdf).
- **Access:** the typescript scan's OCR text layer, which is noisy. This is the thesis, **not** the 1988 Museum Association book. The book's wording and pages were not checked.
- **Printed p. 94 (PDF p. 109):** Yatim notes that his Type A stone at Pasai is dated 1297 (after Moquette 1913), while Type A elsewhere is 15th-century and the Peninsular examples date to 1475–88. He offers **two alternatives**:
  - the Pasai dating is a mistake; or
  - the Malik al-Salih gravestones "were erected to replace the earlier, possibly damaged stones and given the original date" (OCR cleaned of scanning noise).

  This is a conjecture based on typology, not a documented replacement.
- **Printed pp. 27–28 (PDF pp. 42–43):** Yatim reports Moquette's view that early Pasai stones came from Gujarat. He cites Fatimi (1963: 32) as showing that the Malik al-Salih stones differ from the Gujarati ones. He also corrects Fatimi, who had treated stones from **two separate graves** (Malik al-Zahir's and Malik al-Salih's) as one set (his Pl. 4a–d).
- **What it cannot establish:** the date of any replacement, whether an earlier stone existed, or what the 1988 book says.

### How the positions relate

Yatim (1985, p. 94) and Lambourn 2008 (extant markers c. 1470s–1520s, possibly replacing earlier ones) overlap on the replacement possibility. Yatim, however, leaves an error in the dating as an equal alternative. Ismail et al. 2022 argue the stone is contemporary with the death. These are three named positions, and none is a consensus. Guillot–Kalus remain unseen, so their place in this is unknown.

## 3. Abash bishop story

### Trimingham 1952: SEEN (page images)

- **Edition:** J. Spencer Trimingham, *Islam in Ethiopia*, London: Geoffrey Cumberlege / Oxford University Press, 1952 (title page and imprint viewed).
- **Copy:** user-uploaded archive.org scan [`islam-in-ethiopia-j-spencer-trimingham`](https://archive.org/details/islam-in-ethiopia-j-spencer-trimingham). Its provenance is a Pakistani public-library stamp, and the OCR is unusable, so page images were read.
- **Index p. 293:** "Marco Polo, 69, 74." There is **no** "Polo" entry under P (p. 295).
- **p. 69, main text:** names Yāgbe'a Ṣyōn (1285–94) as the next king after Yekuno Amlāk. "If we can trust Marco Polo" (n. 3), he began his reign with a successful campaign against Adal (presumably Zaila'); this sentence continues on p. 70.
- **p. 69, note 3:** paraphrases Polo from the Everyman's ed., pp. 398–401:
  - in 1288 the Negus sent a bishop to Jerusalem;
  - the Sultan of Adal had him forcibly circumcised. Trimingham remarks this is "impossible" because he would already have been circumcised;
  - the Negus then sacked "the city of Adal".

  Trimingham then states that Polo **confuses Aden and Adal**, and that "it is certainly the latter" that is meant. He adds that an embassy of Yāgbe'a Ṣyōn to Jerusalem reaching Cairo in AH 689 (AD 1290) is confirmed by Arab sources, citing the "Life of Qalā'ūn" quoted by Quatremère, *Mémoires géographiques*, ii. 267–8.
- **p. 74, note 7:** Adal is first mentioned c. 1288 (R.S.E. i. 9), and "Marco Polo refers to it about A.D. 1295."
- **What it cannot establish:** the identification rests on reign dates plus Polo's 1288. The Cairo-embassy corroboration is Trimingham's citation and was not checked here. Note that Trimingham's identification (Yāgbe'a Ṣyōn) **differs from Yule's** (Yikunno Amlak c. 1278). Both are scholarly attributions.

## 4. Takashima wrecks

### Ikeda, Sasaki & Kimura 2019: SEEN (publisher PDF, full text)

- **Source:** *Current Science* 117(10), 25 Nov 2019, pp. 1635–39, [doi:10.18520/cs/v117/i10/1635-1639](https://www.currentscience.ac.in/Volumes/117/10/1635.pdf).
- **p. 1636:** a partially preserved hull, named Takashima No. 1, was revealed under silt in **October 2011**.
- **p. 1637:** the site became the first shipwreck site registered as a national historic site (2011). A more complete vessel, **Takashima No. 2**, was found in **2014**.
- **Wreck descriptions:**
  - No. 1: 23–25 m deep; keel surviving 12 m; double-planked; nailed; bulkheads missing; c. 300 bricks.
  - No. 2: 13–15 m deep; c. 12 × 3 m; nine bulkhead positions; V-shaped hull; nailed double planking; cargo of stone blocks.
- **pp. 1637–38, hull identification:** both are **Chinese-built**, because they have nails, bulkheads, hardwood and a V-section, unlike Korean flat-bottomed, wooden-joined hulls. No. 1 is compared to Penglai and Ningbo ships, with the garboard closer to Penglai. No. 2 is compared to the Quanzhou ship, reconstructed at about 20 m.
- **p. 1635, storm:** "strong winds and waves" are reported from Chinese and Korean historical sources (ref. 4).
- **p. 1638:** the authors argue the Korean fleet was probably not at Takashima when the typhoon struck, citing Sasaki's timber analysis and Hattori.
- **Absent from this article's text** (full-text grep plus reading): **no fire traces, no seal, and no radiocarbon or other absolute date for either hull.** The association with 1281 is contextual. This silence holds for this article only.

## 5. Orlandini 1926, document 6, and the will date

### Orlandini 1926: SEEN (OCR)

- **Source:** G. Orlandini, "Marco Polo e la sua famiglia", *Archivio Veneto-Tridentino* 9 (1926), via archive.org [`archiviovenetotridentino9`](https://archive.org/details/archiviovenetotridentino9). Access was by OCR; page images were not viewed. Pages were taken from the OCR running heads.
- **Document 6, pp. 25–31:**
  - Orlandini's heading reads "**1310. 6 febbraio.** — Testamento di Matteo Polo seniore."
  - The transcribed text reads "Anno ab incarnacione … **millesimo trecentesimo nono** mensis februarii die sexta intrante **Indictione octava** Rivalto". The testator is "Matheus Paulo de confinio sancti Johanis Crisostomi".
  - The notary is Petrus Paganus, priest of S. Felice.
  - Archival citation (p. 31): "Arch. di Stato di Venezia – Sezione Notarile – testamenti b. 830 prog. N. 5 del protocollo – not. Pagano Pietro".
- **The loss clause (p. 27):**
  - Marco received 1,000 lire "a domino duce et a comuni Veneciarum **pro parte damni nobis facti tam per dominum Comnenum de Trapesonda quam in districtu ipsius domini Comneni quam etiam in aliis nostris rebus**".
  - The loss "per predictum dominum Comnenum de Trapesonda quam in eius districtu fuit in suma circa yperpera quatuor millia".
  - **Named:** the lord Comnenus of Trebizond, and his district.
  - **Mechanism:** none is given. The text says *damnum*, not robbery.
- **Orlandini's commentary (p. 14 and note 1):** he interprets the loss as "violenze indigene" at a time of Greek–Venetian tension. He links it to a Greater Council act of 4 July 1301 about reimbursing Venetians "damnificati per gentem dicti Imperatoris" from Comnenian goods seized at Caffa in 1296. This is **Orlandini's interpretation**, not the will's wording.

### Moule–Pelliot vol. I: SEEN (archive.org OCR)

- **Source:** [`descriptionofwor01polo`](https://archive.org/details/descriptionofwor01polo).
- The Introduction gives "will of Maffeo, dated 6 February 1309 (1310)", with an English translation of the same clause: "loss inflicted on us both by the lord Comnenus of Trebizond and in the territory of the same lord Comnenus and also in other affairs of ours".
- The appendix heading for doc. 6 reads "Will of Maffeo Polo senior, 6 February 1309/10". It is labelled "Copy in the Notary's Register" and has the same incipit, year and indiction.
- MP cites "Archivio di Stato. Sezione notarile, Testamenti b. 830 … Register of Pietro Pagano", which is **the same instrument as Orlandini's**.
- The English "inflicted" is MP's rendering of *factum*. Neither text gives a mechanism.

### Archival original: NOT SEEN

ASVe Notarile, Testamenti b. 830, n. 5 was not viewed. Both editions describe it as a protocol or register copy.

### Date discrepancy

- **Orlandini and MP agree with each other.** The text's "1309" in Venetian style (year beginning 1 March) is February **1310 modern**. Indiction 8 runs Sept 1309–Aug 1310, which confirms 1310; Feb 1309 modern would be indiction 7. Orlandini's own note also says "nel 1309" was paid in part, which is loose wording on his side.
- **Yule's "6th February, 1308 (i.e. 1309)" comes from a different document.** It is his summary of a **March 1328 Domini Advocatores act** (`book/refs/yule-vol1.txt` ll. 8537–8544), not a reading of the will.
- **The content matches.** That act concerns imprestita of the late Maffeo the Elder bequeathing 1,000 lire to Marcolino, alienated in May 1318. Doc. 6 (pp. 26–27) leaves Nicolò 1,000 lire "de meis imprestitis". If Nicolò defaults, these pass to his son Marcolino, protected against alienation. The bequest plainly matches, which **suggests** the 1328 act refers to this will.
- **The one-year gap is unexplained.** It could come from the 1328 act itself, from Yule's transcription, or from a second instrument. The 1328 act was **not seen**, so the gap is **unresolved**. Do not merge the dates without that act.
