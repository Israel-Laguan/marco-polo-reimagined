## Findings, corrections and chapter handoffs

- **Ahmad, II-05:** the *Yuan shi* 205 transcription has Wang Zhu 王著 and monk Gao 高和尚 in the plot; a man impersonates the crown prince, Wang Zhu strikes Ahmad with a copper hammer, Bodun 博敦 strikes the mounted impersonator, arrows are fired, and Wang and Gao are later executed. It does not follow that Polo's Vanchu = Wang Zhu and Chenchu = Gao in that order. Yule's editorial note proposes a different possibility for Chenchu; do not select an identity without specialist support. Bolad 孛羅 appears as investigator in this transcription; the passage does not name Marco. Compare characters and events carefully, not as one-to-one proof or proof of Polo's presence. The *Yuan shi* is a later compilation, not itself an immediate eyewitness.
- **Nayan, II-01:** this return's inability to access the annals does not undo the project's direct reading of juan 14, 154 and 162 in the [S-17 priority intake](S-17-priority-return-2026-11-16-intake.md). Use that intake's bounds, including remaining uncertainties.
- **Kököchin, III-09/III-12:** keep the [existing scanned Persian and Thackston comparison](../../registry/comparanda.md): *khwāja* as Polo's Coja is an identification argued by scholars, not a secure proper name read off the Persian. Neither checked passage names a Polo, sea voyage or ships. Cleaves himself and the Chinese original remain unseen in this intake. Do not treat Ma's *Yuanshi* label and the cited *Yongle dadian* location as a demonstrated debate about two records; inspect the chain. Do not attribute the report's three-name fallback to Jackson.
- **Rubruck, I-12 / III-01:** existing local Rockhill OCR at printed pp. 55, 66–67 and 85 supports cart, drink-making and reaction leads. Source-image check is still needed for exact wording and pagination; this audit did not perform it. These are earlier steppe context, not evidence that Rubruck saw Polo's episode or that Polo traveled. Independence is not established just by the absence of a known dependence.
- **Ibn Battuta, III-08:** Fordham's excerpt attributes horse export to Dhofar. The current manuscript `book/vol-3/ch-08-aden-hormos.md` already attributes this comparison to Dhofar; the report's warning does not establish a manuscript error. Its Qays/Siraf identification and passages on ships still need the relevant edition and underlying source checked. Do not quote the Fordham excerpt as Lee.
- **Juvaini, I-08:** Daftary is a modern interpretation; Juvaini's relevant narrative was not read in this intake. Earlier traditions, including Arnold of Lübeck, predate Polo. Avoid claiming direct verification of Juvaini's silence or that Polo invented every element.
- **Wang Dayuan, III-02–08:** [Kauz and Ptak 2001](https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_2001_num_88_1_3509?pageId=T1_60), n. 6, cites Rockhill's 1915 *T'oung Pao* 16, p. 623 on Ganmaili/Hormuz; an [ISEAS bibliography](https://bookshop.iseas.edu.sg/component/get/21593) lists the 1914–15 articles. These are bibliographic leads only: neither Rockhill's passage nor its exact-edition rights were inspected. The return's archive identifier is for volume 15 and is not evidence for volume 16 p. 623.
- **Jahn:** [Encyclopaedia Iranica's biography](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jahn-karl-emil-oskar/) states 7 November 1985 as his death date. This resolves the biographical discrepancy in the return, not the rights of his Persian edition or any translation.
- **Rights generally:** leave established project clearances at their previously documented scope. Do not extend them on this report's authority, treat open access as permission, or use the 1929 Gibb text verbatim without a separate edition-and-jurisdiction check. This is a project source-control finding, not a legal opinion.
