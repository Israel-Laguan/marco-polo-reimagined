The [supplied report](S-17-raw.md) is preserved verbatim. This intake checks selected claims and reconciles prior project research; it is **not** an independent verification of every row. A source cited here was opened at the relevant passage unless explicitly described otherwise. `verified` means the cited source says the proposition stated, not that the underlying event or an eyewitness visit has been established.

## Shortcomings identified before intake

1. **Evidence laundering and circular corroboration.** The report itself says Vogel, Haw, Allsen, Biran, Pelliot and several other central works were not read; many rows nevertheless call a claim “confirmed” because an encyclopedia repeats a later historian or Polo. A historian's repetition does not show the *Yuan shi* independently attests the detail. This particularly affects the Nayan elephant platform, cross and execution, and precise family or campaign details. Treat those labels as leads, not verification.
2. **Missing the existing research base.** It proposes Pelliot as an unread lead, but `gazetteer.csv` already records direct Pelliot readings for Acbalec, Caugigu, Anin and Toloman. `registry/scholarship.md` already reports Mascherpa on Kinsay's numbers and the queen's letter, and modern Haw/Atwood/Dang/Luo research. Do not claim there is no post-1920 research; the report only failed to locate it. Existing references do not automatically validate every place identification.
3. **Not the requested direct source comparison.** There is no systematic *Yuan shi*–Polo table, direct Vogel reading, direct Pelliot reading, or substantial Chinese-language specialist bibliography; no reliable synthesis of Book IV narrative origins. Lack of a source is not proof that no scholarship exists.
4. **False specificity and inadequate provenance.** Disputed identifications, circulation dates, modern site coordinates, and institutional ranks need separate textual and evidentiary labels. For example, apparent agreement with Polo on Yuan practices does not prove a specific visit or office. Row 18's blanket claim that all pre-Ming walls were militarily irrelevant is too sweeping for the evidence presented. Do not infer the Yuan had no *baojia* merely because a tertiary summary omits it.
5. **Bibliography and report mechanics.** Many supplied BibTeX entries lack stable links or give access only to a secondary surrogate; some titles and dates require checking. “NEW” means strictly *after* 2013, so a 2012/2013 edition of Vogel is not NEW. The report lists UNESCO decision **44 COM 8B.24**; the actual Quanzhou inscription is **44 COM 8B.15**. A report's citation to an unread book is not evidence that its specific pages support the claim.
6. **Coverage and quantification.** The promised Yuan station counts, no. of bridge arches/lions, Kinsay figures and paper-note specimens require independent checks. Its “not found” means only not found by that search. Particularly important omissions remain the Nayan comparative sources, Yuan Hangzhou registers, the Vochan/Pagan chronology, Fugul/Cuiju, and the specific source of the Book IV war accounts. The S-14 findings remain separately controlled and are not re-adjudicated here.

## Coverage still open and follow-up priorities

The timeboxed intake leaves **substantive research** open, not merely publication polish. Before any stronger new claims are written, obtain and compare (1) the *Yuan shi* passages on Nayan, Ahmad, court ritual and postal system with exact Polo witness and Chinese scholarship; (2) Vogel's own chapters on salt, money and Yangzhou, and the named Chinese historians' arguments; (3) current archaeological publications and Xu's full Dadu reconstruction, separating evidence from reconstructive inference; (4) Pelliot's actual relevant entries and post-Pelliot regional geography on all six names; (5) a specialist treatment of Vochan/Ngasaunggyan and the Pagan chronology, and scholarship directly interpreting the Book IV war narratives; (6) Song/Yuan Hangzhou household and fire administration and the original Chinese source of Mascherpa's bridge figures; (7) UNESCO's nomination dossier and a philological source for Tyunju, inscriptions and pepper before adding exact quantities; (8) reliable chronologically matched station figures and bridge/paiza objects for captions. See the raw report's section-by-section unresolved list for further leads, but do not import its citations or verdicts without checking.
