[The supplied Consensus dossier](S-20-third-pass-raw.md) is preserved verbatim. It identifies potentially useful map and Dominican reception material, but much of its bibliography describes access in the external agent's session, not ours. The following targeted checks distinguish texts actually read here from the map, parchment and earlier witnesses. The checks do not establish an error rate for the full dossier.

| **Fra Mauro's Japan label** | [Marianne O'Doherty 2011, *Wasafiri* 26(2), p. 34](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/175057/2/MOD_wasafiri_june2011_redacted.pdf), article text read, describes the map's Japan name as *Cimpangu*, connects it to Polo, and identifies Falchetta's entry **#1334**. [A separately hosted English transcription index](https://www.myoldmaps.com/late-medieval-maps-1300/249-fra-mauros-mappamundi/fra-mauro-transcriptions.pdf), entry 1334, renders the name as “Island of Cimpagu” and provides location **(25, f 16)**; [Cattaneo 2022](https://www.academia.edu/117367561/Cattaneo_A_2022_Connected_Histories_The_Mongol_Empire_and_the_Creation_of_New_Worldviews_in_the_Fifteenth_Century_Fra_Mauros_Mappa_Mundi_and_the_Honil_Gangni_Yeokdae_Gukdo_Ji_Do_In_Dunlop_A_ed_The_Mongol_Empire_in_Global_History_and_Art_History_Harvard_University_Press_p_265_294), n. 32, also cites Falchetta *1334 and plate 25, f 16. **Supported as an attributed label and interpretation**, not a direct image reading or an unqualified cartographic “first.” O'Doherty's *Cimpangu* and the index's *Cimpagu* differ; do not silently harmonize them as a verified map spelling. This does not settle what the Catalan Atlas shows. |
| **FM *2442 / *2306** | [Burgio–Simion 2018, pp. 191–92](https://www.academia.edu/37398962/Eugenio_Burgio_Samuela_Simion_La_ricezione_medievale_del_Devisement_dou_monde_secoli_XIV_XV_Medioevo_Romanzo_XLII_1_2018_pp_173_94), text read, identify “Provincia Iogoristan” *2442 and “Hachbaluch” *2306 as evidence favoring Ghisi as a map source. [Eusebi, Burgio and Simion 2024, pp. 108–10](https://www.academia.edu/121703262/M_Eusebi_E_Burgio_S_Simion_On_the_Way_to_an_Integral_Edition_of_the_Book_of_Marco_Polo_A_First_Attempt_to_Create_a_Digital_Edition_in_U_Theobald_H_U_Vogel_eds_Marco_Polo_Research_Past_Present_Future_T%C3%BCbingen_Library_Publishing_2024), text read, give *2442 and relay Pelliot's *Hacbaluch* alongside other comparison data. [The transcription index](https://www.myoldmaps.com/late-medieval-maps-1300/249-fra-mauros-mappamundi/fra-mauro-transcriptions.pdf), entries 2442 and 2306, gives **(33, S 6) / (39, A 1)** and **(32, G 31)** respectively, and renders *2306 as Hacbaluch. These are **scholarly transcriptions and a separately hosted index**, not an independent visual reading of the map; preserve differing spellings and the interpretive status of proposed sources. The source's 1459 title is not proof of the surviving map's identity with the Portuguese-commissioned map. |
| **Z, Ghisi and Quinsai** | [Giuseppe Mascherpa, *Quaderni Veneti* 6(2) (2017; online 2018), pp. 62–64](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/article/quaderni-veneti/2017/2/art-10.14277-QV-1724-188X-2017-02-003_1dOykyO.pdf), conclusion read, proposes Quinsai additions may draw on notes and recollections preserved by Polo or family, with or without his supervision; he also explicitly presents interpolation from other travel collections as a less economical alternative. **Attributed textual hypothesis**, not proof that additional passages were authored or witnessed by Polo. Distinguish the surviving Toledo witness from the larger Z tradition. The dossier's “confirmed” wording is too strong. |
| **1323 Dominican document** | [Marcello Bolognari 2020, pp. 25–27](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-440-0/978-88-6969-440-0-ch-02_5Lfc2Qh.pdf), critical edition and commentary read, transcribes the 31 Mar 1323 act of accepting Giovanni dalle Boccole's testamentary bequest and identifies the named Marco Paulo of San Giovanni Grisostomo as the traveller. Archive locator: ASVe, SS. Giovanni e Paolo, Atti, b. 36 (B), fasc. VII, pergamene, no. 9, line 3. The original parchment was not inspected; identification is Bolognari's interpretation. His article also notes the friars Benevenuto and Centorio appear in the act and the 1324 will. This documents an association; it does not demonstrate that those friars produced Z. The editors Conte, Montefusco and Simion are not the author of Bolognari's chapter. |
| **Ridewall and earliest English reception** | The dossier attributes an approximately 1330–32 Ridewall example and an “earliest” claim to Gadrat, but her full relevant passage was not independently read here. [Edmond Eh's account](https://mrijournal.riccimac.org/index.php/en/issues/issue-14and15/353-mrij14-15-4-en), read, reports an earlier Thomas Waleys *Moralitates* completed by 1327 and citing Polo, while attributing material to Gadrat's scholarship. This is **a potential earlier use by an English Dominican**, not independently established reception in England: Eh suggests Waleys may have composed it while teaching in Bologna. Compare geographic location and dating before asserting the first English use. Neither medieval text nor Gadrat's cited passages were inspected here. |
| **Digital Ramusio edition** | [Samuela Simion 2017 review, p. 1257](https://www.academia.edu/103931332/Giovanni_Battista_Ramusio_Dei_Viaggi_di_Messer_Marco_Polo_Edizione_critica_digitale_progettata_e_coordinata_da_Eugenio_Burgio_Marina_Buzzoni_Antonella_Ghersetti_a_cura_di_Eugenio_Burgio_e_Samuela_Simion), author-posted text read, reports online availability from 4 Feb 2015; [AIUCD's advance announcement](https://www.aiucd.it/presentazione-delledizione-digitale-dei-viaggi-di-messer-marco-polo/) scheduled the presentation for that date. The announcement is evidence of the scheduled event, not independent proof it happened. No personal assumption that all later versions remain unchanged. |
| **Map dating and other claims** | The dossier's “lost copy sent in 1459” must not override the project’s existing F-20 hedge: a Portuguese-commissioned map was dispatched by 24 Apr 1459, but its relationship to the surviving Marciana map is disputed. Fra Mauro map dates vary by scholar. The dossier's English reception, Gadrat's suggested Dominican origin of Z, and remaining Asian, food, and Columbus claims are not thereby established. |
