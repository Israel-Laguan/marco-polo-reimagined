| Question | Decision for the book | Evidence inspected and limits |

| Columbus's exemplar and notes | **Use:** Columbus's surviving copy is Pipino's Latin version, printed by Gerard Leeu at Gouda in 1483–84 and now in Seville; it bears annotations attributable to Columbus and others in his circle. **Do not imply that this particular exemplar or any surviving postil helped plan the first voyage.** Dates and sequence of the notes are unresolved. Shawcross reads Columbus's notes primarily as attending to things of material value; attribute that interpretation. Malfatto's count of 366 concerns this copy, with method and hands unspecified; omit the count unless useful and attributed. | Shawcross, [2023 abstract and opening p. 377](https://www.academia.edu/116101732/Teresa_Shawcross_Materiality_and_Marginalia_Across_the_World_The_Role_of_Things_in_Christopher_Columbus_s_Annotations_on_Marco_Polo_Medieval_History_Journal_2023_376_424), read; [Malfatto 2020](https://ethos.lps.library.cmu.edu/article/id/41/), read. Its caption identifies a Maabar opening in Shawcross's fig. 4, not necessarily the project's selected photograph. |

Do not say the Atlas definitively lacks any Japanese element. | `liscak2018atlas`, pp. 5–7, attributes the Great Khan and Chanbalech legends to Polo (map and earlier editions not inspected); Cattaneo 2022, [pp. 287–88 n. 26](https://www.academia.edu/117367561/Cattaneo_A_2022_Connected_Histories_The_Mongol_Empire_and_the_Creation_of_New_Worldviews_in_the_Fifteenth_Century_Fra_Mauros_Mappa_Mundi_and_the_Honil_Gangni_Yeokdae_Gukdo_Ji_Do_In_Dunlop_A_ed_The_Mongol_Empire_in_Global_History_and_Art_History_Harvard_University_Press_p_265_294), read; [Jung 2015 abstract](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002027111) proposes mixed Ceylon, Sumatra, Japan and Korea material in Taprobana, a scholarly interpretation rather than a separate labelled Japan. The supplied dossier's Knox 2016 DOI identifies *The Social Atlas of Europe*, not evidence for Korea on the medieval map. |

| *Milione* | **Use attributed competing explanations:** Ramusio linked the name to Marco's repeated accounts of vast wealth; Jacopo d'Acqui linked it to family wealth; scholars also propose a family-name form from Emilione. For a dated personal record use **1319**, attributed to Pozza via Bolognari or Vogel; do not describe it as the first possible record. Yule dates that entry to 10 April 1305. | Bolognari 2024 pp. 38–39 read previously, S15-H3; [Vogel 2013, author preface pp. xxiii–xxiv](https://www.academia.edu/4258345/Marco_Polo_Was_in_China_New_Evidence_from_Currencies_Salts_and_Revenues), read, citing Pozza 2006 pp. 288–89 and distinguishing a same-named uncle. [Yule volume I](../../refs/yule-vol1.txt) and [volume II](../../refs/yule-vol2.txt) checked: both give 10 April. Neither the 1305 record nor Pozza's original 1319 document was independently inspected. |

## Myth and afterlife decisions

| Topic | Classification and handoff |
|---|---|
| Pasta | **Myth as introduction claim:** Italian pasta predates the Polos' return. Montanari discusses a Ramusio strand and the 1929 trade-journal story; neither establishes the first origin of all versions. S20-E4/E7. |
| Ice cream, gunpowder, paper money, spectacles | **Unverified as Polo-introduction attributions;** omit claims of earliest origin. The alleged Carlsson 1930 quotation is not verified; S20-E8/E9 preserve a second-hand early spectacles account. A technology's history is distinct from the provenance of an attribution story. |
| Deathbed saying | **Anecdote:** attribute to Jacopo d'Acqui's later chronicle; not authenticated last words. S15-H2; earlier-than-Jacopo origin unknown. |
| Korčula birthplace | **Disputed/unsupported as certainty:** separate modern birthplace claims from proposed family origins; don't represent either as established. S20-E19 and source intake. |
| Authentic likeness | **Unverified:** use clearly identified later representations, not an unqualified portrait from life. |
| Swimming-pool game | **Unverified etymology:** omit an origin story unless sourced; optional trivia only. |
| Modern works | **Selected examples:** Calvino 1972, O'Neill's *Marco Millions* (publication 1927; opening 1928), Montaldo's RAI broadcast 1982–83, Netflix 2014–16, Xue's 2012 book, and the Venice exhibition 2024. Cite the specific event, version and date in S20-E10/E13/E14/E20/E24/E25 and B5; selection is not a claim of exhaustive global coverage. |
| Diplomatic reception | **Optional dated examples:** Xi's 2017 and 2023 remarks cite Polo (S20-E41); frame the rhetoric as the speaker's. The 2017 text is not a separate 2024 speech. |
