# Older witnesses carry most "Ramusio-only" passages

Of the 27 passages that Yule–Cordier (1903) brackets from Ramusio in the Prologue and Book I, **only 3 can be labelled "R only" on the evidence read**: the Kerman army killed by the hot wind (#8), "as far as the city of Lop" (#18) and the phantom cavalcade (#19). Even these three rest on the absence of the passage from F, Z and (for #18–19) V, and other witnesses were not checked. **Seven passages are in Z as well as R** (#2, 6, 7, 9, 13, 15, 22), and **ten also stand, wholly or in part, in another older witness**: F, V, or a witness that Moule & Pelliot mark in the margin (#3, 4, 12, 14, 16, 17, 20, 21, 25, 26). **Seven cannot be decided** from what could be read (#1, 5, 10, 11, 23, 24, 27). The blanket label "Ramusio-only" is therefore wrong for at least 17 of the 27 passages. Many passages are split. A core reading stands in F, Z or V, and Ramusio adds a detail found nowhere else in the witnesses read: the lake in #14, the horn-and-bone road markers in #15, the drinking-water cause in #16, the flax, hemp and grain in #17, the dragons in #25, and the nails against the wind in #26. Those R-only details, not the whole bracket, are what an endnote can safely call "found only in Ramusio". Yule's brackets mark where he took his text, not where the text survives. He says so himself at I.61, where he followed Ramusio because his account was "so much more lucid" ([Wikisource, YC I.61](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Travels_of_Marco_Polo/Book_1/Chapter_61)). Several readings below are inferred from secondary sources or from OCR of Moule & Pelliot's marginal sigla. They are marked as such and carry low confidence.

## Methods: machine-read texts, no page images

**Every reading came through machine extraction, never from a page image viewed by eye.** The researchers' shell network was blocked. All texts were read through a fetch tool that returns a model-mediated extract of a publisher PDF's text layer (Eusebi's F, Simion's V, Burgio 2011), of OCR (Moule & Pelliot vols. 1–2 on NII Digital Silk Road and archive.org, Ramusio 1559 on archive.org), or of HTML (Wikisource, Gutenberg). Quotations are short and keep OCR forms. They must be re-checked against scans before print.

The OCR of Moule & Pelliot vol. 1 (the 1938 composite translation) **loses the italics that mark non-F readings and scatters the marginal sigla**. On the DSR OCR a siglum attaches to a whole line. In the archive.org OCR it is jumbled into the running text. Any witness assigned from Moule & Pelliot sigla below is therefore labelled "MP OCR, inferred" and given low confidence. **The Ca' Foscari digital Ramusio (Burgio–Simion, 2015) could not be read**: virgo.unive.it disallows robots, and only a catalogue capture was seen. Its passage-by-passage source notes were therefore unavailable. Benedetto's 1928 apparatus, Barbieri's 1998 edition of Z, Ménard's edition, Mascherpa 2018 and Pipino's Latin were not consulted.

**Page numbers on the DSR scans of Moule & Pelliot follow two rules found in the notes.** For **vol. 1, image number = printed page + 8** (image 0301 = p. 293). For **vol. 2 (the Z transcription), image number = roman page + 26**. This second rule was confirmed by the printed folios vii (0033), viii (0034), ix (0035) and xi (0037), and it agrees with the earlier finding that 0092 = p. lxvi. **All other Moule & Pelliot page numbers in this report were derived from these rules, not read off the page**, and are marked "(derived)". Yule–Cordier was read in Wikisource and Gutenberg transcriptions by Book and chapter. **No printed 1903 page numbers were established.** For passages 7–13 and 21–27 the Ramusio 1559 Italian was not re-read: R's presence there rests on Yule's bracket. Gennari and Burgio's list of R–V agreements against F, confirmed by Z, is used to infer V's readings for passages 2–6. It is a secondary source, so those V cells are low confidence.

**How the verdicts are applied.** "R and Z only" means that among the witnesses actually checked, only Z carries any part. "Also in an older witness" means that some part also stands in F, V or another witness besides Z; the row names the part that remains R-only. "R only" means that F and Z (and V, where read) lack the passage. These are all statements about the witnesses checked, not about the whole tradition.

## (2) Split passages and disagreements: who holds which reading

**Ramusio's Book I leans on a Z-type Latin source, with Venetian and Pipino layers.** Gennari and Burgio say Book I of R is "in massima parte debitori di Z1". Z1 is the lost Latin "codice Ghisi", close to the Toledo Z. The exception is the long proem (R i 1), which makes "ampio utilizzo di P". VB is "il terzo modello in ordine di importanza" ([Burgio ed. 2011, Introduzione, pp. xxii, xxxiii](https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/e4239ddb-d0a1-7180-e053-3705fe0a3322/Introduzione.pdf)). They list "tutti i ventitré (salvo errore) loci" where R and V agree against F and Z confirms them (p. xxvi, n. 63). Two of those loci are passages 3 and 4. The V readings for #2 and #6 are inferred to be absent because those passages are missing from the list. That is an argument from silence, and the list itself gives 22 numbered loci for 23. The pattern of the 27 passages fits the Gennari/Burgio picture. Z alone supplies #2, 7, 9, 15 and 22. A V-type text supplies the goitre (#16), the wine (#17) and the bells (#20). The proem passage (#1) is absent from both F and Z, which is consistent with R i 1 drawing on P.

**Split passages: the core in an older witness, a clause in R.** In **#2**, Z holds the unclimbable, never-melting snow ([DSR MP2 0032](https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/III-2-F-c-39/V-2/page-hr/0032.html.en)). The herbage and mud were not found in Z's OCR, but Z's "never melts *altogether*" anticipates them, so the OCR may simply have stopped short. In **#3**, Z has sturgeon and salmon but not R's "at the river-mouths" ([DSR MP2 0033](https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/III-2-F-c-39/V-2/page-hr/0033.html.en)). In **#6**, Z has the objection and the boiled-wine rule; MP1 prints the name-change rationale, but the OCR shows no legible siglum for it ([DSR MP2 0037](https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/III-2-F-c-39/V-2/page-hr/0037.html.en)). In **#12**, F already says the Badakhshan horses are great runners, unshod, and travel the mountains. Only the downhill speed is R's, and MP1's OCR marks it "p r", which suggests P also has it ([Eusebi PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-224-6/978-88-6969-224-6_69TdyXc.pdf)). In **#14**, F, Z and V all have a plain between two mountains with a river. R alone, among those three, makes it a lake, and MP1's OCR puts "TA" on that line ([Simion PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-322-9/978-88-6969-322-9_DmPDsnn.pdf); [DSR MP1 0149](https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/III-2-F-c-39/V-1/page-hr/0149.html.en)). In **#15**, Z holds the wolves; the hearsay frame and the road markers in snow are R's ([DSR MP2 0044](https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/III-2-F-c-39/V-2/page-hr/0044.html.en)). In **#16**, Z has one thick foot and V has the goitre. R has swollen legs and a lump in the throat, and adds cotton, craftsmen and the water cause. In **#17**, V has wine and F and Z have vineyards; flax, hemp and grain ("biada", which Yule renders "wheat") are R's. In **#20**, V has bells on the beasts' necks, but so that the travellers stay awake, not so that strays can be found. In **#22**, Z has the poisonous herb, the lost hooves and the native cattle that avoid it ([DSR MP2 0046](https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/III-2-F-c-39/V-2/page-hr/0046.html.en)); the rule that travellers bring only local cattle is R's. In **#25**, V has gilded or varnished columns, and the dragons are R's. In **#26**, F has the canes cut from knot to knot and V has them split into tiles; the nails against the wind are R's, possibly shared with L, VB or P on MP1's sigla ([DSR MP1 0194](https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/III-2-F-c-39/V-1/page-hr/0194.html.en)). Passage **#9** is not counted as split: F has only the surrounding custom (women mourn four years), not the hired mourners.

**Where the sources disagree.** Three times MP1's OCR prints a "V" siglum where Simion's edition of V lacks the reading: the native-cattle rule in #22, the grove in #24, and the twelve days in #5 (where V itself was not read). The "V" is probably OCR for VB or a misplaced siglum, but only the page image can tell ([archive.org MP1 OCR](https://archive.org/stream/descriptionofwor01polo/descriptionofwor01polo_djvu.txt)). For #5, MP1's OCR hints at V, whereas the Gennari/Burgio locator for R i 9 matches Z 8 10–11, and Z lacks the twelve-day count. That counts against the sentence being one of their R–V–Z loci, though V could still carry it alone. In #21, Simion's V text has a lacuna right after the Mangu prohibition (32.7), so V's absence there is not conclusive ([Simion ch. 5 PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-322-9/978-88-6969-322-9-ch-05_11BZ0Ou.pdf)). On the related Tauris Saracen-law passage (R i 9 7–8, not one of the 27), Benedetto's apparatus (p. 23a) says "R traduce fedelmente Z e vi aggiunge l'ultima parte di V". Gennari and Burgio prefer a fuller Z1 as R's single source. This disagreement over whether R conflated witnesses or copied one fuller Latin text affects how every R-only detail below should be read ([Burgio ed. 2011, p. xxvii](https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/e4239ddb-d0a1-7180-e053-3705fe0a3322/Introduzione.pdf)).

**Possible glosses by Ramusio: the researchers' inference, not a published claim.** **No published scholar was found calling any of these 27 passages Ramusio's own gloss.** The nearest published judgement is Gennari and Burgio's verdict that some L-derived readings in the proem (R i 1 2 and i 1 17) are "quasi sicuramente spurie" (pp. xxx–xxxi). Whether #1 is among them was not determined. The following candidates are the researchers' own inferences. In **#14** R appears to turn F, Z and V's plain-with-a-river into a lake from which the river flows. In **#16**, "gambe grosse" normalises Z's single thick foot, and the water cause reads like a rationalisation. In **#17**, R may have moved Z's Cascar "linum & canapum" to Cotan. In **#18**, "as far as the city of Lop" looks like R's precision of F's "et encore … avant". In **#20**, R reworks the bells' purpose. In **#21**, the public hostelries read like a rationalising expansion of "preserving the honour of their wives". In **#22**, Z's "nam in eis…" presupposes a preceding statement about the mountains. So Z may have lost the travellers clause, or R may have supplied it as a gloss. In **#15**, "fu detto a messer Marco" is a hearsay marker of uncertain origin. In **#3**, "alle bocche di fiumi" may belong to R or to Z1. In #24–27, the cane palace, R looks like a conflation that builds on the F and V core with details (wood, dragons, nails, wind) that MP1 assigns to R and sometimes to the VB, L, P or FB groups. Conversely, in **#4, 7 and 9** R renders Z almost word for word ("penes istam prouinciam" = "Appresso questa provincia"), which rules out a gloss there.

**Yule's note 4 to I.61 explains the cane-palace brackets.** Yule writes: "Ramusio's is here so much more lucid than the other texts, that I have adhered mainly to his account of the building" ([Wikisource, YC I.61](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Travels_of_Marco_Polo/Book_1/Chapter_61)). For #24–27 the brackets therefore record an editorial choice made for clarity, not a claim that the details are Ramusio's alone; F and V carry much of the same building. Wikisource may reproduce the 1875 text, so the note's wording in 1903 was presumed, not verified. This matches what Yule says in his Introduction §90: he bracketed what was peculiar to Ramusio's version relative to his own base texts, and Z was not among them.

## Conclusion

The practical consequence for the retelling is that **"Ramusio-only" should label clauses, not brackets**. In most passages the bracket holds a mix: material Ramusio shares with Z (his Latin model) or with F and V, plus a smaller residue found nowhere else in the witnesses read. That residue is where Ramusio, or his lost Ghisi codex, most likely clarified, rationalised or conflated. The safe endnote wording is therefore "this passage reaches Yule through Ramusio; it also stands in Z (and/or F, V)". Where the evidence supports it, a closing phrase can be added naming the specific detail found only in Ramusio. For the seven undecided rows, the endnote should hold back until the page images are checked.

Two cautions follow from the evidence pattern. First, an absence from F, Z and V does not make a passage Ramusian. MP1's margins keep pointing to VB, VA, L and P for exactly the details that look like Ramusio's additions (#21, 24, 26, 27). Those groups are the next place a "Ramusio-only" claim is likely to fail. Second, no published scholar has called any of these passages a Ramusian gloss. The gloss readings offered here are hypotheses to test against the digital Ramusio's source notes, not findings to print.
