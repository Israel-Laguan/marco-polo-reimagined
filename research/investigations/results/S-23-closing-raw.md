**A2. 《元大都：1964～1974年考古报告》, 2024 — NEW**
1. Confirmed real via Xinhua, 2025-01-10, https://www.news.cn/20250110/4b61f7d5f6de44bfa523ba27a12c27f5/c.html — published March 2024, documents the Yuan Dadu Archaeological Team's 1964–1974 fieldwork (first systematic survey of Dadu), led by Xu Pingfang (d. 2011, finished posthumously by Jiang Zhongyi et al.). Covers the Hehuamen gate complex, Yuan residential remains, street-grid layout. *Found in a citation (secondary news coverage), not a primary library/publisher record.*
2. Not viewable free anywhere found (no PDF, Google Books, or archive.org).
3. Real excavation content exists (gate complex, residential remains, street grid) but no specific accession numbers or holder credit found in any source opened — only secondary reporting.
4. **Verdict: follow its sources, provisionally** — but locate the actual CNKI/National Library/文物出版社 catalogue record before citing it directly.

1. Both real, but **not the same venue** — a mismatch in the original lead. Exhibition: held at the **China National Silk Museum**, Hangzhou (not Dexishou), opened ~March 27–30 2026 (NEW), co-organized with Fujian Museum and Huangyan District Museum (Hangzhou News https://hznews.hangzhou.com.cn/wenti/content/2026-03/30/content_9198765.htm; Tencent https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260330A02MP200). *Opened and confirmed.*
2. No official museum/exhibition homepage, PDF or video found — only free news coverage (above).
3. Real objects behind the exhibition: **Zhao Bowen tomb** (趙伯澐墓), Huangyan District, Taizhou, Zhejiang, excavated 2016, tomb dated 1216 — round-collared gauze robe (rank-8 green), damask jacket, gauze skirt, lined jacket, embroidered shoes. **Huang Sheng tomb** (黄昇墓), Fuzhou, Fujian, excavated 1975, tomb dated 1243 — smoke-colored wide-sleeved robe, gold-stamped phoenix/peony gauze skirt, painted-trim robe, camellia-pattern garment, trousers. No accession numbers or verbatim credit lines found.
4. **Verdict: follow its sources** for the 1975 Huang Sheng and 2016 Zhao Bowen finds directly — but correct the attribution: Dexishou is a site/architecture museum (foundations, column bases, AR reconstructions — no garments on display); the textile exhibition belongs to the China National Silk Museum.

## PART B — Four period gaps

**B1. Ships 1250–1300 (rig + raisable rudder + sewn hull)**
No single hull or image combining all three traits in this exact window was found — a genuine source gap, not a search failure.
1. **Quanzhou ship** (sank shortly after 1272, excavated 1974) — rudder slot and mast/rig evidence, but explicitly **nail/resin-fastened, not sewn**. Confirmed via the Western Australian Museum's primary excavation report PDF, and Jun Kimura's Flinders University PhD/MA thesis (2011). *Opened and confirmed.*
2. Ko Si Chang 2 wreck (dated 1290±60, in-window) — also nailed; no rudder/rig data recovered. *Opened and confirmed (fastening only).*
3. UNESCO "Java Sea Wreck" page — mid/late-13th-c. date given, but no hull-fastening, rudder or rig detail. *Opened, not usable for this question.*
4. Not opened / nothing found: any sewn-hull image or wreck in this exact window for the Gulf, India or Southeast Asia.

Specific check — Green & Burningham, IJNA 27.4 (1998): **the title the brief assumed is wrong.** Per the Dutch Royal Library (KB) catalogue record (opened), the actual article is "The ship from Quanzhou, Fujian Province, People's Republic of China," not "The Ships from the Belitung Wreck." No open full-text copy found anywhere (academia.edu's search page failed to load; nothing on archive.org). Citation/catalogue-record only — flag to the illustrator that the remembered title conflates two different things.

**B2. Dadu and Shangdu buildings, excavation-dated**
No freely viewable plates/plans of excavated halls, platforms or column bases from either capital were found.
1. Shangdu: a 2015 Archaeology Magazine piece (opened) with one real photo of an excavated dragon-head roof ornament, and a parallel Ancient Origins piece (opened) confirming excavation year 2009 and naming the Inner Mongolia Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology / Inner Mongolia Normal University — but covering only decorative elements (dragon heads, glazed fish, dripstones, mandao ramps), not halls/platforms/column bases.
2. UNESCO Xanadu dossier pages (whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/4792 and .../en/list/1389/, both opened) confirm excavation happened in general terms, with no plates/plans/photos and only historical, not excavation, dates.
3. Dadu: a September 2025 China Daily piece (opened) quotes an archaeologist: "We haven't excavated an entire Yuan palace" — the only concrete material is one photographed brick with dragon motifs from the "Zaobanchu site" (no excavation date given) and a Yuan road segment found in Jingshan Park "in the early 1970s."
4. CNKI/napstic.cn index entries for 内蒙古文物考古 periodicals turned up in search but were **not opened** (likely paywalled previews).

**B3. Venice/Genoa/Ayas object dated 1270–1298 by colophon/document**
Only one item was actually opened and confirmed: a British Museum gold ducat of Doge Giovanni Dandolo of Venice (reign 1280–1289), accessed via a Harvard Art Museums mirror (https://haaimagehosting.omeka.fas.harvard.edu/items/show/1236) after the British Museum's own object page (C_1862-1001-15) failed to render (JS-only). Date printed: "1280," range 1280–1289. Viewable free via the linked full-size file and IIIF manifest. *Opened and confirmed.* No mariegola, statute book, seal, notarial-register image, mosaic or fresco was actually opened and confirmed dated 1270–1298 — a real gap, not padded.

Ayas notarial deeds edition — **confirmed**: Laura Balletto (ed.), *Notai genovesi in Oltremare: Atti rogati a Laiazzo da Federico di Piazzalunga (1274) e Pietro di Bargone (1277, 1279)*, 1989. Title/author/year corroborated from two independent sources; publisher/series number not confirmed (likely the "Notai genovesi in Oltremare" series, same family as Balbi/Raiteri 1973, but inferred, not verified). No open/free full-text copy found anywhere.
