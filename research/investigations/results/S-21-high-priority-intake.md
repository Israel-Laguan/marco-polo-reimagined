## Textual and factual findings

### Basma and Fansur (III-03)

The current [draft III-03](../../../manuscript/vol-3/ch-03-java-less-sumatra.md) says Marco *saw* the rhinoceros, and its Note [6] calls it seen from life. Yule's Basma passage does not state that, and naturalistic features alone cannot prove sight. More than one modern interpretation exists: Talbot's composite interpretation is recorded at S21-E9; the project has also read Kovesi's competing Sumatran-rhino interpretation at S18-E8. Neither species interpretation establishes a personal encounter. Revise the assertion during the chapter's planned rewrite to “the Book describes” and attribute species interpretations if included; do not erase the narrative's own unicorn and maiden contrast.

The relevant [2018 Eusebi edition of F](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-224-6/978-88-6969-224-6-ch-02_39X5zyK.pdf), ch. CLXV §§9–12, pp. 187–188, was read here in the edited text and apparatus. It also describes the Basma animal without an explicit personal-sighting declaration in that passage. The editor marks and explains some reconstructed tongue wording from Venetian V 87; this is an edition-mediated comparison, not a fresh inspection of F or V manuscript images and not a blanket statement about all witnesses. Yule's square-bracketed *crushing-and-rasping* detail requires a separate witness check; do not assume the editor's angle-bracket reconstruction is the same variant.

The Fansur tree-flour account offers a useful contrast. Yule's III.XI expressly represents Marco and his party eating the food. Eusebi's F-text, ch. CLXIX §6, p. 190, says “we” tried and ate it repeatedly. These are claims in the texts, not independent proof the event occurred. Cordier 1920 pp. 108–109 supplies historical sago-palm identifications, not proof of a specific modern species at Fansur.

The same Basma passage in Yule presents the tiny human figures as frauds made from altered monkeys; Angamanain III.XIII separately presents dehumanizing dog-head claims without that rejection. They are different narrative claims, not evidence for actual physiology or verified individual fraud cases. Cordier's Laufer material is a source for a *proposed* broader family of traditions, not proof of Polo's direct source; regionally grounded Andamanese history remains to be researched.

### Java, Nicobar and Sumatra goods (III-02–04)

Yule III.VI says Java produces six named spices and also calls it a commercial destination. Yule's own note disputes locally produced nutmeg and cloves and suggests a market. The 2023 archaeobotanical paper supports older movement of aromatics and identifies a Javan find reported from a broad-dated layer, but expressly does not settle whether that clove was imported or planted. Óc Eo's directly dated nutmeg is a Vietnam find, not a Java planting record. These do not prove local production or its absence in 1290. Preserve the Book's production claim as such, and make the re-export explanation a sourced, qualified interpretation; verify every commodity and its period separately. Do not treat a point-of-origin claim as proof that cultivation elsewhere was impossible.

Lambri's cultivated “brazil,” Nicobar cloves and Champa aloeswood remain as scoped in the report: botanical and period-specific identification is not settled merely by a modern commodity name. The FAO Barus source and UNESCO Dhofar source already checked in the [low-priority pass](S-21-low-priority-research.md) support **regional context**, not a specific thirteenth-century crop or price. Sago denotes the starch category and does not automatically identify one palm species.
