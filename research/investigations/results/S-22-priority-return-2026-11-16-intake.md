## New independent checks and what to do

### II-01, II-04: Liu Guandao's hunting scroll

The previous project session already viewed a Commons crop and recorded limited cues C3–C5 in [visual references](../../registry/visual-references.md). The new return accurately reports that **its own researchers** did not view it; that is not a reason to erase the project's prior viewed status. The crop did not show the upper landscape and does not establish the shape or cut of military lamellar armor. Preserve the existing qualifications and verify the appropriate image before final production.

The museum's [official object record](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/745?dep=P), independently opened in this intake, confirms **故畫000866N000000000**, Liu Guandao, an inscription dated 1280, and separate image options: 1-megapixel **CC0**, 6-megapixel **CC BY 4.0**, with the museum's specified attribution. These are terms for the particular NPM files, not an automatic license for every Commons or third-party reproduction. Museum record and its stated terms read; download and image quality for the planned print size were not tested. Compare the exact file and license at production and follow the required attribution. The record's metadata date line prints 至正 but its transcribed signature says **至元十七年**; do not silently copy the conflicting reign label. Both museum lines give 1280.

### I-05, II-01, II-08: Takashima

The return only found its own restricted set of sources. The project's earlier [S-23 gap intake](S-23-gaps-intake.md) and [visual registry](../../registry/visual-references.md) already record read Matsuura reports and individual evidence for lamellae, bows and arrows/quiver context. Independently, the [Asian Research Institute of Underwater Archaeology's excavation summary](https://www.ariua.org/english/projects_en/takashima_en/) lists small armor plates and says arrows appear bundled or in a quiver (plates 28–29). Summary text read, illustrations not inspected. Do not promote “seem” to a proven complete quiver or assert the return found nothing **in the project**. Historical appearance needs the actual finds and images; avoid reconstructing lacing without them. Takashima context does not imply a particular crew member's ethnicity.

### II-12 / II-14: Quanzhou hull

The project's earlier intake read Burningham and Green's 1997 primary-report chapter, pp. 32–48: nail fastenings, bulkheads, seam materials and a rudder-stock slot are recorded in the registry. The authors' approximately **28 m** reconstruction is distinct from a 1975 reconstruction of 34.6 m; neither measurement is the surviving hull. An adjustable/lifting rudder is not directly established by the Quanzhou hull. The return's encyclopedia-only limits cannot downgrade the prior direct report check or make its reconstruction dimensions settled. These remain textual evidence, not newly viewed ship imagery.

### I-04: Ayas / Laiazzo

The return's own source is a single uncited popular account; it moves from “appears almost entirely” Ottoman to “confirmed not to be 13th-century fabric.” Do not use that certainty. Independently, [Robert W. Edwards's ChArLv site record](https://charlvarchive.org/Site/76) distinguishes the **land-castle circuit**, with multiple construction periods, from a separate **island castle**, whose surviving masonry he describes as bearing Armenian construction characteristics. The record cites his *Fortifications* pp. 77–83 and other specialist works; those full studies and images were not read in this intake. This is a source-specific comparison, not proof every island wall dates to 1260–95 or that the two accounts describe precisely the same fabric. Keep reconstruction and individual phases open; avoid a categorical all-Ottoman claim.

### II-14: Quanzhou streets

The return says the UNESCO list lacks city walls. The more detailed [ICOMOS/UNESCO evaluation](https://whc.unesco.org/document/189212), describes the **excavated Deji Gate site**, built 1230 and repaired in the Yuan, Ming and Qing, and the Shunji Bridge, built 1211. The gate is evidence of an urban defensive site, not automatically a surviving, visually reconstructable street or proof every exposed stone is pre-1295. No photograph or excavation plan was viewed. The same evaluation dates Qingjing Mosque's construction to 1009 and records a 1310 restoration; present appearance cannot simply be backdated.

### III-03: Kota Cina

The NUS site report and its footnote make the timber a well-documented **lead for a likely boat component**, mostly pre-window by the reported intervals, not a blueprint or securely Polo-era vessel. The independently opened [Perret et al. 2016 excavation article](https://revue-archipel.fr/pdfnumeros/ARCHIPEL91%282016%29.pdf) also describes a 2012 boat-framing find; whether its discussion and NUS sample identify the exact same piece should be checked before merging records. The report also includes illustrations, but none was viewed in this intake and figure rights remain to be checked.

### Other guardrails

The return's statements that no period image or relief “survives” go beyond a limited search. Treat them as “not found in this pass.” The project's early-14th-century Cocharelli merchant/harbor scenes are viewed **later comparanda**, not contemporaneous 1260–95 sources. The Sant'Alipio image was previously viewed (C39); restoration and use as elite/ceremonial imagery still need care. Do not replace an evidence gap by asserting that unverified wax tablets, hull shapes, or colonial methods were necessarily used in a particular scene. Reproduction rights are file- and holder-specific; no generic commercial clearance follows from this intake.
