# Deep dive: *The World of Khubilai Khan: Chinese Art in the Yuan Dynasty* (Met, 2010)

**The catalogue itself was not opened** (the free PDF was not fetched; the Met's web pages refuse automated reading). What was done instead: its contents were read from library records, and the Met's open-access API was used to pull records and images for Met objects that bear on our scenes. One object was viewed in full.

## 1. The work
Exhibition 28 Sept 2010 – 2 Jan 2011; over 200 works, 1215–1368, mostly lent from China. Catalogue by James C. Y. Watt, with Maxwell K. Hearn, Denise Patry Leidy, Zhixin Jason Sun, John Guy, Joyce Denney, Birgitta Augustin and Nancy S. Steinhardt. Part I, "Daily Life", has **Sun on Dadu, Steinhardt on "the architecture of living and dying", Denney on Mongol dress**; then Denney on textiles and Watt on the decorative arts; further parts on religion and on painting (Hearn). Sources: [Met publication page](https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/the-world-of-khubilai-khan-chinese-art-in-the-yuan-dynasty); Ricci Institute and CiNii records. No review was found.

## 5. Takeaways
1. **Still no period picture of a tent carried on a cart.** The one painting put forward for it does not show it. I-13's ger-cart rests on Rubruck's words alone, as the registry already said.
2. A high-status steppe camp, as Chinese painters drew it, is a tent inside cloth screen walls with an open cloth pavilion in front and banners racked outside. Draft cue below, with its date warning.
3. The two-wheeled covered cart with tall spoked wheels, canopy and ladder is a ready model for a court woman's travelling cart (I-13, III-9 secondaries), again as a comparandum.
4. Five Met objects dated to the late 13th century or to 1294 exist with public-domain images and have not been looked at: 2012.399, 1997.33.1–.6, 2020.122, 11.8.4, 2015.500.1.21. These are the cheapest next cues for II-3's tableware and cloth.
5. Read Denney's and Steinhardt's essays before any further work on dress or Dadu: they are free and written by the people who handled the objects.

**Draft cue from what was viewed (not yet in the plan):**
`[PERIOD] A lord's camp on the steppe: a round domed felt tent with a square flap at the crown and a white scalloped valance under the roof, set inside a rectangle of tall cloth screens on poles; in front, an open cloth pavilion on posts with guy-ropes over a carpet; furled banners racked on a stand outside; a two-wheeled cart with tall red spoked wheels, a railed body and a pitched canopy parked beside it.`
Limit: from a Ming copy (early 15th c.) of a Song picture of Khitan-style nomads. It shows how such a camp was imagined in China, 150 years either side of Polo. Use for layout, not for Mongol-specific detail.
