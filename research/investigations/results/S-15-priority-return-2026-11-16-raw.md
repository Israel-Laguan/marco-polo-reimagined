## Question 1 — Maffeo Polo's will: date, and Marta/Juliana/Clara

**Governing fact established in this pass:** Yule-Cordier's own apparatus (3rd ed. 1903, ed. Cordier) independently documents **two different Maffeo Polos with two different testamentary acts**, and conflating them is the likely source of the "1308 vs. 1310" confusion in downstream scholarship.

### Finding 1.1 — There are two Maffeos, not one
- **Claim evaluated:** A single will of "Maffeo Polo" exists and can be dated either 1308/1309 or 1310.
- **Verdict:** Contradicted (as stated) / disputed in its premise. There are at minimum two acts: (a) the will of **Maffeo Polo, brother of Marco the Traveller** (son of Niccolò), executed at Venice **31 August 1300**; and (b) a will of **Maffeo Polo "the Elder,"** Marco's paternal uncle and travel companion, recorded as drafted **"facti in anno domini mcccviii mense Februarii die vi"** (6 February 1308 *more veneto* = 1309 modern style), known only through a **1328** judicial act on a plaint by "Marco Polo, called Marcolino" concerning a legacy from that will.
- **Source and locator:** Yule-Cordier, *The Book of Ser Marco Polo* (3rd ed., 1903, rev. Henri Cordier), vol. ii, Appendix C ("Calendar of Documents relating to Marco Polo and his Family"), Entry 2 (1300 will) and Entry 12 (1328 act, dated "Die xv. Mensis Marcii," with cross-reference "See I. p. 77"). [Project Gutenberg #12410, vol. 2](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/12410/pg12410-images.html)
- **What the source says:** Entry 2 — "Will of Maffeo Polo, brother of the Traveller, executed at Venice, 31st August, 1300" (abstract promised at vol. i, pp. 64–65). Entry 12 — a 1328 judgment on a plaint by "Marco Polo, called Marcolino," concerning a legacy from "Maffeo Polo the Elder," quoting that will's own execution date in Latin as "facti in anno domini mcccviii mense Februarii die vi."
- **Access level:** Edition/transcription read in full (direct quotation from the Gutenberg e-text of the Cordier edition).
- **Limitations:** This is a 1903 scholarly *calendar* of documents, not the archival originals; Cordier's own abstracting conventions are not independently checked against the Venetian originals in this pass. The 1328 act is an *independent later witness quoting* the earlier will's date — not the will itself inspected directly.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "Yule-Cordier's calendar of documents distinguishes a 1300 will of Marco's brother Maffeo from a will of Marco's uncle, 'Maffeo the Elder,' dated by internal citation to 6 February 1308 Venetian style (1309 modern); the two are not the same instrument and should not be merged."

### Finding 1.2 — The "1310" date is unverified
- **Claim evaluated:** Other scholarship dates "the" Maffeo will to 1310 (modern style).
- **Verdict:** Not found. No source located in this research — not Yule-Cordier, not any secondary author searched (Gasparrini Leporace, Olschki, Pelliot, Hennig, Lazari, Zorzi, Larner, or any Bartoli Langeli-circle author) — states a 1310 date for any Maffeo Polo will.
- **Source and locator:** Absence confirmed only against Yule-Cordier (as above) and the web-search-accessible secondary literature; Orlandini 1926 (the project's own cited authority for "1310") was never reached (see Gaps below).
- **What the source says:** N/A — this is an absence finding, not a positive statement from any text.
- **Access level:** Inaccessible/NOT SEEN for the one source (Orlandini 1926) that might actually contain or explain the figure.
- **Limitations:** Calendar conversion cannot resolve this by itself — 1308 Venetian-style, 1309 modern, and a putative "1310" are three different numbers, and no amount of *more veneto* arithmetic turns 1309 into 1310. The discrepancy is either (a) a transcription/rounding slip traceable to 1308/1309, (b) a different, more precise date given uniquely by Orlandini's document 6, or (c) a probate/publication date distinct from the drafting date (Venetian notarial practice could record a will's opening or probate well after its drafting).
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "A '1310' date reported in some secondary literature for a Maffeo Polo will has not been traced to a verifiable primary or secondary source in this research pass and should not be presented as established; it may describe a different instrument, or a probate date, not yet distinguished from the 1308/1309 drafting date discussed in Yule-Cordier."

### Finding 1.3 — Marta, Juliana, Clara
- **Claim evaluated:** Maffeo's will names Marta, Juliana, and/or Clara and states their relationships to him or to Marco.
- **Verdict:** Not found. No source consulted in this pass — Yule-Cordier's Appendix C, Appendix B ("The Polo Families," as excerpted), the biographical-notice sections sampled, the vol. ii index, or any secondary work found — mentions Marta, Juliana, or Clara/Chiara in connection with any Maffeo Polo. "Chiara/Clara" appears once in the Yule apparatus, but as the widow of an unrelated Ermolao (Almorò) Polo, a different branch of the family.
- **Source and locator:** Yule-Cordier, *Book of Ser Marco Polo* (3rd ed. 1903), vol. ii index and Appendix B/C. [Gutenberg #12410](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/12410/pg12410-images.html)
- **What the source says:** No relevant text exists to quote; this is a negative result from an index and appendix search, not a positive statement.
- **Access level:** Edition/transcription read for the index and Appendix C; Appendix B was read only as previously excerpted, not exhaustively re-checked. The full prose abstract of the 1300 will itself (Yule vol. i, pp. 64–66, where such names — if real — would most plausibly appear) could **not be located** in the Gutenberg e-text despite repeated offset searches; it is possible, not confirmed, that this un-recovered passage names Marta/Juliana/Clara.
- **Limitations:** This is an absence-of-evidence finding bounded by incomplete recovery of one relevant passage (vol. i, pp. 64–66) and by total non-access to Orlandini's document 6 — the project's own designated authority, and the single most likely place these names could be documented or explained.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "No source examined in this pass documents Marta, Juliana, or Clara as kin of either Maffeo Polo. If these names derive from Orlandini (1926), document 6, that text has not yet been consulted and the relationships cannot currently be verified from any source inspected."

### Finding 1.4 — The surviving facts about the 1300 brother-Maffeo will
- **Claim evaluated:** What the 1300 will (of Marco's brother Maffeo) is independently known to say.
- **Verdict:** Supported, but only for a few specific points reported by Yule, not for its full text.
- **Source and locator:** Yule, *Book of Ser Marco Polo* (3rd ed. 1903), vol. i, §22 n., §23, §43. [Gutenberg #10636, vol. 1](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/10636/pg10636-images.html)
- **What the source says:** The will "seems to imply strong regard for the testator's brother Marco, who is made inheritor of the bulk of the property, failing the possible birth of a son"; by 31 August 1300 "Messer Nicolo [the testator's and Marco's father] was no longer in life"; the will includes a bequest to an uncle, Jordan Trevisan (from which Yule infers a Trevisan mother for this Maffeo); and it was drawn up with clerks of the church of S. Felice, with annuities for prayers for the testator's parents and himself.
- **Access level:** Edition/transcription read in full (Yule's own summarizing prose, not the Latin original).
- **Limitations:** This is Yule's 19th-century *paraphrase* of the will, not a transcription of its Latin text; the Trevisan-mother point is explicitly Yule's own inference from a bequest, not a stated kinship in the will.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "Yule's abstract of the 1300 will of Marco's brother Maffeo records that Niccolò Polo (Marco's father) had already died, and that Maffeo named his brother Marco principal heir failing a son of his own; a bequest to an uncle, Jordan Trevisan, has been read by Yule as implying a Trevisan mother, but this is Yule's inference, not the will's own statement."

## Question 2 — Agnes uxor Nicolai Calbo (1319 testament)

**Access-level disclosure carried over from the notes:** the primary 1319 testament was not read, and Bolognari's full *Studi Medievali* 62 (2021) article was not reached (CISAM/Brepols paywall; no open-access copy located). All findings below rest on secondary press/academic accounts, several of which cite Bolognari 2021 only in a footnote.

### Finding 2.1 — Explicit kinship stated in secondary accounts
- **Claim evaluated:** The will names Agnes's father, husband, and other kin.
- **Verdict:** Supported, but only via intermediary sources, not the document itself.
- **Source and locator:** Reported via medievalists.net and thehistoryblog.com, both summarizing **Marcello Bolognari, "«Agnes uxor Nicolai Calbo de confinio Sancti Iohannis Grisostomi»: un nuovo documento inedito sulla famiglia Polo," *Studi Medievali*, serie 3, vol. 62, fasc. 2 (2021), pp. 745–758** (citation confirmed independently across media.fupress.com and two 2024/2025 Ateneo Veneto articles' footnote apparatus). [medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2022/02/historian-discovers-marco-polo-had-another-daughter/); [thehistoryblog.com](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/date/2022/02/07)
- **What the source says (paraphrase via intermediary):** The testatrix is named in the document's own heading as "Agnes uxor Nicolai Calbo de confinio Sancti Iohannis Grisostomi." Per the press summaries of Bolognari's article, she asks that her father **"Marco"** (given name only, no surname quoted) deliver the will to the notary-priest Pietro Pagano of San Felice; three executors (fideicommissarii) are named — her husband Nicolò ("Nicoletto") Calbo, her father Marco, and a relative, Stefano Polo; the will also names a tutor (Raffaele da Cremona), a godmother (Benvenuta), a maid (Reni), and three children (Barbarella, Papon, Franceschino).
- **Access level:** Abstract/review read (secondary press and academic footnotes); the Latin document and the full journal article were **not read**. Note: no source accessed quotes any phrase from the document itself using the surname "Polo" attached to the father — every full-name "Marco Polo" appearing in these accounts is the journalists'/scholars' own explanatory prose, not a verbatim quotation from the will.
- **Limitations:** This is a report via medievalists.net/thehistoryblog.com of Bolognari's own reading of the manuscript — an intermediary chain, not direct inspection. The claim should be attributed as "reported via medievalists.net and thehistoryblog.com, summarizing Bolognari 2021," not cited as if the testament or the journal article were read directly.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "Press accounts summarizing Bolognari (2021) report that the 1319 will of Agnes, wife of Nicolò Calbo, names her father only as 'Marco' and lists him, her husband, and a relative named Stefano Polo as executors; the surname 'Polo' for the father does not appear to be quoted from the document itself in any source consulted."

### Finding 2.2 — Archival shelfmark
- **Claim evaluated:** The testament is held at a specific ASVe shelfmark.
- **Verdict:** Disputed/unverified — reported only secondhand.
- **Source and locator:** Reported via medievalists.net (itself summarizing Ca' Foscari press materials): **"State Archive of Venice (ASVe), Notarile, Testamenti, Testamenti, b. 830, n. 36, f. 11r,"** part of the protocol of notary-priest Pietro Pagano, pievano of San Felice, found during a Ca' Foscari project coordinated by Eugenio Burgio and Antonio Montefusco. [medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2022/02/historian-discovers-marco-polo-had-another-daughter/)
- **What the source says:** As quoted above; this specific shelfmark string is not corroborated by any second independent source and was not checked against an ASVe finding aid.
- **Access level:** Abstract/review read (journalistic secondary source only); catalog metadata was not independently confirmed.
- **Limitations:** Flag this citation explicitly as "reported via press coverage of Bolognari 2021," never as confirmed from the primary document or the journal article's own footnotes.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "The testament's archival location is reported in press coverage as ASVe, Notarile, Testamenti, b. 830, n. 36, f. 11r; this shelfmark has not been independently verified against the journal article or an archival finding aid."

### Finding 2.3 — The paternity-identification is explicitly a hypothesis, not a document-internal statement
- **Claim evaluated:** The document itself identifies the father as Marco Polo the traveller, distinct from other contemporaneous Marco Polos in Venice.
- **Verdict:** Contradicted as a documentary claim / supported only as a scholarly hypothesis. Every source accessed treats the identification as inference.
- **Source and locator:** medievalists.net (summarizing Bolognari 2021); media.fupress.com (academic PDF); Il Resto del Carlino; an Ateneo Veneto footnote (Ambrosini, 2024/2025); a second Ateneo Veneto article (Schiavon). [medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2022/02/historian-discovers-marco-polo-had-another-daughter/); [media.fupress.com](https://media.fupress.com/files/pdf/24/16703/46492); [ilrestodelcarlino.it](https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/venezia/cultura/marco-polo-archivio-stato-agnese-figlia-primogenita-42e530a2); [edizionicafoscari.unive.it (Ambrosini)](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/979-12-5742-064-2/979-12-5742-064-2-ch-01.pdf); [Ateneo Veneto (Schiavon)](https://ateneoveneto.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Ateneo_Veneto_CCXI.23.1.Schiavon.pdf)
- **What the source says (quoted/paraphrased):** media.fupress.com states explicitly, in Italian, that Agnes "**potrebbe essere** una figlia illegittima di Marco Polo" ("**could be** an illegitimate daughter of Marco Polo") and that "the paternity claim rests on inference, not on a direct statement in the document." medievalists.net states the identification is "likely, not certain," and explicitly notes Bolognari's article "does not explicitly rule out other men named Marco Polo in Venice" and "does not explain how the will's references distinguish the traveller from them." The supporting circumstantial evidence cited across sources: shared parish (San Giovanni Grisostomo), the co-executor "Stefano Polo" (a known relative name in the traveller's family), chronological fit (an estimated birth window of 1295–1299, matching Marco's return from Asia ~1295 and his Genoese captivity 1298–99, before his ~1300 marriage to Donata Badoer), and Agnes's absence from Marco's own 1324 will (explained as consistent with her having predeceased him, since her own will dates to 1319).
- **Access level:** Abstract/review read for all cited sources; no primary document or full journal article inspected.
- **Limitations:** No source distinguishes the "Marco" of the 1319 will from other Venetians of that name via any document-internal marker (patronymic, occupation, residence) beyond the circumstantial case above; the Ambrosini and Schiavon footnotes each cite Bolognari 2021 only, without independently re-arguing the identification, and Schiavon's separately discovered 1366 document (ASVe, Procuratori di San Marco, Misti, b. 152, fasc. 2, naming "Agnesina" in the vernacular) does not name "Marco Polo" at all and is a **separate record**, not corroboration of the 1319 testament's paternity claim.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "Bolognari (2021) proposes, on circumstantial grounds — shared parish, a co-executor named Stefano Polo, and chronological fit with Marco Polo's biography — that Agnes's father 'Marco' is Marco Polo the traveller; this is presented in the secondary literature as a plausible but explicitly unproven hypothesis, not a fact stated in the will itself, and no source consulted rules out other contemporaneous Venetians named Marco Polo."

### Finding 2.4 — Birth date and mother: not stated, not inferable from the notes
- **Claim evaluated:** Agnes's birth date (c. 1295–1299) and mother are documented facts.
- **Verdict:** Not found as documentary fact — explicitly framed by every source as a modern estimate, and no mother is named by any source.
- **Source and locator:** medievalists.net; Il Resto del Carlino. [medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2022/02/historian-discovers-marco-polo-had-another-daughter/); [ilrestodelcarlino.it](https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/venezia/cultura/marco-polo-archivio-stato-agnese-figlia-primogenita-42e530a2)
- **What the source says:** "Her birth is estimated at 1295–1299 ... This would place her birth soon after his return to Italy and just before his imprisonment in Genoa" (medievalists.net, explicitly framed as estimate); "probabilmente nata tra il 1295 e il 1299" (Il Resto del Carlino, same framing). No source names a mother; whether Marco had an earlier partner/wife before Donata Badoer is raised explicitly as an open question, not a documented fact.
- **Access level:** Abstract/review read.
- **Limitations:** None of this is drawn from the testament itself; it is chronological reasoning by modern commentators working backward from Agnes's 1319 will and Marco's known itinerary.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "No document names a mother for Agnes or states her birth date; the commonly repeated 1295–1299 window is a modern scholarly estimate from circumstantial chronology, not a documentary fact, and should be presented as such."

### Gaps / what would resolve Question 2
- Direct access to Bolognari, *Studi Medievali* 62 (2021), fasc. 2, pp. 745–758 (via JSTOR, CISAM/Brepols, or an Italian university library) — the single highest-value next step; it would confirm the exact Latin wording of the kinship clauses, the shelfmark, and whether Bolognari himself addresses or rules out other contemporary Marco Polos.
- The original 1319 testament at the reported shelfmark (ASVe, Notarile, Testamenti, b. 830, n. 36, f. 11r) — unverified and not independently inspected.
- No source located corroborates the Agnes identification against Marco Polo's own 1324 testament via shared property, witnesses, or named associates; this gap is reported as absent from the secondary literature itself, not merely from this search.

## Question 4 — Rustichello: lay or clerical status

### Finding 4.1 — No contemporary document states his civil or clerical status
- **Claim evaluated:** A contemporary document (notarial act, chronicle, tax/property record) identifies Rustichello as lay or clerical.
- **Verdict:** Not found — confirmed as a genuine absence across every source type searched.
- **Source and locator:** Synthesis across Lagomarsini, "Le cas du compilateur compilé" (PDF); data.bnf.fr authority record for "Rusticien de Pise"; Wikipedia "Rustichello da Pisa" (citing Hoffman 1991, Lacy ed. 1991, Cigni 1994/1996). [Lagomarsini PDF](https://www.fefonlus.it/images/pdf/gg/lagomarsini/Lagomarsini%20-%20Le%20cas%20du%20compilateur%20compil%C3%A9.pdf); [data.bnf.fr](https://data.bnf.fr/12015661/rusticien_de_pise/en.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustichello_da_Pisa)
- **What the source says:** "No external source documents Rusticien's literary activity or biography at all; everything known comes only from mentions in the prologues of the *Compilation arthurienne* (Ca) and the *Devisement du monde*" (Lagomarsini, paraphrased). The BnF authority record gives only approximate dates ("12..–13..") and lists him as "Auteur," with no documentary source for profession or status.
- **Access level:** Extract/abstract read for Lagomarsini and Wikipedia; catalog metadata for data.bnf.fr. Fabrizio Cigni's full critical edition (*Il romanzo arturiano di Rustichello da Pisa*, Pisa: Pacini, 1994/1996) was **not accessed** — only cited by others.
- **Limitations:** Per the task's own framing, this absence cannot be read as proof of lay status — undocumented lower clergy are just as plausible in surviving lay archives as an undocumented layman of modest status.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "No contemporary document identifies Rustichello's lay or clerical status in either direction; this is a genuine historiographical void, not evidence that he was a layman."

### Finding 4.2 — The Arthurian-compilation naming is likely a later, spurious addition
- **Claim evaluated:** The Arthurian romance's epilogue, naming "maistre Rusticien de Pise," is reliable authorial self-identification.
- **Verdict:** Disputed — at least one specialist treats it as probably not original.
- **Source and locator:** Lagomarsini, "Le cas du compilateur compilé," discussing manuscripts 340 and 355 of the *Compilation*. [Lagomarsini PDF](https://www.fefonlus.it/images/pdf/gg/lagomarsini/Lagomarsini%20-%20Le%20cas%20du%20compilateur%20compil%C3%A9.pdf)
- **What the source says:** The epilogue naming "maistre Rusticien de Pise" and claiming authorship of the Guiron material is judged to be a later addition inserted by a "recompilateur," not the original author's own prologue (the Guiron Compilation proper has no prologue or self-naming narrator at all). A later redaction ("Gp") replaced the fictive pseudo-author "Hélie de Boron" with "Rusticien," probably relying on this same spurious epilogue.
- **Access level:** Extract/summary read (Lagomarsini's own synthesis of the manuscript scholarship, not the manuscripts themselves).
- **Limitations:** The honorific "maistre" attached here is not treated by any source as a clerical marker.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "The naming of 'maistre Rusticien de Pise' at the end of the Arthurian compilation is treated by at least one specialist (via Lagomarsini) as a probable later addition rather than the original author's self-identification, weakening its value as independent biographical evidence."

### Finding 4.3 — The Devisement du Monde prologue (BnF fr. 1116, "F") and "messire"
- **Claim evaluated:** The prologue's honorific "messire"/"messer" indicates Rustichello was a layman (or a cleric).
- **Verdict:** Not found / evidentially neutral — no source draws either conclusion.
- **Source and locator:** iris.unive.it, "Introduzione. La tradizione del Milione" (PDF), quoting BnF fr. 1116 ("F") and the "V" redaction. [iris.unive.it PDF](https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/e4239ddb-e762-7180-e053-3705fe0a3322/Introduzione.%20La%20tradizione%20del%20Milione.pdf)
- **What the source says (direct quotation from the scholarly transcription):** F's prologue: "fist retraire toutes cestes chouses a messire Rustaciaus de Pise," internally dated "au tens qu'il avoit .MCCXCVIII. anç que Jeçucrit nesqui" (1298); and "demorant en la charchre de Jene," with Rustichello stated to have been "en celle meissme chartre" (in that same prison). The independent "V" redaction: "lo qual era nela dita prixone chon el dito misie' Marcho Polo." Note: this transcription gives "messire Rustaciaus de Pise," not the "Rustacians" spelling sometimes seen in secondary literature — a minor, unresolved discrepancy.
- **Access level:** Extract read — a scholarly transcription/discussion of the manuscript, not the manuscript (Gallica BnF fr. 1116) itself, which was not viewed directly in this pass.
- **Limitations:** "Messire/messer/misser" is a generic 13th-century courtesy title usable by knights, officials, notaries, and other laymen of standing, and in some contexts by clergy; no scholar reviewed treats its use here as settling lay or clerical status, and this prologue is internal/literary evidence (part of the text being introduced), not an independent archival attestation.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "The Devisement's own prologue calls him 'messire Rustaciaus de Pise' and places him with Marco Polo in a Genoese prison in 1298; this is the text's own internal account, corroborated independently within the manuscript tradition by the separate 'V' redaction, but it is literary self-presentation, not an external archival record, and the honorific used carries no established lay/clerical implication."

### Finding 4.4 — Proposed real-world identifications are explicitly unconfirmed
- **Claim evaluated:** Rustichello can be identified with a known, documented historical Pisan individual whose status is recorded.
- **Verdict:** Not found / explicitly disputed by the scholars who proposed them.
- **Source and locator:** Lagomarsini, "Le cas du compilateur compilé." [Lagomarsini PDF](https://www.fefonlus.it/images/pdf/gg/lagomarsini/Lagomarsini%20-%20Le%20cas%20du%20compilateur%20compil%C3%A9.pdf)
- **What the source says:** Giorgio Del Guerra proposed identifying him with "Rustichello di Vico Pisano" (d. 1321–1333), from a family of notaries; André Joris recorded a "Rustike de societate Maççis," secretary to Emperor Henry VII, explicitly flagged as not automatically identifiable with the romance compiler; Adriano Michieli's proposal is characterized as mere conjecture; Richard Trachsler suggests "Rusticien" in the Compilation may simply be a borrowed name from the separate *Devisement* author-figure — i.e., not even an independent attestation.
- **Access level:** Extract/summary read.
- **Limitations:** None of these identifications is adopted as established by the source reporting them; all are explicitly hedged as unconfirmed.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "Several real-world identifications for Rustichello have been proposed (a Pisan notarial family, an imperial secretary) but each is explicitly flagged by the proposing or reviewing scholars as unconfirmed, and none supplies a documented civil or clerical status."

### Finding 4.5 — Modern scholarship treats his whole biography as reconstruction
- **Claim evaluated:** Current scholarship (Cigni, Gadrat-Ouerfelli, Larner, Ailes, Bartoli Langeli circle) has established his status as fact.
- **Verdict:** Not found — scholarship consistently presents his biography, including status, as hypothesis/inference, not documented fact; most of the named authorities' own texts were not directly accessible in this pass.
- **Source and locator:** iris.unive.it ("Introduzione. La tradizione del Milione"); Cerise Press, "Reading The Book of Marco Polo" (summarizing John Larner). [iris.unive.it PDF](https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/e4239ddb-e762-7180-e053-3705fe0a3322/Introduzione.%20La%20tradizione%20del%20Milione.pdf); [Cerise Press](https://www.cerisepress.com/03/09/reading-the-book-of-marco-polo/3)
- **What the source says:** "Rustichello è il nome a cui si attribuisce il Roman de Meliadus" (he is the name to which the Roman de Meliadus is attributed — framed as attribution, not proof); "Quali siano stati i termini della collaborazione tra i due è difficile dire" (what the exact terms of the collaboration were is difficult to say).
- **Access level:** Extract read for iris.unive.it and Cerise Press. **Not accessed**: Christine Gadrat-Ouerfelli, *Lire Marco Polo au Moyen Âge* (Brepols, 2015); John Larner, *Marco Polo and the Discovery of the World* (Yale UP, 1999) — read only via secondary summary; Marianne Ailes's articles; Fabrizio Cigni's full edition; Consolato Pellegrino; any "Bartoli Langeli circle" scholarship on Rustichello specifically — none located.
- **Limitations:** This finding is bounded by substantial non-access to the four or five scholars named by the task as most likely to engage the question directly; it is possible (not confirmed) that one of them states an explicit position not reflected in the sources actually reached.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "No modern scholarly source actually consulted in this research states a conclusion, as established fact, about Rustichello's lay or clerical status; several of the most relevant specialist works (Gadrat-Ouerfelli 2015, Larner 1999 in full, Ailes, Cigni's full edition) were not accessed in this pass and should be checked directly before the biography asserts anything on this point."

## Question 5 — Chabi's birth year; Dorjé's mother and birth date

### Finding 5.1 — Yuan shi juan 114 gives no birth date for Chabi
- **Claim evaluated:** Yuan shi juan 114 records Chabi's birth year or date.
- **Verdict:** Contradicted — the primary text gives no such date.
- **Source and locator:** *Yuan shi* 元史, juan 114 (後妃傳一, Biographies of Empresses and Consorts 1). [zh.wikisource, juan 114](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%85%83%E5%8F%B2/%E5%8D%B7114)
- **What the source says (direct quotation/translation):** "濟寧忠武王按陳之女也" — "daughter of Anchen (按陳), Prince Zhongwu of Jining" [i.e., of the Onggirat/Khunggirat clan]. "中統初，立為皇后" — "At the start of the Zhongtong era [1260], she was installed as empress." Her death is recorded with a textually corrected date (the Wikisource edition flags the transmitted "fourteenth year" as an error for "eighteenth year," matching the Shizu annals and the empresses' table, juan 106) — placing her death in the second month of Zhiyuan 18 (至元十八年), early 1281. The text states she "生裕宗" — "bore [the future] Yuzong," i.e., Zhenjin — but itemizes no other children and gives no dates for them in the passage retrieved.
- **Access level:** Edition/transcription read directly (Chinese text on Wikisource, a scholarly-edited digital transcription of the dynastic history, not the original manuscript/woodblock).
- **Limitations:** Wikisource is a crowd-maintained digital edition; its textual-correction note (14th year → 18th year) was cross-checked only against its own cited corroboration (the Shizu annals and juan 106), not against a print critical edition (e.g., Zhonghua Shuju).
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "Yuan shi juan 114 records Chabi as daughter of Anchen (Alchi Noyan), her installation as empress in 1260, and her death in early 1281 (Zhiyuan 18, 2nd month, per a textual correction); it states no birth year or birth date for her at all."

### Finding 5.2 — The "c. 1216" birth year is a modern reconstruction
- **Claim evaluated:** Chabi was born c. 1216 (sometimes given precisely as 28 January 1216).
- **Verdict:** Disputed as to source — repeated in modern reference works, but not traceable to Yuan shi, and its derivation was not independently verified.
- **Source and locator:** English Wikipedia, "Chabi," citing *Biographical Dictionary of Chinese Women, Volume II* for the "c. 1216–1281" span, and citing Yuan shi juan 114 only for the Anchen-paternity fact, not for any date. [Wikipedia: Chabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabi)
- **What the source says:** Gives "c. 1216 – 1281," with an infobox exact date of 28 January 1216; provides no derivation or reasoning for the figure, and does not claim Yuan shi itself states a birth date.
- **Access level:** Abstract/review read via Wikipedia; the underlying *Biographical Dictionary of Chinese Women, Vol. II* entry was **not independently retrieved** — its own reasoning is unseen.
- **Limitations:** A plausible (but unverified) reconstruction is that the figure is worked back from the known birth year of her eldest recorded son, Zhenjin (1240), and/or from a presumed interval before her 1260 installation as empress — but this is this research's own speculative reconstruction of method, not a confirmed citation of anyone's stated reasoning. Morris Rossabi's *Khubilai Khan: His Life and Times* (1988) and Louis Hambis's "Le chapitre CVII du Yuan che" — the two works most likely to state and justify this figure rigorously — were **not accessed**.
- **Cautious wording for the biography:** "Chabi's commonly cited birth year of c. 1216 derives from modern reference works (e.g., the *Biographical Dictionary of Chinese Women*), not from Yuan shi itself, and the chain of reasoning behind the figure has not been independently verified in this research; it should be presented as a secondary estimate, not a primary-source date."

### Finding 5.3 — "Dorjé": no documented figure matching the task's description was located
- **Claim evaluated:** Yuan shi juan 107 (or 114) records a prince "Dorjé"/"Dorji" with a named mother and birth date, connected to Chabi's or Marco Polo's narrative.
- **Verdict:** Not found. This sub-question could not be answered and should not be treated as resolved by any family tree not itself examined.
- **Source and locator:** Searches of zh.wikisource for juan 107 (宗室世系表) and juan 139 failed (page-not-found) in this pass; a glossary (ChineseNotes) lists a name "安定王朶兒只班" (Dorjiban, Prince of Anding) in juan 107's word-frequency index only, with no parentage or dates given; CBDB's kinship record for Darmabala (答剌麻八剌, 1264–1292, Zhenjin's second son) lists his sons as the future Renzong, the future Wuzong, and Amugha — no son named Dorje/朵兒只. [ChineseNotes glossary, juan 107](https://chinesenotes.com/analysis/yuanshi/yuanshi107_analysis.html); [CBDB via data.ctext.org](https://data.ctext.org/entity/544105); [Wikipedia: Kököchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6k%C3%B6chin) (no Dorje/Dorji mentioned at all, and no connection to Marco Polo's escort mission found)
- **What the source says:** No positive statement exists to quote; the ChineseNotes glossary entry gives a bare name with no gloss, parentage, or date. The CBDB record positively lists Darmabala's sons without any Dorje among them.
- **Access level:** Inaccessible/NOT SEEN for Yuan shi juan 107 itself (the specific table requested) — direct retrieval attempts failed. Abstract/metadata-level for the ChineseNotes and CBDB entries.
- **Limitations:** This is a genuine unresolved identification problem, not a confirmed negative about history — juan 107 itself was never actually read, only glossary metadata about it. - **Cautious wording for the biography:** "No documentary basis for a prince 'Dorjé' with a stated mother and birth date, connected to Chabi or to Marco Polo's escort of Kökechin, was located in this research. ## Cross-cutting disagreements and source-chain attributions

- **Maffeo will dating:** Yule-Cordier's apparatus itself documents two distinct dates for two distinct Maffeos (1300 for the brother; 1308 Venetian style/1309 modern for the uncle/"Elder," known only via a 1328 citing act). A "1310" figure appearing elsewhere in scholarship is **unverified** and not demonstrably the same instrument as either of these two. Calendar conversion (Venetian *more veneto* to modern style) explains the 1308→1309 shift but cannot by itself explain a jump to 1310; this gap should be presented to the reader as open, not resolved by arithmetic.
- **Source-chain attribution for Maffeo–Marta marriage (project's existing chain):** reported via Burgio, citing Bolognari–Simion — not independently verified in this pass (no note located a source discussing "Marta" in connection with Maffeo at all; see Finding 1.3). This chain should continue to be cited as "reported via Burgio citing Bolognari–Simion," not upgraded.
- **Source-chain attribution for Bellela–Bertuccio Querini marriage:** the project's existing chain is "reported via Bolognari, citing Gallo." This research pass independently found a **different** secondhand chain for the same marriage claim — Italian Wikipedia reporting it via **Bergreen (2007)**, citing no visible Gallo citation. These two chains (Bolognari→Gallo, and Bergreen) were not reconciled in this pass; it is unconfirmed whether they converge on the same ultimate source. Both should be kept separate and labeled by their own intermediary until Gallo's or Bergreen's text is read directly.
- **Agnese paternity:** every source that discusses it explicitly flags the identification with "Marco Polo the traveller" as an inference built on circumstance (parish, co-executor's surname, chronology, absence from the 1324 will), not a documentary certainty; no source rules out other contemporaneous Marco Polos in Venice. This should not be conflated with the separate, independently discovered 1366 "Agnesina" document (Schiavon), which does not name any Marco Polo at all.
- **Multiple Maffeo Polos:** this report, following Yule-Cordier, distinguishes **Maffeo Polo, Marco's brother** (will of 1300) from **Maffeo Polo "the Elder,"** Marco's uncle and travel companion (will cited in the 1328 act). Any biography text must keep these separate; Orlandini's unreached "document 6" could concern either, or neither.
- **Rustichello's status:** the absence of any document on lay/clerical status is itself the finding; per the task's explicit instruction, this absence must not be converted into a default assumption of lay status.
- **Chabi/Dorjé:** the "c. 1216" birth year for Chabi is a secondary estimate of unverified derivation, not a Yuan shi statement; "Dorjé" could not be matched to any documented figure with a stated mother or birth date, and no connection to Chabi or to Marco Polo's own narrative was found. Any family tree in circulation that asserts these relationships has not been examined against the primary Yuan shi text in this pass.
