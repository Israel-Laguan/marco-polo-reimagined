# S-21 Pass 2: Marco Polo publication-critical research synthesis

**Note on structure:** This report follows the five-section structure (A–E) specified in the original research brief, rather than the generic narrative report template, because the brief explicitly mandates this format for downstream editorial use. Throughout, findings are tagged by evidentiary type: **(1) Book** = the medieval Book's own claim + locator; **(2) Editor** = Yule's/Cordier's (or Moule–Pelliot's) editorial notes; **(3) Independent** = independently attested historical/archaeological/biological context; **(4) Inference** = the researchers' own reasoning. No geological, biological, or archaeological fact is presented below as evidence of Polo's personal eyewitness status. Items marked **NEW** are post-2013 scholarship, as flagged by the original researchers.

## (D) Named disagreements and unresolved questions

- **Moule–Pelliot's manuscript basis (Z vs. F):** The research brief's apparent premise that Moule–Pelliot (1938) is based on the Zelada/Toledo "Z" manuscript is **incorrect**; Volume 1's English text is explicitly "the first complete English version of F" (Paris, BnF fr. 1116), with Z reprinted separately, as a parallel critical text, in Volume 2 (not located in this pass). This is a factual correction to the brief, not a disagreement among sources.
- **San Marco qingbai jar attribution:** Contested between (a) Oscar Raphael's 1932 Polo attribution and (b) a rival Fourth Crusade (1204) Byzantine-loot provenance; Lin & Zhang 2018 date the jar's type to the Polo-contemporary period but explicitly do not resolve ownership, using only "could have" language. SEACS's blog treats the two explanations as live alternatives, "just as likely."
- **Tingui = Dehua vs. Changting:** Lin & Zhang (following Feng Chengjun) favor Dehua on scale grounds (Changting's Song-period industry judged too small); no source found rebuts this directly, but the brief explicitly flags Changting as a named rival that should not be erased from the retelling.
- **Roc = Aepyornis vs. vulture/condor vs. fossil eagle:** Unresolved across all sources. Yule's own note already offers four competing accounts. Buffetaut (2019, **NEW**) explicitly calls the Aepyornis-eggs-inspired-the-legend thesis "a disputed question." Bianconi's vulture/condor hypothesis has no post-2013 scholarly standing-check located. The AMNH museum synthesis explicitly states "The Roc is more than a misidentified Aepyornis."
- **Whether Yule flags the Socotra whale-hunt method as Ramusio-only:** Not resolved to full confidence. Yule explicitly invokes Ramusio only for the whale-*species* identification (sperm whale), and the researcher notes that Yule elsewhere in the edition is demonstrably willing to flag Ramusio-specific readings by name (place names, numerals) — making the *absence* of such a flag on the hunting-method details notable, but this is presented as a cautious inference from an absence, not a proven fact. A targeted read of Moule & Pelliot's critical apparatus, Benedetto's critical Italian edition, or Cordier's 1920 notes volume was recommended but not completed.
- **The barrel/cask-lashing ambergris detail:** Explicitly **unlocated** in any source inspected (Socotra or Madagascar passages, Ramusio or otherwise). The researcher flags this as possibly a conflation with Basque/Biscayan or Nantucket whaling lore (which does use barrel imagery, but for lookouts/oil storage, not harpooning) — and recommends this detail be omitted or explicitly flagged as unverified if it appears in a draft.
- **Pamir fire-dims-and-food-cooks-slower single-cause conflation:** Polo's text presents both effects as caused by "this great cold," but the research identifies two genuinely separate mechanisms — fuel quality (plus a secondary reduced-O2-partial-pressure effect) for dim flames, and boiling-point depression at altitude for slow cooking. Yule's own footnote (quoting Montgomerie) already partially registers this split by judging the claim "perfectly correct" for boiling but doubtful for roasting. This is not a disagreement between sources so much as an under-differentiation in both Polo's text and a naive modern retelling that should be corrected.
- **Singing-dunes evidence gap for Taklamakan/Lop Nor specifically:** The only modern acoustic/geophysics field studies located (2015 Caltech/Cambridge study) cover Death Valley and the Mojave Desert, not Lop Nor or the Taklamakan. The Lop-area link rests solely on Yule's own citation of an unspecified (in excerpt) 10th-century Chinese textual account near Kwachau/Guazhou, plus the modern Mingsha Shan tourist site near Dunhuang — whose Sima Qian attribution (via Wikipedia) was explicitly **not independently verified**. This gap should remain a gap in the final retelling, not be resolved by analogy to Death Valley.
- **Eagle/gem-valley motif's absence from Polo's own text, and unverified Epiphanius content:** Confirmed absent from Polo's book (it is a Sindbad/1001 Nights motif, independently attested via Buzurg ibn Shahriyar, c. mid-10th c.). Separately, Epiphanius's *De Gemmis* is a genuine 4th-century treatise on gemstones (breastplate stones), surviving only in Old Georgian with Armenian/Coptic fragments — but **no source read confirms that the extant text actually contains the eagle-and-meat diamond-retrieval episode**; this should remain an open, unresolved question, not be asserted either way.
- **Rubruck's tree vs. Polo's cups — materially distinct:** Confirmed as two different devices/claims by direct reading of a source describing Rubruck's silver tree (a mechanically engineered fountain operated by hidden human servants) against Polo's claim (cups allegedly moving by unassisted magic, witnessed by "more than 10,000 people"). No Yule footnote links them in the material reached, and the source describing Rubruck's tree nowhere mentions Polo, the Bacsi, or self-moving cups. These should never be conflated as the same motif in the retelling.
- **Pearl-fishery taxation across periods (diachronic variation, not a single "Polo-era" figure):** Independently dated sources give differing ratios — Polo's text: 1/10 to king, 1/20 to charmers; Ma Huan (1421–33): royal-monopoly confiscation model; Wang Ta Yuan (14th c.): half to the king; Barbosa (16th c.): fixed wages, large royal monopoly income; a 2023 popular source (undated division): one-third to diver, rest to government via auction. The researcher explicitly concludes this reflects real variation by period/ruler/location, not a single stable "Polo-era baseline independently confirmed elsewhere."
- **Nasij/"nac" uniformity across workshops:** Explicitly flagged as **not established** — the Met's single cataloged nasij specimen, combined with Allsen's documented mechanism of artisans being forcibly relocated across a multi-region empire, implies regional production variation, and the retelling should avoid asserting a single standardized "nac" product.
- **Toyo Bunko viewer 403 status:** The researcher could not re-test the exact originally-failing URL; a different NII viewer (dsr.nii.ac.jp) for the same underlying Toyo Bunko collection now works for at least the title page of Pelliot's *Notes*, Vol. 1. Whether this constitutes a genuine fix of the earlier 403, versus a different access route to the same resource, remains an open inference, not a confirmed resolution.
