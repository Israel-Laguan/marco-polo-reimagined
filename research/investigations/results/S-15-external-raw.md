# Marco Polo: the documentary record vs. the legend

Nov 11, 2026 · @Someone

About a hundred archival documents (1280–1388) carry the record of the Polo family; the best-known stories (the jewel feast, Curzola, the origin of 'Milione') rest on Ramusio's 1559 preface or on one Dominican chronicle written before 1334. Several standard 'facts' need correcting: the inventory dates from February 1324, not 1366; Maffeo was married; Niccolò was alive in 1300; the place of capture is still open.

Evidence classes in the Finding column: **DOCUMENT** (archival record), **EARLY TESTIMONY** (a 14th-century writer, or the book's own prologue), **RAMUSIO TRADITION** (1559), **MODERN INFERENCE** (named scholar), **LEGEND** (no traceable source). **NEW** marks work published after 2013. Shelfmarks are quoted from the editions named; I saw no archival original. 'via' means I did not open that work and cite it through the one named. No page numbers are given unless I saw them in the citing source.

**Where scholars disagree.** Orlandini (1926) had Marco the elder settle at Constantinople first and call his brothers out to join him. Jacoby (2006) reads the 1280 will the other way: the elder Marco's Constantinople and Soldaia years belong to the 1270s, and Niccolò and Maffeo left Venice only in 1260. Tanase (2025) accepts Jacoby's dating of the will but not the late departure, because it would turn the pregnant wife left behind into fiction. On Elena, Molà suggested an annulment obtained while Niccolò was presumed dead; Orlando (2025) rejects this on canon-law grounds and holds that Elena cannot be the traveller's mother.

**Where scholars disagree.** Curzola is still the majority view: Burgio (2024) writes 'according to most', Cigni (2017) calls it 'the most probable', and Gullino (2015) states it as fact. But its only authority is Ramusio, 260 years after the event. Iacopo d'Acqui is early and close to Venetian Dominicans, yet his date cannot be right as written. Moule's small-engagement hypothesis rescues Iacopo's place and year at the cost of positing an unrecorded fight. Zorzi (2000) is reported to have leaned towards Iacopo; I could not check him. A retelling can safely say that Marco was taken in the Venetian–Genoese war of 1294–99 and was in a Genoese prison in 1298; the place and year of capture should be given as alternatives.

**Where scholars disagree.** Benedetto (1928) and Bertolucci Pizzorusso (1977) established the two-voice model that Cigni and Burgio follow: Marco dictates or supplies notes, Rustichello writes. Tanase (2016, 2025) shrinks Rustichello's part further by moving the completion to Venice.

What all modern scholars share is that the name is older than the book's fame and was not coined for its 'tall tales'. Second, the 1305 act: Tanase reports Gallo (1955) as assigning the 'Milion' of 1305 to Marco the elder, who would then have outlived his 1280 will and entered the nobility; most other writers take it for the traveller. Reports that the by-name was also applied to Niccolò and other relatives appear in secondary literature I could not trace to an edition.

**Where scholars disagree.** On the death date, see 6.10. On the will's tone, Bartoli Langeli (2019) reads its narrow family horizon as the mark of an isolated and perhaps resentful man; Bolognari and Simion (2024) read the same facts as a deliberate strategy to keep the estate from the collateral branches. On Marco's standing, Bolognari (2020) uses the 1323 act to argue that he was a trusted associate of the Dominicans and that they helped revise the book; this is now the working hypothesis of the Ca' Foscari group (Burgio, Montefusco, Andreose) rather than a proven fact.

**Where scholars disagree.** Hardly at all on classification: Burgio (2024) treats Ramusio as the first maker of the Polo myth, writing from much the same thin record we have plus neighbourhood hearsay. The open point is how much weight Iacopo d'Acqui can bear. Benedetto (1928) and Chiesa (2004) kept open the possibility that Iacopo's Polo chapters reflect a fuller lost text; Bolognari (2024) confirms Iacopo's authorship of them but finds his biographical details partly confused.

## Questions I could not resolve

- [ ] **Was Marco in Venice in October 1298?** A recent non-scholarly summary says Bolognari discusses a document in which Marco refers to a colleganza concluded in Venice in October 1298, which would rule out capture at Curzola in September. I found no publication stating this. Check Bolognari & Simion 2024 and the CDP.
- [ ] **Basis of the 1300 marriage date.** No source I opened cites a contract or dowry receipt for Marco and Donata. Check Orlandini 1926 and the CDP for the dowry acts.
- [ ] **Which Marco is 'Milion' in the Great Council act of 10 April 1305?** I have Gallo's dissent only through Tanase. Check Moule & Pelliot 1938, vol. 1, documents, and Gallo 1955.
- [ ] **Text of Moule's capture hypothesis.** I report the '1296 skirmish' view through Chiappori. Check Moule & Pelliot 1938, vol. 1, introduction.
- [ ] **Details of the 1311 musk judgment.** Quantities and the exact date need checking against Yule's document appendix or the CDP.
- [ ] **Niccolò's death in 1300.** This rests on Gullino's reading of the Candia notary Pietro Pizolo. Does the CDP accept that the Niccolò Polo in Crete is the traveller's father?
- [ ] **Date and terms of the Polo purchase at San Giovanni Grisostomo.** Not found.
- [ ] **Treaty of 1299.** Date (25 May), place (Milan) and the prisoner clause should be checked in the edition of the Genoese Libri Iurium (Pallavicino 2001).
- [ ] **Where the prisoners were held.** I found no study that tests the Palazzo San Giorgio tradition against Genoese records. Cambi 2016 on the prison milieu is the place to look.
- [ ] **Fate of the tomb.** Napoleonic destruction (Gullino) or loss in the rebuilding of 1592–1602? Sansovino 1581 and the 1663 Martinioni edition, De Min 1990 and the reported searches of 1908 and 1923 were not seen.
- [ ] **Excavation report.** I saw only the abstract of Agazzi & Cester 2024; the dating of the Polo phase and the place of publication need confirming.
- [ ] **The 'sire Marc Pol' at Famagusta, 21 January 1293.** Cited by Bolognari 2020 from Bertolucci Pizzorusso 1988; whether this can be the traveller, who was then on the voyage from China, was not established.
- [ ] **'Milion' applied to Niccolò or other relatives.** Asserted in secondary literature; no edition traced.
- [ ] **Zorzi 2000 and Orlando 2023.** Neither was accessible; their positions are given second-hand or not at all.
- [ ] **CDP numbering.** The online edition blocked automated access, so I could not give CDP document numbers beyond the two that Burgio cites (nos. 25 and 57).
