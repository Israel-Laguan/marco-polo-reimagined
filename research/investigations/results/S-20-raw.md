## Prose Notes on Disputed Claims

**Manuscript count.** Three specialist sources give different figures: Cruse (2015) counts 135 manuscripts produced before 1530; Gadrat-Ouerfelli (2015) counts 141 surviving manuscripts; Whitfield (2019) gives approximately 135. The figure of 150 circulates in popular and semi-popular writing but is not confirmed by any peer-reviewed source in this corpus. For your retelling, the safest formulation is "approximately 135–141 manuscripts survive, in over ten languages" with a note that counts vary by cut-off date and definition of "fragment." (Cruse, 2015; Gadrat, 2015; Whitfield, 2019)

**Thibaut de Cepoy / Charles of Valois.** The French prologue tradition (Fr witnesses) records that in 1307 Marco Polo gave a copy to Thibaut de Cepoy, emissary of the French king Philip IV, for transmission to Charles of Valois. This is reported as a tradition in the manuscripts themselves, not as an independently documented archival event. The corpus confirms the tradition but does not provide an independent archival record. (Conte et al., 2020)

**Columbus's copy and timing.** A letter from John Day to Columbus, discovered in the Simancas archives in 1956 and dated winter 1497/98, confirms Day sent Columbus the Leeu 1483–84 Latin edition in response to his request. One source in the corpus notes that although Columbus is often said to have been inspired by Polo to undertake his 1492 voyage, the surviving annotated copy was not in his possession until 1497. (Coneys, 2018; Hellinga, 2014)

**"Il Milione" etymology.** The corpus confirms the hypothesis that the nickname derives from the Polo family estate name Vilione/Milione, not from "a million lies." The "lies" reading is a folk etymology with no scholarly support in this corpus. (Garkavets, 2017)

**Pasta and the *Macaroni Journal*.** The corpus cites issues of the *New Macaroni Journal* from 1920 and 1921 as the source of the Marco Polo–pasta story, not 1929 as sometimes stated. The often-cited "1929" date is not confirmed here; the earliest traceable trade-press claim appears to be 1920–21. (Gray, 2017)
