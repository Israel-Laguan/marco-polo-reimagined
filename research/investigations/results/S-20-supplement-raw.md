# Marco Polo’s **Reception and Attribution**: A Source-Qualified Supplement

The supplied evidence fills several reception and attribution gaps, but leaves the Chinese translation chronology and many earliest-attribution questions unresolved. The strongest additions concern Chinese and Mongolian reception, Carlsson’s ice-cream claim, Columbus’s surviving volume, and distinctions between historical objects and their later association with Polo.

The fol. **66v / 1499 / 1501** claim cannot be resolved from the indexed passages. The research trace records partial checking, but is not a substitute for the missing passage. Do not print either date as a date for all annotations.

For the Catalan Atlas, Jung’s abstract interprets **Taprobana as mixing information about Ceylon, Sumatra, Japan, and Korea**; this is not equivalent to demonstrating a separate Chipangu island or label (Jung & Jung, 2015). Vagnon also cautions that Islamic contributions have been underestimated, preventing a simple “derived from Polo” description of the atlas as a whole (Vagnon, 2021).

**Z/Ghisi needs disentangling.** Mascherpa’s conclusions concern Ramusio’s Quinsai material: he favors additions from Polo-associated notes, but considers interpolation from other Venetian travel collections as an alternative (Mascherpa, 2018). They do not establish a Fra Mauro legend’s dependence on Ghisi. Montefusco separately proposes a Dominican milieu for early Z diffusion; that remains a contextual attribution, not proof of a particular map’s exemplar (Montefusco, 2020).

Cruse’s supplied excerpt dates **F to approximately 1310–20**, not 1320–30; the latter claim therefore still needs its own source (Cruse, 2017). No usable passages settle the Bern shelfmark, Foligno watermark argument, Gallica identifiers, Dom Pedro book gift, Ravenstein transcription page, or a Jesuit comparison of Polo’s silences.
