# Deep dive: Yuan-period ships as reconstructed (Quanzhou hull, Takashima, Sasaki, Delgado)

## 1. Period fit
Quanzhou hull: latest coin 1272 [P]; "about 1277" [W]. Takashima: the fleet of 1281. Both inside 1260–1295. The Sinan ship (1323) is 30 years late.

## 3. Research trail
Excavation of 7 June – 31 August 1974 at Houzhu, reported in *Wenwu* 1975 [W]; Green 1983 (IJNA 12.3) and Green and Burningham 1998 (IJNA 27.4), based on the Chinese reports, their own examination and Burningham's analysis [W]; Sasaki, MA thesis, Texas A&M, 2008, book 2014 or 2015 (sources differ) [M][U]; Ikeda Yoshifumi's Takashima project.

## 4. Criticism
- [W] itself warns that its translation of the Chinese reports "may contain errors".
- T. Conlan's review of Sasaki (per a search summary, not read): the archaeology is the strength, the history weaker, and the book predates the 2011 intact-hull find.
- [W] and [P] differ on the sinking date (c. 1277 against "a few years after 1272").

## 5. Takeaways
1. A sea-going Fujian trader of the 1270s was about 34 m long and nearly 10 m in beam: broad, deep, and V-bottomed. (Quanzhou hull.)
2. Its hold was cut into thirteen compartments by twelve plank bulkheads, and the cargo for II-14 is known: bundles of incense wood, pepper, frankincense. Polo's pepper is literally in the hull.
3. **Nothing above the waterline survives.** Masts, batten sails, deck houses and the lifting rudder (III-2) must come from period paintings or from later ships; a plate should not claim the hull as its source for them.
4. The Khan's fleets were mixed: many small river craft beside a few large sea ships. III-9's "13 junks" should not be thirteen identical great ships.
5. Hull seams and the keel sides were packed with white putty; anchors were wooden, V-shaped, with stone stocks (stone stocks from memory: check).
