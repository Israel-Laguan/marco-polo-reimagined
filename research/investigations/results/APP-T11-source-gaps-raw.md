## 1. Wen’s Crocodilian Records

**Status: Wen et al. (1980) not seen directly (reported via Iijima et al. 2022).** Neither Wen’s distribution table nor Iijima’s supplementary table S1 is available in the supplied excerpts.

### Bibliographic Correction

Iijima et al.’s bibliography, **p. 9, reference 81**, identifies the source as:

> “Wen H, Xu J, Huang Z, He Y. 1980 The preliminary analysis of the distribution and the cause of change of *Crocodilus porosus* of China in the historical periods.”

The following lines give the publication as *J. Shanghai Norm. Univ. (Nat. Sci.)* **3: 109–121** (Iijima et al., 2022).

This differs materially from the proposed author list and from the proposed identification as a Malayan-gharial study: the reference names **Wen, Xu, Huang, and He**, and its title explicitly names *Crocodilus porosus*. The reference does **not** establish the exact Chinese title, the authors’ complete Chinese names, or whether “3” should be expanded as an issue rather than a volume; these details should remain unverified.

### Geographic Scope

Iijima et al., **§3(a)(iv), “Distribution,” p. 3**, state:

> “Archaeological records and historical literature revealed the past occurrences of *H. sinensis* across Fujian, Guangdong, Guangxi, and Hainan provinces in southeastern China from the late fourth millennium BC to the mid second millennium AD” (Iijima et al., 2022).

Their discussion, **§4(c), p. 7**, describes the historical animal as “most likely *H. sinensis*,” rather than treating every historical identification as taxonomically certain (Iijima et al., 2022).

**Unresolved:** these passages cannot establish whether Wen includes additional undated, disputed, or differently classified Yunnan records. Nor can Iijima’s geographic summary prove that Wen explicitly excludes Yunnan.

**Endnote disposition:** retain the distinction between “Yunnan is absent from the reported selection” and “Wen excludes Yunnan.” Only the former is presently defensible, and the supplied claim that S1 contains exactly twenty records has not been independently checked here.

## 5. Dutschke and Pipino

### Dating and Its Evidence

> “1310 (date of the will of Maffeo Polo; Pipino reported Maffeo’s deathbed words …)”  
> “1322 (date of the completion of Pipino’s *Chronicon*, which mentions the translation …)” (Gadrat, 2022).

Her **note 3, p. 297**, directs readers to **Dutschke 1993, pp. 216–217**, together with **Gadrat-Ouerfelli 2015, pp. 66–67** (Gadrat, 2022).

An additional excerpt from the 2020 Dominican collection likewise attributes a 1310–1322 dating to Gadrat-Ouerfelli, although its extracted page reference is typographically ambiguous and should not be silently expanded (Conte et al., 2020).

### Commission and Vernacular Exemplar

Gadrat-Ouerfelli, **p. 288**, says:

> “The official character of this translation has been questioned, notably by Consuelo Dutschke” (Gadrat, 2022).

Her **note 8, p. 297**, cites **Dutschke, p. 216** (Gadrat, 2022). This does **not** establish whether Dutschke specifically accepts or rejects Yule’s **1315 Bologna General Chapter** conjecture.

On the exemplar, Gadrat-Ouerfelli, **p. 287**, explicitly identifies **VA** as the version used by Pipino, citing Andreose’s linguistic work (Gadrat, 2022). A separate specialist contribution reproduces Pipino’s own description, at **Chronicon XXIV.71**, as:

> “a me in Latinum ex vulgari ydiomate Lombardico translato”

Translation: **“translated by me into Latin from the Lombard vernacular.”** Its apparatus locates the passage in **Modena, Biblioteca Estense, α.X.1.5, fol. 115ra**; this is a reproduced transcription, not direct manuscript inspection (Conte et al., 2020).

### Manuscript Count

Gadrat-Ouerfelli, **2022, p. 289**, reports:

> “Extant in sixty manuscripts, Pipino’s version can be traced throughout Europe” (Gadrat, 2022).

Her **note 13, p. 297**, attributes this count to **her 2015 monograph, p. 63**, not to Dutschke’s census (Gadrat, 2022). Likewise, the later figure of **104 manuscripts deriving directly or indirectly from VA** counts a broader transmission category, not Pipino’s Latin witnesses alone (Simion et al., 2026).

**Endnote disposition:** cite Gadrat-Ouerfelli directly for **1310–1322**, **VA**, and **sixty manuscripts**. Dutschke’s exact dating formulation, VA sub-family, census total, and judgment on the 1315 conjecture remain unverified.

### Item 7: Ahmad and Z

**NEW:** Andreose distinguishes Ramusio’s source from the surviving Zelada witness, suggesting that Ramusio used a text containing additional authentic variants relative to the Zelada manuscript’s model (Andreose, 2018). **NEW:** Mascherpa interprets Z as a second redaction prepared in Venice, probably in a Dominican environment; this is a broader textual-history argument, not an Ahmad-chapter collation (Mascherpa, 2025).

**Editorial consequence:** neither “Z contains Ahmad” nor “R alone preserves Ahmad” is verified here.

### Item 8: Fujian Sugar-Refiners

**Chinese source behind Phillips: not found.** No inspected passage identifies *Fuzhou fuzhi*, *Minshu*, or another Chinese work as Phillips’s actual source.

Mazumdar’s available description concerns long-term sugar production, trade, technology, and consumption, without discussing Phillips, Unken, western refiners, or the alleged Yuan introduction (Mazumdar, 1998). **Editorial consequence:** preserve Phillips’s statement as an attributed report, not as independently verified Chinese corroboration of Ramusio.

### Item 9: Ceylon Chronology

**Geiger’s tables and Paranavitana’s regnal chronology: not seen directly.** Their exact assignments cannot be supplied.

One directly available specialist passage is decisive about the older chronological uncertainty. Hultzsch, **p. 531, §IX**, writes:

> “Bhuvanekabahu I would have died shortly after the commencement of A.D. 1288” (Hultzsch, 1913).

He immediately qualifies this:

> “If the date given in the Attanagaluvamsa is correct, the same event would have to be placed four years earlier, in A.D. 1284” (Hultzsch, 1913).

The first calculation sums traditional reign lengths from Sahasamalla’s coronation; the second depends on the *Attanagaluvamsa* date. Neither is Geiger’s or Paranavitana’s later table. Hultzsch also identifies the relevant Pāṇḍyan Kulasekhara as **Māravarman Kulasekhara I**, explicitly withdrawing his earlier identification as Kulasekhara II (Hultzsch, 1913).

**Unresolved:** the requested five reign/interregnum ranges, the ruler in 1284, the ruler in 1292–1293, the Yiheimishi embassy synchronization, and a specialist identification of “Sendemain.” No identification should be inferred merely by choosing the ruler whose proposed dates overlap Polo’s visit.
