## 1. Intake Findings by Queue Item

### `APP.T11a` — Wen et al. (1980) Crocodilian Records
- **Target:** Primary distribution table in Wen et al. (1980) for dated Carajan/Yunnan occurrences.
- **Finding:** Iijima et al. (2022, p. 9 ref. 81) reveals that Wen et al. (1980) is:
  > Wen H, Xu J, Huang Z, He Y. 1980. "The preliminary analysis of the distribution and the cause of change of *Crocodilus porosus* of China in the historical periods." *J. Shanghai Norm. Univ. (Nat. Sci.)* 3: 109–121.
- **Analysis:** The title specifically names *Crocodilus porosus* (saltwater crocodile), not Malayan gharial; the authors are Wen, Xu, Huang, and He. Iijima et al. (2022, §3(a)(iv), p. 3) tabulate historical occurrences across southeastern China (Fujian, Guangdong, Guangxi, Hainan); Yunnan remains absent from their reported selection. Wen's original Chinese article and full distribution table were not seen directly.
- **Disposition:** Bibliographic correction made to `@wen1980malai` in `bibliography.bib`. The hedge in `vol-2/ch-07-tebet-carajan.md` Note [25] stands: Carajan is absent from the reported selection, but Wen's original table remains uninspected directly.

### `APP.T11b` — Moule (1957) & Hangzhou Boundary Counts
- **Target:** A. C. Moule (*Quinsai*, 1957, pp. 23–24) and the underlying Chinese gazetteer passages behind Mascherpa (2024, p. 138) for the 117 city / 230 environs bridge split and 35-*li* perimeter.
- **Finding:** Target pages of Moule (1957) and Mascherpa (2024) were not seen directly. Modern bridge study by Mu et al. (2024) pertains to Suzhou (Pingjiang), not Hangzhou.
- **Disposition:** Hedge stands in `vol-2/ch-13-kinsay.md` Note [3], `vol-3/ch-12-reliability-essay.md` Note [24], and `facts.md` (`S17-F5`). Do not treat 35 *li* and 347 bridges as verified measurements of the same footprint until Moule's text and the underlying Chinese gazetteer *juan* are inspected.

### `APP.T11c` — Burmese Chronicle Opening Engagement
- **Target:** Opening narrative of the Mongol-Burmese war in Pe Maung Tin & Luce (*Glass Palace Chronicle*, 1923) to check Yule's assertion of omission.
- **Finding:** Target chronicle pages not exposed in retrieved text. Shiratori (1951) debates the location of Ngasaunggyan (near Bhamo vs Nang-song), not whether an opening engagement was omitted. Goh (2009) cautions that *Tarup* changed in meaning over time.
- **Disposition:** Hedge stands in `vol-2/ch-08-mien-bangala.md` Note [10]. Yule's statement of omission remains an attributed report, not an independently verified fact about the Burmese chronicle.

### `APP.T11e` — Pipino Dating, Transmission, and Exemplar
- **Target:** Dutschke (1993, pp. 216–217) on the dating and manuscript transmission of Francesco Pipino's Latin translation.
- **Finding:** Substantive specialist report from Christine Gadrat-Ouerfelli (2022, p. 287; citing Dutschke 1993, pp. 216–217, and Gadrat-Ouerfelli 2015, pp. 66–67):
  - **Dating Window:** 1310–1322 (bounded by Maffeo Polo's 1310 will, whose deathbed words Pipino reports, and the 1322 completion of Pipino's *Chronicon* mentioning the translation).
  - **Vernacular Exemplar:** Pipino describes his source text (*Chronicon* XXIV.71, Modena Estense α.X.1.5 fol. 115ra) as translated "ex vulgari ydiomate Lombardico"; Andreose identifies this model as belonging to the VA branch.
  - **Manuscript Count:** Surviving witnesses number sixty manuscripts (Gadrat-Ouerfelli 2015, p. 63).
- **Disposition:** Confirmed secondary report. Cites `@gadrat2022dominicans, p. 287` in `vol-3/ch-10-ca-polo-milione.md` Note [20], `vol-3/ch-12-reliability-essay.md` Note [8], and `facts.md` (`F-18`), while explicitly noting that Dutschke's dissertation text was accessed via Gadrat-Ouerfelli.

### `APP.T11f` — *Yuan dianzhang* Closed Hunting Season

### `APP.T11g` — Ahmad Episode in Toledo Z
- **Target:** Collation of Moule–Pelliot ch. 85 / Toledo Z (Zelada 49.20; Barbieri 1998) to verify whether any part of the Ahmad assassination is preserved in Z.
- **Finding:** Collation apparatus not found. Barbieri (1998) confirms Z is abbreviated in its opening; Andreose (2018) notes Ramusio's model contained authentic variants beyond surviving Z; Mascherpa (2025) analyzes Z as a Dominican-linked Venetian redaction.
- **Disposition:** Hedge stands in `vol-2/ch-05-ahmad-paper-coal.md` Note [1] and `facts.md` (`F-09`). Ramusio remains the primary witness for the full assassination narrative in Yule's edition.

- **Finding:** Specific gazetteer passage not found. Mazumdar (1998) and Daniels (2001) document Chinese sugar technology comprehensively but do not expose Phillips's target text.

### `APP.T11i` — Ceylon Chronology and Sendemain
- **Target:** Geiger's *Cūlavaṃsa* regnal tables and modern epigraphic chronology for late-13th-century Ceylon.
- **Finding:** Geiger's tables not seen directly. Hultzsch (1913, p. 531, §IX) documents competing chronological calculations placing Bhuvanekabāhu I's death in 1284 (*Attanagaluvamsa*) or 1288 (traditional reign lengths), and identifies Māravarman Kulasekhara I.
- **Disposition:** Added `@hultzsch1913singhalese` to `bibliography.bib`. Updated `vol-3/ch-04-necuveran-seilan.md` Note [11] and `facts.md` (`F-44`) with Hultzsch's 1913 findings, preserving the hedge on modern regnal tables.
