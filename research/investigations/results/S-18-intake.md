> **Superseded in part by the [follow-up intake](S-18-followup-intake.md)**, which reads Pelliot's BASMAN/SEMENAT, EI XII, Talbot, Moule–Pelliot and Maffeo's will. Where they differ, the follow-up wins.

The [supplied report](S-18-raw.md) is preserved verbatim (the user's file "Marco Polo sea chapters evidence.md"). This intake audits it, checks the base text for the questions the report left open, and checks citations and a few gaps on its own. It is **not** an independent verification of every row. `verified` means the cited source says what is stated. It does not mean the event, or Polo's own presence, is established.

## Trust check

**Verdict: usable as a map of leads; most of its verdicts are not evidence.** About half the rows rest on Wikipedia, tourism or news pages, and the report says so. Its honesty about gaps is good. Its main failures:

1. **It did not read the base text, and three of its "unresolved" questions are answered there** (§ Base text, below). It also gives the fleet as 14 ships; Yule's text says thirteen.
2. **It missed the project's own research.** The gazetteer already holds Pelliot's direct readings of Locac, Malaiur and Dagroian. `facts.md` already has the Quanzhou wreck (F-14), the 1290 embassy and Cleaves (F-15), Adam's Peak in the Book (F-23), Rudrama Devi (F-31), Argon's death (F-33) and Sendemain (F-44). `bibliography.bib` already has Jack-Hinton 1964, Wheatley 1961, Cleaves 1976, Haw 2023 on the departure, Pearson 2002 and Green 1998 on the Quanzhou ship, and Daly 2019 on Lamreh. Those results stand; this intake does not re-adjudicate them.
3. **"Langkasuka appears in no source" is wrong.** Pelliot names the Langkasuka reading and rejects it (Notes II pp. 766–770, already in the gazetteer). The brief was right to list it as a historical candidate.
