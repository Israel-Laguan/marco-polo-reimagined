## Nizari legend: a usable narrower chronology

- Burchard of Strasbourg (1175, as dated and translated by Lincoln) reports a promise of paradise for obedience; his text survives in Arnold's chronicle, according to Lincoln. This is a reported literary motif, not proof of a real training method.
- Arnold of Lübeck (before 1210 in Lincoln's comparison) describes an intoxicant and deceptive visions of pleasures. Lincoln's quoted Latin and translation do not establish hashish as the substance. This is earlier than Polo and contradicts the supplied report's claim that any drug motif originated with him.
- Polo's account combines an earthly garden, induced sleep and simulated paradise; Virani's quoted version specifies opium. This is a distinct configuration, not proof Polo invented every component or borrowed directly from Arnold.

Source: `lincoln2006terrorism`, especially pp. 250–52; `virani2021garden`; `daftary1994introduction`. Lincoln and Virani quote the older texts; no direct manuscript or primary-edition collation was performed. Neither the complete earliest-witness sequence nor the paths of transmission are established by this check.
