# S-15 — Venice, Genoa and the Polo family: research output

_Historical first intake; key findings superseded after source checks in [S-15-external-intake.md](S-15-external-intake.md), especially #1, #6–7, #10, #14, #16 and #22; for #19 see also [the final intake](S-15-final-intake.md), correcting the will shelfmark to no. 33. Research and intake, 2026-11-11. One web research run and direct checks against local Yule–Cordier. NEW marks publications after 2013. This is a researcher's synthesis, not output from a separate deep-research service. Documents cited through editions or scholarship were not inspected in the archive. Verdicts concern the finding stated, not proof that every historical event happened. “Not found” means not found in the sources consulted, not proof of absence._

Evidence classes: **DOCUMENT** contemporary record; **EARLY TESTIMONY** narrative witness or the travel book (which is not a notarial birth record); **RAMUSIO TRADITION** 1559 preface; **MODERN INFERENCE** scholar's reconstruction. Sources are linked in the bibliography; open-access and access limits are recorded there. Section IDs: I-01, I-02, III-10, III-11, III-B1.

Yule and Bolognari doubt Jacopo's Ayas chronology because the major documented battle was 1294; Orlando, as the Treccani review reports, favors a 1296 encounter. This is a disagreement over identification and chronology, not proof that the 1294 battle failed to occur. Gullino's reported 1310 will cannot automatically be equated with Yule's 1309 reference. Ca' Foscari's news release presents Agnese as Marco's daughter; Orlando's scholarly review explicitly qualifies the identification. No causal explanation of the two competing death dates is settled here.
