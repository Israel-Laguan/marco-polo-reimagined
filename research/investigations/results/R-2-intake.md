## What changes

- **The "silver belt of a Tartar knight" and the Mongol *boghta* headdress are not in the inventory**, on the editors' reading of the 1366 copy. There are two plain silver belts and a gold jewel set with stones and pearls (*bocheta*). This confirms `scholarship.md` with the primary edition rather than through a summary.
- **What the inventory does show:** a large gold *tavola de comandamento* (a paiza, worth 20 lire of grossi, the most valuable single item); three quilts of "Tartar work"; a "Tartar-style" white garment; musk, aloe wood and rhubarb. None of this shows that Marco was an eyewitness of Asia; these are objects in a Venetian house in 1324.
- **No manuscript correction is needed:** the retelling does not mention the inventory. It is enrichment for III-10 (Marco's death and will) and III-B1 (trivia), and for plate captions.
