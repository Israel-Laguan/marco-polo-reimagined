# Navigating the Milione: A Synthesis of Five Major Debates in Modern Marco Polo Scholarship

## The Hormuz Outward Route: Re-evaluating Yule's Reconstruction

The initial overland journey of the Polo party from Persia to the court of Kublai Khan in China has been a subject of intense scholarly scrutiny, with Sir Henry Yule’s reconstruction forming the traditional but contested cornerstone of modern analysis [[39](https://www.enotes.com/topics/marco-polo/criticism/criticism/e-denison-ross-lecture-date-1934)]. Yule's theory posits that after traveling overland from Kerman to Hormuz, the Polos embarked on a perilous sea voyage across the Persian Gulf [[5](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10636/10636-h/10636-h.htm), [39](https://www.enotes.com/topics/marco-polo/criticism/criticism/e-denison-ross-lecture-date-1934)]. This route, detailed extensively in Yule's annotated translation of *The Book of Ser Marco Polo*, forced them to sail from Hormuz to Basra before continuing eastward [[5](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10636/10636-h/10636-h.htm)]. Yule's meticulous geographical notes provide a comprehensive justification for this path, which includes detailed observations on the site of Old Hormuz, its history, and the challenges of travel in the region [[5](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10636/10636-h/10636-h.htm)]. However, this long-standing model has faced significant criticism from subsequent generations of historians who have applied different methodological lenses, shifting the debate from pure philological reconstruction to pragmatic logistical analysis.

A pivotal critique emerged from John Larner, who identified a fundamental flaw in Yule's reconstructed itinerary [[34](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polo-and-the-encounter-of-east-and-west-9780802099280.html)]. Larner argued that forcing the Polos onto the described sea route from Hormuz to China was an unbelievable detour. He pointed out that this maritime passage was considered by contemporaries to be "the most difficult and dangerous way," taking at least double the time required by the overland Mongol post-system [[34](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polo-and-the-encounter-of-east-and-west-9780802099280.html)]. This argument gains immense weight when considering the Polos' documented possession of a gold *paiza*, a high-level safe-conduct pass that guaranteed them safe passage and access to the efficient and secure Mongol postal relay network throughout their entire journey [[34](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polo-and-the-encounter-of-east-and-west-9780802099280.html)]. To sail across the Persian Gulf when a fast and protected land route was available directly contradicts the practical logic of their circumstances. Larner's critique effectively reframed the debate, suggesting that Yule's purely textual reconstruction failed to account for the real-world constraints and advantages available to the travelers.

In response to Yule's hypothesis, Major Sykes proposed an alternative land-based itinerary designed to bypass the problematic elements of Yule's route [[6](https://www.bookrags.com/ebooks/10636/104.html)]. Sykes rejected the portion of Yule's theory involving a visit to Baghdad, arguing that if Marco Polo had actually traveled there, it would be inexplicable why he referred to the Volga River as the "Tigris" while leaving the actual Tigris river of Baghdad unnamed [[6](https://www.bookrags.com/ebooks/10636/104.html)]. To resolve this contradiction, Sykes suggested a revised route where the Venetians entered Persia near Tabriz, proceeded through Sultania and Kashan, and then traveled south to Yezd before reaching Kerman and Hormuz [[6](https://www.bookrags.com/ebooks/10636/104.html)]. From Hormuz, they would then adopt the established Khorasan route back towards China, thus avoiding both the treacherous sea voyage and the un-named major city of Baghdad [[6](https://www.bookrags.com/ebooks/10636/104.html)]. This alternative demonstrates a shift in historical methodology, prioritizing internal consistency within the text itself alongside known geographical realities over a strict adherence to place-name correlations derived solely from the text.

## The Return Voyage to China: Champa and Chronological Disputes

The chronology of Marco Polo's return voyage from China in 1291–92 is another area of significant scholarly contention, particularly concerning the port calls made by his expedition. The standard interpretation, codified in authoritative editions such as those by A.C. Moule and Paul Pelliot, holds that after departing the southern Chinese port of Zaitun (modern Quanzhou) in 1291, the fleet sailed directly west across the South China Sea to the kingdom of Champa, which corresponds to roughly the southern half of modern Vietnam [[9](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/200504/the.explorer.marco.polo.htm), [41](https://archive.org/stream/mostnoblefamoust00polo/mostnoblefamoust00polo_djvu.txt)]. According to this widely accepted reading, the Polos spent approximately two days in Champa before continuing their journey towards the Malay Peninsula [[41](https://archive.org/stream/mostnoblefamoust00polo/mostnoblefamoust00polo_djvu.txt)]. This reconstruction forms the backbone of most modern accounts of the homeward trip, establishing Champa as a crucial first stop on the maritime leg of their journey.

However, this conventional understanding has been challenged by Stephen G. Haw in his work *Marco Polo's China*. Haw presents a compelling alternative hypothesis based on a re-examination of the sailing distances and the predictable patterns of monsoon winds that governed maritime travel in the region during the 13th century [[40](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polos-asia-an-introduction-to-his-description-of-the-world-called-il-milione-reprint-2020nbsped-9780520318274.html)]. He argues that the first mention of Champa in Polo's narrative cannot refer to a port call during the 1291 departure. Instead, Haw contends that this initial reference points to a previous visit made by Marco Polo on an earlier mission to Southeast Asia, which he dates to around 1285 [[40](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polos-asia-an-introduction-to-his-description-of-the-world-called-il-milione-reprint-2020nbsped-9780520318274.html)]. This earlier mission would have occurred shortly after his arrival in China in 1276, during which he served as an informant for Kublai Khan [[40](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polos-asia-an-introduction-to-his-description-of-the-world-called-il-milione-reprint-2020nbsped-9780520318274.html)].

According to Haw's revised chronology, the second mention of Ciamba (the name used by Polo for Champa) in the text should not be interpreted as a new port call after leaving Quanzhou [[52](https://dokumen.pub/the-martyrdom-of-the-franciscans-islam-the-papacy-and-an-order-in-conflict-9780812296778.html)]. Rather, Haw suggests it functions as a geographical summary, indicating that the fleet had now arrived in the general vicinity of the Champa region [[50](https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12657/115374/9788869691034.pdf?sequence=4&isAllowed=y)]. This reinterpretation fundamentally alters the timeline and sequence of the return voyage. By removing the stop at Champa from the 1291 departure, Haw's model requires a re-evaluation of the subsequent stops and the duration of the voyage. For instance, his analysis implies that the fleet would have sailed directly from Quanzhou towards the Strait of Malacca, encountering various islands and stopping at Sumatra for five months to wait for favorable winds, before proceeding along the Indian coast [[9](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/200504/the.explorer.marco.polo.htm)]. This argument is methodologically significant as it introduces environmental data—specifically wind patterns—to refine a purely textual analysis. Haw's broader project involves using external evidence, including geography and climate, to validate and clarify Polo's account, and his challenge to the standard reading of the Champa passage is a key example of this approach [[7](https://www.routledge.com/Marco-Polos-China-A-Venetian-in-the-Realm-of-Khubilai-Khan/Haw/p/book/9780415546003), [43](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287083478_Marco_Polo%27s_China_A_Venetian_in_the_realm_of_Khubilai_Khan)]. The debate hinges on the interpretation of a single ambiguous phrase in the text—"He also told of the country of Ciamba"—which Haw argues was added later as a geographical marker rather than describing a new event [[52](https://dokumen.pub/the-martyrdom-of-the-franciscans-islam-the-papacy-and-an-order-in-conflict-9780812296778.html)]. This dispute highlights the ongoing effort to reconcile the sometimes-vague descriptions in Polo's narrative with the physical realities of medieval navigation.

## Yuan Dynasty Records and Envoy Departure Dates

One of the most critical pieces of corroborating evidence for the historicity of Marco Polo's travels comes from a Yuan dynasty official document discovered by the Chinese scholar Yang Zhijiu in 1941 [[45](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299733230_Marco_Polo_Chinese_cultural_identity_and_an_alternative_model_of_east-west_encounter)]. This discovery has provided a tangible link between the narrative in *Il Milione* and contemporary Chinese historical records, significantly bolstering the case for Polo's authenticity. The document in question was found in juan (volume) 19,418 of the *Yongle dadian* (Great Compendium Composed during the Yongle Reign), a massive 15th-century encyclopedia compiled from older texts [[34](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polo-and-the-encounter-of-east-and-west-9780802099280.html), [45](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299733230_Marco_Polo_Chinese_cultural_identity_and_an_alternative_model_of_east-west_encounter)]. It is important to note that this crucial record is located within the *Yongle dadian* and not in the official dynastic history, the *Yuan shi* [[45](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299733230_Marco_Polo_Chinese_cultural_identity_and_an_alternative_model_of_east-west_encounter)]. The document is a report from the governor of Quanzhou (Polo's Zaiton) detailing an imperial decree issued in the third month of the 27th year of the Zhiyuan reign, corresponding to April/May 1290 [[45](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299733230_Marco_Polo_Chinese_cultural_identity_and_an_alternative_model_of_east-west_encounter)]. This decree ordered the dispatch of three officials—Wu-lu-dai, A-bi-shi-ke, and Huo-zhe—to the court of Arghun Khan in the Ilkhanate, instructing them to travel via Ma-ba-er (the Coromandel Coast of India) [[45](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299733230_Marco_Polo_Chinese_cultural_identity_and_an_alternative_model_of_east-west_encounter)].

This document is directly relevant to Polo's account because he describes being part of a large delegation that escorted the Mongol princess Kokachin from China to her prospective husband, the Ilkhan Arghun, in Persia [[40](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polos-asia-an-introduction-to-his-description-of-the-world-called-il-milione-reprint-2020nbsped-9780520318274.html)]. Before the 1290 document's discovery, some skeptics argued that the absence of Marco Polo's name in Chinese records cast doubt on his presence in China. Yang Zhijiu's find provided a powerful rebuttal, demonstrating that a significant diplomatic mission involving envoys named Ulatai, Abushka, and Koja was indeed dispatched from Quanzhou around this time, perfectly matching the individuals Polo mentions [[34](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polo-and-the-encounter-of-east-and-west-9780802099280.html), [45](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299733230_Marco_Polo_Chinese_cultural_identity_and_an_alternative_model_of_east-west_encounter)]. The correspondence between the names in the Chinese record and those in Polo's narrative provides strong circumstantial evidence supporting the core events of his story.

Scholars have used this document to establish a precise chronological framework for the Polos' departure from China. The interpretations offered by key sinologists are summarized below.

## Textual Redaction Theories: The Single vs. Multiple Redaction Debate

The textual history of *Il Milione*—the book of Marco Polo's travels—is exceptionally complex, with over 140 manuscript versions existing today, each exhibiting significant variations [[13](https://www.academia.edu/121679182/Marco_Polo_Research_Past_Present_Future)]. This has led to a deep schism in scholarship between proponents of a single-redaction theory and those who advocate for a multiple-redaction model. The single-redaction theory posits that the differences between manuscripts are primarily the result of scribal error and linguistic corruption during the translation process from the original Italian (Venetian) into Medieval French and subsequently into Latin. This view considers the passages found only in certain redactions, such as the Latin version Z and the later Ramusio version, to be either additions or corruptions of a single, stable "original" text. The arguments for this position are seen as compelling, yet they are often considered unsatisfactory because they fail to adequately address the complexities of the manuscript tradition [[11](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/b3b7119f-e838-4452-a8fb-d541865d0e91/2024%20The%20textual%20Transmission.pdf)].

In contrast, the multiple-redaction theory, championed by a cohort of contemporary textual critics, argues that the text underwent active and deliberate editorial interventions over time. This school of thought views the manuscript families not as corrupted versions of a single original, but as distinct branches of a textual family tree that evolved through human agency. Scholars like Eugenio Burgio, Samuela Simion, Giuseppe Mascherpa, and Alvise Andreose are central figures in developing this sophisticated model [[14](https://arts.units.it/retrieve/e2913fdc-5fbe-f688-e053-3705fe0a67e0/iss-6-2-2017_Dr4qEZY.pdf), [53](https://www.academia.edu/88181275/Il_Devisement_du_Monde_e_la_storia_della_tradizione_poliana_in_margine_a_unedizione_recente_)]. Their work involves creating detailed stemmatic analyses to map the relationships between the various redactions. For example, Burgio and Mario Eusebi established a stemma codicum in 2008 that distinguishes between two main groups of manuscripts: Group α, represented directly by the Venetian redaction V, and Group β, represented indirectly by the Latin redaction Z [[8](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381854591_Marco_Polo_Research_Past_Present_Future)]. Within these groups, further subgroups are identified, reflecting different editorial histories [[8](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381854591_Marco_Polo_Research_Past_Present_Future)]. These scholars argue that copyists and editors were not merely passive transmitters of a fixed text but active participants who altered the narrative, adding, removing, or modifying content [[53](https://www.academia.edu/88181275/Il_Devisement_du_Monde_e_la_storia_della_tradizione_poliana_in_margine_a_unedizione_recente_)]. The project led by Simion, Eusebi, and Burgio aims to create a digital critical edition that can reconstruct a hypothetical "original version" by systematically analyzing these layers of redaction [[8](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381854591_Marco_Polo_Research_Past_Present_Future), [61](https://iris.unive.it/handle/10278/5062381)].

Alvise Andreose, a prominent advocate of this view, cautions that the current state of research does not permit a definitive classification of all important manuscripts [[8](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381854591_Marco_Polo_Research_Past_Present_Future)]. He highlights the need for further investigation into questions of whether individual manuscripts underwent single or multiple redactions, underscoring the complexity of the textual transmission [[8](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381854591_Marco_Polo_Research_Past_Present_Future)]. This body of work collectively represents a significant shift away from older integral editions, such as those by Luigi Foscolo Benedetto and A.C. Moule and Paul Pelliot, which attempted to create a single, unified text without clear editorial signals, often resulting in an unintelligible "mishmash" [[8](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381854591_Marco_Polo_Research_Past_Present_Future), [40](https://dokumen.pub/marco-polos-asia-an-introduction-to-his-description-of-the-world-called-il-milione-reprint-2020nbsped-9780520318274.html)].

The provided sources do not contain the names of any specific, currently active scholars who hold a firm single-redactionist position and have published explicit counter-arguments directly targeting the multi-layered models proposed by Burgio, Simion, Mascherpa, and Andreose. The statement that the arguments for the single-redaction theory are "compelling, but far from... satisfactory" because they fail to account for the textual complexities highlighted by the multiple-redactionists suggests that this older view is no longer considered a dominant force in the field [[11](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/b3b7119f-e838-4452-a8fb-d541865d0e91/2024%20The%20textual%20Transmission.pdf)]. The scholarly conversation has clearly pivoted towards the nuanced, multi-layered models developed by the proponents of the multiple-redaction theory. Therefore, while the single-redaction theory exists, it appears to be a defunct or minority position in contemporary scholarship, unable to offer a robust alternative to the detailed stemmatic and comparative analyses advanced by its primary critics. The debate remains a fundamental issue in the study of the text, defining how scholars understand the nature of *Il Milione* itself—as a static original or a dynamic, evolving literary artifact.
