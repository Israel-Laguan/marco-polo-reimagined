This pass is for additional depth after the [high-priority pass](S-20-follow-up-prompt.md). Items are optional unless selected for a specific claim, timeline entry or image; [the priority assessment](S-20-research-priorities.md) gives a safe fallback for each.

```text
I am preparing an English retelling of Marco Polo's book. Investigate the optional unresolved questions below only where sources are accessible and the findings would add a useful, attributable example. A selective partial answer is welcome. Do not repeat the established S-20 history, pad gaps with speculation, or present “not found” as proof of absence.

Originals, critical editions, institutional catalogues, named scholarly reconstructions and second-hand accounts are welcome. Identify the source actually read, date, page/section where available, underlying earlier source, and inspection level: original/object; edition; catalogue; full secondary passage; earlier account quoted by a later author; abstract/snippet; inaccessible. Attribute all second-hand claims. Snippets are leads. Do not invent sources, dates, quotations, pages, URLs, or claim inspection. Distinguish a historical event, a later claim about it and the earliest attribution you actually located.

1. GLOBAL AND MODERN RECEPTION. Check Zhang Xinglang's and Feng Chengjun's edition dates, titles and translation bases; the relationship and translator roles of the reported 2019 and 2024 Mongolian Ramusio works; and a concrete named Iranian reception example. Find a dated Polo-specific Chinese textbook, government speech or exhibit if available. The 2012 Italian Embassy book launch in Beijing is not proof of Chinese government policy. Seek another significant modern novel beyond Bukal and the previously recorded works. Find country-specific Montaldo broadcasts or evidence behind a first Italian–Chinese co-production claim, and the formal naming date of Venice airport. Do not infer a “first” or a naming date from a construction/opening date.

2. MYTHS AND OPTIONAL TRIVIA. Seek an authentic contemporary likeness only if supported; distinguish it from later representations. Find early attestations of the pool game's name and later origin stories; locate the earliest Korčula birthplace claim and Pasini's actual wording on alleged family origins; seek a deathbed account earlier than Acqui. Separate a family-origin theory from a birthplace claim and a chronicle anecdote from authenticated last words. The high-priority pass handles the existing III-10 nickname claim; do not repeat it.

3. FOOD AND TECHNOLOGY. Seek Victoria Carlsson's 1930 ice-cream article page or a named independent transcription; the recycled AI extraction is not verification. Locate Ramusio's 1559 passage and the 1929 Macaroni Journal story, or independent named retellings, and do not assume either marks the origin of all pasta stories. Find earliest located Polo-introduction attributions for gunpowder, paper money and eyeglasses, separating the technology's history from the history of the story. Montanari has already been recorded as attributing one pasta-legend strand to Ramusio and citing the 1929 journal; originals were not inspected.

4. MAPS AND RECORDS. Resolve spellings of Fra Mauro's Japan label #1334 (Cimpangu/Cimpagu) and *2306 (Hachbaluch/Hacbaluch) with a critical edition or inspected map image, and investigate further competing source proposals only if useful. Locate Ravenstein's Behaim Cipangu page or a named secondary account. Reconcile the 145/146 manuscript census scopes, locate the Foligno watermark argument and proposed 1320–30 date for F, and check the Gallica identifiers for fr. 1116 and fr. 2810. Trace the Dom Pedro Polo-book acquisition/gift separately from the map story. Locate any Jesuit comparison of Polo's silences on tea, foot-binding or the Wall; the draft can instead use the already documented Ricci/Góis material. Avoid assuming a Portuguese-commissioned map was the surviving Marciana object, or making an unqualified cartographic “first”.

5. OPTIONAL COLUMBUS DETAIL. If you can improve on the high-priority pass, compare counts and hands in the surviving copy with locators and explain methodology. Malfatto reports 366 annotations but gives an outdated Antwerp imprint and does not specify the hands in that passage; do not ascribe the whole count to Columbus. Avoid duplicating the central postil/first-voyage work from pass 1.

Return a concise findings table with the actual source read, locator, underlying source, inspection level, safe wording and verdict; include only newly verified or materially refined results. List unresolved items and give validated bibliographic details and access status for works actually read. Mark post-2013 scholarship NEW.
```
