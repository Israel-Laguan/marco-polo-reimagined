# U-1A — Open-source checks: Mongol political history

This paste-ready prompt selects three manuscript-facing claims from [U-1](2026-11-17-unresearched-claims-prompts.md). It prioritizes material the research agent can read online and does not ask the user to obtain inaccessible works.

```text
I am checking an annotated English retelling of Marco Polo based on Yule–Cordier. Please investigate three specific claims using sources you can actually open online. They are manuscript claims to test, not conclusions to repeat. Work in order; it is better to resolve part of a question than to guess.

1. LI TAN'S REVOLT — Volume II, chapter 10, Note 6.
The retelling gives Polo's year as 1273 and names the imperial commanders Aguil and Mongotay. Its note says Chinese annals date the revolt to 1262, place a siege at Jinan, name different commanders, and report pardon for lower-ranking participants but execution of the leaders. Check the rebellion against an accessible text or reliable translation of the relevant Yuan shi passages and open scholarship. Identify each passage's chapter or other stable locator. Which details are explicitly attested? Can either of Polo's commanders be identified securely, tentatively, or not at all? Distinguish the revolt's date from the particular siege or battle date, and do not assume a similar name is the same person.

2. KAIDU AND NOMOGHAN — Volume II, chapter 15, Note 3.
The Book places one Kaidu battle in 1266; it then describes a separate battle, after two years' quiet, against Nomoghan and George near Karakorum. The note suggests the Chinese record of Nomoghan's command and capture around 1275–77 may fit better. Check the sequence against accessible Yuan shi passages and modern scholarship on the princes' revolt. What are the recorded dates, participants, places and outcome? Can the second battle actually be matched to a specific event? Keep Polo's two battles distinct and distinguish Shiregi's revolt and capture of Nomoghan from any battle involving Kaidu. Do not assign the 1266 date to the second battle without checking the textual sequence.

3. MÖNGKE'S FUNERAL — Volume I, chapter 12, Notes 12–13.
Polo reports that more than 20,000 people encountered during the funeral procession were killed. The retelling also states that Rashid al-Din confirms escort killings and horse sacrifice for Chinggis Khan. Check exactly what an accessible edition or identifiable translation of Rashid says, which ruler and funeral it concerns, and whether the author is reporting an event, a custom, or a story. Search other readable contemporary accounts and modern scholarship for evidence concerning Mongol royal funerals and Möngke specifically. Does any source independently support the 20,000 figure or the broad claim that every royal funeral involved killing passersby? Do not infer corroboration from a similar burial custom or from a source that may depend on Polo. If no exact passage is accessible, leave the statement unverified and suggest conservative wording.

SOURCE RULES
- Start with freely readable digitized primary texts, reliable translations, open scholarly publications and institutional resources. A useful source does not have to be the named source if it directly addresses the claim; explain why it is relevant.
- State exactly what you accessed: page image, full text, transcription, OCR, excerpt, abstract, catalogue record, or another author's report. Give stable URLs and exact chapter, section or printed page only when actually seen; never treat a catalogue record or search snippet as a passage.
- Keep the medieval witness's words, modern translator or editor's comments, and a scholar's interpretation separate. Identify possible textual dependence before calling witnesses independent. Translate short passages accurately and label your own translation.
- When a source is inaccessible, mark NOT SEEN and move on; do not ask me to visit a library or archive, and do not guess around the gap. A failed search does not establish that evidence does not exist.

OUTPUT
Provide one numbered ledger per topic with: specific claim; supported, contradicted, disputed, or unresolved; what the accessible source actually says; exact source and locator; access level; limits or competing interpretation; and one sentence of safe wording for the retelling or its note. List any concrete corrections separately, followed by unresolved gaps. Include complete source details and working links so the results can be independently checked.
```
