# Follow-up research prompt — primary texts and quotation rights

```text
I am editing a commercially published English retelling of Marco Polo. The base is Henry Yule's English translation, revised by Henri Cordier, The Book of Ser Marco Polo, third edition (1903), two volumes, with Cordier's later addenda. The book's endnotes compare Polo with contemporary Persian and Chinese sources. I need precise, independently checkable primary-text passages, not a general assessment of whether Polo went to China.

A prior research report gave incompatible copyright claims and invented or unverified page numbers. Start this task afresh: do not assume the prior report is correct, and do not rely on an output from another research agent as a source.

PRIORITY 1 — THE PRINCESS AND THE RETURN VOYAGE

Compare Polo's account in Yule–Cordier's Prologue, chapters XVII–XVIII, with Rashid al-Din's account of the princess Kököchin, Arghun, and Ghazan. Locate the exact passages in a published Persian text and/or a named English translation, especially W. M. Thackston's Jamiʿu't-tawarikh / Compendium of Chronicles (1998–99). If accessible, inspect Francis W. Cleaves, “A Chinese Source Bearing on Marco Polo's Departure from China and a Persian Source on His Arrival in Persia,” Harvard Journal of Asiatic Studies 36 (1976), 181–203. Distinguish what the Persian text itself says from Cleaves's or another scholar's interpretation. Does Rashid actually mention an embassy, a sea journey, the princess's marriage, and/or the Polo family? Report only what you can verify and explicitly mark any part you cannot inspect. Give the exact volume, page or section in the edition actually seen. Explain the succession chronology and distinguish Arghun's death from Ghazan's later accession.

Do not use John Andrew Boyle's The Successors of Genghis Khan (1971), pp. 240–250, as a presumed reference to the princess or to the Ilkhanid paper-money issue: those pages begin an account of Qubilai. If you find another relevant passage in Boyle, show the verified page and describe its actual scope.

PRIORITY 2 — CHINESE SOURCES AND TWO DISPUTED EPISODES

For the siege of Xiangyang/Saianfu and the Burma campaign, identify the exact Yuan shi juan and passage, the published translation used, and how that text compares to Polo. Polo's siege account is in Yule–Cordier, vol. II, book II, chapter LXX. Distinguish the Chinese chronicle from Yule's notes citing or summarizing it and from the Persian accounts of siege engineers. Compare the relevant dates and explain uncertainty in the Polos' route and arrival. For Burma, verify whether the numbers attributed to each text refer to the same battle. If the original or a named translation cannot be inspected, say so; a historical overview or table of contents is not sufficient to confirm an exact passage.

PRIORITY 3 — SOURCE RIGHTS FOR QUOTATION

Find the copyright and publication evidence for Boyle's 1971 English translation, Francis W. Cleaves's 1982 English translation of the Secret History of the Mongols, Peter Harris's 2007 English translation of Zhou Daguan, and the English version of Eusebius Renaudot's Ancient Accounts of India and China (1733). Identify the 1733 English translator if possible. Assess US and EU rights separately, cite the evidence, and give the author/translator death date and any relevant publication or renewal facts. Report uncertainty instead of calling a translation public domain because the medieval original is old or a PDF is accessible. Our editorial policy is to use public-domain translation wording for proposed verbatim quotations; this policy is separate from statutory quotation exceptions.

Check the translator's own public-domain statement for Robert Bedrosian's Hayton translation, and distinguish the 2015 de Rachewiltz/John C. Street shorter edition's CC BY-NC-ND licence from the 2004 Brill edition. Do not assume the two editions have the same rights or pagination.

METHOD AND OUTPUT

- Prefer directly viewed scans, authoritative text editions, library catalogs, publisher records, and peer-reviewed scholarship. Supply stable links for each item. Explicitly state whether you read the full primary passage, only an image/contents page, or a secondary reference. Do not call a passage inspected on the strength of a search snippet, another report, or a contents entry.
- Return a small table: question; primary edition and precise locator; what it says in one or two sentences; source and access link; inspection status; confidence; and remaining uncertainty. Say “not found” when the passage cannot be seen. Paraphrase copyrighted work and do not reproduce lengthy quotations.
- Provide a separate rights table with source and rights evidence. Give a short bibliography of works actually consulted. Do not invent volumes, page numbers, original-language readings, translation rights, or URLs.
- If sources do not resolve a question, name the specific page, archive, library, critical edition or rights record needed next. A few genuinely verified answers are preferable to a complete-looking table of guesses.
```
