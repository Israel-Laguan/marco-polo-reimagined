# Priority research prompts after the spike audit

Copy one complete fenced block at a time into a research tool capable of opening sources. Each prompt is self-contained. Work in numerical order unless an upcoming chapter or plate makes another more urgent. If a source requires a library, an honest access failure and exact request are useful; do not ask a research tool to guess around a paywall.

The existing research conclusions are qualified handoffs. A result is a lead until this project checks it against the cited source. Save each complete, unedited return under `research/results/` with the tool, model and date, and conduct the intake checks in [the research protocol](README.md#trust-rules-intake) before changing claims.

The five already identified natural-history manuscript corrections can be made using their existing [S-21 closure](results/S-21-closure.md#chapter-handoffs); more research is not required to apply those cautious decisions. Optional stronger-claim archival questions (Twynam, Wen 1980, Weisgerber and manuscript images) have a separate [S-21 prompt](S-21-primary-audit-open-questions-prompt.md). ## 1. Yuan China and the route — highest priority

```text
I am preparing annotated Volume II chapters of a commercial English retelling of Marco Polo based on Yule–Cordier. I need source-level checks of a few unresolved questions, not a general history of the Yuan dynasty. Work in order and answer fewer questions well rather than fill gaps by inference.

1. Nayan (II-01): locate the relevant dated passages in the Yuan shi and any other independent contemporary witnesses for the 1287 campaign. Compare them with Yule–Cordier's account, separating command, locations, chronology and battlefield detail. Is the elephant platform independently attested, or does the accessible evidence derive from Polo? A failure to find it is not proof it never existed.
2. Yangzhou (II-11): read the relevant parts of Hans Ulrich Vogel, Marco Polo Was in China (2013), and, where accessible, the Chinese research he cites by Zhu Jiang, Peng Hai or others. What exact position, date and documentary evidence are proposed? Separate a historically possible appointment, a specific scholarly hypothesis and a document actually naming Polo. Identify the witnesses for séjourna/governa where relevant.
3. Kinsay/Hangzhou (II-13): trace Mascherpa's figures of 347 bridges and 35 li to the exact Chinese source, date and geographic boundaries. Compare them with the specific Polo witness and avoid treating different jurisdictions as the same city. Are there Yuan-period sources for household registration, door plaques, fire watches or baths? Do not silently transfer Song or Ming evidence into the Yuan.
4. Dadu and Shangdu (II-02; I-13): use an excavation report or scholarship based on one, such as Xu Bin's 2019 Dadu reconstruction review or the 2024 archaeological report if accessible. What walls, foundations and artifacts are excavated, and what plan, dimensions and cane-palace identification are reconstructions? Do not identify later Ming buildings as Yuan.
5. Vochan/Burma (II-08): compare the independently dated Yuan shi juan 210 passages for the third and tenth months of 1277, other Burmese/Yuan evidence where directly available, and Polo's narrative. Distinguish commanders, dates and which army each figure describes. Do not combine the two 1277 expeditions or later events into one battle.

OUTPUT AND SOURCE RULES
- Cover every numbered question in a ledger: answered, partly answered, or not found. For each finding give the precise claim; verdict (supported / contradicted / disputed / not found); exact source and locator; what it actually says; access level; limitations; and recommended cautious wording. Separate a manuscript's reading, independent contemporary witness, physical evidence, and a modern interpretation. A related fact does not establish Marco's presence or eyewitness status.
- Access levels: original image/object seen; edition/transcription or full text read; extract/OCR read; abstract/review read; catalog metadata only; or inaccessible. Give stable institutional, publisher, library or DOI links. Supply printed pages only if actually visible; clearly distinguish a printed page from a scan number. Attribute any second-hand report to its actual intermediary. A search snippet, an unexamined citation or catalog entry does not verify a passage. Do not invent texts, page numbers, links, dates or consensus.
- Cite sources actually consulted with complete bibliographic details, access status, and BibTeX if verifiable. Cite a short original-language passage and translation only where seen. Name disagreements and identify where the underlying evidence stops. End with unresolved questions and the best safe fallback. Say NOT FOUND or NOT SEEN when appropriate; that is not proof that evidence does not exist.
```

Maffeo's marriage to Marta is reported via Burgio citing Bolognari–Simion; Bellela's marriage to Bertuccio Querini is reported by Bolognari citing Gallo. Those source chains are not substitutes for inspecting the original records.

1. Obtain G. Orlandini, “Marco Polo e la sua famiglia,” Archivio Veneto-Tridentino 9 (1926), document 6, pp. 25–31, or the relevant original in Archivio di Stato di Venezia.
