## U-1. Mongol politics, 1250s–1290s (Vols. I–II) — Tier 1 #1–4, Tier 2 #7, #9

```text
I am checking endnotes in a commercial English retelling of Marco Polo, based on Yule–Cordier (3rd ed., 1903). Its verdicts on the claims below rest only on Yule-era sources (Gaubil, De Mailla, D'Ohsson, Pauthier). I need source-level checks against the Yuan shi, Rashid al-Din and modern scholarship, not a general history. Work in order and answer fewer questions well rather than fill gaps by inference.

1. Li Tan's revolt (II-10): Polo dates it 1273 and names the Kaan's captains Aguil and Mongotay. What do the Yuan shi (Li Tan's biography and the basic annals) and modern work (e.g. Rossabi 1988; Franke and Twitchett, Cambridge History of China vol. 6) give for the year, the besieged city (Jinan), the length of the siege, the commanders sent, Li Tan's end and the amnesty? Are Aguil and Mongotay identifiable (e.g. Aqul; Mangqudai), and were they at Jinan?
2. Negüder and the Negüderis (I-07): what is known of Negüder, the Negüderi or Qaraunas bands in Afghanistan and their raids into India in the second half of the 13th century? Who is Polo's "Asedin Soldan" (Ghiyath al-Din Balban is one proposal) and where is "Dalivar"? Use Jean Aubin, "L'ethnogénèse des Qaraunas" (1969), Peter Jackson, The Delhi Sultanate (1999), and Rashid al-Din where accessible. Separate what is attested from Polo's genealogy (Chagatai as uncle) and itinerary.
3. Möngke's funeral (I-12): Polo says about 20,000 people met on the road were killed as Möngke's body was carried to burial. Do Rashid al-Din, the Yuan shi or other contemporary sources report killings during Mongol royal funeral processions, for Möngke or any other khan? What do modern studies of Mongol burial practice say (e.g. Johan Elverskog; Christopher Atwood; Igor de Rachewiltz on the Secret History)? Distinguish attested practice, contemporary rumour and a European topos.
4. Kaidu against Nomoghan (II-15): Polo has Kaidu fight Nomoghan, Kublai's son, and George, "grandson of Prester John", near Karakorum, in a battle without a victor. What do the Yuan shi and Rashid al-Din give for Nomoghan's western command, his capture by Shiregi and other princes (c. 1276–77), and battles near Karakorum with Kaidu? Does George of the Önggüd (Körgüz) appear in these campaigns, and when? Which battle, if any, does Polo's account fit, and is his dating defensible?
5. Jews in Yuan China (II-01): Polo has Jews and Saracens gibe the Christians after Nayan's defeat (1287). What independent evidence is there for Jewish communities in China (Kaifeng, Hangzhou, Dadu) at that date, and for Yuan regulations naming Jews (zhuhu / wuhu)? Is anything known of Jews in the Kaan's army or court c. 1287? A lack of evidence is not proof; say what the evidence is.
6. Chagatai's conversion and the Samarkand church (I-10): Polo says Chagatai became a Christian and that a church of St John the Baptist in Samarkand had a column resting on a stone taken from the Muslims, which miraculously hung in the air. Is there any independent evidence for Chagatai's conversion, for a church of St John in Samarkand, or for this legend in other sources (Syriac, Persian, mission reports)? What do modern studies of Christianity in Central Asia say (e.g. Erica Hunter; Mark Dickens)?
```
