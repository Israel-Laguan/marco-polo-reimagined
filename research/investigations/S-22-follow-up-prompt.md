I am illustrating a three-volume English retelling of Marco Polo's book (events c. 1260–1295). Each plate is painted in the period art tradition of its region. For every scene I need PERIOD VISUAL SOURCES I can look at online: a specific museum object, manuscript folio, wall painting, relief, excavated object or excavation report, with a stable link to an image. I will describe what the object shows in words; I will not copy it. A first research pass found good sources for Mongol court dress and the Mongol hunt. This prompt covers the gaps.

METHOD — read before answering:
• One finding = one object. Give: what it is; where it is held; accession number, shelfmark and folio, cave number, or excavation site and report; its date and who dates it so; a stable URL to the holding institution's own record and, if different, to a zoomable or downloadable image; the image's stated licence (public domain, CC0, CC BY, "all rights reserved", "not stated").
• Say how you inspected each one: "image viewed", "record read, image not viewed", or "known from a publication only" (name it). Describe what the image shows ONLY if you viewed it; otherwise write "not viewed". Do not describe from memory.
• Do not invent accession numbers, folio numbers, cave numbers or URLs. If you know an object exists but cannot find its record, list it under "leads" with what is missing.
• Date discipline: mark every object as inside 1260–1295, earlier (by how much) or later (by how much), and say what a later or earlier object can and cannot safely be used for.
• Prefer museum databases, library digital collections (Gallica, Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin, British Library, e-codices, NPM Taipei, Palace Museum Beijing, National Museum of Korea, ColBase Japan), excavation reports and peer-reviewed costume, armour and ship archaeology over encyclopaedias and blogs. Search in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Persian, Arabic, Italian, French, German, Russian, Mongolian, Tamil and Indonesian where the subject calls for it.
• Already have — do not repeat: Liu Guandao's Khubilai Khan Hunting (NPM); the NPM bust portraits of Khubilai and Chabi; the Vajrabhairava mandala (Met 1992.54); Cleveland Museum cloths of gold 1989.50, 1996.297, 1990.2, 1993.140; Diez Album A fol. 70–72 (Berlin); Jami' al-tawarikh Edinburgh Or.MS 20; Maqamat al-Hariri BnF Arabe 5847; the Quanzhou ship of 1974; the Moko Shurai Ekotoba and the Takashima wrecks; the Bayon naval reliefs.
