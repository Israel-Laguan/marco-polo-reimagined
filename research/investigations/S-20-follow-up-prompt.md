Use this prompt first. The [priority assessment](S-20-research-priorities.md) explains the choices and the safe fallback for each.

```text
I am preparing an English retelling of Marco Polo's book. Research only the high-priority unresolved questions below for the reception chapter III-11, the nickname passage III-10, the B5 reception timeline, and the Columbus plate. The previous research has already supplied a substantial source-qualified reception history. Do not repeat it or turn this into a general survey. A short partial answer is useful.

Evidence rules: Direct originals, institutional catalogues, critical editions, named scholarly reconstructions and second-hand tellings are all welcome. For each finding name the actual source read, date, precise page or section where possible, and distinguish the source's own account from the original or older work it reports. State whether you saw an original/object, edition, catalogue, full secondary passage, an earlier account quoted by a later author, an abstract/snippet, or no source. A well-attributed second-hand account is acceptable; a snippet is only a lead. Do not present an uninspected source as read or an inference as fact. Never invent quotations, pages, URLs or certainty. “Earliest located” is not proof of an absolute first. If evidence is inaccessible or conflicting, say so and give cautious wording or an omission; a partial answer is better than repeated unsupported extraction.

1. COLUMBUS, THE SURVIVING COPY AND THE PLATE. The chapter and its selected hero image currently imply that Columbus's surviving annotated Polo fed his first voyage. The words “WAS this island” begin Morison’s narrative, not a transcribed postil; do not reuse them as Columbus’s words. Identify one or two locatable, meaningful postils in the Seville copy, with the hand and dating attributed to a named source; establish what can responsibly be said about their relationship to 1492. Its Gouda Leeu/Pipino edition and present/retired classmarks are already sourced. Shawcross 2023 p. 380 n. 12 is a lead: earlier AI reports gave conflicting post-1499/1500/1501 interpretations and shelfmarks; obtain her actual note or a named independent account and specify what it dates. The 1497/98 John Day letter mentions a Polo book but does not by itself identify the surviving copy: give the strongest identification argument and any named counterargument if available. Explain what the hero photo actually shows only if its image or catalogue is inspected. If unavailable, propose a caption for the surviving copy that attributes the notes to Columbus and his circle and leaves their dates uncertain; do not assume the copy or any specific note was used before 1492.

2. CATALAN ATLAS. The draft places Chipangu on the 1375 Atlas and attributes eastern names to Polo. Find an image, critical catalogue or named scholar explaining whether it has a Chipangu label or a Japan-like island, and establish one or two concrete Polo-text links if supported. Distinguish a missing label from a missing island and similarities from demonstrable borrowing. If the evidence does not support the specific Chipangu statement, recommend cutting that sentence and provide an independently supported alternative. The surviving Marciana Fra Mauro map's relationship to the Portuguese-commissioned map dispatched by April 1459 is disputed; preserve that qualification and avoid an unqualified “first”.

3. IL MILIONE. The III-10 draft treats one nickname explanation as natural and says the “million lies” insult is late. Locate early evidence of the family/name and the main named explanations through an original, critical edition or scholarly account; identify any dated “million lies” telling without assuming the earliest located is the first. Sangorrín Guallar surveys Emilione, Vilione and wealth/exaggeration proposals. A previous AI report falsely turned the unrelated word pelorce into a nickname etymology. If the insult's chronology cannot be established, recommend dropping the claim that it is late and present competing explanations as attributed interpretations.

Do not re-research Yang Zhijiu, the Zhang/Feng translation bases or a generic Toscanelli controversy: usable attributed accounts and fallbacks are already recorded in the intake. Return a brief findings table: question, evidence actually read and locator, underlying source if different, inspection level, safe wording, and status (supported / attributed / disputed / not found). For every question give an explicit fallback if unresolved. Include only newly verified or materially refined facts, and a short list of works actually consulted with access status and validated bibliographic information. Do not report a failed search as historical proof.
```
