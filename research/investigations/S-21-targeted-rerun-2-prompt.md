# S-21 — narrow source-inspection rerun

The latest returned audit fell below the project source-check threshold. This is a stricter, deliberately narrow prompt; use only if the corresponding manuscript claim is retained. Do not repeat the broad six-dossier survey.

```text
Act as a source auditor for a Marco Polo retelling. Resolve only the following
specific discrepancies. Show the actual source passage, precise named edition,
page/folio/section and stable URL. If you cannot inspect a source, say so;
never present a catalogue entry, search snippet or a scholar's quotation as
having inspected the quoted original. Distinguish the Book's narrative,
editorial notes, and independent observations.

1. Shark-charmers: Tamara Fernando, “Seeing Like the Sea,” Past & Present 254
(2022), 127–160, DOI 10.1093/pastj/gtab002, says the Ceylon government banned
the charmers in 1856; Kunz and Stevenson, The Book of the Pearl (1908),
pp. 115–116, say employment ended in 1885. Consult Fernando's footnote 121,
Ostroff's Beds of Empire p. 133, and the underlying dated Ceylon administrative
records if available. Identify whether a legal prohibition, public salary,
private engagement, or different practice is meant. If the archival evidence
cannot resolve it, report a named disagreement and omit a definitive end year.

2. Socotra: Yule–Cordier II, III.XXXII brackets the whale-hunting paragraph,
attributes the text to Ramusio, and mentions a buoy and head oil without
casks. Other English renderings have “casks of oil”; Srinivasan 2015 attributes
that wording to Yule. Inspect the 1559 Italian, named F and Z witnesses, and
the actual English translation(s). Map the individual words for the float and
the oil; do not assume a cask is a float or transfer a modern translation's
wording into a witness without evidence. Clearly flag inaccessible witnesses.

3. Yunnan crocodilians: directly inspect Wen Huan-ran, He Yeheng, Huang
Zhujian and colleagues, “A preliminary study on the changes of the
distribution of Chinese Malay crocodiles in historical periods and its causes,”
Journal of East China Normal University: Natural Science, 1980(3), 109–121,
or another specialist original with dated records. The Wen 1980 item in
Yunnan Forestry Survey and Planning, pp. 1–44, is about historical FORESTS,
not a substitute crocodile study. Map any individual evidence to locality,
date, species as identified then and now, and confidence. Do not infer
13th-century Carajan presence from a modern distribution or a fossil elsewhere.

4. Kerman: inspect Gerd Weisgerber (2004), “The turquoise mines at Nishapur,”
in T. Stöllner, R. Slotta and A. Vatandoust, eds., Persian Antiques Splendor,
pp. 502–509, the work cited by Shirdam et al. (2021). Determine whether it
mentions Shahr-i Babak, Polo, or 1390–1430 and what the dates refer to.
Do not assume Polo's 1298 composition date disproves a later manuscript date.

For each item return: precise proposition; direct evidence and locator;
verdict confirmed/corrected/disputed/not found; counterevidence; access level;
confidence; and a one-sentence safe manuscript fallback. Provide a complete
bibliography with verified titles and links. Do not invent quotations, dates,
authors, page numbers, archaeological identifications, or witness readings.
```
