# The Cocachin/Kököchin embassy: the Chinese notice, the sailing date, and Arghun's death

Scope: an evidence note for a scholarly retelling. It is not a defence or a refutation of Polo. Each claim is marked with how it was accessed:
- **[DIRECT]**: I read the passage myself, either in a local repo file or on a fetched page.
- **[PRIOR-DIRECT]**: the project read the passage on page scans or a PDF in an earlier pass, as recorded in `book/plans/registry/` or `book/plans/research/results/`. I did not re-open it this session.
- **[REPORT]**: I know it only through another scholar's account or a secondary web page.
- **[SNIPPET/META]**: search snippet or bibliographic record only.

Calendars: Chinese lunisolar (Shoushi calendar, in use from 1281); Hijri; Julian (European dates for 1290–1294 are Julian).

## Q1. What does the Chinese notice (Yongle dadian, from the Jingshi dadian "Zhanchi" section) actually say?

### Takeaway
The notice is an administrative memorial about **rations**. It is not a departure record. On day 17 (of the 8th month, as reported) of Zhiyuan 27 (1290), officials of the Department of State Affairs (尚書省) relayed a request from the *pingzhang* Shabuding (沙不丁). It recalls an imperial order of the **3rd month** of that year to send Uladai (兀魯䚟), Abishqa (阿必失呵) and Khoja (火者) "by way of Ma'bar" (取道馬八兒) to Prince Arghun (阿魯渾大王). Of 160 travellers, 90 had already drawn rations. Shabuding asked that the other 70, said to be gifts from officials or purchased persons, be refused rations. The notice records preparation and provisioning. It names no princess and no Polo, and it gives no sailing date.

### Cited Findings
- **Text (simplified, as quoted in a 2025 popular article):** 「十七日，尚书阿难答、都事别不花等奏：平章沙不丁上言："今年三月奉旨，遣兀鲁歹、阿必失阿、火者，取道马八儿，往阿鲁浑大王位下。同行一百六十人，内九十人已支份例，余七十人，闻是诸官所赠遗及买得者，乞不给分例口粮。"」 The article glosses the year as 至元二十七年 (1290) and calls the record 泉州官员为即将出海的元朝使臣分配口粮. [DIRECT on the fetched page, but the page is a tertiary popular source (我是艾公子, 澎湃号, 2025-04-05)] — [The Paper](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_30552365)
  - The excerpt quoted there begins at 十七日. **The month 八月 is not in the quoted text.** It comes from the year/month heading of the 站赤 entries, as reported by Ma (below). The quotation also ends without the imperial reply. I have not seen any text of the reply and do not assert one.
  - Name spellings vary between transmissions: 兀鲁歹 / 兀魯䚟 / 兀鲁得; 阿必失阿 / 阿必失呵. Treat these as variants, not as different people.
- **Fragment in traditional characters, with juan number:** 「上言今年三月奉㫖遣兀魯䚟阿必失呵火者」, cited as *Yongle dadian* **juan 19418**, from a page paraphrasing Yang Zhijiu. That page reports Yang's explanation: the entry is about distributing rations, so it had no reason to mention the princess or the Polos. [DIRECT on the page, which is a student research site] — [NYU "Did Marco Polo go to China?" page](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/d2-did-marco-polo-go-to-china/the-return-journey-of-marco-polo)
- **Provenance:** Yang Zhijiu found the entry in summer 1941 in 《永乐大典》卷一九四一八 《经世大典·站赤》 and wrote 《关于马可波罗离华的一段汉文记载》. [SNIPPET: Chinese search summaries of chinawriter.com.cn / thepaper.cn items, not opened individually.] A 2018 roundtable with Ma Xiaolin, Li Zhi'an and others, transcribed in The Paper (2019-01-13), confirms that Yang copied the 《经世大典·站赤》 entry on 沙不丁. [DIRECT] — [The Paper 2019](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_2816319)
- **Date as reported in English scholarship:** Ma Xiaolin (2025) gives the notice as "of the eighth lunar month of 1290", naming Uludai, Abishqa and Qoja "together with 160 companions". [PRIOR-DIRECT: the project read the FUP open-access PDF, `ma2025chinese`; see `book/plans/registry/scholarship.md`, "The date of departure". The PDF returned HTTP 403 to me this session.] Ma's wording "in the *Yuanshi*" is a slip for the *Yongle dadian*: Lin & Zhang 2017 (summary only) and the Chinese accounts all give the *Yongle dadian*. [PRIOR, scholarship.md]
- **Jackson (1998)** reports an April–May 1290 order to the three envoys. He says the Europeans "are not mentioned in either of these eastern accounts" (p. 98, n. 87, citing Cleaves 1976). [PRIOR-DIRECT: Amherst-hosted PDF, read in S-19; it returned HTTP 405 to me this session] — [Jackson PDF](https://www.amherst.edu/system/files/Jackson%20Marco%20Polo.pdf)

### Calendar conversion (my computation; check against a published table)
- Zhiyuan began in 1264, so Zhiyuan 27 = 1290.
- I computed true new moons with Meeus's algorithm (ch. 49, main periodic terms), in Beijing local mean time with ΔT ignored (about 0.5 h). Lunations in 1290 (Julian): Apr 11 (10:36), May 10, … Sep 5 (16:06), Oct 5. The month containing the autumn equinox (Julian about Sep 14–15 in 1290) is month 8. The month containing 谷雨 (about Apr 12–13 Julian) is month 3. Counting from the 1290 new year (new moon Feb 11) gives the same result with no intercalary month before month 8.
  - **3rd month, Zhiyuan 27 ≈ 11 April – 9 May 1290 (Julian).** This matches Jackson's "April–May 1290" for the order. The supposed "8th month vs April–May" discrepancy in earlier project notes is therefore not a conflict: they are two different events, the order in month 3 and the memorial in month 8.
  - **8th month, day 17, Zhiyuan 27 ≈ 21 September 1290 (Julian), ±1 day.** The ±1 covers Shoushi-calendar practice versus modern computation. The month-1 new moon falls in mid-afternoon, so the margin is small.
- The script is in the session scratchpad (not durable). These conversions are my own and have not been checked in Cleaves or a calendar concordance, such as 《二十史朔闰表》 or the Academia Sinica 兩千年中西曆轉換. Run one of those before printing a day.

### Inferences
- The notice establishes that in the 3rd month of 1290 an order went out for three named envoys to go to Arghun by the Ma'bar sea route. By the 8th month the party was being provisioned (its ration status was being settled). It does not record the ships' departure.
- It also implies the route was maritime, since Ma'bar is the Coromandel coast. It does not explicitly describe a voyage or a port. Popular accounts place the provisioning at Quanzhou because Shabuding was the Fujian/maritime-trade *pingzhang*. That is inference, not text.

### Gaps
- I did not see the full notice in a primary edition: the Zhonghua shuju facsimile of the *Yongle dadian*, the 站赤 reprint, or Yang's 1941 transcription. The month heading 八月 is reported (Ma; Yang via the Chinese press), not seen. Wikisource juan 19418 was not found (FINAL-ACCESS-1). Two exact-phrase searches this session ("取道馬八兒", "八月十七日 尚书阿难答") returned nothing.
- **Narrowest safe wording:** "a Chinese administrative notice of 1290, preserved in the *Yongle dadian*, concerning rations for three envoys sent to Arghun by way of Ma'bar".

## Q2. What did Yang Zhijiu infer about the sailing date, and on what grounds?

### Takeaway
Yang dated the sailing to **the very end of 1290 or the first days of 1291**, against the then-standard Western date of early 1292 (Yule). Every account I could reach agrees on that. The reasoning attributed to him is the monsoon logic: a party being provisioned in the 8th month would sail with the next NE monsoon, at the turn of 1290/91. I have not read his own words.

### Cited Findings
- Ma 2025 reports Yang's conclusion: "at the very end of 1290 or the first days of 1291". [PRIOR-DIRECT, `ma2025chinese`] Ma lists Yang 1941 and an English note of 1944 (title transcribed secondhand as "A New Discovery referring to Marco Polo's Departure from the Chinese Source") with Ho. Lin & Zhang cite "Yang and Ho (1945)". [PRIOR, scholarship.md; the 1944/1945 date is unresolved]
- The 2018 Tianjin roundtable (The Paper, 2019) reports that Yang's paper put the departure in 1291, not 1292 (「不是1292年，而是1291年」). It credits Pelliot with reaching the same 1291 date independently from d'Ohsson (多桑) and Persian sources. It notes that both the Book and the 站赤 record point to departure from 泉州. [DIRECT; the quoted words are the roundtable's, not Yang's] — [The Paper 2019](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_2816319)
- A Chinese search summary says Yang "把马可·波罗离华时间定在1291年初". [SNIPPET]
- A 2025 popular piece says 按照季候风的规律，他们将于1291年从泉州出发. This is the article's own reasoning, not quoted from Yang. [DIRECT, tertiary] — [The Paper 2025](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_30552365)
- The NYU page paraphrases two of Yang's arguments: the ration context explains the silence about the princess and the Polos; and Polo's precise naming of the three envoys, with no other written source he could have copied, supports his presence. [DIRECT, tertiary] — [NYU page](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/d2-did-marco-polo-go-to-china/the-return-journey-of-marco-polo)

### Inferences
- The "end of 1290 / start of 1291" window fits the monsoon logic. NE-monsoon sailings from Fujian normally left between about November and February, and the provisioning date is about late September 1290 Julian. The window is still an inference from the notice plus sailing practice. It is not a recorded departure.

### Gaps
- Yang's 1941 article (《文史杂志》 1:12, as commonly cited) and its 1944/45 English version were not inspected. Nor was his later book 《马可波罗在中国》 (1999).
- **Fallback wording:** "Yang Zhijiu, who identified the notice in 1941, inferred a sailing at the end of 1290 or the first days of 1291."

## Q3. What do Cleaves, Haw, Jackson, Ma, Vogel, Wood, de Rachewiltz and Pelliot argue?

### Takeaway
None of the key works (Cleaves 1976, Haw 2006 or 2023, Pelliot's "Cocachin") was read directly this session or before. The positions below come through reports. They agree that the Chinese and Persian sources corroborate the **embassy** and do not name the Polos. They differ on the date of departure. The Yang/Pelliot line gives the beginning of 1291; Yule gives early 1292. Arrival estimates run from late 1292 to about winter 1293–94. Haw has a dedicated 2023 article on departure whose dates are unread.

### Cited Findings
- **Cleaves 1976** (*HJAS* 36: 181–203, [DOI 10.2307/2718743](https://doi.org/10.2307/2718743)). Only p. 181 has been seen [PRIOR-DIRECT, JSTOR preview, FINAL-ACCESS-2]. It says the chronology was established thanks to "three scholars—Chinese, French, and English" and names Oulatai, Apusca and Coja via Moule–Pelliot. Jackson reports that Cleaves confirms the mission from Chinese and Persian sources (Jackson p. 98, n. 87) [REPORT]. Cleaves did **not** discover the Chinese notice; Yang did (Ma pp. 83, 95) [REPORT]. Cleaves's own departure date, his citation of the Chinese text and his Persian edition are **unseen**.
- **Jackson 1998** (*BSOAS* 61: 82–101). He gives the April–May 1290 order and an arrival of about 1292–93. Rashid gives no explicit arrival date, though the arrival was clearly before winter 1293–94 (n. 87). Neither eastern account names the Europeans (p. 98). [PRIOR-DIRECT, S-19 read of the Amherst PDF]
- **Ma Xiaolin 2025** reports Yang's date (above) and calls the notice direct confirmation. That is her interpretation; the notice does not identify the travellers. [PRIOR-DIRECT; scholarship.md]
- **Haw 2023**, "Marco Polo's Departure from China", *ZDMG* 173: 145–171, [DOI 10.13173/zdmg.173.1.145](https://doi.org/10.13173/zdmg.173.1.145). The abstract was read earlier: it treats the dates of the envoys' arrival, their failed overland return and the sea departure, rejects Ménard's Java = Borneo, and discusses Gauenispola. [PRIOR-DIRECT abstract; `kept-gaps-indirect-routes-2026-11-17.md`] **The dates Haw proposes are unread.** A search this session found no open copy. Haw 2006 (*Marco Polo's China*, Routledge): not inspected.
- **de Rachewiltz** ("F. Wood's *Did Marco Polo go to China?* A critical appraisal", 1997/2002). He reports that Rashid indirectly confirms the deaths of two envoys and estimates arrival in early 1293. [REPORT via NYU page; his text not seen this session]
- **Vogel** (*Marco Polo Was in China*, 2013). He estimates arrival in about September–October 1293, when Arghun was already dead, and says Rashid confirms the marriage to Ghazan. [REPORT via NYU page; Vogel not seen]
- **Pelliot.** As reported in the 2018 roundtable, he reached a 1291 departure from d'Ohsson and Persian sources. [REPORT] His *Notes on Marco Polo* I, s.v. "Cocachin", is **not inspected**. Archive.org holds only vol. 2 (`notesonmarcopolo0000paul`); the "Cocachin" entry is in vol. 1. [DIRECT check of the archive.org search API this session]
- **Wood 1995.** She notes that no Chinese source names Polo (Gadrat-Ouerfelli 2026 §§19–20, PRIOR-DIRECT). Her handling of the 1290 notice was not checked.
- **zh.wikipedia "阔阔真 (卜鲁罕氏)"** gives: sailing from Quanzhou 1291; arrival in late 1292; Arghun dead in 1291; marriage to Ghazan in 1293. It cites Cleaves 1976, Jeong 2016 and de Rachewiltz without page-level attribution. [DIRECT, tertiary; do not cite as authority] — [zh.wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E9%98%94%E9%98%94%E7%9C%9F_(%E5%8D%9C%E9%B2%81%E7%BD%95%E6%B0%8F))
- **Yule (1903)** gives "the beginning of 1292" from Zayton, a working chronology of January 1292 departure → Hormuz about November 1293 → Ghazan's camp a month or two later, and arrival in Persia in winter 1293–94 after Hammer. [DIRECT: `book/refs/yule-vol1.txt` lines 6002–6004, 6255–6284 (Introduction, notes 19–20)]

### Inferences
- The scholarship divides into a **Chinese-source-anchored** chronology (Yang, Pelliot, Ma, Lin & Zhang: sailing at the turn of 1290/91) and **Yule's** reconstruction from Polo's figures and Hammer (sailing January 1292). Yule's reconstruction predates the Chinese notice.
- Yule worked back 26 months from a winter 1293–94 arrival. A 1290/91 sailing implies either a longer total voyage or an earlier arrival (late 1292 or 1293, as de Rachewiltz and Vogel estimate). Neither Persian source used here dates the arrival.

### Gaps
- Not seen: Cleaves's own date and argument; Haw 2006 and 2023; Pelliot's "Cocachin"; Vogel 2013; de Rachewiltz; Wood's treatment. No modern author was found arguing for a 1292 departure apart from Yule (scholarship.md).
- **Fallback wording:** "Modern estimates of the sailing range from the end of 1290 to early 1292."

## Q4. When did Arghun die, and could he have died before or after the departure?

### Takeaway
Arghun died on **7 Rabīʿ I 690 AH = 10 March 1291 (Julian)**. This is the standard date. Yule prints it in his Prologue note, but his Introduction footnote says "12th March, 1291". The ordering against the sailing **depends on the sailing chronology**:
- Yang's end-1290/early-1291 window puts the sailing before Arghun's death, so he died while the fleet was at sea.
- Yule's January 1292 puts the death before the sailing.

The Book itself says only that on arrival "they found that Argon was dead". It does not say when he died relative to the sailing.

### Cited Findings
- **Polo (Yule's translation):** "And when they got thither they found that Argon was dead, so the Lady was delivered to CASAN, his son." [DIRECT: `book/refs/yule-vol1.txt` 14013–14014, Prologue ch. XVIII]
- **Yule's Prologue XVIII note:** "Arghún Khan died 10th March, 1291. He was succeeded (23rd July) by his brother Kaikhátú … put to death 24th March, 1295." [DIRECT, yule-vol1.txt 14135–14137]
- **Yule's Introduction:** "Arghún Khan too had been dead even before they quitted China", with footnote [21] "Died 12th March, 1291". [DIRECT, yule-vol1.txt 6016–6017, 6287] This "before they quitted China" is **Yule's inference from his own 1292 sailing date**. It is not a statement in the Book.
- **Rashid al-Din (Thackston):** Arghun's death on 10 March 1291 is at p. 575; Gaikhatu's enthronement on 23 July 1291 at pp. 580–581 (OCR only). [PRIOR-DIRECT, `book/plans/registry/comparanda.md` row III-09/III-12] The Hijri wording on p. 575 is not quoted in the project notes.
- **Conversion check:** my tabular (arithmetical) Hijri conversion gives 7 Rabīʿ I 690 → JD 2192663.5 = Saturday, 10 March 1291 (Julian). The tabular calendar can differ from an observed date by 1–2 days, which may explain Yule's 12 March variant. Earlier project work (CHR-people-2-intake #1) reached the same result. A "7 March" in some secondary material is the Hijri day misread as a Julian date (facts.md F-33).
- **Arghun's last illness:** Hammer (I, p. 391) and Yule, via Rashid, describe a mercury–sulphur elixir taken for eight months. [PRIOR-DIRECT, page image; facts.md F-61] This is context only.
- **Alternative dates:** I found none in scholarship. The only variant seen is Yule's internal 10 vs 12 March.

### Inferences
- Death and sailing on each chronology:
  - On Yang's window (sailing by about January 1291), Arghun was alive when the fleet sailed and died about 2–3 months later, during the voyage.
  - On Yule's January 1292, he had been dead for about 10 months before the sailing.
  - The death date itself is firm. The ordering is not.
- News of a 10 March 1291 death could hardly have reached Quanzhou before a December 1290/January 1291 sailing. If the sailing was in 1292, Kublai's court might have known. This bears on why the mission continued, but it is speculation and should not be printed.
- **Manuscript conflict (do not edit; flag):**
  - `book/vol-3/ch-09-cocachin-return.md` line 29 says Argon "had died on the tenth of March in the year 1291 — even before the ships left Cathay".
  - Note [11] (line 69) says "dead before sailing".
  - Both assert Yule's ordering, which the chapter's own Note [10] (line 67) undercuts by reporting Yang's end-1290/early-1291 sailing.

### Recommended wording (no ordering asserted)
- Body text: "When they came to Persia they found Argon dead. He had died on 10 March 1291, and his brother Kiacatu had taken the throne that July. Whether the fleet had sailed before or after his death depends on the date of the sailing, which is disputed."
- Note: "Arghun died 7 Rabīʿ I 690 / 10 March 1291 (Rashid al-Din, tr. Thackston p. 575; Yule, Prol. XVIII n.). A Chinese notice preserved in the *Yongle dadian* shows that the envoys were ordered west in the 3rd month of 1290 and were being provisioned for the Ma'bar route in the 8th month. Yang Zhijiu inferred a sailing at the end of 1290 or the first days of 1291, which would put Arghun's death during the voyage. Yule's reconstruction (January 1292) would put it before. The Book says only that the travellers found him dead on arrival."
- Narrowest fallback: "Arghun died in March 1291; the Book says the travellers learned of it on arrival."

### Gaps
- I have not seen Rashid's Hijri wording at Thackston p. 575 or the Persian text. Wassaf (`book/refs/wassaf-elliot-dowson-1871.txt`) has no Arghun-death or Kököchin passage: a grep for Arghun/Kökächin/Ghazan found only general mentions. The Elliot–Dowson extract does not cover this.

## Q5. What do Rashid al-Din and Wassaf say about the bride's arrival, marriage, route and the Polos?

### Takeaway
Rashid records the arrival and marriage at Abhar. The khwāja and the other envoys whom **Arghun** had sent to the Qa'an bring Kökächin and Chinese rarities, and Ghazan marries her. **No date, no route, no ship or sea, no Frank or Polo.** No Chinese or Persian source seen names the Polos or explicitly describes the sea voyage. The Chinese notice's "via Ma'bar" is the only route indicator.

### Cited Findings
- **Rashid, Thackston Part 3 p. 606** (margin sigla [868]–[869] Karimi, [A280] Alizade); Persian text in Jahn, *Geschichte Ġāzān-Ḫān's*, p. 40. At Abhar, the khwāja and envoys Arghun had sent to the Qa'an for a kinswoman of the senior Bulughan arrive with Kökächin. Ghazan marries her, then sends a tiger and gifts to Gaikhatu. No Polos, Franks, ships, sea or route. [PRIOR-DIRECT, page scans, English and Persian; comparanda.md] Wives list: Thackston p. 593. HathiTrust search-only hits for Kökächin: P2 p. 351, pp. 454–469; P3 p. 606. No hit for Uladai or Abishqa. [PRIOR, search-only; thackston-hathitrust intake]
- **Boyle 1970** ("Rashīd al-Dīn and the Franks", *CAJ* 14: 62–67), p. 66, renders the same passage. He identifies Khwāja with Polo's Coja and infers on pp. 66–67 that the unnamed companions were presumably the Polos. That is his inference. Thackston prints *khwāja* as a title. [PRIOR-DIRECT, S-19-retry intake]
- Yule quotes Hammer as saying Ghazan "met at Abher the ambassador whom **he** had sent". Rashid says Arghun sent the envoys: Hammer's or Yule's error. [DIRECT, yule-vol1.txt 14139–14146; PRIOR comparison in comparanda.md]
- Rashid elsewhere, via Yule: Tukiti Khatun's *ordu* was given to Kukachi Khatun, "brought from the Kaan's Court", who died in Shaʿbān 695 (June 1296). [DIRECT in Yule, 14152–14157; citing Quatremère p. 97, not seen]
- **Wassaf:** Yule notes that Hammer's account is "not Wassáf, I find". [DIRECT, yule-vol1.txt 14149, 6276] The local Elliot–Dowson Wassaf extract has no Kököchin material (grep). [DIRECT]
- Jackson confirms that neither the Chinese nor the Persian account mentions the Europeans (p. 98). [PRIOR-DIRECT]
- Two of the three envoys died en route: this is Polo's claim (Yule Prol. XVIII). That Rashid names only Khwāja and so "indirectly confirms" the deaths is de Rachewiltz's or Yang's inference. [REPORT]

### Inferences
- The three-way match (Chinese: three named envoys; Polo: the same three names; Rashid: a khwāja arriving with Kökächin) supports the embassy and Polo's knowledge of it. Polo's presence aboard remains an inference.

### Gaps
- Not seen: Alizade III pp. 280–281 or Karimi; Thackston p. 575 Hijri text; Quatremère p. 97; Wassaf in a full edition.
- **Fallback wording:** "Rashid al-Din records the bride's arrival and her marriage to Ghazan at Abhar, without a date and without naming the Venetians or the voyage."
