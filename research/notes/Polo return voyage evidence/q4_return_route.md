# Evidence ledger: the Polos' return route from the Yuan realm to Venice (c. 1290/91–1295)

Abbreviations: **YC** = Yule–Cordier 1903 (local OCR, `book/refs/yule-vol1.txt`, `yule-vol2.txt`; Yule's base is Pauthier's French, with Ramusio additions in square brackets). **M-P** = Moule & Pelliot, *The Description of the World* I (1938), composite translation with witness sigla in the margin ([archive.org `descriptionofwor01polo`](https://archive.org/details/descriptionofwor01polo), full djvu OCR downloaded and grepped this pass). **F** = Franco-Italian BnF fr. 1116 (the "G.T." of Yule), seen here only through M-P and YC. **R** = Ramusio 1559. **Z** = Toledo Latin.

Access key: **[read]** = I read the passage in this pass (OCR or local text). **[prior]** = read by an earlier project pass and recorded in the registry; not re-read by me. **[reported]** = known only through another scholar.

## Q1. Stage-by-stage classification

### Takeaway
Only five stages are explicit in the Prologue return passage of every witness checked: "Java" after about three months' sailing, the land of Argon, Trebizond, Constantinople, Negropont and Venice (1295). The departure port, Hormuz and Tabriz are named in no Polo witness for this journey; they come from editors, Ramusio's own preface among them. Abhar comes only from Rashid al-Din, who names no Polo. Ma'bar is the one Indian stop with both a witness claim (Z/R) and an independent Chinese document, which names it as the embassy's planned route.

### Inferences
- A continuous solid line from Quanzhou to Hormuz to Tabriz to Trebizond cannot be supported. Only the Sumatra ("Java") stop, the Persian arrival (place unnamed), and Trebizond → Constantinople → Negropont → Venice are textual. Everything between Sumatra and Trebizond is either a single attested point (Ma'bar; Abhar from Rashid) or an editor's guess (Hormuz, Tabriz, Ceylon, Kayal, Tana).
- Hormuz has an editorial pedigree older than Yule: Ramusio in 1559 framed it explicitly as conjecture. Citing "Ramusio" for Hormuz without saying it comes from his *preface* would mislead.

### Gaps
- I could not reach a facsimile of *Yongle dadian* j. 19418; the Wikisource transcription is the only text I saw. Check against the 1960 Zhonghua facsimile or Cleaves 1976 (HJAS 36: 181–203, not seen).
- The Simion–Burgio digital Ramusio edition was not inspected. Ramusio's preface is known here only from M-P's reprint (OCR, p. 583), so its 1559 page and folio are unverified.
- Orlandini 1926 and the original text of Maffeo's will were not seen; I relied on M-P's translation and Latin.

## Q2. Which stages rest on Polo witnesses, Persian or Chinese evidence, or editors?

### Takeaway
Polo witnesses name Java, the land of Argon, Ghazan or the Arbre Sec (R), Kiacatu, Trebizond, Constantinople, Negropont and Venice, plus Ma'bar "on his way home" (Z/R). Persian evidence (Rashid) supplies Abhar. Chinese evidence (the 1290 *Zhanchi* notice) supplies the envoys and Ma'bar as their ordered route. Zayton, Hormuz, Tabriz, Ceylon, Kayal and Tana as stages are editors' inferences: Ramusio's preface, Yule, and Moule–Pelliot.

### Cited Findings
- F and the composite: the three months to Java, "eighteen months" in the Indian sea, then the land of Argon, Trepesonde, Constantinople, Negrepont, Venice, 1295. No port of departure, no landfall, no Persian city — [M-P I pp. 90–93](https://archive.org/details/descriptionofwor01polo) [read, OCR].
- Pauthier/Yule base text: same sequence, with 13 ships against F's 14 — `book/refs/yule-vol1.txt` 13976–14058 [read].
- Ramusio's text additions (via YC notes 3, 5, 6, 7 and M-P's R sigla): the eight-month aborted overland attempt; 600 dead and only Coja surviving; word sent to Kiacatu; Casan at the Arbre Sec; the nine-month stay; news of Kublai's death on the road. **No port names** — `yule-vol1.txt` 14110–58 [read].
- Ramusio's preface (editorial, 1559): the departure port is not named in the text; Ormus and Carmania are offered as conjecture — [M-P I p. 583 reprint](https://archive.org/details/descriptionofwor01polo) [read, OCR].
- Rashid al-Din: Abhar, Arghun's envoys, Kökächin, Ghazan; no Polos — Thackston 1998 p. 606 [prior; registry comparanda].
- Wassaf: Yule says Hammer's Abhar account is "not… copying from Wassáf" (`yule-vol1.txt` 14149) [read]. A grep of the local Elliot–Dowson Wassaf extract (`book/refs/wassaf-elliot-dowson-1871.txt`) for Kökächin, Abhar and Bulughan returned nothing [read, negative search only].
- Chinese notice: three envoys, 160 people, "取道馬八兒" — [Yongle dadian j. 19418, Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/永樂大典/卷19418) [read, transcription].

### Inferences
- The independent sources are evidence for the *embassy* and its bride: the envoys' names, the size of the party, the planned Ma'bar route, and the delivery at Abhar to Ghazan. Neither source names the Polos. The Polos' presence rests on the Book alone. The project registry (F-15) already notes that Jackson 1998, pp. 98–99, makes this point [prior].
- The Book matches the Chinese notice in its envoy names and Rashid in having Ghazan as husband after Arghun's death. That is consistent with insider knowledge, but it does not corroborate any route point except, loosely, Ma'bar.

### Gaps
- I did not check whether Haw 2023 ("departure") or Cleaves 1976 discuss the departure port or the meaning of 取道馬八兒. Both are recorded as unread in the registry.

## Q3. Specific questions from the brief

### Takeaway
(a) Ramusio names Hormuz only as a conjecture in his preface; no earlier witness names it as the landfall. (b) Zayton as the departure port is inferred; Bk III ch. V's "leaving the port of Zayton" is a sailing direction, not the 1291 departure. (c) Abhar appears only in Rashid al-Din, through Hammer into Yule's note. (d) Trebizond, Constantinople and Negropont appear in Prologue ch. XVIII (Yule) / ch. 19 (M-P). Tauris does not appear on the return. Trebizond also appears in Maffeo's 1310 will, as a loss.

### Cited Findings
- Ramusio's preface: "si può se non per conietture pensare… se ne venissero per mare fino nel sine Persico all'isola di Ormus" (OCR, normalised) — [M-P I p. 583](https://archive.org/details/descriptionofwor01polo) [read].
- Ramusio's preface on departure: "da che porto… si partissero, non si può dire cosa alcuna, perche non lo nominano" — M-P I p. 582–583 [read, OCR].
- Bk III ch. V: "on leaving the port of Zayton you sail west-south-west for 1500 miles" — `yule-vol2.txt` 14510–11 [read]. Bk III ch. I describes the Manzi ships, not a departure [read: heading only].
- Abhar: YC Prol. XVIII n. 6, quoting Hammer, *Ilchane* II p. 20 [read]; Rashid in Thackston p. 606 [prior].
- Trebizond in Marco's own 1324 will: the project's S-18 follow-up found Marco's will "silent" on it (`book/plans/research/results/S-18-followup-intake.md` FU-6) [prior]. The loss is in **Maffeo's** will of 6 Feb. 1309 m.v., not Marco's — M-P I p. 28 [read]. Yule reports a related act under 1308 Venetian style; this discrepancy is still open (registry S18-F8) [prior].

### Inferences
- The manuscript's ch-09 n. [8] says "Tradition of the Book and of later commentary places the embarkation at… Zayton". The Book does not place it there. "Commentary (Yule; Moule–Pelliot) places…" would be accurate. This is a note for the author; I did not edit the manuscript.

### Gaps
- Marco's 1324 will was not read directly in this pass.

## Q4. Outward voyage vs return voyage: what does Bk III claim Marco saw?

### Takeaway
Bk III mostly describes places in an impersonal "when you leave X…" sequence. Explicit personal-presence claims are few. Chamba in 1285 is a separate mission. Sumatra (Samara, five months; Lambri seed; Fansur sago) is personal but not tied to the bride voyage. Ma'bar is personal and explicitly "on his way home", but only in Z/R. For Ceylon, Kayal, Quilon, Tana and Cambay, the chapters describe the places without claiming a return visit.

### Cited Findings
- Chamba: "In the year of Christ 1285, Messer Marco Polo was in that country" — YC III ch. V, `yule-vol2.txt` 14550 [read].
- Samara: detained five months; with about 2,000 men he built stockades against the "man-eaters" (bracketed R addition) — III ch. X, 15988–96 [read].
- Lambri: brazil seed taken to Venice; Fansur: Marco "and his party" ate sago bread; Marco "never was there" on the other side of the island — III ch. XI, 16413–45 [read].
- Ma'bar: "on his way home" — Z/R only, M-P I p. 389; YC III ch. XVII in brackets [read].
- Hormos in Bk I ch. XIX is described as visited ("befell when he was there"; YC I index i. 107) [read: grep only]. Ramusio's preface argues the Hormuz and Kerman material could only come from the return, not the Ayas–Cathay outward route (M-P I p. 583) [read]. Yule instead routes the outward journey via Hormuz (project manuscript I-F1 treats that as Yule's inference) [prior].

### Inferences
- Map rule: draw Bk III places as "described" by default; mark only Samara, Lambri/Fansur and Ma'bar (Z/R) as "narrator claims presence", and link only Ma'bar textually to the homeward leg.

### Gaps
- I did not check M-P's sigla for the Samara and Fansur presence statements (F versus R/Z). They may also be partly additions; Yule brackets part of the Samara passage.
- Sources I could not inspect, with the narrowest safe fallback: Cleaves 1976 and Haw 2023 ("departure date debated; the 1290 notice dates the envoys' orders"); Jackson 1998 (Amherst PDF URL now returns HTML; rely on the registry's prior reading); the Simion–Burgio Ramusio (cite Ramusio via M-P's reprint); Orlandini 1926 and the original wills (say "loss", not "robbery"; date unspecified); the Thackston scans (prior pass only; keep the wording to "Rashid places the marriage at Abhar and names no Polo").
