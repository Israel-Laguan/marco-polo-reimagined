# G4 — Venetian and Genoese life, ships c. 1270, and the harbour of Ayas/Laiazzo: source leads

Scope: archives and collections (not single objects) likely to hold dated, viewable images from c. 1260–1300, for an illustrator who describes the images in words and does not copy them. Confidence labels: **opened and confirmed** / **found in a catalogue or citation** / **from memory**. NEW = published after 2013.

Access note (tested by script on 2026-11-13): Gallica (`gallica.bnf.fr/iiif/...`) returned a **Cloudflare bot challenge**. Heidelberg digi (`digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de`) returned a **"not a bot" check**. cini.it returned **HTTP 403**. Only a viewer in a normal browser will reach these sites. Scripted IIIF harvesting did not work from this environment.

## Which sources are most likely to hold a dated, viewable image from 1260–1300?

### Takeaway
No candidate found holds a firmly dated image of Venetian or Genoese people or ships that falls inside 1260–1300. The two strongest leads sit at either end of the window. The first is the Genoese Annals codex (BnF lat. 10136), whose drawings date from c. 1150 to the 1230s. The second is the Carte Pisane (c. 1270, but disputed), which is a chart and has no ship or city vignettes. The best material for women, merchants and civic life is the Venetian mariegole (from 1260 onwards, but most are 14th century) and the Genoese Cocharelli fragments (c. 1330). For Ayas there is no period image. Survey photographs of the ruins and the portolan charts' toponyms are the only usable material.

### Ranked proposals

**1. Annales Ianuenses (Caffaro and continuators), Paris, BnF, ms. lat. 10136 ("Codex N")** — *found in a catalogue or citation (the shelfmark and image corpus confirmed by opening the front matter of Haug 2016); the Gallica record was not opened*
- (a) The official Genoese civic chronicle, 1099–1294, held at BnF Manuscrits. I did not find a Gallica ARK. The only Gallica attempt was the IIIF pattern, and it was blocked. The shelfmark appears in [Haug 2016 (publisher's reading sample, opened)](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-4237396-47cf0890f7.pdf) and on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_ianuenses) (used only for orientation).
- (b) Haug counts **116 images**: pen marginal drawings, plus body-colour miniatures in spaces left during copying. They were added **in stages after the text was copied into the city's register, "from the mid-12th century to the 1230s"** (Haug, p. 15: "seit der Mitte des 12. Jahrhunderts bis in die 30er Jahre des 13. Jahrhunderts"). Haug includes a catalogue (ch. 8) and a chapter on dating the drawings (§3.5). The Art Bulletin cites miniatures on **fols. 110r and 116r** ([Art Bulletin 97:3, 2015](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00043079.2015.1009318), seen only as a search snippet).
- (c) Viewer-only, on Gallica if it is digitised (scripts get a bot check). A phototype facsimile of the text was printed in 1890 in 186 loose plates ([listing](https://maremagnum.com/en/libri-moderni/mxcix-mcclxxxvii-cafari-et-continuatorum-annales-januenses-edizione-in-fototipia-in-186-tavole-sciolte/167258920)).
- (d) Gallica / archivesetmanuscrits.bnf.fr: "Latin 10136", "Annales Ianuenses", "Annales januenses", "Caffaro". Haug's catalogue numbers (Kat. Nr.) give the folio for each drawing.
- (e) **Risk: the dates are early.** The images stop c. 1230, about 30 years before the window. Whether there are ship drawings is **not confirmed** in what I read: neither Haug's excerpt nor any search result describes a galley drawing. The section for 1270–1294 (Jacopo Doria, including Meloria 1284) appears not to be illustrated.

**2. Carte Pisane, Paris, BnF, Cartes et plans, GE B-1118 (RES)** — *found in a catalogue or citation; not opened*
- (a) The oldest surviving portolan chart, 1030 × 480 mm. The Gallica ARK `btv1b52503226n` is cited by [CNRS Images](https://images.cnrs.fr/en/video/6873). The BnF catalogue notice is [cb406673515](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb406673515) (seen in search; it gives "ca 1270", probably Genoa).
- (b) Radiocarbon dating of the parchment gives 1170–1270 (95%). The content cannot predate c. 1265 ([Gaspar, MEDEA-CHART "Chart of the week", 18 Mar 2021](https://medea-chart.wixsite.com/medea-chart/single-post/chart-of-the-week-carte-pisane-anonymous-circa-1270), opened and confirmed). Ramon Pujades argues for a late-14th-century date, and Tony Campbell contests that ([maphistory.info](https://www.maphistory.info/CartePisaneTEXT.html), seen in search).
- (c) Viewer-only on Gallica (scripts get Cloudflare). There is also an [entry in the MEDEA database](https://medea.fc.ul.pt/view/chart/50) (not opened).
- (d) "Carte Pisane", "GE B-1118", "portulan"; for Ayas, look for the toponym "Laiazzo / Lajazzo / Aiazzo / Layas" on the Cilician coast.
- (e) **It has no ships, figures or city vignettes**, only hazard symbols (Gaspar). I did not confirm that Ayas is named on it. The date is disputed. It is useful only for the coastline and harbour position.

**3. Pietro Vesconte, portolan atlas for Marino Sanudo's *Liber secretorum*, BAV Pal. lat. 1362 A (c. 1320–21)** — *found in a catalogue or citation; Heidelberg page not opened (bot check)*
- (a) Vatican Library. Heidelberg's digitisation is at [digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/bav_pal_lat_1362a](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/bav_pal_lat_1362a), dated "1320". Wikipedia gives c. 1320–21.
- (b) Signed by Vesconte, a Genoese mapmaker working in Venice. Its eastern Mediterranean sheets should show Cilicia and Laiazzo, which was the main crusade-planning port in Sanudo's text. Laiazzo is **not confirmed** on the sheet.
- (c) Heidelberg normally offers IIIF and free download (from memory). From this environment it is blocked by a bot check.
- (d) "Pal. lat. 1362 A", "Vesconte", "Sanudo", "Laiazzo".
- (e) **It is late**, 20+ years after the window. Sanudo manuscripts sometimes include ship and city vignettes, but I did not verify any in this copy.

**4. Venetian mariegole (statute books of guilds and confraternities) — guided by Lyle Humphrey's corpus** — *found in a catalogue or citation*
- (a) The main holders are the Museo Correr library, the Archivio di Stato di Venezia and the Biblioteca Marciana. Many leaves are dispersed (Cleveland, Morgan, Gardner, Boston). Book: Lyle Humphrey, *La miniatura per le confraternite e le arti veneziane. Mariegole dal 1260 al 1460* (Cierre Edizioni, series "Cultura popolare veneta"). It is listed on the [Fondazione Cini page](https://www.cini.it/en/?p=51517) (403 to script; seen in search). **NEW (2015, year from memory)**. It derives from her NYU IFA dissertation (2007), "The Illumination of Confraternity and Guild Statutes in Venice, ca. 1260–1500".
- (b) The corpus starts in 1260. Mariegole often carry founding dates and dated colophons, and their frontispieces show confraternity members: men and women, notaries and officers before a saint. Humphrey's dating method is the key to finding which manuscripts are pre-1300. The Scuola di San Giovanni Evangelista (founded 1261) has a mariegola frontispiece, but it is in Cleveland, which is excluded.
- (c) Mostly viewer-only or not digitised. The Correr miniatures circulate through Bridgeman as paywalled images (e.g. [Wool Guild mariegola, 1385](https://www.bridgemanimages.com/en/noartistknown/miniature-from-the-wool-guild-corporation-mariegola-venice-manuscript-1385-italy-14th-century/nomedium/asset/765256)).
- (d) "mariegola", "capitolare", "scuola", "arte dei …", "Classe IV" (Correr mariegole), "Scuole piccole" (ASVe).
- (e) **Most surviving illuminated mariegole are 14th century**, and "14th century" labels count as undated here. I did not identify a specific pre-1300 illuminated mariegola. The images are not free.

**5. Cocharelli treatise fragments (Genoa, c. 1330 or before 1324), British Library Add MS 27695 (with 28841, Egerton 3127, 3781), Cleveland and Bargello** — *found in a catalogue or citation; not opened*
- (a) 27 leaves dispersed across six volumes. The BL record is at [searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002031109](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002031109) (seen in search; the record has a "Digitised Content" field, but I did not verify any images). There is also a Müller & Schindler facsimile from 2025 (**NEW**) ([facsimilefinder](https://www.facsimilefinder.com/facsimiles/cocharelli-codex-facsimile)).
- (b) It is the richest Genoese pictorial source for dress, merchants, families and court scenes. Its dating basis is the text's dedication and family context, which [Medioevi 2020 (Concina & Faunce)](https://iris.univr.it/retrieve/93a524a5-7171-414f-914e-b3bb1b580d30/Concina_Faunce%20Estratto%20Medioevi%206_2020.pdf) discusses (not opened).
- (c) BL viewer, which may still be degraded after the 2023 cyberattack. The Cleveland leaves are excluded.
- (d) "Cocharelli", "Add MS 27695", "Tractatus de septem vitiis".
- (e) **It is late (c. 1330)**, and its dating is debated.

**6. Ayas/Laiazzo site documentation — Robert W. Edwards survey photographs (1974–81) and recent Turkish fieldwork** — *found in a catalogue or citation; not opened*
- (a) [CHARLVAR archive, site 76 "Ayas / Yumurtalık"](https://charlvarchive.org/Site/76) has photographs of the sea castle, the land castle, the harbour towers and the town. The underlying publication is Edwards, *The Fortifications of Armenian Cilicia* (Dumbarton Oaks Studies 23, 1987). Erdal Eser (Cumhuriyet University) has an article, "From the Roman Settlement of Aigai to Ayas", that reports cleaning and excavation of "locations A and B". It is a PDF on [hrcak.srce.hr/file/95082](https://hrcak.srce.hr/file/95082), and the file path names *Vjesnik Arheološkog muzeja u Zagrebu* 2009. The text could not be extracted, so the title, author and journal are **not confirmed**.
- (b) These are 20th/21st-century photographs and plans of 13th-century fabric, not period images. They are useful for the harbour layout, the towers and the mole.
- (c) CHARLVAR is open web pages (licence not checked). The hrcak PDF is a direct PDF.
- (d) "Ayas", "Yumurtalık", "Aigai/Aegae", "Laiazzo/Lajazzo", "Kız Kalesi" (sea castle), "kazı" (excavation).
- (e) It is not a period image. Hellenkemper's 1976 survey (below) is in print only.

### Cited findings
- The drawings in BnF lat. 10136 number 116 and were made successively from the mid-12th century to the 1230s — [Haug 2016](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-4237396-47cf0890f7.pdf)
- The Carte Pisane's parchment is radiocarbon-dated to 1170–1270, its content is not before c. 1265, and it has no figure vignettes — [MEDEA-CHART](https://medea-chart.wixsite.com/medea-chart/single-post/chart-of-the-week-carte-pisane-anonymous-circa-1270)
- The Carte Pisane's Gallica ARK is btv1b52503226n — [CNRS Images](https://images.cnrs.fr/en/video/6873)
- Pal. lat. 1362 A is digitised at Heidelberg and dated 1320 — [Heidelberg (search result)](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/bav_pal_lat_1362a)
- Humphrey's mariegole corpus begins in 1260 — [Fondazione Cini](https://www.cini.it/en/?p=51517)

### Inferences
- For a ship of c. 1270, period images will not do the job by themselves. The reliable route is documentary reconstruction: Pryor's work on the 1268 contracts for Louis IX's ships (Venetian and Genoese naves), together with Martin's corpus of Venetian ship images. San Marco's 13th-century atrium mosaics are the nearest in-window Venetian images of vessels (from memory; I did not verify them here).
- For Ayas, the honest plate basis is modern site plans plus the portolan coastline. Anything more is imagination and should be labelled as such.

### Gaps
- Leads not checked because of limited time: San Marco atrium Genesis mosaics (ships and dating), the Contarina wrecks, Culip VI, Boccalama, Venetian ship graffiti, the Archivio di Stato di Genova notarial cartularies (these almost never carry images), Marciana and Correr digital IIIF, and Matenadaran digitisation.
- I could not confirm whether lat. 10136 contains ship drawings, or whether Laiazzo is named on the Carte Pisane or on Pal. lat. 1362 A.
- Gallica, Heidelberg and Cini all blocked scripted access, so no IIIF manifest was verified.

## Which scholars and works would a specialist read first?

### Takeaway
Read Pryor for ships, Martin for the Venetian image corpus, Haug for the Genoese Annals images, Humphrey for the mariegole, and Edwards/Hellenkemper for Ayas.
