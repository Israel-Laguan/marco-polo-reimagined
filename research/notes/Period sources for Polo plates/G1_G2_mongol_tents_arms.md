# G1/G2: Period image sources for Mongol tents on wagons, and Mongol and Yuan arms and armour (1250–1300)

Confidence labels: **OPENED** = page opened and confirmed in this session; **CITED** = seen in a search result or catalogue snippet but not opened; **MEMORY** = from prior knowledge, not checked in this session. Check every MEMORY item before it goes into the bibliography. The research was cut short by a rate limit: about 17 tool calls in total, and several key pages were not opened.

## Is there any image of a ger carried whole on a wagon dated before 1350?

### Takeaway
I could not find a good source for G1. None of my searches turned up a dated image from before 1350 that shows a round felt tent carried whole on a wagon. No Golden Horde model or figurine of one turned up either. The best dated sources are texts, chiefly Rubruck (1253–55). The closest images are either too early (steppe clay wagon models, Liao tomb murals of carts) or later or undated (Topkapı Jami' al-tawarikh copies and the Topkapı albums).

### Cited Findings
- The search turned up no medieval image identified as the earliest depiction of a wagon-tent. Popular pages describe the *ger tergen* or khibitkha, but they cite no manuscript or object — [WHE image page](https://www.worldhistory.org/image/11226/wagon-yurt-or-khibitkha/.md) (CITED; this is an aggregator, not a source to propose).
- A search for Golden Horde clay wagon or yurt models found only Bronze Age analogues at the State Historical Museum, Moscow. One is a clay kibitka-wagon model from a mid-3rd-millennium BC kurgan (excavated by M.V. Andreeva, 1979). The other is a Srubnaya vessel (18th–17th c. BC) with a two-wheeled chariot and a probable tent beside it — [SHM exhibit 2422](https://nav.shm.ru/en/exhibits/2422/), [SHM exhibit 2424](https://nav.shm.ru/en/exhibits/2424/) (CITED). Both are far out of window. Use them only as a structural analogue.
- Topkapı Rashid al-Din copies: Hazine 1653 is dated 1314 and Hazine 1654 was copied three years later (1317). Hazine 1654 holds only about 7 original paintings, with blank spaces for the rest. Both later received Timurid (Shahrukh-era, c. 1425) additions — [Ghiasian 2022, Cambridge Core record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/D298994042B2643F0E25DAA747EA7E09) (CITED); [Commons category H.1654 (mirror)](https://download.osmand.net/wiki/Category:Jami_al-Tawarikh_(1317-1425)_H.1654,_TSMK) (CITED). I found no description of a tent-on-wagon scene in either copy.
- The "Mongol tents and prisoners of Genghis Khan" images on stock sites come from a Paris BnF copy, not Topkapı — [Photo12](https://www.photo12.com/en/image/asi15a00_012) (CITED). This is probably BnF Supplément persan 1113, which is c. 1430s and Timurid, so out of window (MEMORY).
- The Karakorum excavation reports are open access through the DAI series *Forschungen zur Archäologie Außereuropäischer Kulturen*. Bd. 8 is Mongolian-German Karakorum Expedition Vol. 1, *Excavations in the Craftsmen Quarter at the Main Road* (DOI 10.34780/133a-8dr5, ISBN 9783895006975). Bd. 12 is Franken, *Die "Große Halle" von Karakorum*, on the 2000–2006 excavations (DOI 10.34780/coic-cbqc; digital edition 2022) — [Zenon record](https://zenon.dainst.org/Record/000850527), [DAI publications catalogue](https://publications.dainst.org/books/dai/catalog/view/2052/2379/4075) (CITED, not opened). These hold urban material (ceramics, metalwork, architecture). The search showed no ger or wagon depiction in them.

### Proposals for G1 (ranked; none is an in-window ger-on-wagon image)
1. **Topkapı Palace Museum Library, Hazine 1653 and 1654 (Jami' al-tawarikh)**, Istanbul.
   - (a) What and where: holder TSMK. No stable viewer URL was opened.
   - (b) Why in window: the colophons are dated 1314 and 1317, so these copies hold the best chance of an Ilkhanid nomad-camp or migration scene outside Edinburgh and the Diez Albums.
   - (c) Access: Topkapı has no public IIIF that I know of. Images exist only as scattered Commons uploads and in publications.
   - (d) Search terms: "Hazine 1653", "H.1654", "TSMK", "Câmiü't-tevârîh"; Turkish *göçebe çadır*, *araba*.
   - (e) Main risk: much of H.1653 is Timurid, c. 1425 (painted for Shahrukh). Tell the 1314 paintings from the c. 1425 ones using Inal's and Ghiasian's folio lists. A wagon-tent scene there is unconfirmed.
   - Confidence: CITED. Ghiasian 2022 is NEW.
2. **Topkapı albums Hazine 2152, 2153 and 2160 ("Siyah Qalam" and Jalayirid/Ilkhanid album paintings).**
   - (a) What and where: holder TSMK. Not opened.
   - (b) Why: the albums contain nomad camp scenes with carts and tents, and some leaves are attributed to the 14th century.
   - (c) Access: viewer-only and in publications; Commons has partial uploads.
   - (d) Search terms: "Hazine 2153", "Saray albums", "Siyah Qalam".
   - (e) Main risk: the dating is stylistic and disputed (late 14th to 15th c.), so these are not dated.
   - Confidence: MEMORY.
3. **Liao tomb murals of carts with felt canopies** (e.g. Kulun Banner tombs, Inner Mongolia).
   - (a) What and where: Chinese excavation reports. Not opened.
   - (b) Why: excavation-dated pre-Mongol steppe carts with tent superstructures, 11th–12th c. A dated analogue only.
   - (c) Access: Chinese journals (*Wenwu*, *Kaogu*), CNKI, which is often paywalled.
   - (d) Search terms: 库伦辽墓 壁画 车帐 / 毡车 / 出行图.
   - (e) Main risk: too early, and these are box-carts, not round gers.
   - Confidence: MEMORY.
4. **Yuan tomb murals in Inner Mongolia and Shanxi** (e.g. Chifeng Yuanbaoshan; Liangcheng; 内蒙古元墓壁画).
   - (a) What and where: excavation reports. Not opened.
   - (b) Why: some Yuan tombs carry dated inscriptions and show Mongol-dress tomb occupants beneath tent-like settings. A wagon-tent scene is unconfirmed.
   - (c) Access: CNKI; sometimes as PDFs.
   - (d) Search terms: 元墓壁画 毡帐 / 穹庐 / 车.
   - (e) Main risk: probably no wagon, and many are labelled only "Yuan".
   - Confidence: MEMORY.
5. **DAI Karakorum volumes (Bd. 8, Bd. 12)** — as cited above. Use for urban context only (CITED).
6. **Steppe clay wagon models (State Historical Museum, Moscow; Kerch)** — structural analogues only, far too early (CITED for the SHM items).

### Inferences
- The only dated pre-1350 witness to a ger carried whole on a wagon is textual: Rubruck (1253–55) and, broadly, Marco Polo himself. The illustrator should work from Rubruck's description of a 20 ft tent on a cart drawn by 22 oxen. Use the Ilkhanid paintings the project already has for tent fabric and door details only.

### Gaps
- No Golden Horde or Mongolian archaeological find of a wagon-tent (axles plus a lattice) turned up. The Avraga and Kharbukh Balgas reports were not searched, because of the rate limit.
- The folio-level contents of H.1653 and H.1654 were not checked.
- Rubruck's Latin: not looked up here. He is assumed to be already in the project registry.

## Which dated Mongol-era armour and weapon finds have published plates freely online?

### Takeaway
The firmest dated material is Takashima, the 1281 shipwreck site, which has Japanese government excavation reports as free PDFs on 全国遺跡報告総覧, plus Sasaki's open Texas A&M thesis. Mongolian burials have recent (NEW) open-access Russian-language papers with plates, but these are mostly dated broadly to the 13th–14th c., not to 1250–1300.

### Cited Findings
- **Takashima reports on 全国遺跡報告総覧 (Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, sitereports.nabunken.go.jp)** (CITED):
  - Nagasaki Prefecture Buried Cultural Properties Center Report 25, 『鷹島海底遺跡』 (2018, NEW), covering the FY2013–17 surveys. PDF viewable — [record 21714](https://sitereports.nabunken.go.jp/21714) (identity of this ID not confirmed).
  - Matsuura City Report 12, 『松浦市鷹島海底遺跡』 (2023, NEW).
  - Matsuura City Report 4, 『松浦市鷹島海底遺跡』 総集編 (summary volume).
  - Takashima Town Report 1 (1993).
  - Sources: [62578](https://sitereports.nabunken.go.jp/62578), [62589](https://sitereports.nabunken.go.jp/62589), [131601](https://sitereports.nabunken.go.jp/131601).
  - I did not confirm which report illustrates the helmet or the *tetsuhau*.
- **KAKEN final report for project 23222002** (Ikeda Yoshifumi's large grant on Takashima, FY2011–15) is a free PDF — [kaken 23222002seika.pdf](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/file/KAKENHI-PROJECT-23222002/23222002seika.pdf) (CITED, not opened; the attribution to Ikeda is MEMORY).
- **Sasaki, Randall James, *The Origin of the Lost Fleet of the Mongol Empire*, M.A. thesis, Texas A&M University, issued 2009-05-15**; handle 1969.1/ETD-TAMU-3100 — [TDL record](https://tdl-ir.tdl.org/handle/1969.1/ETD-TAMU-3100) (OPENED: title, author and abstract confirmed).
  - The abstract mentions weaponry among the artefacts, but the record does not confirm plates.
  - The full-item page should carry the PDF.
  - Revised as a book, Texas A&M University Press, 2015 (NEW) — [MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/38210) (CITED).
  - An archive.org PDF of the book exists, but its authorisation is doubtful. Do not use it.
- **Karasuyr cemetery, Ulytau, Kazakhstan (Jochid ulus).** Radiocarbon dating puts the burials in the late 13th to early 14th c. Grave goods include fragments of laminar armour, arrowheads, knives and fire steels. Published in *Archaeology, Ethnology & Anthropology of Eurasia* 46/2 (2018, NEW) — [IAET abstract page](https://archaeology.nsc.ru/en/publications/jr-aeae-en/18-46-2/annot-12/) (CITED). The journal is open access (journal.archaeology.nsc.ru).
- **Yashil, northern Mongolia.** A study of armour plates from Mongol-period barrows used X-radiography on 7 plates. Its authors stress how little 12th–14th-c. Mongolian weaponry has been studied — [journal.archaeology.nsc.ru download 358](https://journal.archaeology.nsc.ru/jour/article/download/358/490) (CITED; 2024 per the search snippet, NEW). No direct radiocarbon date was seen.
- **Kharabchi bracers.** A lamellar-riveted coat with segmented bracers, dated mostly to the 14th c.; the earliest plates are possibly 13th c. — [Universum Humanitarium download](https://www.universum-humanitarium.ru/jour/article/download/61/60) (CITED). Partly out of window.
- **Tavan Tolgoi (Sükhbaatar aimag).** An elite Mongol cemetery. AMS dating of coffin wood from burial 2004-6 gave 860 ± 60 BP. Published in *Radiocarbon* — [Radiocarbon download 2964](https://journals.uair.arizona.edu/index.php/radiocarbon/article/download/2964/2723) (CITED). The finds are gold and belt fittings, not armour. The date is wide (it probably calibrates to 12th–13th c.).
- **Nogoon Gozgor burial 6.** A noblewoman's costume, 14th c. — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/outputs/670469364) (CITED). Out of window. It may suit G-costume, not G2.
- **Paul E. Chevedden, "The Invention of the Counterweight Trebuchet: A Study in Cultural Diffusion."** *Dumbarton Oaks Papers* 54 (2000): 71–116, doi:10.2307/1291833 (CITED). The JSTOR copy is paywalled, but older DOP papers are often free on doaks.org; that was not checked. Chevedden dates the Xiangyang engine to 1272 rather than 1273 — [medievalists.net listing](https://www.medievalists.net/2010/09/the-invention-of-the-counterweight-trebuchet-a-study-in-cultural-diffusion/) (CITED).

### Proposals for G2 (ranked)
1. **Takashima site reports on 全国遺跡報告総覧.**
   - (a) Holders: Nagasaki Prefecture and the Matsuura City Board of Education. URL: https://sitereports.nabunken.go.jp/ (search page not opened; individual records CITED).
   - (b) Dating: tied to the Kōan invasion, typhoon of 1281. The site yielded iron helmets, swords, arrows, crossbow fittings, *tetsuhau* ceramic bombs, and the bronze Phags-pa seal of a 管軍総把 dated 至元十四年 (1277) (MEMORY). The 1281 date is about as firm as dating gets.
   - (c) Access: direct PDF; the site has an API, but bot access was not tested.
   - (d) Search terms: 鷹島海底遺跡, 元寇, 冑, 刀剣, てつはう, 管軍総把印, 蒙古襲来.
   - (e) Main risks: corroded concretions, so many "objects" are X-ray or CT images; check copyright before reuse (describing in words is fine).
   - Confidence: CITED; the content is MEMORY.
2. **Mōko Shūrai Ekotoba (蒙古襲来絵詞), original.**
   - (a) Holder: Museum of the Imperial Collections, Sannomaru Shōzōkan. Designated a National Treasure in 2021 (MEMORY, NEW). Not opened.
   - (b) Dating: Suenaga's text is dated Einin 1 (1293) (MEMORY). Conlan places the making in the 1320s (snippet of Conlan's essay, CITED). It shows Mongol and Korean lamellar coats, helmets, bows, shields, and the exploding bomb.
   - (c) Access: Sannomaru's online images are viewer-only, with licence unknown. There is no IIIF for the original that I know of.
   - (d) Search terms: 蒙古襲来絵詞 宮内庁 三の丸尚蔵館.
   - (e) Main risk: later additions (see the next section).
3. **Kyushu University Library copy of the Ekotoba.**
   - (a) Kyushu University Library, call no. 726/モ/1, 2 scrolls, "肥後国阿蘇宮蔵画巻物寫" (a copy of the version then held at Aso Shrine), dated only "[近世後期]" — [handle 2324/411726](https://hdl.handle.net/2324/411726). **IIIF manifest:** https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/image/manifest_custom/1/820/411726.json. **Public Domain.** (OPENED)
   - (b) Why: the full composition, freely reusable at high resolution (9823 × 7825 px per the library notice — [lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp news 21444](https://www.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/en/news/21444), CITED).
   - (c) Access: open IIIF.
   - (d) Search terms: 蒙古襲来絵詞, 模本. Kyushu University's 「前近代日本－アジア関係資料デジタルアーカイブ」 has transcriptions.
   - (e) Main risk: **a late-Edo copy, not period work**, and it reproduces the scroll in its post-addition state. Use it to navigate, then check details against the original.
4. **Golden Horde and Mongolian burial papers in open Russian and Mongolian journals.**
   - (a) *Archaeology, Ethnology & Anthropology of Eurasia* (IAET SB RAS), *Археология евразийских степей*, *Поволжская археология*, *Studia Archaeologica* (mongoliajol.info). URLs as cited above.
   - (b) Why: radiocarbon or coin-dated kurgans with plates, bows and stirrups.
   - (c) Access: OA PDFs, all on DOAJ.
   - (d) Search terms: доспех монгольского времени, панцирные пластины, курган золотоордынского времени, стремена, налучье, сабля; Mongolian Cyrillic: Монголын эзэнт гүрний үеийн булш, хуяг, дуулга, сэлэм, нум.
   - (e) Main risk: most items are labelled "13th–14th c.", so they need a date from the burial itself.
   - Confidence: CITED.
5. **Hermitage Golden Horde finds** (e.g. the Gashun-Uste / Чингульский курган helmets and saddle mounts). Not opened. These are dated by context and coins. Access: viewer-only through the Hermitage collection site; no open API found. Search: Золотая Орда, шлем, Эрмитаж. Main risk: many are dated "13th–14th c."; the Chingul kurgan is pre-1240 (MEMORY).
6. **Yuan shi (元史) biographies of the Muslim engineers 阿老瓦丁 (ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn) and 亦思马因 (Ismāʿīl)** for the Xiangyang engine (囘囘砲 / 襄陽砲). These are text only, compiled in 1369–70 (MEMORY). Free full text is on ctext.org (Chinese Text Project; not opened). There is no image of the Xiangyang trebuchet from 1250–1300 that I could find. The siege scenes in the Ilkhanid paintings the project already has (Edinburgh, Diez) are the nearest images.

## Is the Mōko Shūrai Ekotoba viewable via IIIF, and which parts are later repaints?

### Takeaway
A complete Public Domain IIIF copy exists at Kyushu University. It is a late-Edo hand copy, not the original. The original (Sannomaru Shōzōkan) has no IIIF that I could find. Conlan's scholarship on the later additions holds that the three Mongol soldiers facing Suenaga in scroll 1, and probably the exploding bomb, were added in the 18th c. That part is MEMORY: Conlan's Princeton page returned 403 and the Bowdoin site redirects away.

### Cited Findings
- Kyushu University Library holds 蒙古襲来絵詞 (模本), 2 scrolls (upper 40×2255 cm, lower 40×1821 cm), dated "[近世後期]" (late Edo, estimated). Its note says it was copied from the scroll then held at Aso Shrine in Higo. The IIIF manifest is https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/image/manifest_custom/1/820/411726.json, and the record is marked Public Domain — [Kyushu record](https://hdl.handle.net/2324/411726) (OPENED).
- High-resolution images (9823 × 7825 px) are reusable without application, including commercially, under the library's open-reuse policy — [Kyushu Library notice](https://www.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/en/news/21444) (CITED).
- Conlan's essay says Takezaki Suenaga commissioned the scrolls in the 1320s, and that they remained with a few northern Kyushu warrior families for five centuries — [tconlan.scholar.princeton.edu/document/41](https://tconlan.scholar.princeton.edu/document/41) (CITED from a snippet; returned 403 when fetched, so **blocks scripts**).
- The Freer and Sackler holding is a 1916 woodblock-printed edition, not the original. Smithsonian (asia.si.edu) is known to fail — [asia.si.edu record](https://asia.si.edu/object/FSC-GR-780.829.1-3/) (CITED).
- The Princeton Manuscripts blog's 2014 "Japanese Scrolls Digitized" post covers 17th-c. Garrett scrolls, not this one — [blog](https://manuscripts.blogs.princeton.edu/2014/09/22/japanese-scrolls-digitized/) (CITED).

### Inferences
- Conflict: the 1293 date (Einin 1, in Suenaga's text) versus Conlan's 1320s date for the making of the scrolls. Treat the paintings as "c. 1293–1320s", which is still close to the window.
- The famous image of three Mongol bowmen beside the exploding *tetsuhau* is the passage most likely to be a later addition (MEMORY of Conlan's argument). Avoid it as a model for Mongol kit. Use instead the Mongol figures elsewhere in the scrolls (e.g. the ship-boarding scenes in scroll 2) once checked against Conlan's list.

### Gaps
- Conlan's list of added figures, by scroll and section, was not retrieved. Next steps: the Wayback Machine for bowdoin.edu/mongol-scrolls/, or Conlan 2001, pp. 1–30.
- No IIIF of the original was found. The Sannomaru Shōzōkan online images were not checked.
